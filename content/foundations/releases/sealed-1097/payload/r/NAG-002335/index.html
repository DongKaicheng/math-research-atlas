Integral Gauss charts give branch certificates

4 Analytic curves and skeleta

Open in the English reader · PDF page 797

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{theorem}[Integral Gauss charts give branch certificates]
\label{v037:f5:gausschartcertificate}
Suppose $U$ is an actual affinoid domain of $C^{\an}$ as
in Lemma~\ref{v037:f5:gausschartpointlifts}. Every component
point $v_E$ of $Y$ has an unramified branch certificate.
If $C$ admits a semistable algebraic model over the original
$O$, then every $v_E$ occurs as a component vertex of such
a model. Finitely many points obtained from finitely many
such integral charts can be retained simultaneously on one
original-field semistable algebraic model.
\end{theorem}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Fix the algebraically closed comparison field $\Omega$ of
Lemma~\ref{v036:f5:comparisonfield}. Put
\[
 R_{\Omega,0}=\Omega^\circ\langle T,Z\rangle/(g(T)Z-1),
 \qquad A_{\Omega,0}=A_0\otimes_{R_0}R_{\Omega,0}.
\]
The first algebra is admissible by
Proposition~\ref{fnd:formalprincipalopenflat}.
The second is finite projective over it, hence complete:
an idempotent makes it a closed direct summand of a finite
free complete module. It is $\pi$-torsion-free and
topologically of finite type, and is therefore admissible
by Theorem~\ref{fnd:formaltorsionfreepresentation}.
For the finite-type assertion, finite module generators
give finitely many algebra generators; reducing monomials
by their integral equations bounds all their products in
a finite module, so restricted series converge.
\textup{\'Etaleness} is preserved by
\cite[Tag 02GO]{StacksAlg}.

The actual scalar presentation
\ref{fnd:generalscalarpresentation} identifies
$R_{\Omega,0}[1/\pi]=R\widehat\otimes_K\Omega$.
Finite tensor comparison
\ref{fnd:generalizedfinitetensor} consequently identifies
$A_{\Omega,0}[1/\pi]=A\widehat\otimes_K\Omega$.
The domain base-change theorem
\ref{fnd:generalizeddomainbasechange} makes its spectrum
the actual domain $U_\Omega\subset C_\Omega^{\an}$.
Its special fibre is $Y_{\widetilde\Omega}$. Proposition
\ref{v037:f5:integralgaussdegree}, applied over $\Omega$,
makes it a reduced pure curve.

Choose a component $E'$ of $Y_{\widetilde\Omega}$ above
$E$. Such a component exists, and the component fibres
are exactly those described in
Lemma~\ref{v037:f5:geometriccomponentorbits}.
Let $w$ be its component point. Functoriality of
specialization in Proposition~\ref{fnd:formalglobalgenericfaithful}
and uniqueness in
Proposition~\ref{cand:reducedmodelcomponentpoints}
show that $w$ maps to $v_E$.

Choose three closed separable points $q_i$ of $Y$ and
geometric lifts $q_i'$ lying on $E'$, with the $q_i$
pairwise distinct. Here is a construction over the
original residue field. Inside $\widetilde\Omega$ take
the separable closure $k^{\mathrm{sep}}$ of $k$.
The purely inseparable step in
Lemma~\ref{v037:f5:geometriccomponentorbits} identifies
the components over these two fields. In the component
corresponding to $E'$, take a nonempty open subset avoiding
the other components and any finitely many previously
selected Galois orbits. Its \textup{\'etale} map to the
principal open of $\mathbb A^1_{k^{\mathrm{sep}}}$ has
open image by \cite[Tag 03WT]{StacksAlg}.
The field $k^{\mathrm{sep}}$ is infinite: a finite field
of cardinality $q$ has the separable polynomial
$X^q-X-1$ without a root. Thus that image contains a
rational point. Its nonempty \textup{\'etale} fibre has
a rational point by \cite[Tag 02GL]{StacksAlg}.
It is defined over a finite separable extension of $k$,
because the algebra has finitely many generators. Its
image in $Y$ is a closed separable point. Repeating this
three times gives the required distinct $q_i$ and $q_i'$.

Use Lemma~\ref{v037:f5:gausschartpointlifts} to form the
unramified marking datum $P=\coprod_i\operatorname{Spec}L_i^\circ$.
Each prescribed residue embedding
$\widetilde L_i\to\widetilde\Omega$ lifts to an isometric
$K$-embedding $L_i\to\Omega$. Indeed, in the monic
presentation of
Lemma~\ref{cand:unramifiedmodelbasechange}, the prescribed
residual root is simple. The henselian root construction
\ref{cand:completevaluationhenselian} in $\Omega^\circ$
lifts it to a root of the same polynomial. Irreducibility
over $K$ gives the embedding, and uniqueness in
Lemma~\ref{fnd:finiteextensionnorms} makes it isometric.
The resulting member of $P_K(\Omega)$ specializes to $q_i'$.

Let $B_i=]q_i'[$ in $\operatorname{Spf}A_{\Omega,0}$.
The formal map to the principal open of the unit disc
is adic, topologically finitely presented and formally
\textup{\'etale}. Composition with the formal open
immersion into the disc has the same properties: unique
lifting composes, and the relative presentation is obtained
by adjoining $Z$ with $g(T)Z=1$ to a finite presentation.
Completion gives the restricted-series presentation by
Lemma~\ref{fnd:adicquotientcompletion}.
Translate the disc coordinate by a lift of $t(q_i')$.
Proposition~\ref{v035:formal:tubeends} now makes $B_i$
an actual open unit disc whose closure in $U_\Omega$ is
$B_i\cup\{w\}$. In particular the selected marked point
lies in $B_i$, and the three tubes are disjoint.

These discs are open also in $C_\Omega^{\an}$.
To verify this ordinary-topological assertion, fix
$x\in B_i$ and an ambient affinoid neighbourhood $W$ of
$x$. The disc is an analytic domain and is absolutely
boundaryless: in its disc coordinate, every point belongs
to the strict interior of some smaller closed disc.
Since $W$ is an ambient ordinary neighbourhood, $B_i\cap W$
is an ordinary neighbourhood of $x$ in $B_i$. Choose an
affinoid neighbourhood $V$ of $x$ in $B_i$ with
$V\subset B_i\cap W$. Proposition~\ref{fnd:goodinteriorlocaltesting}
gives $x\in\operatorname{Int}(V/\Omega)$.
The composition formula
\ref{fnd:affinoidinteriorcompositionequality} for
$V\to W\to\mathcal M(\Omega)$ then gives
$x\in\operatorname{Int}(V/W)$. The domain-interior
theorem \ref{fnd:affinoiddomainordinaryinterior}
makes $V$ a neighbourhood of $x$ in $W$, and hence in
$C_\Omega^{\an}$. This proves openness.
Finally $U_\Omega$ is compact and closed in the Hausdorff
curve $C_\Omega^{\an}$, by
Propositions~\ref{fnd:boundedspectrumcompact} and
\ref{fnd:analytificationseparatedtopology}. Thus the same
closure formula holds in the ambient curve.
The data $w,P,B_1,B_2,B_3$ form the certificate of
Definition~\ref{v037:f5:branchcertificate}.

If an original-field semistable model exists, the two
remaining assertions are precisely the realization and
finite simultaneous-insertion assertions of
Theorem~\ref{v037:f5:vertexcertificate}.
\end{proof}
