Global additivity for a finite affinoid cover

5 Tropicalization, multiplicities and balancing

Open in the English reader · PDF page 1026

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{theorem}[Global additivity for a finite affinoid cover]
\label{v037:localization:globalcoveradditivity}
Let $X=\mathcal M(A)$ be nonempty and pure of analytic dimension
$d$, let $M$ be a finite $A$-module, and let a finite tuple of
analytic units $f$ define $t:X\to\mathbb R^n$.
Suppose that $V_1,\ldots,V_q$ is a finite affinoid-domain cover
of $X$. For nonempty $I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,q\}$ put
\[
 V_I=\bigcap_{i\in I}V_i,\qquad
 A_I=\mathcal O(V_I),\qquad
 M_I=M\otimes_A A_I,\qquad f_I=f|_{V_I}.
\]
An empty intersection contributes the zero chain and zero integral.
Then, as integral weighted $d$-chains in the same $\mathbb R^n$,
\begin{equation}
\label{v037:localization:eqglobalcoverchain}
 \mathcal T_f(M)=
 \sum_{\varnothing\ne I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,q\}}
       (-1)^{|I|+1}\mathcal T_{f_I}(M_I).
\end{equation}
In particular, for every smooth real superform $\alpha$ of
bidegree $(d,d)$ defined on a neighborhood of the actual compact
image $t(X)$,
\begin{equation}
\label{v037:localization:eqglobalcoverintegral}
 \mathcal I_{M,f}(\alpha)=
 \sum_{\varnothing\ne I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,q\}}
       (-1)^{|I|+1}\mathcal I_{M_I,f_I}(\alpha).
\end{equation}
The identities are unchanged by finite refinements of the cover
or of the polyhedral complexes used to represent the chains.
\end{theorem}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Corollary~\ref{fnd:generalizeddomainintersections} makes every
$V_I$ an actual affinoid domain of $X$, with its compatible
restriction algebra. Every nonempty $V_I$ is pure of analytic
dimension $d$ by Theorem~\ref{v036:analytic:domainpurity}.
The finite module $M_I$ has its canonical complete norm by
Theorem~\ref{fnd:generalizedfinitemodules}. Thus all the nonzero
terms in the claimed identity have the stated analytic meaning.

Theorem~\ref{v037:globaltrop:finiteimage} makes the actual images
$t(X)$ and $t(V_I)$ finite integral affine polyhedral sets.
It also makes
\[
 E=t(\partial_KX)\ \cup\!
       \bigcup_{\varnothing\ne I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,q\}}
                 t(\partial_KV_I)
\]
a finite polyhedral subset of $t(X)$ of dimension at most $d-1$;
for $d=0$ it is empty. By
Lemma~\ref{cand:polyhedralrefinementproduct}, choose one finite
common subdivision of $t(X)$ in which every $t(V_I)$ and $E$
is a subcomplex, and which also refines the representatives of
all the finitely many chains under consideration.

Fix a $d$-cell $\sigma$ of this subdivision and a point
$w\in\operatorname{relint}(\sigma)$. Its relative interior
misses $E$. Since the whole image has dimension at most $d$,
near $w$ no cell other than $\sigma$ is present. Hence the
local moment image is contained in the affine $d$-plane
$w+(N_\sigma)_{\mathbb R}$, where $N_\sigma$ is its saturated
tangent lattice. The whole fibre $F_w$ is interior in $X$, and
each $F_w\cap V_i$ is interior in $V_i$, by the definition of
$E$. The domains cover $F_w$ because they cover $X$.

Apply Theorem~\ref{v037:localization:fibrecover} to this fibre
and the finite module $M$. It gives
\[
 m_{M,f}(w)=
 \sum_{\varnothing\ne I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,q\}}
           (-1)^{|I|+1}m_{M_I,f_I}(w),
\]
with zero for the empty selected fibres. The common subdivision
ensures that $\operatorname{relint}(\sigma)$ is either contained
in $t(V_I)$ or disjoint from it. By
the \hyperref[fnd:nag002405:global-finite-module-chain-weights]{finite-module chain and weights}, the terms in this
identity are exactly the coefficients of the corresponding
global chains on $\sigma$. Thus these coefficients agree on
every $d$-cell. Lower-dimensional cells carry no coefficient
in a $d$-chain. This proves
\eqref{v037:localization:eqglobalcoverchain}; it also covers the
case $\dim t(X)<d$, when there are no $d$-cells.

All these chains have compact polyhedral support contained in
$t(X)$. Apply the \hyperref[fnd:nag002405:finite-cell-functional]{finite-cell functional} to the same smooth form on each term. Their common representatives have finitely many
cells, and the integral is the sum of the cell integrals times
their integer coefficients. The coefficient identity proves
\eqref{v037:localization:eqglobalcoverintegral}.
Subdivision invariance is part of
the \hyperref[fnd:nag002405:finite-cell-functional]{finite-cell functional}. Repeating the
argument for any finite refinement of the affinoid cover gives
the same global chain $\mathcal T_f(M)$ and the same integral,
which proves the final assertion.
\end{proof}
