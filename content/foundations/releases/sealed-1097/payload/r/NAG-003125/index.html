Statement · exact TeX
\begin{proposition}[A support-sensitive counterexample in every dimension]
\label{f9sc:dropcounter}
Let $\cX=\mathbf P^d_R$, $d\geq2$, $V=\cX_k$, and take
$(\cL,s)=(\cO(1),\pi Z_0)$. With prescribed support $T=V$,
\[
 G_{(\cL,s),V}[V]=c_1(\cO_V(1))\cap[V]\ne0
                           \quad\text{in }\CH_{d-1}(V).
\]
After another $d-1$ hyperplane operations its degree is one. Thus an
operation that discards a component when its restricted section is zero
fails the required vertical Chern compatibility.

More strongly, let $D=Z(\pi Z_0)$, with closed support $V\cup H_0$,
where $H_0=\{Z_0=0\}$. The same nonzero class maps to zero in
$\CH_{d-1}(D)$. Knowing only its image in the ambient zero scheme
therefore does not determine the operation on the original support $V$.
\end{proposition}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
The restricted section is identically zero on $V$, so the definition
gives the first Chern class. Its representative $V\cap H_0$ followed
by $d-1$ other coordinate hyperplanes is one rational point with local
quotient length one. Successive coordinate quotients compute its degree as one, proving nonvanishing on $V$.
Inside $D$, the horizontal integral $H_0\simeq\mathbf P^{d-1}_R$
is an allowed scheme for rational equivalence. Its rational function
$\pi$ has divisor $V\cap H_0$ with coefficient one. The image of
our class is consequently zero in $\CH_{d-1}(D)$. Inside $V$ that
horizontal rational-equivalence relation is unavailable. This is why
the operation must be constructed on each $T$, not recovered from an
ambient image after the first intersection.
\end{proof}
