The algebraic tropical degree formula

5 Tropicalization, multiplicities and balancing

Open in the English reader · PDF page 925

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{theorem}[The algebraic tropical degree formula]
\label{v035:degreeformula}
Let $\varphi:T_N\to T_{N'}$ be a homomorphism of split tori
over a complete real-valued non-Archimedean field $K$, and write $a:N\to N'$ for
its lattice map. Let $X\subset T_N$ be a pure
$d$-dimensional closed subscheme and $Y\subset T_{N'}$ an
integral $d$-dimensional closed subscheme. Suppose that
$\varphi|_X:X\to Y$ is dominant with nonempty zero-dimensional
generic fibre of scheme degree $\delta$.

There is a finite union $E_{\mathrm{trop}}$ of polyhedra of
dimension less than $d$ in $\operatorname{Trop}(Y)$ such that,
for $w'\notin E_{\mathrm{trop}}$, its tropical preimages form
a finite set $\{w_i\}_i$. Each $w_i$ lies in the relative
interior of a maximal initial cell $\sigma_i$, while $w'$
lies in the relative interior of a maximal initial cell
$\sigma'$, and
\begin{equation}
 \delta\,m_Y(\sigma')
   =\sum_i m_X(\sigma_i)
       [N'_{\sigma'}:a(N_{\sigma_i})].
 \label{v035:eqdegreeformula}
\end{equation}
For the polyhedral pushforward of
Theorem~\ref{cand:balancedpolyhedralpushforward}, this is the
weighted-cycle identity
\[
 a_*\operatorname{Trop}(X)=\delta\operatorname{Trop}(Y).
\]
\end{theorem}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: choose the finite free locus and the regular weights.}
Choose $h$ by Lemma~\ref{v035:degreefinitefreelocus}, and let
$E=Y\setminus Y_h$ have its reduced closed structure. Every
irreducible component of $E$ has dimension less than $d$:
a chain of primes above a nonzero prime of the domain
$\Gamma(Y,\mathcal O_Y)$ becomes one step longer after
adjoining its zero prime. Apply
Theorem~\ref{cand:tropicalpuredimension} to the finitely many
components of $E$. Their tropicalizations form a finite union
of lower-dimensional polyhedra.

Also exclude the nonmaximal cells of $\operatorname{Trop}(Y)$,
the images of nonmaximal cells of $\operatorname{Trop}(X)$,
and the images of maximal source cells on which $a$ has rank
less than $d$. Intersect these images with the target complex.
The finite polyhedral elimination and refinement in
Theorem~\ref{cand:balancedpolyhedralpushforward} give a finite
union $E_{\mathrm{trop}}$ of polyhedra of dimension less than
$d$. It cannot contain the relative interior of a maximal
target cell.

Fix $w'\notin E_{\mathrm{trop}}$. Each of its tropical
preimages is in the relative interior of a maximal source
cell on whose direction space $a$ is injective. Each of
finitely many such cells contributes at most one point.
Thus the preimage set is finite. Near each $w_i$, the image
of its full-dimensional source cell is contained in the
local tropical support at $w'$, which is the direction space
of $\sigma'$. The dimensions are equal, so
\[
 a(N_{\sigma_i})_{\mathbf R}=N'_{\sigma',\mathbf R}.
\]
The indices in \eqref{v035:eqdegreeformula} are therefore finite.

\emph{Step 2: decompose the actual inverse-image algebra.}
Use Theorem~\ref{cand:realinitialdegeneration} to make one
complete isometric extension $L/K$ realizing $w'$ and all
the $w_i$, with nontrivial valuation. The finite free algebra
on $Y_h$ remains free of rank $\delta$ after extension, and
Proposition~\ref{cand:tropicalweightlocalconstancy} preserves
all the weights. In particular, the subsequent argument uses
the same finite free module even if $Y_L$ is reducible or
nonreduced.

Write $Y_{w'}=\mathcal M(C)$ for the actual target tropical
fibre over $L$. Since $w'\notin\operatorname{Trop}(E)$,
$h$ has no zero on this fibre. It is therefore a unit in $C$
by Theorem~\ref{fnd:generalizedpointunitcriterion}, and
$Y_{w'}\subset(Y_h)^{\an}$. The explicit tensor description
in Theorem~\ref{fnd:analytificationfiniteclosed} shows that
the inverse image is $\mathcal M(D)$, where $D$ is a finite
$C$-algebra free of rank $\delta$.

Every point of this inverse image has tropical weight one
of the $w_i$. Its subset of weight $w_i$ is the actual
affinoid domain $X_{w_i}=\mathcal M(B_i)$ of
Proposition~\ref{cand:torusfibrealgebra}. These subsets are
open and closed: they are fibres of a continuous map with
values in a finite Hausdorff set. Applying the structure
sheaf property of Theorem~\ref{fnd:affinoidfullgcomparison}
to this finite disjoint cover gives
\begin{equation}
 D\simeq\prod_i B_i.
 \label{v035:eqdegreefibreproduct}
\end{equation}
The inverse image is nonempty. Indeed, $C\ne0$ and
$\delta>0$, so $D\ne0$; the affinoid nonemptiness theorem
\ref{fnd:generalizedpointunitcriterion} gives a point of
$\mathcal M(D)$. Consequently the set of $w_i$ is nonempty.

\emph{Step 3: compare all degrees over one Gauss field.}
The saturated lattice $N'_{\sigma'}$ is a direct summand
of $N'$ by Lemma~\ref{cand:elementarylatticesplitting}.
Choose $q:N'\to\mathbf Z^d$ whose restriction to it is an
isomorphism. Divide the corresponding target monomials by
their values at a translating point of weight $w'$. This
gives one unit-polycircle algebra
$A=L\langle U_1^{\pm1},\ldots,U_d^{\pm1}\rangle$ mapping to
$C$ and to every $B_i$.
After the respective source translations, the induced
monomials differ from those for $q\circ a$ by constants
of absolute value one, because $a(w_i)=w'$.

Let $H$ be the completed Gauss residue field of $A$.
Proposition~\ref{v035:degreeinitialweight} gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \dim_H(C\widehat\otimes_A H)&=m_Y(\sigma'),\\
 \dim_H(B_i\widehat\otimes_A H)
   &=m_X(\sigma_i)[N'_{\sigma'}:a(N_{\sigma_i})].
 \end{aligned}
\]
All the maps are finite with the canonical finite-module
norms, so Proposition~\ref{fnd:generalizedfinitebasechange}
identifies these completed tensors with the algebraic ones.
Since $D$ is free of rank $\delta$ over $C$, tensoring its
free basis over $A$ with $H$ gives
\[
 \dim_H(D\otimes_AH)=\delta\dim_H(C\otimes_AH).
\]
Taking dimensions in \eqref{v035:eqdegreefibreproduct}
proves \eqref{v035:eqdegreeformula}.

Theorem~\ref{cand:tropicalbalancing} makes both weighted
tropicalizations balanced cycles. Choose compatible source
and target subdivisions as in the polyhedral pushforward
theorem. The equality holds on a
dense relative open subset of every maximal target cell.
Both weights are constant on each such cell by
Proposition~\ref{cand:tropicalweightlocalconstancy} and the
definition of polyhedral pushforward. This proves the
weighted-cycle identity.
\end{proof}
