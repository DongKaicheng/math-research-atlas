Purity of affinoid domains

5 Tropicalization, multiplicities and balancing

Open in the English reader · PDF page 953

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{theorem}[Purity of affinoid domains]
\label{v036:analytic:domainpurity}
Let $X=\mathcal M(B)$ be pure of analytic dimension $d$. Every
nonempty affinoid domain $V=\mathcal M(C)\subseteq X$ is pure of
analytic dimension $d$.
\end{theorem}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
First assume that $K$ is nontrivially valued and that $B$ and $C$
are strict. The restriction $B\to C$ is flat by
Proposition~\ref{fnd:generalizeddomainflatness}. If $\mathfrak n$ is
maximal in $C$, then $C/\mathfrak n$ is finite over $K$ by
Theorem~\ref{fnd:strictpointunitcriterion}. The image of $B$ in this
field is a finite-dimensional domain, hence a field by
\cite[Tag 00GS(1)]{StacksAlg}. Thus
$\mathfrak m=\mathfrak n\cap B$ is maximal. Flat going down,
\cite[Tag 00HS]{StacksAlg}, and
Lemma~\ref{v036:analytic:maximaldimension} give
\[
                         \dim C_{\mathfrak n}\geq d.
\]
On the other hand, Proposition~\ref{fnd:affinoiddimensiondomaincover}
gives $\dim C\leq d$. Analytic and Krull dimensions agree here
because both algebras are strict.

Fix a minimal prime $\mathfrak q$ of $C$. The minimal primes are
finite by \cite[Tag 00FR]{StacksAlg}. Choose an element $h$ in all
the other minimal primes and outside $\mathfrak q$: for each other
prime choose an element outside $\mathfrak q$ and take their
product; take $h=1$ if there is no other prime. By
Lemma~\ref{fnd:genericanalyticpointdensity}, the integral affinoid
$C/\mathfrak q$ has a point where $h$ does not vanish. Choose
$c\in K^\times$ with $|c|\leq|h|$ at that point. The strict Laurent
domain
\[
             \mathcal M\bigl((C/\mathfrak q)\langle U\rangle/
                                      (hU-c)\bigr)
\]
is nonempty by Theorem~\ref{fnd:generalizedrationalpoints}.
A maximal residue field of its algebra is finite over
$K$. Its map from $C$ has maximal kernel $\mathfrak n$, contains
$\mathfrak q$, and does not contain $h$. Hence
$\mathfrak qC_{\mathfrak n}$ is the only minimal prime of
$C_{\mathfrak n}$. The spectra of this local ring and its quotient
by that prime have the same prime chains. It follows that
\[
 d\leq\dim C_{\mathfrak n}
      =\dim(C/\mathfrak q)_{\mathfrak n}
      \leq\dim(C/\mathfrak q)\leq d.
\]
Every minimal-prime quotient of $C$ consequently has dimension $d$.

For arbitrary $K$, $B$, and $C$, choose one free-radius extension
$L=K_r$ making both algebras strict, by
Theorem~\ref{fnd:simultaneousstrictification}. If $K$ is trivially
valued, include a radius different from $1$ among the additional
radii, so that $L$ is nontrivially valued. Theorem
\ref{fnd:generalizeddomainbasechange} identifies $\mathcal M(C_L)$
with the corresponding affinoid domain in $\mathcal M(B_L)$.
Lemma~\ref{v036:analytic:freecomponents} makes $B_L$ pure of Krull
dimension $d$ and ensures $C_L\ne0$. The strict case makes $C_L$
pure of dimension $d$. Apply the same lemma to $C$ to descend this
purity, with analytic dimension, to the original field.
\end{proof}
Standing context 1 · exact TeX

Throughout this section $K$ is complete for its specified real-valued
non-Archimedean absolute value. Generalized affinoid algebras and the
trivial valuation are allowed. An affinoid algebra $B$ is pure of
analytic dimension $d$ if
$\dim_K\mathcal M(B/\mathfrak p)=d$ for every minimal prime
$\mathfrak p$ of $B$. We use analytic dimension before strictification;
it need not equal the Krull dimension of a generalized affinoid algebra.

\begin{lemma}[Components under a free-radius extension]
\label{v036:analytic:freecomponents}
Let $B$ be a generalized $K$-affinoid algebra, let $r$ be a finite
$K$-free polyradius, and put $L=K_r$ and
$B_L=B\widehat\otimes_KL$. If $B$ is integral, then $B_L$ is integral.
