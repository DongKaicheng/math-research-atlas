Statement · exact TeX
\begin{proposition}[Finite-presentation accounting; F8ST-F]
\label{prop:finite}
For a norm-admissible finite map \(p:\cM(C)\to\cM(A)\),
\(R=\cO_{\cM(A),x}\), and a finite \(C\)-module \(M\), there are
canonical identifications
\[
 C\otimes_A R=\prod_{y\in p^{-1}(x)}\cO_{\cM(C),y},\qquad
 M\otimes_A R=\bigoplus_{y\in p^{-1}(x)}\widetilde M_y.
\]
If the module on the right has finite \(R\)-length, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finitepush}
 \length_R(M\otimes_AR)
 =\sum_{y\in p^{-1}(x)}[\kappa(y):\kappa(x)]
       \length_{\cO_{\cM(C),y}}\widetilde M_y.
\end{equation}
For a finite algebra \(E\) over a field \(H\), correspondingly,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:degree}
 \dim_HE=\sum_{\mathfrak q\in\Spec E}
       [\kappa(\mathfrak q):H]\,\length_{E_{\mathfrak q}}E_{\mathfrak q}.
\end{equation}
These formulas do not assert that a finite map preserves each local length.
\end{proposition}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
The ring product is \tagg{NAG-001735}. Tensor a finite presentation of
\(M\) with that product to obtain the module formula; a finite direct
product is a direct sum as modules. Each local factor is finite over
\(R\). A composition factor at \(y\) is \(\kappa(y)\), which has
\(R\)-length \([\kappa(y):\kappa(x)]\). Additivity proves
\eqref{eq:finitepush}; the same argument over \(H\) proves
\eqref{eq:degree}. Finite tensor comparison \tagg{NAG-001565}
identifies the ordinary and completed finite-module tensors with their
canonical norms. It does not by itself assert flatness or a reduced
closed fibre.
\end{proof}
