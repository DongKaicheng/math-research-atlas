Geometric components over one original component

4 Analytic curves and skeleta

Open in the English reader · PDF page 788

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{lemma}[Geometric components over one original component]
\label{v037:f5:geometriccomponentorbits}
Let $Y$ be a scheme of finite type over a field $k$, and let
$\overline k$ be an algebraic closure. The geometric irreducible
components over any one irreducible component of $Y$ form one orbit
under $\operatorname{Aut}(\overline k/k)$. Consequently an invariant
subset of the geometric components is the entire inverse image of
a subset of the original components.
\end{lemma}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Let $k^{\mathrm{sep}}\subset\overline k$ be the separable closure
of $k$ in $\overline k$. The assertion over $k^{\mathrm{sep}}$ is
\cite[Tag 04KY]{StacksAlg}. In characteristic zero this is already
the assertion. In characteristic $p>0$, the remaining extension
$\overline k/k^{\mathrm{sep}}$ is purely inseparable: an irreducible
polynomial over $k$ has the form $Q(T^{p^e})$ with $Q$ separable,
by successively removing zero derivatives. A root therefore has
a $p^e$-th power separable over $k$.

For a $k^{\mathrm{sep}}$-algebra $A$, write
$B=A\otimes_{k^{\mathrm{sep}}}\overline k$.
This is a faithfully flat extension: a field extension is flat by
the finite-ideal criterion \cite[Tag 00HD]{StacksAlg}, is faithfully
flat by \cite[Tag 00HP]{StacksAlg}, and its tensor functor on
$A$-modules is the same coefficient tensor functor.
Thus every prime of $A$ has a prime above it by
\cite[Tag 00HQ]{StacksAlg}.
For every $b\in B$, some $p$-power $b^{p^e}$ belongs to $A$:
write $b$ as a finite coefficient sum and choose $e$ simultaneously
for its finitely many coefficients.
For a prime $Q$ above $P$, one therefore has
\[
                 b\in Q\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad b^{p^e}\in P.
\]
This determines $Q$ uniquely and preserves inclusions in both
directions. Hence minimal primes, and consequently irreducible
components, correspond. These correspondences commute with affine
restriction and with automorphisms of $k^{\mathrm{sep}}/k$.
Every such automorphism extends uniquely to $\overline k$:
the image of $a$ is the unique $p^e$-th root of the prescribed
image of $a^{p^e}\in k^{\mathrm{sep}}$.
The component orbit assertion over $k^{\mathrm{sep}}$ therefore
gives the assertion over $\overline k$. The final statement follows
by taking unions of these orbits.
\end{proof}
