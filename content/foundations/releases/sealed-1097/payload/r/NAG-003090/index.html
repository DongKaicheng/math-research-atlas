8 Intrinsic smooth forms, integration and Stokes

For the following estimate, let $D_M:E\to\mathbb R$ be linear for each real $M\geq0$.
\begin{proposition}[A quantitative estimate across presentations]\label{prop:gauge}
Let $p_i$ be a continuous seminorm on $E_i$, let $w_i>0$, and let
$\varepsilon(M)>0$. Suppose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:commonmodulus}
 |D_M Q_i(\eta)|\leq\varepsilon(M)w_i p_i(\eta)
 \quad\hbox{for all }i,M,\eta.
\end{equation}
Then the finite-decomposition gauge
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gauge}
 P_w(x)=\inf_{x=\sum_{i\in F}Q_i\eta_i}
                  \sum_{i\in F}w_i p_i(\eta_i),\qquad F\text{ finite},
\end{equation}
is a continuous seminorm on $E$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strongbound}
 \sup_{x\in B}|D_M(x)|
 \leq\varepsilon(M)\sup_{x\in B}P_w(x)
\end{equation}
for every bounded set $B$. Hence $\varepsilon(M)\to0$ gives strong-dual
convergence, even when no presentation is fixed for the members of $B$.
The premise must hold with the actual generators of the topology whose
bounded sets are used.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The spanning assumption makes the infimum finite. Concatenating finite
decompositions proves subadditivity (with arbitrary small errors before
taking infima); scaling proves absolute homogeneity. Repeated indices
can be merged by the seminorm triangle inequality. Thus $P_w$ is a
seminorm, possibly with a kernel. The one-term decomposition gives
$P_w Q_i\leq w_i p_i$, so every composite is continuous. The final
criterion gives continuity of $P_w$. Apply \eqref{eq:commonmodulus}
to any finite decomposition, sum, and take its infimum to get
$|D_M(x)|\leq\varepsilon(M)P_w(x)$. Boundedness now proves
\eqref{eq:strongbound}.
\end{proof}\endgroup
