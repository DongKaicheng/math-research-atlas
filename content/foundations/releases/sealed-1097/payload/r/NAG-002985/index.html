Full faithfulness for polarized isomorphisms

D Polarized towers with compatible cyclic actions

Open in the English reader · PDF page 1976

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{lemma}[Full faithfulness for polarized isomorphisms]
\label{cand:f5eq:polarizedfullfaithfulness}
Let $R$ be a ring, let $\pi\in R$, and suppose that the canonical
map $R\to\varprojlim_{n\geq1}R/\pi^nR$ is an isomorphism.
Write $R_n=R/\pi^nR$. Let $X,Y$ be proper, flat, finitely presented
$R$-schemes, and let $\mathcal L,\mathcal M$ be relatively ample
invertible sheaves on them. Reduction gives a bijection
\[
 \operatorname{Isom}^{\rm pol}_R((X,\mathcal L),(Y,\mathcal M))
 \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
 \varprojlim_n\operatorname{Isom}^{\rm pol}_{R_n}
 ((X_n,\mathcal L_n),(Y_n,\mathcal M_n)).
 \tag{1}
\]
Here the inverse limit means compatible polarized isomorphisms with
respect to the specified reduction maps. The analogous statement
holds for polarized isomorphisms covering a fixed automorphism
$b$ of $\operatorname{Spec}R$ that preserves the closed subscheme
$\operatorname{Spec}R_n$ for every $n$. No Noetherian hypothesis
on $R$ is imposed.
\end{lemma}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Apply \stacks{0D4F} to each polarized scheme, allowing the identity
thickening of its affine base, and enlarge the two bounds to a
common $d_0\geq1$. For every $d\geq d_0$, the modules
\[
 P_d=H^0(X,\mathcal L^{\otimes d}),\qquad
 Q_d=H^0(Y,\mathcal M^{\otimes d})
\]
are finite projective over $R$. The arbitrary-base-change assertion
in the proof of that lemma identifies their reductions with
$H^0(X_n,\mathcal L_n^{\otimes d})$ and
$H^0(Y_n,\mathcal M_n^{\otimes d})$. Moreover the graded algebras
\[
 A_X=R\oplus\bigoplus_{d\geq d_0}P_d,
 \qquad A_Y=R\oplus\bigoplus_{d\geq d_0}Q_d
 \tag{2}
\]
functorially reconstruct the pairs as
$(\operatorname{Proj}A_X,\mathcal O(1))$ and
$(\operatorname{Proj}A_Y,\mathcal O(1))$.

For any finite projective $R$-module $P$, the canonical map
$P\to\varprojlim_n P/\pi^nP$ is an isomorphism. Indeed, write
$P$ as the image of an idempotent matrix $E$ on $R^m$.
The assertion becomes
$E R^m=\varprojlim_n\overline E R_n^m$, which follows from the
assumed identity for $R$. This is the finite-projective completeness
argument in the proof of \stacks{0D4B}.

Let $(f_n,\lambda_n)$ be a compatible system on the right of (1).
For every $d\geq d_0$, its maps on sections are
\[
 u_{n,d}:Q_d/\pi^nQ_d\longrightarrow P_d/\pi^nP_d,
 \qquad s\longmapsto\lambda_n^{\otimes d}(f_n^*s).
 \tag{3}
\]
Taking the inverse limit in each degree gives $u_d:Q_d\to P_d$.
The same construction applied to the inverse polarized isomorphisms
gives inverse maps. The $u_d$ preserve products: after reduction
modulo every $\pi^n$ the required equality holds by (3), and its
values lie in the separated module $P_{d+d'}$.
Together with the identity on degree zero, they therefore give a
graded isomorphism $A_Y\simeq A_X$.

Functoriality of Proj, including the maps on all twists in
\stacks{01MY}, gives an isomorphism of schemes and an isomorphism
of their $\mathcal O(1)$ sheaves. The canonical identifications
from \stacks{0D4F} turn this into $(f,\lambda)$ on the original
pairs. Its reductions are exactly $(f_n,\lambda_n)$, by the
functoriality of those identifications. Conversely, any polarized
isomorphism inducing the prescribed reductions has the same maps
(3), hence the same $u_d$ by completeness and separation. The same
functorial reconstruction makes it equal to $(f,\lambda)$.
This proves both directions of (1).

The limit was taken degree by degree; it was not interchanged with
an infinite direct sum. All degrees $d\geq d_0$ were retained,
so the reconstruction recovers the actual invertible sheaf and
its map, not merely a selected tensor power.
Finally, an isomorphism covering $b$ is an $R$-isomorphism to
the pullback pair $b^*(Y,\mathcal M)$. This twist commutes with
reduction by the stated hypothesis on $b$. Applying (1) to the
twisted target proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}
