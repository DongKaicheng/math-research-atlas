Sectorwise full-fibre overlap and projection comparison

I Descent of the polarized mixed tower

Open in the English reader · PDF page 2102

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{theorem}[Sectorwise full-fibre overlap and projection comparison]
\label{mso:sectorcomparison}
Use Definition~\ref{mso:setup}, the $V_j$, and the neighbourhoods
$U_{ij},W_j$ of Lemma~\ref{mso:unionoverlap}.
After shrinking the real neighbourhoods, the following hold.

For each top cone $\sigma$ there is a finite union $E_\sigma$
of proper affine hyperplanes in the affine plane
$w_0+\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\sigma$ such that, whenever
$v\in w_0+\operatorname{relint}\sigma$ is sufficiently close to
$w_0$ and $v\notin E_\sigma$, the whole original moment fibre
$F_v=t^{-1}(v)$ satisfies
\[
 F_v=\coprod_j F_{j,v},\qquad
 F_{j,v}=F_v\cap W_j=F_v\cap U_{1j}=F_v\cap U_{2j}.
\]
Every nonempty $F_{j,v}$ lies in the absolute interior of all
three domains in the displayed equality.
The equalities are equalities of actual analytic domains,
so their full algebras are canonically the same algebra $D_{j,v}$.

For $u=Q_iv$, let $\Lambda_i(u)$ be the finite set of original
moment weights on the entire projected level
$\{y\in X:Q_it(y)=u\}$.
For every such regular $u$, restriction gives a bounded
isomorphism with bounded inverse
\begin{equation}
\label{mso:product}
 B_i(u)\ \simeq\
       \prod_{v'\in\Lambda_i(u)}\ \prod_{j=1}^r D_{j,v'}.
\end{equation}
Zero factors are omitted. The corresponding idempotent
$e_{i,j,v'}$ is uniquely determined by the actual labelled domain
$F_{j,v'}$, not by a numerical match of lengths.
All original moment weights and all Gauss-lift neighbourhoods
are included.

Let $H_i(v)$ be the Gauss field of $R_i(Q_iv)$ and put
\[
 E_{i,j}(v)=D_{j,v}
                \widehat\otimes_{R_i(Q_iv)}H_i(v),\qquad
 L_{i,j}(v)=M\otimes_AE_{i,j}(v).
\]
Then $D_{j,v}$ is norm-admissible finite over both indicated
target algebras, and
\begin{equation}
\label{mso:weightcomparison}
 \frac{\dim_{H_1(v)}L_{1,j}(v)}{e_{1,\sigma}}
       =\frac{\dim_{H_2(v)}L_{2,j}(v)}{e_{2,\sigma}}
       =m_{M|W_j,f}(v)\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}.
\end{equation}
The same value is obtained using $U_{1j}$ or $U_{2j}$.
An empty factor has value zero.

For either projection the numerator retains the full arithmetic:
\begin{equation}
\label{mso:artinianlength}
 \dim_{H_i(v)}L_{i,j}(v)
 =\sum_{\mathfrak m\in\operatorname{Max}E_{i,j}(v)}
  \operatorname{length}_{E_{i,j}(v)_{\mathfrak m}}
          L_{i,j}(v)_{\mathfrak m}
       \,[\kappa(\mathfrak m):H_i(v)].
\end{equation}
This includes all local factors, full inseparable degrees and
nilpotent lengths. Generic lengths of the restricted module
enter through the inherited module-weight formula.
\end{theorem}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
We give the choices uniformly; there is no choice of an analytic
sheet for an individual projection.

\emph{Control of all inverse weights.}
Since $Q_i$ is injective on the span of each cone of the finite
fan $\Sigma$, compactness of its intersection with a unit sphere
gives $c_i>0$ such that
\[
                 \|Q_i a\|\geq c_i\|a\|
                        \qquad(a\in|\Sigma|).
\]
Use \eqref{mso:fancontainment} for every $W_j$ and $U_{ij}$.
The full-stabilization theorem gives one $\rho>0$ on which all
their moment images agree with their respective $w_0+C_{x_j}$.
Shrink $B_i$ to have $\|u-u_i^0\|<c_i\rho$.
By \eqref{mso:unionretention}, every point of a projected level
is in some $W_j$, and its original moment weight is consequently
in $w_0+|\Sigma|$ and within distance $\rho$ of $w_0$.
Thus no distant inverse weight remains in that projected level.

Let $J_i$ contain, for every cone $\tau$ of $\Sigma$ of dimension
less than $d$, an affine hyperplane containing
$u_i^0+Q_i\tau$. These hyperplanes are proper.
Replace $H_i$ by $H_i\cup J_i$.
For $u\in B_i\setminus H_i$, every inverse weight belongs to
the relative interior of a top cone.
There is at most one inverse weight on each top cone,
by injectivity there. Hence $\Lambda_i(u)$ is finite.
For a fixed $\sigma$, pull back both exceptional sets along
$Q_i:w_0+\operatorname{span}\sigma\to\mathbb R^d$.
This map is an affine isomorphism, so the pullbacks are proper
hyperplanes. They give $E_\sigma$.

\emph{Common whole factors.}
Take $v$ as in the statement. Saturation implies that the entire
$F_v$ belongs to both $C_i$. The retention lemma puts it inside
the union of the ordinary interiors of the $W_j$.
Because $U_{ij}\subset O_j$ and the $O_j$ are disjoint,
intersecting with $U_{ij}$ gives exactly $F_v\cap W_j$.
These are actual domain identities, not identifications of
reduced spectra. The domain universal property and
\Dep{fnd:generalizeddomainbasechange} identify their actual
algebras and their restrictions of $M$.

For the entire projected level at $u$, first split its finite
set of moment values $\Lambda_i(u)$, then intersect with the
$W_j$. Each exact moment fibre is an affinoid domain obtained
by imposing all the equal-radius conditions for the $f_k$.
The intersections are affinoid by the actual domain theorems.
This is a finite disjoint affinoid cover of the entire projected
level. For any $v'\in\Lambda_i(u)$, retention from this one
projection already proves equality of its factors in $W_j$,
$U_{1j}$ and $U_{2j}$. The flat-germ argument below therefore
compares both projections on that factor, even if $Q_kv'$ lies
in the exceptional set for the other projection $k$.
Apply \Dep{fnd:generalizedaffinoidcovercech}, exactly as in
Lemma~\ref{mso:unionoverlap}. It gives \eqref{mso:product}
including the module product and the bounded inverse.
The characteristic idempotents are unique because the
restriction isomorphism fixes every factor. Since $B_i(u)$ is
a canonical finite $R_i(u)$-module, its idempotent factors have
the canonical finite $R_i(u)$-module topology.
The finite-module comparisons are
\Dep{fnd:generalizedfinitemodules} and
\Dep{fnd:generalizedfinitetensor}.

\emph{Interior and the two normalized degrees.}
All points of the projected level have target weights strictly
inside $P_i$. They are absolute interior in $C_i$: the target
polyannulus bounds are strict, the finite map is relatively
interior by \Dep{fnd:generalizedfinitealgebras}, and interior
composes by \Dep{fnd:affinoidinteriorcalculus}.
By \hyperref[fnd:nag001626:source-factor-interior]{the source-factor inclusion} and \hyperref[fnd:goodinteriorlocaltesting]{ordinary local testing} they are
absolute interior in $X$.
Each point labelled by $j$ lies in the ordinary interiors of
$W_j$, $U_{1j}$ and $U_{2j}$.
The domain interior theorem
\Dep{fnd:affinoiddomainordinaryinterior} and composition then
make them absolute interior in these domains as well.

Every nonempty $W_j$ and $U_{ij}$ is pure of dimension $d$ by
\Dep{v036:analytic:domainpurity}.
At $v$ in the relative interior of $\sigma$, their local moment
images are either empty or contained in the affine plane
$v+(N_\sigma)_{\mathbb R}$, by the chosen fan subdivision and
the uniform stabilization radius.
Apply \Dep{v037:degree:moduleweight} to each nonempty whole
fibre in $W_j$ and to its restrictions from the two $U_{ij}$.
All of these computations use the same $D_{j,v}$ and the same
module, so that theorem gives \eqref{mso:weightcomparison}.
We have not compared two sums with their original-weight
labels erased.

\emph{Lengths and field normalization.}
The algebra $E_{i,j}(v)$ is finite dimensional over $H_i(v)$
and hence Artinian. Decompose it into its local factors and use
a composition series of the finite module in each factor.
Every simple quotient is its residue field; taking dimensions
proves \eqref{mso:artinianlength}.
For $A_j=\mathcal O(W_j)$ and $M_j=M\otimes_AA_j$, the generic
version is exactly the inherited formula
\[
 m_{M_j,f}(v)
   =\sum_{\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Min}A_j}
       \operatorname{length}_{(A_j)_{\mathfrak p}}
                       (M_j)_{\mathfrak p}\,
                   m_{A_j/\mathfrak p,f}(v).
\]
Finite tensors are already complete by
\Dep{fnd:generalizedfinitetensor}.

If radii are normalized after a common complete isometric
extension and $|a_k|=e^{-v_k}$, use
$\prod_k a_k^{q_{i\ell k}}$ for the projected unit
$f^{q_{i\ell}}$.
The idempotents in \eqref{mso:product} base change as actual
idempotents; retain every factor into which they split.
The finite Gauss-field comparison
\Dep{v036:gaussbasechange} and model normalization
\Dep{v036:gaussmodeldescent} preserve the total dimensions and
their full length-degree formulas. This is a finite-fibre
calculation, with no selected geometric lift and no assertion
about scalar extension of an integral.
\end{proof}
