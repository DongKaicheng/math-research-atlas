Order-zero coefficient representations; F8MG-O

I Descent of the polarized mixed tower

Open in the English reader · PDF page 2081

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{proposition}[Order-zero coefficient representations; F8MG-O]
\label{prop:orderzero}
For every cutoff presentation, each applicable pairing
\[
 L_f\quad(r=s=n),\qquad G_s\quad(r=s=n),\qquad
 \delta_{D}\quad(r=s=n-1),\qquad
 T_\omega(\alpha)=\int\omega\wedge\alpha
\]
(where \(\omega\) is a fixed ordinary smooth complementary form)
has a bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:orderzero}
             |T(Q_i\alpha)|\leq C_i p_{t_g(V),0}(\alpha),
             \qquad C_i<\infty.
\end{equation}
Here \(D\) is any of the locally finite Cartier differences above.
More precisely, the functional on each coefficient presentation has
a finite representation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cells}
 T(Q_i\alpha)=\sum_{\ell,I,J}
       \int_{\sigma_\ell}b_{\ell,IJ}(w)
                         \alpha_{IJ}(P_\ell w)\,d\lambda_\ell(w),
 \qquad P_\ell(\sigma_\ell)\subseteq t_g(V),
\end{equation}
with fixed compact polytopes, lattice-normalized real cell integrals,
and fixed smooth bounded densities. They retain all actual integer
cycle weights and finite-cover signs. This is a local order-zero
representation on \emph{coefficient spaces}; no native signed Radon
measure on the analytic space is assumed or constructed here.

For a globally represented quotient \(f=a/b\), smooth constant
truncations of the two logarithms give pairings exactly equal to
\(L_f\) on the entire image of each fixed top-degree \(Q_i\), once
the truncation is sufficiently deep. The threshold depends on \(i\).
The logarithmic parts of this proposition require regular factors
and the frozen logarithmic lemma, not ambient \(S_1\) or scalar PL.
\end{proposition}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
First suppose \(f=a/b\) on a neighbourhood of \(\Supp\chi\).
For every constant ambient basis form
\(e_{IJ}=d't_I\wedge d''t_J\) of bidegree \((n,n)\), put
\(\beta_{IJ}=Q_i e_{IJ}\). There are finitely many of these fixed
compact ordinary forms. By frozen \tagg{NAG-002806}, each vanishes
on an ordinary neighbourhood of \(V(a)\cup V(b)\). Hence
\[
 K_i=\bigcup_{I,J}\Supp\beta_{IJ}
\]
is compact and disjoint from these zero loci. Every
\(Q_i\alpha=\sum_{I,J}\alpha_{IJ}(t_g)\beta_{IJ}\) has support in
this \emph{same} \(K_i\), independently of the variable coefficients.
This finite-basis argument is the uniform step; fixed-test
integrability alone would not suffice. If there are no coefficient
slots, the entire presentation is zero.

The logarithms are bounded on \(K_i\). Choose a smooth compact
cutoff \(\theta\), supported where both factors are units and equal
to one near \(K_i\), using \tagg{NAG-002427}/\tagg{002428}. The
zero extension \(b_0=\ext(\theta\log|a/b|)\) is an ordinary smooth
function and
\(L_f(Q_i\alpha)=\int b_0Q_i\alpha\).
For a meromorphic function given only locally, choose finitely many
quotient neighbourhoods and a fixed smooth partition \(\rho_j\)
near \(\Supp\chi\). Apply the same argument to the finitely many
fixed basis forms \(\rho_j\beta_{IJ}\) on those neighbourhoods.
It produces finitely many fixed smooth multipliers \(b_j\) and
reduces \(L_f Q_i\) to integrals of \(b_j\rho_j Q_i\alpha\).
These functions and their support bounds are chosen before
\(\alpha\). In particular this argument uses no result from the
unreviewed component packet.

For any fixed smooth multiplier or complementary form, cover the
fixed compact support by finitely many actual ordinary affinoid
neighbourhoods inside \(V\), and take joint unit tuples representing
that fixed data and \(g\). The variable coefficient appears only
as \(\alpha_{IJ}(P w)\), where \(P\) is projection to the original
\(g\)-coordinates. The native finite-chart integral
\tagg{NAG-002405}/\tagg{002416}/\tagg{002425} supplies finitely many
compact cells. Restriction of the two covector factors to a lattice
tangent basis contributes fixed minors. Multiplying those minors,
the fixed coefficient functions, actual weights, and inclusion--
exclusion signs gives the densities in \eqref{eq:cells}. This proves
that formula for \(L_f\), \(T_\omega\), and therefore \(G_s\) after
a finite frame partition. For \(\delta_D\), the same construction
is made on the actual pure \((n-1)\)-dimensional zero spaces, using
R-ZP and \tagg{NAG-002778}. Closed-immersion pullback preserves compact
support because its underlying image is closed. Its moment coordinates
are the restricted \(g\), and the full quotient weights are retained.

Every summand is bounded in absolute value by
\(\lambda_\ell(\sigma_\ell)\|b_{\ell,IJ}\|_\infty
 p_{t_g(V),0}(\alpha)\). The finite sum gives \eqref{eq:orderzero}.
These integrals also make sense for continuous coefficient functions
on the compact moment image: the continuous real polytope integral is
constructed in frozen \tagg{NAG-002389}, with a boundary-box estimate
and uniform continuity. Smooth restrictions are uniformly dense in
continuous functions on a compact subset of \(\Omega\): take finitely
many Euclidean smooth bumps of small support, normalize their sum near
the compact set, and use the value of the continuous function at one
point in each small piece. Uniform continuity bounds the error.
Thus \eqref{eq:cells} extends the coefficient functional uniquely in
its zeroth-order norm. This is distinct from allowing arbitrary
measurable multipliers in the frozen grid integral or from representing
all these refinements by one analytic-space measure.

For the final assertion choose a smooth \(\lambda_M:\R\to\R\)
which equals \(t\) for \(t\geq-M\) and is the constant \(-M\)
for \(t\leq-M-1\); the real smooth profile in
\tagg{NAG-002427} supplies one. Define \(\ell_M(a)=\lambda_M(\log|a|)\)
on the nonzero locus and \(-M\) at its zeros. It is ordinary smooth,
being constant on a neighbourhood of the zeros and a smooth function
of a unit moment coordinate elsewhere. Set \(u_M=\ell_M(a)-\ell_M(b)\).
On the common \(K_i\) the two logarithms have fixed finite lower
bounds. For all sufficiently large \(M\), \(u_M=\log|a/b|\) there,
so their products with every \(Q_i\alpha\) are identical. The cutoff
presentations span compact tests by the finite partition theorem;
hence this also gives eventual equality on every individual top test.
No threshold uniform over varying presentations is asserted.
\end{proof}
