Three marked branches force a vertex

4 Analytic curves and skeleta

Open in the English reader · PDF page 783

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{lemma}[Three marked branches force a vertex]
\label{v037:f5:threebranches}
Assume the ground field is algebraically closed. Let
$\mathcal X$ be a semistable algebraic model of a smooth
projective curve, and let $P$ be a finite set of rational
points of its generic fibre whose sections have pairwise distinct smooth
specializations. Suppose $v\in C^{\an}$ admits three
pairwise disjoint connected open subsets $B_1,B_2,B_3$
with $v\notin\bigcup_i B_i$ such that
\[
           \overline{B_i}=B_i\cup\{v\},
                   \qquad B_i\cap P\ne\varnothing.
\]
Then $v$ is a component vertex of $\mathcal X$.
\end{lemma}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Suppose $v$ is not a vertex, and let $U$ be its connected
component in the complement of the model vertices.
The split pointed charts of
Theorem~\ref{cand:simultaneousnodalsplitting} complete to
the pointed formal charts of Definition~\ref{v035:formal:modelclass}.
Corollary~\ref{v035:formal:partition} and
Proposition~\ref{v035:formal:tubeends} identify $U$ with
an actual open-disc or open-annulus tube. Its boundary has
one point in the disc case and at most two in the annulus
case. Every $B_i$ meets $U$, since $v\in U\cap\overline{B_i}$
and $U$ is open.

If $B_i$ is not contained in $U$, it meets $\partial U$.
Indeed, otherwise $B_i\cap U$ would be a nonempty proper
subset both open and closed in the connected space $B_i$.
The disjoint $B_i$ meet distinct boundary points when they
meet $\partial U$. Thus at most one of them is not
contained in a disc tube, and at most two are not contained
in an annular tube. In the first case $U$ contains at
least two members of $P$, contradicting their distinct
specializations. In the second it contains at least one
member of $P$, contradicting smoothness of its marked
specialization. These contradictions prove the assertion.
\end{proof}
