Small scalar shifts

5 Tropicalization, multiplicities and balancing

Open in the English reader · PDF page 931

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{lemma}[Small scalar shifts]
\label{v035:faithfulscalarshifts}
For $h\in B$, at most finitely many $c\in K$ make $h+c$
vanish at a point of $\Sigma$. Consequently a nonvanishing
shift can be chosen with arbitrarily small positive $|c|$.
\end{lemma}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
On each relative open cell use the fixed coefficient field
and polyannulus from the preceding proof. If $h+c$ vanishes
at one Gauss point, every Laurent coefficient vanishes.
Its pullback is then the zero function on that polyannulus.
Since the inclusion of $K$ is isometric, at most one scalar
has this property on a given cell. The same argument for a
zero-dimensional cell takes place in its coefficient field.
There are finitely many cells. Powers of any $\pi\in K$ with
$0<|\pi|<1$ give infinitely many distinct nonzero scalars
converging to zero, so they avoid the finite exceptional set.
\end{proof}
Standing context 1 · exact TeX
Let $K$ be complete and nontrivially valued, with its specified
real-valued absolute value. Put $O=K^\circ$, $k=\widetilde K$,
$v=-\log|\cdot|$, and $\Gamma=v(K^\times)$.
Consider a topologically finitely presented formally \etale map
of admissible algebras
\[
 A_0=O\langle X_0,\ldots,X_r,T_1,\ldots,T_q\rangle/
              (X_0\cdots X_r-a)\longrightarrow B_0,
 \qquad 0<|a|<1.
\]
Write $B=B_0\otimes_O K$, $Y=\mathcal M(B)$, and
$\Sigma\subset Y$ for the inverse image of the standard skeleton.
The notation $\Delta_I$, $Q_I$, and $x_{\eta,w}$ is that of
Proposition~\ref{cand:actualskeletalfibrestrata} and
Theorem~\ref{cand:etaleskeletonfinitefaces}. In particular, $\Sigma$
is a compact finite complex, and a point over the relative interior
of $\Delta_I$ is specified by $w$ and $\eta\in Q_I$.
A finite piecewise $\Gamma$-integral-affine function has integral
linear parts and constants in $\Gamma$ on a finite subdivision
defined by integral linear inequalities with constants in $\Gamma$.
