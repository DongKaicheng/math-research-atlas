Integral models computing an arbitrary-radius degree

5 Tropicalization, multiplicities and balancing

Open in the English reader · PDF page 961

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{theorem}[Integral models computing an arbitrary-radius degree]
\label{v036:gaussmodeldescent}
Let $D$ be as above. Let $L/K$ be a complete nontrivially valued
isometric extension, choose $b_j\in L^\times$ with $|b_j|=r_j$,
and write $S_j=T_j/b_j$. Put
\[
 O_L=L^\circ,\quad k_L=\widetilde L,\quad
 A_{L,0}=O_L\langle S_1^{\pm1},\ldots,S_d^{\pm1}\rangle.
\]
For any finite list of power-bounded elements of $D_L$ there is an
admissible topologically finitely presented algebra
$D_{L,0}\subset D_L$, finite over $A_{L,0}$, containing that list
and having generic algebra $D_L$. For every such finite model put
\[
 C_{L,0}=(D_{L,0}/L^{\circ\circ}D_{L,0})
                \otimes_{k_L[S^{\pm1}]}k_L(S_1,\ldots,S_d).
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
\label{v036:eq:realradiuslength}
 \delta_{K,r}(D)=
 \sum_{\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Max}C_{L,0}}
   \operatorname{length}_{(C_{L,0})_{\mathfrak p}}
                         ((C_{L,0})_{\mathfrak p})
   [\kappa(\mathfrak p):k_L(S)].
\end{equation}
In particular this integer is independent of $L$, of the scalars
$b_j$, and of the finite model. Such an extension $L$ always exists.

If $D$ is the actual full fibre at a weight $w$ of analytic units
$f_1,\ldots,f_n$, and is finite with its canonical module norm over
the polytorus of their monomials $f^{q_j}$, one may choose $L$
realizing all $\exp(-w_i)$. For $a_i\in L^\times$ of these absolute
values, the model may be chosen to contain all $f_i/a_i$ and
$a_i/f_i$. The target normalizers can be taken as
$b_j=\prod_i a_i^{q_{ji}}$.
\end{theorem}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
The coordinate change $S_j=T_j/b_j$ is an isometric isomorphism
between $A_{L,r}$ and the unit-polytorus algebra over $L$; it sends
their Gauss points to each other. Proposition~\ref{v036:gaussbasechange}
gives $D_L$ its canonical finite-module Banach structure. Apply
Theorem~\ref{v035:analytic:finitequotientmodel} over $L$. It constructs
the required actual integral quotient model, with the prescribed
elements, and computes its special-fibre length sum as
$\delta_{L,r}(D_L)$. The same theorem gives this equality for every
admissible finite model with that generic algebra. Proposition~
\ref{v036:gaussbasechange} identifies this number with
$\delta_{K,r}(D)$, proving \eqref{v036:eq:realradiuslength} and
all the independence assertions.

To obtain an extension realizing any finite list of radii, append
one radius $0<\rho<1$ and take the completion of the fraction
field of its Laurent algebra for the multiplicative Gauss norm.
Its coordinate elements realize the prescribed radii and $\rho$,
so this is a complete nontrivially valued extension of $K$ with
the original absolute value on $K$.

In the last assertion the full fibre is the actual simultaneous
Laurent domain $|f_i|=\exp(-w_i)$. The relative pushout
\ref{fnd:generalizedrelativepushout} shows that its base change
is the same actual Laurent domain over $L$. On every point of
this domain both $f_i/a_i$ and $a_i/f_i$ have absolute value one.
Theorem~\ref{fnd:generalizedpointwisepowerbound} makes these
elements power bounded, also with the canonical equivalent norm.
They may therefore be included in the finite prescribed list.
The displayed choice of $b_j$ makes the normalized target
coordinates exactly the indicated monomials in these elements.
\end{proof}
