Purity after an arbitrary complete extension

C Native scalar extension and finite restriction of constants

Open in the English reader · PDF page 1759

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{lemma}[Purity after an arbitrary complete extension]
\label{f9cand:purity}
If $\M_K(A)$ is nonempty and pure of analytic dimension $d$, then
$\M_L(A_L)$ is nonempty and pure of analytic dimension $d$.
\end{lemma}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
\textbf{Strict integral algebras.}
Suppose first that $A$ is strict and integral. Finite normalization
[\ref{fnd:strictfinitenormalization}] gives a bounded finite injection
$R=K\langle T_1,\ldots,T_d\rangle\hookrightarrow A$.
Its finite-module norm is equivalent to the given affinoid norm, by
the norm argument in [\ref{fnd:stricttatedimension}, \ref{fnd:strictdimensionbasechange}]: for nontrivial valuation use the
bounded lifting theorem on a finite-generator surjection, while for
trivial valuation both strict quotient norms take only the values zero
and one.

Put $F=\Frac(R)$. The torsion-free finite $R$-module $A$ embeds into
$R^m$: embed it into the finite-dimensional vector space $A\otimes_R F$,
choose a basis, and clear the finitely many denominators of the
coordinates of a finite generating list. By the finite-module theorem
[\ref{fnd:generalizedfinitemodules}], this embedding is norm-strict onto a closed submodule.
Strict exactness of completed scalar tensors [\ref{fnd:grusonstrictexact}] preserves
its injectivity. The module comparisons in [\ref{fnd:generalscalarpresentation}, \ref{fnd:generalscalarfaithful}] therefore
give an injection
\[
                         A_L\hookrightarrow R_L^m .
\]
Consequently $A_L$ is torsion free over the domain
$R_L=L\langle T_1,\ldots,T_d\rangle$ and is finite over it.

Let $\mathfrak q$ be a minimal prime of $A_L$. No nonzero element of
$R_L$ can lie in $\mathfrak q$. To check this without a reducedness
assumption, localize at $\mathfrak q$. This is an Artinian local ring;
an element of its maximal ideal is nilpotent. If a nonzero $r\in R_L$
lay in $\mathfrak q$, some power of $r$ would annihilate a nonzero element
after localization. Clearing its denominator would give a nonzero
element of $A_L$ killed by that power of $r$, contradicting torsion
freeness. Thus
\[
                    R_L\hookrightarrow A_L/\mathfrak q
\]
is a finite injection. The dimension theorem for strict normalization
[\ref{fnd:stricttatedimension}, \ref{fnd:strictdimensionbasechange}] makes every such quotient have dimension $d$.

\textbf{Strict pure algebras.}
Now let $A$ be strict and pure, allowing nilpotents. For
$\mathfrak q\in\Min(A_L)$, Lemma~\ref{f9cand:generic-length} makes
$\mathfrak p=\mathfrak q\cap A$ minimal. The actual quotient identity
\[
                    A_L/\mathfrak pA_L=(A/\mathfrak p)_L
\]
is part of faithful scalar extension [\ref{fnd:generalscalarpresentation}, \ref{fnd:generalscalarfaithful}].
The prime $\mathfrak q/\mathfrak pA_L$ is minimal there.
The strict integral case, applied to $A/\mathfrak p$, gives dimension
$d$ for $A_L/\mathfrak q$.

\textbf{Arbitrary radii.}
Choose a finite free-radius strictification $K_s/K$ by [\ref{fnd:simultaneousstrictification}].
The exact component theorem [\ref{v036:analytic:freecomponents}] makes $A_{K_s}$
pure of dimension $d$. Choose a common complete extension $E$ of $K_s$
and $L$ by [\ref{fnd:gausscommonextension}]. The strict case shows that
$A_E=(A_{K_s})_E$ is pure of dimension $d$.

Set $B=A_L$ and fix $\mathfrak q\in\Min(B)$.
Faithful flatness makes $(B/\mathfrak q)_E$ nonzero.
The observation preceding this lemma shows that its minimal components
are minimal components of $B_E=A_E$, so all have dimension $d$.
The analytic dimension of a nonempty affinoid is the maximum of the
dimensions of its minimal components: each analytic point kernel
contains a minimal prime, passing to that quotient preserves its
completed residue field, and [\ref{fnd:affinoidabhyankarmaximum}] identifies dimension with
the maximum of residual dimensions at points.
Hence $\dim_E\M((B/\mathfrak q)_E)=d$.
Scalar invariance of analytic dimension [\ref{fnd:analyticdimensionindependence}] now gives
$\dim_L\M(B/\mathfrak q)=d$. This holds for every minimal prime of $B$.
Nonvanishing follows from faithful flatness.
\end{proof}
Standing context 1 · exact TeX
Throughout, $K$ is complete for its specified real-valued non-Archimedean
absolute value, and $L/K$ is a complete isometric extension. Trivial
valuation and arbitrary positive affinoid radii are allowed. Write
$p:X_L\to X$ for actual completed scalar extension. All moment coordinates
are $-\log|f_i|$ with the given absolute value.
