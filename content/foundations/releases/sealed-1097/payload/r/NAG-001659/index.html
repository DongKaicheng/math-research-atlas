Separable completion of the Gauss fraction field

3 Reduction, formal models and residual geometry

Open in the English reader · PDF page 595

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{theorem}[\bi{Separable completion of the Gauss fraction field}
{Gauss 分式域完备化的可分性}]
\label{fnd:gaussfractionseparable}
\bi{Let $R=K\langle T_1,\ldots,T_n\rangle$, let $F=\operatorname{Frac}(R)$
carry the Gauss absolute value, and let $H$ be its completion.
Then $H/F$ is geometrically reduced. In particular, for every finite
field extension $E/F$, the finite-dimensional $H$-algebra
$E\otimes_F H$ is reduced.}
{令 $R=K\langle T_1,\ldots,T_n\rangle$，在
$F=\operatorname{Frac}(R)$ 上配备 Gauss 绝对值，以 $H$ 记其完备化。
则 $H/F$ 几何约化。特别地，对每个有限扩域 $E/F$，
有限维 $H$-代数 $E\otimes_F H$ 均约化。}
\end{theorem}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\paragraph{NAG-001659. Geometric reducedness of the strict Gauss completion.}
Let \(R=K\langle T_1,\ldots,T_n\rangle\) be the unit-polyradius Tate
algebra over the complete field \(K\). Set \(F=\operatorname{Frac}R\)
with its Gauss absolute value and \(H=\widehat F\).
For every field extension \(E/F\), \(H\otimes_F E\) is reduced.

In characteristic zero, each element of the tensor comes from a
finitely generated subfield \(E_0/F\) of \(E\), and this smaller tensor
maps injectively to the full tensor. For a transcendence basis \(t\)
of \(E_0/F\), \(H\otimes_F F(t)\) embeds into \(H(t)\) by polynomial
localization. Tensoring this injection over \(F(t)\) with \(E_0\)
preserves injectivity. The resulting ambient algebra
\(H(t)\otimes_{F(t)}E_0\) is reduced. In fact adjoin finitely many
algebraic generators of \(E_0/F(t)\) in succession. At each step the
monic minimal polynomial is relatively prime to its derivative over
the preceding field, so this relation persists after mapping that
field to each current field factor. Quotienting by this polynomial
gives a finite product of fields at the next step.

In characteristic \(p>0\) endow each \(p\)-root field with the
absolute value \(|u|=|u^p|^{1/p}\). Taking \(p\)th powers shows that
\(K^{1/p}\) and \(H^{1/p}\) are complete and identifies
\(R^{1/p}=K^{1/p}\langle S_1,\ldots,S_n\rangle\) with \(S_i^p=T_i\).
For an \(F\)-independent family \(u_1,\ldots,u_m\in F^{1/p}\), first
multiply all its members by a common nonzero element of \(R\) so
they lie in \(R^{1/p}\). If \(u_i^p=a_i/b_i\), \(\prod_i b_i\)
suffices. The \(K\)-algebra generated by all coefficients of the
resulting series is a field, since \(a^p\in K\) and
\(a^{-1}=a^{p-1}/a^p\) for its nonzero elements. Its closure \(K'\)
in \(K^{1/p}\) is a field by continuity of inversion. Monomials
in the countably many coefficients have dense \(K\)-span in \(K'\).

Use NAG-001658 to take bounded \(K\)-linear coordinate maps
\(\lambda_j:K'\to K\) separating its points. For
\(0\le\mu_i<p\) the maps
\[
 L_{j,\mu}\left(\sum_\nu a_\nu S^\nu\right)
       =\sum_{\eta\in\mathbf N^n}\lambda_j(a_{\mu+p\eta})T^\eta
\]
are bounded with norm at most \(\|\lambda_j\|\). They are \(R\)-linear,
first for polynomial multipliers and then by density and continuity.
They separate all series. The columns \((L_{j,\mu}(u_i))_{j,\mu}\)
are independent over \(F\): clear denominators in a putative column
relation and apply separation and \(R\)-linearity. Some \(m\) rows
give an invertible \(m\times m\) matrix over \(F\), since the rows
span the full dual of \(F^m\). Its inverse and the bounds on the rows
give \(C>0\) with
\[
 \max_i|b_i|\le C\left|\sum_i b_i u_i\right| \qquad (b_i\in F).
\]
For coefficients in \(R\) use the bounds directly and for fractions
clear a common denominator and divide the inequality by its Gauss
absolute value. Passing to limits in \(H^{1/p}\) gives the same
inequality for \(b_i\in H\). Thus \(H\otimes_F F^{1/p}\to H^{1/p}\)
is injective.

If \(a_i\in H\) are \(F\)-linearly independent then the \(a_i^p\) are
also independent. In fact an \(F\)-linear relation on the \(p\)th
powers yields, on taking the \(p\)th root, a relation on the \(a_i\)
with coefficients in \(F^{1/p}\). The proved injection followed by
linear independence in the scalar-extended vector space kills all
coefficients. For any \(E/F\), express a nonzero tensor as
\(\sum_i a_i\otimes e_i\), with the \(a_i\) independent over \(F\)
and some \(e_i\ne0\). Its \(p\)th power is nonzero by independence
of the \(a_i^p\). Iterating excludes all nilpotents.
