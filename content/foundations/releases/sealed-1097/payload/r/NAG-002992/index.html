Frame gluing and its signs; F8MG-B

I Descent of the polarized mixed tower

Open in the English reader · PDF page 2080

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{proposition}[Frame gluing and its signs; F8MG-B]
\label{prop:bundle}
Let \(\cL\) have ordinary local frames \(e_i\) on \(U_i\), with
\(e_j=u_{ij}e_i\) on overlaps, \(u_{ij}\) analytic units. A smooth
metric means a norm on each one-dimensional \(\mathcal H(x)\)-fibre
whose weights \(h_i=-\log\|e_i\|\) are ordinary smooth scalar
functions. The compatibility equation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:frames}
 h_j=h_i-\log|u_{ij}|.
\end{equation}
For a regular meromorphic section \(s=f_i e_i\), one has
\(f_j=u_{ij}^{-1}f_i\). Its full divisor \(D_s\) glues from
\(\divis(f_i)\), its ordinary smooth curvature form glues as
\[
                 c_1(\overline{\cL})|_{U_i}=\dd h_i,
\]
and the local top-test pairings
\begin{equation}\label{eq:greenpair}
 G_{s,i}(\alpha)=\int_{U_i}h_i\alpha-L_{f_i}(\alpha)
\end{equation}
glue independently of frames and compact partitions. Off the local
zero and pole loci this is the potential
\(g_s=-\log\|s\|=h_i-\log|f_i|\).
\end{proposition}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
The metric rule \(\|a e_i(x)\|=|a|\exp(-h_i(x))\) is independent
of the frame precisely when \eqref{eq:frames} holds. On any smaller
unit moment neighbourhood, \(\log|u_{ij}|\) is the negative of one
moment coordinate. Its mixed second differential is zero as an
ordinary form. Thus \(\dd h_j=\dd h_i\), and the sheaf property
\tagg{NAG-002424} glues the curvature; it is killed by both \(d'\)
and \(d''\). No PL theorem is needed to prove this unit identity.

The section transformation follows by comparing \(f_i e_i=f_j e_j\).
The divisor of a unit is zero, so Proposition~\ref{prop:quotient}
and frozen Cartier additivity give the same full divisor on overlaps.
For local top tests, logarithmic additivity gives
\(L_{f_j}=L_{f_i}-L_{u_{ij}}\), and
\(L_{u_{ij}}(\alpha)=\int\log|u_{ij}|\alpha\), since the unit has
no zero. Equation \eqref{eq:frames} therefore proves equality of
\eqref{eq:greenpair} on every admissible local top test. In a
common regular-quotient chart the actual support avoids the
regular numerator and denominator zero loci, by the
ordinary top-form vanishing used in the quotient pairing.
The larger compact set used to choose the charts may meet those
loci.
Finite compact partitions and
their product refinements glue it just as in
Proposition~\ref{prop:quotient}. This proves independence, rather
than merely a pointwise equality off a divisor.
\end{proof}
