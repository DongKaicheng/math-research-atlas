Native global chains under complete extension

C Native scalar extension and finite restriction of constants

Open in the English reader · PDF page 1762

Statement and full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proposition}[Native global chains under complete extension]
\label{f9cand:chain}
Let $X=\M_K(A)$ be pure of analytic dimension $d$, $M$ a finite
$A$-module, and $f$ any finite tuple of analytic units. Then, in the
same real moment space and saturated integral lattices,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chain}
                    \T^{\,L}_{p^*f}(M_L)=\T^{\,K}_f(M).
\end{equation}
Consequently their integrals against any smooth ambient $(d,d)$-form
defined near the compact actual moment image agree.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Scalar projection is surjective [\ref{fnd:scalarextensionspectralsurjective}], and the
absolute values of the pulled-back units agree pointwise. Thus the
actual moment images agree. Lemma~\ref{f9cand:purity} supplies purity
upstairs. Use Theorem~\ref{v037:globaltrop:finiteimage} for the finite actual
images and their boundary images. Use
Lemma~\ref{cand:polyhedralrefinementproduct} to subdivide the common
image and both boundary images simultaneously.
Use the
\hyperref[fnd:nag002405:global-finite-module-chain-weights]{closed finite-module chains}
on this subdivision.
Every $d$-cell interior
avoids those boundary images. At a point $w$ there, the full moment
fibres in both spaces are interior, and the local image lies in the
same saturated rank-$d$ affine tangent plane.

Let $D_w$ be the actual algebra of the entire simultaneous level
$|f_i|=e^{-w_i}$. The domain base-change theorem [\ref{fnd:generalizeddomainbasechange}]
identifies the \emph{entire} upstairs level algebra with
$(D_w)_L$. Choose the same integral transverse projection $Q$ on
the tangent lattice $N$, and put $e_Q=[\bb Z^d:Q(N)]$.
The flat-germ theorem in [\ref{v036:gaussbasechange}, \ref{v036:flatgermweight}] makes $D_w$ finite canonical over
$R_{K,\exp(-Qw)}$. Put $P=M\otimes_A D_w$.
All the completed module restrictions are the actual finite tensors
by [\ref{fnd:generalizedfinitetensor}]. Lemma~\ref{f9cand:gauss-module} therefore gives
\[
 \frac{\dim_{H_{L,\exp(-Qw)}}
          (P_L\otimes_{R_{L,\exp(-Qw)}}H_{L,\exp(-Qw)})}{e_Q}
 =
 \frac{\dim_{H_{K,\exp(-Qw)}}
          (P\otimes_{R_{K,\exp(-Qw)}}H_{K,\exp(-Qw)})}{e_Q}.
\]
By the exact module-weight theorem [\ref{v037:degree:genericgauss}, \ref{v037:degree:moduleweight}], these are the
two generic coefficients. That theorem also expresses them through
generic Artinian lengths as in Lemma~\ref{f9cand:generic-length}.
Equality on every top cell proves \eqref{eq:chain}.
Lower-dimensional cells carry no $d$-chain term on either side.
The same real form, cell, orientation and lattice measure are used
in the \hyperref[fnd:nag002405:finite-cell-functional]{finite-cell integral},
so the finite integral sums agree. Boundary-image codimension is used
only to read generic coefficients; no differential boundary term is
being discarded.
\end{proof}
