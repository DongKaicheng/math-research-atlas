Lifting to a generic normalization

4 Analytic curves and skeleta

Open in the English reader · PDF page 758

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{lemma}[Lifting to a generic normalization]
\label{v035:f5:normalizationlift}
Let $O$ be a rank-one valuation domain with fraction field $K$.
Let $\mathcal N\to\mathcal Y$ be a finite relative normalization
in a specified finite normalization of $\mathcal Y_K$.
Let $T$ be a normal integral flat $O$-scheme. A morphism
$f:T\to\mathcal Y$ with a specified lift on $T_K$ has a unique
lift $T\to\mathcal N$. If the generic lift lands in one clopen
generic-labelled piece of $\mathcal N$, then the whole lift lands
in that piece. If $T/O$ is proper and $\mathcal N/O$ is separated,
the lift is proper.
\end{lemma}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Choose $\pi$ with $O[1/\pi]=K$. Flatness makes $T_K$ dense in $T$.
For $Q=\operatorname{Spec}R\subset\mathcal Y$, write
$\mathcal N_Q=\operatorname{Spec}B$. The algebra $B$ is integral
over $R$. The specified generic lift pulls each $b\in B$ back to
a rational function in $K(T)$. Its monic equation over $R$ pulls
back to a monic equation over every local ring of $f^{-1}(Q)$.
Those rings are integrally closed in $K(T)$, so the rational function
is regular at every point of $f^{-1}(Q)$. Its local representatives
glue, yielding an $R$-homomorphism
$B\to\Gamma(f^{-1}(Q),\mathcal O_T)$.
These maps agree on overlaps because their generic restrictions
agree and $T_K$ is dense. They define the lift, and the same argument
proves uniqueness. The inverse image of any other clopen labelled
piece is open and misses the dense generic fibre, so is empty.
For properness, factor the lift through its closed graph in
$T\times_O\mathcal N$ and the proper projection to $\mathcal N$;
this is also \cite[Tag 01W6]{StacksAlg}.
\end{proof}
