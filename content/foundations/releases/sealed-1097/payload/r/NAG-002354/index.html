Local polyhedrality without a purity assumption

5 Tropicalization, multiplicities and balancing

Open in the English reader · PDF page 979

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{corollary}[Local polyhedrality without a purity assumption]
\label{v037:analytic:interiorlocalpolyhedrality}
Let $X=\mathcal M(B)$ be a generalized affinoid space over any
complete non-Archimedean field with its specified absolute value,
and let $t=t_f$ be the moment map of finitely many analytic units.
If $w\in t(X)$ and the whole fibre $F_w$ is interior over $K$,
there is a finite rational polyhedral fan $C$ and a neighbourhood
$V$ of $w$ such that
\[
                       t(X)\cap V=(w+|C|)\cap V.
\]
More precisely, $|C|$ is the union of the pure fans supplied by
Theorem~\ref{v037:analytic:interiorlocalstar} for the irreducible
components of $X$ meeting $F_w$, using their analytic dimensions.
\end{corollary}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
The generalized affinoid algebra $B$ is Noetherian by
Theorem~\ref{fnd:generalizedfinitemodules}, applied to the ideals
of the finite module $B$ over itself, and has finitely
many minimal primes $\mathfrak p_i$, by
\cite[Tag 00FR]{StacksAlg}.
The closed analytic subspaces
$X_i=\mathcal M(B/\mathfrak p_i)$ cover $X$:
the prime kernel of every analytic point contains a minimal
prime by \cite[Tag 00E0(4)]{StacksAlg}, and the point factors
boundedly through that quotient.
The quotient affinoid structure is again given by
Corollary~\ref{fnd:generalizedaffinoidquotients}.
Each nonempty $X_i$ is pure of its analytic dimension, since
its algebra is a domain.

The closed immersion $X_i\to X$ is finite, hence everywhere
inner by Theorem~\ref{fnd:generalizedfinitealgebras}.
Composition in Proposition~\ref{fnd:affinoidinteriorcalculus}
therefore makes every point of $X_i\cap F_w$ interior over $K$.
For those $i$ for which this intersection is nonempty, apply
Theorem~\ref{v037:analytic:interiorlocalstar} to $X_i$.
For every other $i$, Proposition~\ref{fnd:boundedspectrumcompact}
makes $t(X_i)$ compact. It avoids $w$ and can be removed by
shrinking the neighbourhood of $w$.
Intersect the finitely many resulting neighbourhoods.
The equality for $X$ follows by taking their union.
Subdividing by the finitely many rational hyperplanes used to
define all the cones gives a common rational polyhedral
refinement, and hence $C$ with the stated support.
\end{proof}
