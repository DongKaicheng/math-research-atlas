Character modules give an exact integral test

Proper direct image on historical ordinary compact tests

Open in the English reader · PDF page 1856

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{theorem}[Character modules give an exact integral test]
\label{cand:f5tame:charactercriterion}
Assume (K), and let \(B'\) be flat over \(O'\). Set
\[
 B=(B')^G,\qquad
 M_i=\{b\in B':\sigma(b)=\zeta^i b\},\qquad
 \Lambda_i=s^{-i}M_i\subset B_K,\quad B_K=K\otimes_O B .
 \tag{3}
\]
The displayed inclusion is well-defined. In the expression
\(I_iB\subset B_K\), products mean \emph{finite sums} of \(ab\)
with \(a\in I_i\) and \(b\in B\).
Then multiplication
\[
 \mu:O'\otimes_O B\longrightarrow B'
 \tag{4}
\]
is injective, and the following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the given semilinear algebra \(B'\) has effective descent;
\item \(\mu\) is an isomorphism;
\item \(\Lambda_i=I_iB\) for every \(0\leq i<e\).
\end{enumerate}
If \(B'\) is generated as an \(O'\)-algebra by \(z_1,\ldots,z_r\),
condition 3 can equivalently be checked by the finitely many
membership conditions
\[
        s^{-i}P_i(z_j)\in I_iB\qquad(0\leq i<e,\ 1\leq j\leq r),
 \tag{4a}
\]
where \(P_i\) is the Fourier projector defined in the proof.
When these conditions hold, \(B\) is the uniquely identified
invariant descent. It is flat over \(O\). If \(B'\) is finitely
presented over \(O'\), then \(B\) is finitely presented over \(O\);
if its structural morphism is a nodal curve, so is that of \(B\).
Effective descent necessarily makes the action on
\(B'/\mathfrak m'B'\) the identity.
\end{theorem}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
The Fourier operators
\[
 P_i=\frac1e\sum_{j=0}^{e-1}\zeta^{-ij}\sigma^j
\]
are \(O\)-linear orthogonal idempotents summing to the identity.
The usual finite geometric sums prove
\(B'=\bigoplus_iM_i\) and \(M_0=B\).
Flatness of \(B'\) over the domain \(O'\) makes \(B'\), and hence
\(B\), torsion free over \(O\). Thus \(B\) is \(O\)-flat by
\stacks{0539}.

Localizing \(O'\) at \(O\setminus\{0\}\) gives \(L\): multiply any
element of \(L\) by a sufficiently high power of a fixed positive
element of \(O\). Invariants commute with this localization.
Explicitly, if \(b/d\in B'_L\), with \(b\in B'\) and
\(d\in O\setminus\{0\}\), is invariant, then
\((\sigma(b)-b)/d=0\), so torsion-freeness gives \(\sigma(b)=b\).
Consequently \((B'_L)^G=B_K\). For \(m\in M_i\),
\(s^{-i}m\) is invariant in \(B'_L\), proving (3).

Tensor (2) with \(B\). Flatness of \(B\) makes
\(I_i\otimes_O B\longrightarrow K\otimes_OB=B_K\) injective,
with image exactly \(I_iB\). Under this identification, (4) on
the \(i\)-th summand is multiplication by \(s^i\), with image
\(s^iI_iB\subset M_i\). This is injective since \(B'\) embeds in
\(B'_L\). Images of different summands are independent by the
Fourier decomposition. This proves injectivity of \(\mu\) and
the equivalence of conditions 2 and 3. Each test (4a) says that
\(P_i(z_j)\) belongs to the image of \(\mu\). If all the tests
hold, their sums put every \(z_j\) in that image. The image is
an \(O'\)-subalgebra, so then equals \(B'\). Necessity is immediate.

Condition 2 is an effective descent, so implies condition 1.
Conversely, for any effective presentation \(O'\otimes_OD\simeq B'\),
the Fourier projectors on the left act on \(O'\) alone. Their
weight-zero summand is \(I_0\otimes_OD=D\). Thus the isomorphism
identifies \(D\) with \(B\), and its multiplication map is (4).
This proves condition 2, including the asserted uniqueness.

The valuation-ring extension \(O\to O'\) is faithfully flat:
it is torsion free and hence flat by \stacks{0539}, and
\(\mathfrak mO'\subset\mathfrak m'\) makes this local flat
extension faithful. Its affine spectrum map is quasi-compact,
so it is an fpqc cover. Once (4) is an isomorphism, finite
presentation descends by \stacks{02L0}, and the nodal relative
dimension-one property descends by \stacks{0C5E}.
Finally the special fibre of an effective presentation is
\(k\otimes_OD\); the action on \(k=O'/\mathfrak m'\) is the
identity. Thus its entire special-fibre algebra has trivial action.
\end{proof}
