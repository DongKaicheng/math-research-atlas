Finite constant extensions of a Gauss field

4 Analytic curves and skeleta

Open in the English reader · PDF page 816

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{lemma}[Finite constant extensions of a Gauss field]
\label{v038:f5:finitegaussconstants}
Let $L/K$ be a finite extension of complete real-valued fields.
The canonical map
\[
                 G_K(T)\otimes_K L\longrightarrow G_L(T)
\]
is an isomorphism of fields and finite-dimensional normed
$G_K(T)$-algebras. In particular
\[
                         [G_L(T):G_K(T)]=[L:K].
\]
No separability or discrete-valuation hypothesis is required.
\end{lemma}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Choose a $K$-basis $e_1,\ldots,e_n$ of $L$.
Lemma~\ref{fnd:finiteextensionnorms} gives constants $c,C>0$ with
\[
 c\max_i|a_i|\leq\left|\sum_i a_i e_i\right|
                       \leq C\max_i|a_i| \qquad(a_i\in K).
\]
Apply these inequalities coefficient by coefficient to polynomials
$P_i\in K[T]$ and then take the largest coefficient norm. The
Gauss norms satisfy
\[
 c\max_i\|P_i\|\leq\left\|\sum_i e_iP_i\right\|
                          \leq C\max_i\|P_i\|.
\]
The Gauss norm is multiplicative by Proposition~\ref{fnd:gaussnorm}.
Division by a common nonzero denominator in $K[T]$ therefore gives
the same inequalities for any $P_i\in K(T)$.
They also prove that the $e_i$ are independent over $K(T)$.
Their span is a finite-dimensional subalgebra of the field $L(T)$,
so it is a field: multiplication by a nonzero element is an
injective endomorphism and hence surjective. It contains $L$ and
$T$, and is consequently all of $L(T)$.

The displayed equivalent norms identify the completion of this
vector space with $\bigoplus_iG_K(T)e_i$. Multiplication agrees
on the dense subfield $L(T)$ and extends continuously, since the
Gauss norm is multiplicative. By definition its completion is
$G_L(T)$. This proves the asserted map is an isomorphism and
computes its degree.
\end{proof}
