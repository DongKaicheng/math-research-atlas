Lifting strict sectors from a residue field

5 Tropicalization, multiplicities and balancing

Open in the English reader · PDF page 969

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{lemma}[Lifting strict sectors from a residue field]
\label{v037:analytic:residuesectors}
Let $K$ be a complete non-Archimedean field with its specified
absolute value, let $(X,x)$ be a good $K$-analytic germ, and suppose
that $x\in\operatorname{Int}(X/K)$.
Let $h_1,\ldots,h_n$ be invertible analytic functions near $x$
such that $|h_i(x)|=1$.
Put $k=\widetilde K$, and let
\[
 R=k[\widetilde h_1^{\pm1},\ldots,\widetilde h_n^{\pm1}]
       \subset\widetilde{\mathcal H(x)},
 \qquad Z=\operatorname{Spec}R\subset\mathbf G_{m,k}^n.
\]
If $u\in\operatorname{Trop}(Z)$ and
$m_1,\ldots,m_s\in\mathbf Z^n$ satisfy
$\langle m_j,u\rangle>0$ for every $j$, then every neighbourhood
of $x$ contains a point $y$ for which
\[
                 |h^{m_j}(y)|<1\qquad(1\leq j\leq s).
\]
In particular the corresponding strict rational sector contains
moment values arbitrarily close to $0=t_h(x)$.
\end{lemma}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Choose an analytic point of $Z$ whose moment value is $u$.
Its kernel is a prime $\mathfrak p\subset R$, and its absolute
value induces a valuation ring $V_{\mathfrak p}$ in
$\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ containing the trivially valued field $k$.
Let $B\subset R_{\mathfrak p}$ be the inverse image of
$V_{\mathfrak p}$ under the residue map.
This is a local subring of
$\operatorname{Frac}(R)\subset\widetilde{\mathcal H(x)}$.
Indeed, its maximal ideal is the inverse image of the maximal
ideal of $V_{\mathfrak p}$, and an element outside that inverse
image is a unit of $R_{\mathfrak p}$ whose inverse again lies in
$B$.
The character $\widetilde h^{m_j}$ is a unit of
$R_{\mathfrak p}$, and its image in $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ has
absolute value $e^{-\langle m_j,u\rangle}<1$.
It therefore belongs to the maximal ideal of $B$.

By \cite[Tag 00IA]{StacksAlg}, there is a valuation ring
$V\subset\widetilde{\mathcal H(x)}$ dominating $B$.
It contains $k$, and each $\widetilde h^{m_j}$ belongs to its
maximal ideal. Consequently $\widetilde h^{-m_j}\notin V$.
Lemma~\ref{fnd:gradeddegreeonevaluation} extends $V$ to a graded
valuation ring
\[
 W\subset F_x:=\widetilde{\mathcal H(x)}^{\rm gr}
 \quad\hbox{containing }\widetilde K^{\rm gr},
 \qquad W_1=V.
\]
No assertion about the rank of this valuation is needed.

Choose an ordinary affinoid neighbourhood of $x$ on which the
$h_i$ are units. The
\mbox{\hyperref[fnd:goodinteriorlocaltesting]{ordinary local test}}
preserves interiority over $\mathcal M(K)$ on this neighbourhood.
The \mbox{\hyperref[fnd:gradedabsoluteevaluationforward]{absolute evaluation}}
clause makes the image of its graded algebra in $F_x$ finite over
$\widetilde K^{\rm gr}$.
Every graded valuation ring containing $\widetilde K^{\rm gr}$
contains this image: apply the integral-closedness assertion
of Lemma~\ref{fnd:gradedvaluationintegrality} to its homogeneous
elements. Thus
\[
       \mathscr R(X,x)
          =\mathscr P_{\widetilde K^{\rm gr}}(F_x),
\]
using the actual germ reduction of
Proposition~\ref{fnd:gradedaffinoidgerminvariance}.
In particular $W\in\mathscr R(X,x)$.

Suppose that a neighbourhood of $x$ contains no point satisfying
all the strict inequalities in the statement.
Choose a smaller ordinary affinoid neighbourhood of $x$ inside it
on which all $h_i$ are units. The finitely many Weierstrass domains
\[
               |h^{-m_j}|\leq1\qquad(1\leq j\leq s)
\]
cover this representative. They are a finite closed cover, hence
admissible by Lemma~\ref{fnd:quasinetcalculus}, and all contain $x$.
Each inequality is active at $x$.
Proposition~\ref{fnd:gradedgermcovercomparison} says that their
germ reductions cover $\mathscr R(X,x)$.
By Lemma~\ref{fnd:gradedweierstrassgerm}, the $j$-th reduction
consists of the valuation rings containing
$\widetilde h^{-m_j}$. None contains $W$, a contradiction.
The case $s=0$ is immediate. Finally continuity of $t_h$ allows
the neighbourhood to be chosen inside the inverse image of any
prescribed neighbourhood of $0$.
\end{proof}
