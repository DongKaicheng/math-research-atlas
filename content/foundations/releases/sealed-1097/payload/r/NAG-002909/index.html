Picard lifting in topological dimension at most one

B Recovered native algebraization source

Open in the English reader · PDF page 1752

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{lemma}[Picard lifting in topological dimension at most one]
\label{cand:f5alg:nilpicard}
Let $i:Z\hookrightarrow Y$ be a closed immersion of schemes inducing
a homeomorphism of underlying spaces. Suppose that this common space
is Noetherian and has dimension at most one. Then
\[
                    i^*:\operatorname{Pic}(Y)
                       \longrightarrow\operatorname{Pic}(Z)
\]
is surjective. If $\mathcal L_Z$ is ample, every invertible sheaf on
$Y$ whose restriction is isomorphic to $\mathcal L_Z$ is ample.
No finite-generation or nilpotence bound on the ideal of $Z$ is required.
\end{lemma}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Identify the two underlying spaces, and put
$\mathcal J=\ker(\mathcal O_Y\to i_*\mathcal O_Z)$.
On an affine open of $Y$, every prime contains the ideal defining
$Z$, so that ideal is nil. In particular its stalk ideals are nil.
An element of a ring whose image is a unit modulo a nil ideal is a
unit: a lift of its inverse has product $1+j$ with $j$ nilpotent,
and $1+j$ has a finite geometric-series inverse. Applied to stalks,
this gives an exact sequence of abelian sheaves on the common space,
\[
  1\longrightarrow 1+\mathcal J
   \longrightarrow\mathcal O_Y^\times
   \longrightarrow i_*\mathcal O_Z^\times
   \longrightarrow1.
\]
Here $1+\mathcal J$ has its multiplicative group law. There is no
identification with the additive sheaf $\mathcal J$ in this argument.

Grothendieck vanishing for a Noetherian topological space of dimension
at most one, \stacks{02UZ}, applies to every abelian sheaf. Hence
$H^2(Y,1+\mathcal J)=0$. The long exact cohomology sequence of \stacks{01DZ} and the
identification of the Picard group with $H^1(\mathcal O^\times)$,
\stacks{09NU}, show that $i^*$ is surjective. Finally,
\stacks{09MW} identifies ampleness of an invertible sheaf with
ampleness of its restriction along any closed immersion inducing a
homeomorphism. This proves the last assertion without requiring a
nilpotence bound on $\mathcal J$.
\end{proof}
