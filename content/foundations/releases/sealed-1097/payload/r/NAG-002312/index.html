Algebraic uniqueness without a supplied common model

4 Analytic curves and skeleta

Open in the English reader · PDF page 780

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{corollary}[Algebraic uniqueness without a supplied common model]
\label{v037:f5:intrinsicuniqueness}
Two semistable algebraic models of $C$ with the same
component vertices are uniquely isomorphic by an
isomorphism inducing the identity on $C$.
The category of all semistable algebraic models of $C$,
with morphisms inducing the identity on $C$, is equivalent
to the partially ordered set of vertex sets that occur
for such models, ordered by reverse inclusion.

If both models carry finite \textup{\'etale} smooth markings
identified with the same finite \textup{\'etale} $O$-scheme
$D$, with the same generic marking on $C$, every morphism
in this category respects these identifications.
\end{corollary}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
The two vertex inclusions give morphisms in both directions
by Theorem~\ref{v037:f5:algebraicdomination}. Uniqueness in
that theorem makes their composites identities. The same
theorem identifies all morphism sets with the corresponding
inclusion relation; composition agrees because the morphisms
are unique.

For the marking assertion, compare the two maps from $D$
to the target. Their generic restrictions agree. On each
affine open of the finite \textup{\'etale} scheme $D$,
flatness makes its ring inject into its generic localization.
The generic fibre of $D$ is therefore schematically dense.
The target is separated, so the equalizer of the two maps
is closed and must be all of $D$. This proves compatibility
with the specified marking isomorphisms.
\end{proof}
