Statement · exact TeX
\begin{example}[A regular parameter without \(S_1\); F8SC-X]
\label{ex:S1}
For any field \(K\), set
\[
 R=\bigl(K[u,v,\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2,u\epsilon)\bigr)_{(u,v,\epsilon)}.
                                                                    \tag{4.1}
\]
The element \(v\) is a non-zero-divisor in the maximal ideal, but
\(R\) does not satisfy \(S_1\). Its unique minimal component has
dimension two. For the unlocalized algebra
\(A=K[u,v,\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2,u\epsilon)\), the morphism
\(\operatorname{Spec}A\to\mathbf A^1_K\) with parameter \(v\)
has pure one-dimensional fibres; \(R\) is its local ring at the origin.
\end{example}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Before localization the algebra is the direct sum
\(K[u,v]\oplus\epsilon K[v]\) as a \(K[u,v]\)-module.
Multiplication by \(v\) is injective on both summands and remains so
after localization. The unique minimal prime is \((\epsilon)\).
The prime \(\mathfrak q=(u,\epsilon)\) is embedded, with
\(\operatorname{Ann}(\epsilon)=\mathfrak q\).
In \(R_{\mathfrak q}\), the element \(\epsilon\) is nonzero,
\(\dim R_{\mathfrak q}=1\), and every member of the maximal ideal
annihilates \(\epsilon\). Its depth is zero, contradicting \(S_1\).
Every fibre of \(\operatorname{Spec}A\to\mathbf A^1_K\) has
underlying ring \(L[u]\) after reduction, where \(L\) is the residue
field of the parameter; hence it is pure of dimension one.
The same local calculation is available in the convergent analytic
germ at the origin over a complete nontrivially valued \(K\), replacing
the polynomial summands by convergent power-series germs.
\end{proof}
