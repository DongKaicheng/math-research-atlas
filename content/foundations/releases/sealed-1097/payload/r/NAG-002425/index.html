Integration of compactly supported smooth moment forms

5 Tropicalization, multiplicities and balancing

Open in the English reader · PDF page 1047

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{theorem}[Integration of compactly supported smooth moment forms]
\label{v037:forms:compactintegral}
Let $X$ be good, Hausdorff and pure of analytic dimension
$d$, and let $\mathcal F$ be a coherent
$\mathcal O_{X_G}$-module. There is a canonical linear
functional
\[
 \int_{X,\mathcal F}:\mathcal A_{X,c}^{d,d}(X)
                                  \longrightarrow\mathbb R
\]
on sections of compact support. It is nonnegative on
positive sections and additive in short exact sequences
of coherent modules.

Choose finitely many affinoid domains whose ordinary
interiors cover the support, and let $Q$ be their union.
The functional is the finite-chart integral of the
induced data on $Q$ and $\mathcal F|_{Q_G}$ from
Theorem~\ref{v037:integration:compactintegral}.
It is independent of this compact neighbourhood and
of all the presenting charts. It agrees with the
earlier affinoid and compact-space integrals whenever
those are applied to the induced data.
\end{theorem}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Put $S=\operatorname{supp}(\alpha)$. The zero section
has integral zero, so first suppose $S$ is nonempty.
The affinoid-neighbourhood basis in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{v037:forms:germsheaf} and compactness of
$S$ give the required finite family. Its union $Q$
is a compact analytic domain by the finite-union
assertion in Theorem~\ref{v037:integration:compactintegral}.
Its ordinary interior in $X$ contains $S$.
Theorem~\ref{v037:forms:germsheaf} supplies canonical
finite chart data on $Q$. The restriction of a coherent
sheaf is coherent, as is seen from its finite-module
presentations on affinoid domains and
Theorem~\ref{fnd:fullgkiehltheoremb}.
The compact-domain assertion of
Theorem~\ref{v037:integration:compactintegral} therefore
defines a real number, even when $Q$ is not good.

We prove independence of $Q$. Let $Q_1,Q_2$ be two
choices and put
\[
 O=\operatorname{Int}_{\rm top}(Q_1,X)
       \cap\operatorname{Int}_{\rm top}(Q_2,X),
       \qquad Z=X\setminus S.
\]
Both sets are open, $S\subseteq O$, and $O\cup Z=X$.
Cover the compact set $Q_1\cup Q_2$ by the ordinary
interiors of finitely many affinoid domains $W_l$,
each contained either in $O$ or in $Z$. Goodness and
the affinoid basis justify this choice. Mark as central
the members contained in $O$, and mark the remaining
members as zero members. Every central member belongs
to both $Q_1$ and $Q_2$.

Fix finite affinoid presentations $(V_i^{(a)})$ of
$Q_a$, for $a=1,2$. A finite affinoid cover of $Q_a$
is given by all the central $W_l$, together with the
domains $V_i^{(a)}\cap W_l$ for the zero members.
These are actual affinoid intersections by
Lemma~\ref{v037:integration:separatedintersections}
applied in $X$, and they cover $Q_a$ since the $W_l$
cover $Q_1\cup Q_2$.
The section has zero germ at every point of a zero
member. The zero-germ assertion of
Theorem~\ref{v037:forms:germsheaf} makes its induced
finite chart data zero on each zero piece and on every
further intersection involving such a piece.

Use these covers to compute the two integrals by the
finite inclusion-exclusion formula of
Theorem~\ref{v037:integration:compactintegral}.
If a covering member needs several charts, first
replace it by its finite presenting chart cover;
the independence statement of that theorem allows
this refinement. Every term involving a zero piece
is zero. The surviving terms involve only the same
central members, with the same restricted coherent
module and the same section. Choose their finite
presenting charts once for both covers. The two
finite sums are then identical. This proves
independence of the compact neighbourhood. The same
argument with no central members proves zero when
the support is empty.

Independence of the representatives on a fixed $Q$
is Theorem~\ref{v037:integration:compactintegral}.
For two compactly supported sections, choose one
finite affinoid neighbourhood of the union of their
supports. Its finite-chart integral is linear, which
proves linearity of the functional. If $\alpha$ is
positive, compactness permits the finite presenting
charts on $Q$ to be chosen from positive local
representatives and zero representatives outside its
support. Positivity of that same theorem gives the
claimed inequality. On every affinoid intersection
an exact sequence of coherent modules has an exact
sequence of finite section modules by
Theorem~\ref{fnd:fullgkiehltheoremb}. Additivity of
the compact-domain integral therefore proves the
asserted coherent-module additivity.

The comparisons with the earlier integrals follow
by using the same finite affinoid cover and the same
inclusion-exclusion expression. In the compact case
one may compare any support neighbourhood with $X$
itself by the same central-and-zero cover argument.
\end{proof}
