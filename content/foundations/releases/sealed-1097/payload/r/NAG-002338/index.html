\begin{lemma}[Finite \texorpdfstring{\'etale}{etale} integral lifting]
\label{v037:f5:finiteetaleintegrallift}
Let $R_0\to A_0$ be as in
Lemma~\ref{v037:f5:residualfiniteintegral}. Suppose in addition
that $A_0/\pi A_0$ is an \textup{\'etale}
$R_0/\pi R_0$-algebra. Then $A_0$ is finite projective as an
$R_0$-module and is an \textup{\'etale} $R_0$-algebra.
\end{lemma}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
The preceding lemma makes $A_0$ a finite $R_0$-module.
It has no $O$-torsion, so
Theorem~\ref{fnd:formaltorsionfreepresentation} makes this
module finitely presented. Its reduction modulo $\pi$ is
therefore a finitely presented module. It is flat by
\cite[Tag 02GS]{StacksAlg}, and hence finite projective by
\cite[Tag 00NX]{StacksAlg}. Choose an idempotent matrix
$\bar e\in M_m(R_0/\pi R_0)$ whose image is this module.

Lift $\bar e$ to a matrix $e_0$ over $R_0$. If
$\delta=e^2-e\in\pi M_m(R_0)$, then
$(2e-I)^2=I+4\delta$ is invertible by its convergent geometric
series. The Newton correction
\[
                 e'=e-(2e-I)^{-1}(e^2-e)
\]
has error $e'^2-e'$ divisible by $\delta^2$.
All matrices in this calculation commute, being convergent
expressions in $e$. Starting at $e_0$ gives a convergent
sequence with an idempotent limit $e\equiv\bar e\pmod\pi$.
This works in every characteristic. Put $P=eR_0^m$.
It is a complete separated direct summand of $R_0^m$.

Lift the images of the standard basis under the map
$(R_0/\pi R_0)^m\to A_0/\pi A_0$ to $A_0$, and restrict
the resulting map $R_0^m\to A_0$ to $P$.
The map $\varphi:P\to A_0$ is an isomorphism modulo $\pi$.
For $a\in A_0$, lift its class modulo $\pi$ to $p_0\in P$,
write $a-\varphi(p_0)=\pi a_1$, and repeat. Completeness
shows that $\sum_{n\geq0}\pi^np_n$ maps to $a$.
Thus $\varphi$ is surjective. If $\varphi(p)=0$, the
modulo-$\pi$ isomorphism gives $p=\pi p_1$ in $P$.
Since $A_0$ has no $\pi$-torsion, $\varphi(p_1)=0$.
Iteration puts $p$ in $\bigcap_n\pi^nP=0$.
Thus $A_0\simeq P$ is finite projective over $R_0$.

The algebra $A_0$ is finitely presented over $R_0$.
For completeness, choose a finite module presentation with
generators $b_1,\ldots,b_m$. Express $1$ and all products
$b_ib_j$ in these generators. The finite module relations,
the relation for $1$, and these product relations present
$A_0$ as an algebra: they reduce every polynomial to a
linear combination of the $b_i$, whose kernel is exactly
the module relation space.
It follows from the same polynomial presentation that
$\Omega_{A_0/R_0}$ is a finite $R_0$-module and that its
reduction modulo $\pi$ is
$\Omega_{(A_0/\pi A_0)/(R_0/\pi R_0)}=0$.
For every $a\in R_0$, $1-\pi a$ is invertible by a
geometric series. Thus $\pi$ belongs to the Jacobson
radical of $R_0$, and \cite[Tag 00DV]{StacksAlg} gives
$\Omega_{A_0/R_0}=0$. Finite projectivity implies flatness
by \cite[Tag 00NX]{StacksAlg}; the flat, finitely presented,
zero-differential criterion \cite[Tag 02GU]{StacksAlg}
now proves ordinary \textup{\'etaleness}.
\end{proof}
