Scalar extension of a finite Gauss fibre

5 Tropicalization, multiplicities and balancing

Open in the English reader · PDF page 960

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{proposition}[Scalar extension of a finite Gauss fibre]
\label{v036:gaussbasechange}
Let $L/K$ be any complete isometric extension, and put
$D_L=D\widehat\otimes_K L$. There are natural bounded algebra
isomorphisms
\[
 (A_{K,r})_L\simeq A_{L,r},\qquad
 E_{L,r}(D_L)\simeq
 E_{K,r}(D)\otimes_{H_{K,r}}H_{L,r},
\]
and the coefficient inclusion induces an isometric embedding
$H_{K,r}\hookrightarrow H_{L,r}$. In particular
\[
                         \delta_{L,r}(D_L)=\delta_{K,r}(D).
\]
For either of these finite fibre algebras $E$ over its indicated
field $H$, its degree is
\[
 \dim_HE=
 \sum_{\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Max}E}
       \operatorname{length}_{E_{\mathfrak p}}(E_{\mathfrak p})
       [\kappa(\mathfrak p):H].
\]
\end{proposition}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
The actual relative pushout
\ref{fnd:generalizedrelativepushout} extends the Laurent relation
presentation coefficientwise. Its norm is the Laurent coefficient
norm over $L$, by Theorem~\ref{fnd:multilaurentactualpresentation}.
The coefficient inclusion preserves the Gauss norm, so it extends
first to the fraction fields and then to an isometric embedding
$H_{K,r}\hookrightarrow H_{L,r}$.

The two bounded pushouts of $D$ and $L$ identify
\[
 D_L\simeq D\widehat\otimes_{A_{K,r}}A_{L,r}.
\]
By Proposition~\ref{fnd:generalizedfinitebasechange}, this is finite
over $A_{L,r}$ with its canonical module norm. Applying
Corollary~\ref{fnd:iteratedaffinoidbasechange} to the Gauss point,
and then the same finite-base-change proposition, gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 D_L\widehat\otimes_{A_{L,r}}H_{L,r}
   &\simeq D\widehat\otimes_{A_{K,r}}H_{L,r}\\
   &\simeq
     \bigl(D\widehat\otimes_{A_{K,r}}H_{K,r}\bigr)
                        \otimes_{H_{K,r}}H_{L,r}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The tensors on the last line are already complete because the
first factor is finite-dimensional over $H_{K,r}$. A basis over
$H_{K,r}$ becomes a basis after this field extension, proving
the degree equality, including degree zero.

A finite-dimensional algebra over a field is Artinian. Its local
product decomposition is \cite[Tag 00JB]{StacksAlg}. In a
composition series of $E_{\mathfrak p}$ every simple quotient is
$\kappa(\mathfrak p)$: the annihilator of a generator of a simple
module is maximal, and this local ring has only one maximal ideal.
Taking dimensions gives the displayed sum. Apply this argument
to each of the two actual fibre algebras.
\end{proof}
Standing context 1 · exact TeX
Let $K$ be complete for its specified real-valued non-Archimedean
absolute value, which may be trivial. For $r\in\mathbb R_{>0}^d$ put
\[
 A_{K,r}=K\{r^{-1}T,rU\}/(T_iU_i-1)_{i=1}^d.
\]
The actual Laurent presentation of
Theorem~\ref{fnd:multilaurentactualpresentation} identifies this algebra
isometrically with the convergent Laurent series
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \sum_{m\in\mathbb Z^d}a_mT^m,
 \qquad |a_m|r^m\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{outside finite subsets},\\
 \left\|\sum_m a_mT^m\right\|_r=\max_m|a_m|r^m.
 \end{gathered}
\]
By Proposition~\ref{fnd:gaussnorm} and density of finite Laurent sums,
this norm is multiplicative. Write $\xi_{K,r}$ for its spectrum point
and $H_{K,r}$ for the completion of $\operatorname{Frac}(A_{K,r})$
for this norm. Thus $H_{K,r}=\mathcal H(\xi_{K,r})$. Empty tuples
give $A_{K,r}=H_{K,r}=K$.
Standing context 2 · exact TeX
For a finite $A_{K,r}$-algebra $D$ whose given norm is equivalent
to its canonical finite-module norm, define its Gauss fibre and degree by
\begin{equation}
\label{v036:eq:gaussdegree}
 E_{K,r}(D)=D\widehat\otimes_{A_{K,r}}H_{K,r},
 \qquad
 \delta_{K,r}(D)=\dim_{H_{K,r}}E_{K,r}(D).
\end{equation}
These are an actual finite analytic fibre algebra and a nonnegative
integer by Proposition~\ref{fnd:generalizedfinitebasechange} and
Theorem~\ref{fnd:allpointaffinoidfibres}.
