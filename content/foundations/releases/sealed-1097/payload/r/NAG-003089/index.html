\begin{example}[Finality cannot be omitted]\label{ex:coarser}
Let $E=\R^{(\mathbb N)}$ be the finitely supported sequences, but give it
the $\ell^2$ norm topology. Let $E_N$ consist of sequences supported in
$\{1,\ldots,N\}$ and let $D_m(x)=x_m$. Every $D_m$ is continuous;
$D_m|_{E_N}=0$ for $m>N$; and $D_m(x)$ is eventually zero for each $x$.
Nevertheless $D_m\not\to0$ in the strong dual of this normed space.
\end{example}
% CLAIM F8IT-C PROOF
\begin{proof}
The unit vectors form a bounded set $B=\{e_m:m\geq1\}$ and
$\sup_{b\in B}|D_m(b)|=1$ for every $m$. The norm topology is strictly
coarser than the locally convex final topology of the inclusions $E_N$:
the seminorm $x\mapsto\sum_{m\geq1}m|x_m|$ is continuous on every
$E_N$, hence final-continuous, but is unbounded on the norm-bounded set
$B$. With the final topology, this $B$ is not bounded.
\end{proof}
