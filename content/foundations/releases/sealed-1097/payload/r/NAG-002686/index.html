Explicit base collars and their whole Gauss fibre

Proper direct image on historical ordinary compact tests

Open in the English reader · PDF page 1846

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{lemma}[Explicit base collars and their whole Gauss fibre]
\label{psc:basecollars}
The domain \(Y_\varepsilon\) is a nonempty generalized affinoid,
pure of dimension \(d\), and \(\eta\in\operatorname{Int}(Y_\varepsilon/K)\).
For every positive array \(a\), \(q_a\) is finite, with
\[
 q_a^{-1}(\mathbb T_P)
  =\mathcal M\!\left(
       R_P[T_1,\ldots,T_p]/
       (P_{\ell,a}(T_\ell)-S_\ell)_{\ell=1}^p\right),
 \tag{1}\label{psc:monic}
\]
where \(S_\ell\) are the first \(p\) target coordinates.
The displayed algebra is free of rank \(D_a\) over \(R_P\)
and has its canonical finite-module norm.

Put \(b_\ell=\min_j a_{\ell j}\).
If \(P\) satisfies
\[
 |u_\ell|<b_\ell\varepsilon\ (1\leq\ell\leq p),
 \qquad |u_{p+b}|<\varepsilon\ (1\leq b\leq k)
                    \quad\hbox{for every }u\in P,
 \tag{2}\label{psc:boxbounds}
\]
then the \emph{entire} inverse image in (1) lies in the ordinary
interior of \(Y_\varepsilon\) in \(Z^{\rm an}\).
Consequently it is exactly the saturated collar
\((Q_at_g|_{Y_\varepsilon})^{-1}(P)\).

If \(\eta_a\) is the target unit Gauss point, then
\[
                         q_a^{-1}(\eta_a)=\{\eta\}.
 \tag{3}\label{psc:commongauss}
\]
For its actual Artinian fibre algebra \(E_a\),
\[
 \dim_{\mathcal H(\eta_a)}E_a=D_a
  =\sum_{\mathfrak m\in\operatorname{Max}E_a}
       \operatorname{length}((E_a)_{\mathfrak m})
       [\kappa(\mathfrak m):\mathcal H(\eta_a)] .
 \tag{4}\label{psc:basedegree}
\]
Equation (3) concerns points; (4) retains all length and field-degree
data and does not replace \(E_a\) by its reduction.
\end{lemma}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Set \(R=e^\varepsilon>1\). A presentation of \(Y_\varepsilon\)
uses \(t_\ell,z_b,z_b^{-1},(t_\ell-c_{\ell j})^{-1}\),
all with radius \(R\), and their polynomial inverse relations.
Indeed \(|t_\ell|\leq R\) and \(|c_{\ell j}|\leq1<R\)
give every upper bound \(|t_\ell-c_{\ell j}|\leq R\);
conversely one such upper bound gives \(|t_\ell|\leq R\).
The inverse coordinates give the lower bounds.
This is an actual generalized affinoid presentation.
All its coordinates have value \(1<R\) at \(\eta\), so the
presentation witnesses absolute interiority there.
It is an affinoid domain of the closed polyannulus in
\((t_\ell-c_{\ell1},z_b)\), cut out by the additional puncture
bounds. That polyannulus is integral and pure of dimension \(d\)
by \Dep{v036:analytic:polyannulusdegree}; domain purity
\Dep{v036:analytic:domainpurity} proves the assertion for
\(Y_\varepsilon\).

The algebraic coordinate ring of \(Z\) is
\[
 K[S_1^{\pm1},\ldots,S_p^{\pm1},Z_1^{\pm1},\ldots,Z_k^{\pm1}]
 [T_1,\ldots,T_p]/(P_{\ell,a}(T_\ell)-S_\ell)_\ell .
\]
To check the inverse coordinates in this identity, use
\[
 (T_\ell-c_{\ell j})^{-1}
 =S_\ell^{-1}(T_\ell-c_{\ell j})^{a_{\ell j}-1}
                 \prod_{h\ne j}(T_\ell-c_{\ell h})^{a_{\ell h}}.
\]
Successive division by the separate monic polynomials gives
the free basis \(\prod_\ell T_\ell^{i_\ell}\),
\(0\leq i_\ell<A_\ell\).
The frozen analytification theorem
\Dep{fnd:analytificationfiniteclosed} identifies the \emph{whole}
inverse image of any target affinoid with its ordinary tensor
algebra and canonical finite-module norm. It gives (1).

Here is the estimate which supplies saturation.
At any analytic point, fix a block \(\ell\).
If \(|t_\ell|>1\), all its puncture distances are equal to
\(|t_\ell|\). If \(|t_\ell|\leq1\), all those distances are
at most \(1\), and at most one is less than \(1\), because
the difference of two punctures has norm \(1\).
Thus its weight vector belongs to
\[
 F_\ell=\bigcup_{j=1}^{m_\ell}\mathbb R_{\geq0}e_{\ell j}
       \ \cup\
       \mathbb R_{\geq0}(-e_{\ell1}-\cdots-e_{\ell m_\ell}).
 \tag{5}\label{psc:star}
\]
On the positive ray \(j\), \(u_\ell=a_{\ell j}v_{\ell j}\);
on the last ray, \(u_\ell=-A_\ell s\) with all
\(v_{\ell j}=-s\).
In either case
\[
               |v_{\ell j}|\leq |u_\ell|/b_\ell.
 \tag{6}\label{psc:coercive}
\]
The final \(k\) weights are unchanged. Bounds (2) therefore
put every inverse-image point in the strict moment inequalities.
This proves the collar assertion without selecting any branch.

We prove (3) over the original field. The unit Gauss point
maps to \(\eta_a\): after reduction, the separate nonconstant
monic polynomials \(\overline P_{\ell,a}(\overline t_\ell)\),
together with the \(\overline z_b\), are algebraically
independent over \(\widetilde K\).
For example, their distinct leading multidegrees prove the
injectivity of polynomial substitution.
Scaling a largest coefficient of any Laurent polynomial
then proves the unit Gauss norm identity.

Conversely let \(y\) map to \(\eta_a\).
Equation (6), with \(u=0\), gives
\(|t_\ell-c_{\ell j}|=|z_b|=1\) at \(y\), hence
\(|t_\ell|\leq1\).
In the residue field of \(\mathcal H(y)\), the elements
\(\overline P_{\ell,a}(\overline t_\ell),\overline z_b\)
are algebraically independent: any relation would contradict
the target Gauss seminorm after lifting its finitely many
coefficients. Each \(\overline t_\ell\) is integral over the
field generated by these elements. Consequently the field
generated by \(\overline t_1,\ldots,\overline t_p,\overline z_1,
\ldots,\overline z_k\) has transcendence degree \(d\).
These \(d\) elements are therefore algebraically independent.
For any nonzero polynomial in \(t,z\), divide by a largest
coefficient. Its reduction is nonzero at \(y\), so its value
is the maximum coefficient norm. The same holds at \(\eta\).
Inverting the puncture coordinates and the \(z_b\) proves
equality on the entire algebraic coordinate ring, hence
equality of the analytic points \(y=\eta\).
No separability or defectlessness assertion enters this argument.

Finally the finite tensor comparison
\Dep{fnd:generalizedfinitetensor} identifies \(E_a\) with the
ordinary base change of the free module in (1).
Its dimension is \(D_a\). An Artinian composition series gives
the second equality in (4), with full residue-field degrees.
\end{proof}
