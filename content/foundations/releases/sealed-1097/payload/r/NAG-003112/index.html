Refined operations on every prescribed support

I Descent of the polarized mixed tower

Open in the English reader · PDF page 2143

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{theorem}[Refined operations on every prescribed support]
\label{f9sc:refined}
The maps $G_{(\cL,s),T}$ are well-defined on Chow groups and satisfy
all the following assertions.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For an integral $W\subset T$, if $s|_W$ is nonzero, the result
is the divisor cycle of $s|_W$, with its actual local lengths. If
$s|_W=0$, it is the image of $c_1(\cL|_W)\cap[W]$ in the stated
target, not an instruction to discard $W$.
\item Let $p:\cY\to\cX$ be proper, let $T'\subset\cY$ and
$T\subset\cX$ be closed with $p(T')\subset T$, and pull back the
actual line and section. Then
\begin{equation}\label{ex68:support:eq:projection}
 p_*G_{(p^*\cL,p^*s),T'}(\alpha)
       =G_{(\cL,s),T}(p_*\alpha)
       \quad\text{in }C_{r-1}(\cX;T\cap|D|).
\end{equation}
This includes naturality under enlargement of the prescribed support.
\item Two such operations commute in the group on
$T\cap|Z(s)|\cap|Z(t)|$. No proper-intersection hypothesis is needed.
\item For $(\cM,t)$ a second pair, set
$B=|Z(s)|\cup|Z(t)|$. After pushing the three targets into $T\cap B$,
\begin{equation}\label{ex68:support:eq:tensor}
            G_{(\cL\otimes\cM,s\otimes t),T}
                     =G_{(\cL,s),T}+G_{(\cM,t),T}.
\end{equation}
After pushing into $T$, the operation is
$c_1(\cL|_T)\cap-$. If a coherent $\mathcal F$ on $\cX$ is supported in $T$, has
$\dim_\delta\operatorname{Supp}(\mathcal F)\leq r$, and
$s:\mathcal F\otimes\cL^{-1}\to\mathcal F$ is injective, then
\[
 G_{(\cL,s),T}[\mathcal F]_r
   =[\mathcal F/s(\mathcal F\otimes\cL^{-1})]_{r-1}
             \quad\text{in }C_{r-1}(\cX;T\cap|D|).
\]
All generic and quotient lengths are retained.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
The definition gives (1), and \st{02TO} proves descent through
rational equivalence. Apply \st{02TA} to the proper map
$T'_{\mathrm{red}}\to T_{\mathrm{red}}$ and the restricted line and
section. Its target is the zero scheme of the restricted section on
$T$, so its pushforward identity is exactly \eqref{ex68:support:eq:projection}
after reduction. Applying this also to closed immersions proves the
support-enlargement statement. It does not pass through the image of
$\CH(T)$ in the ambient Chow group.

For clarity, the proper-pushforward calculation retains the factor
$[K(W'):K(p(W'))]$ when dimensions agree. If an integral $W'$ is
contracted, choose a rational section of the relevant line on its
integral image $W$. Its pullback divisor is supported over a proper
closed subset of $W$. Every component then has image dimension less
than $\dim_\delta W'-1$, and its cycle pushforward is zero. This also
handles the contained-component Chern term by its divisor representative.
Thus dimension-decreasing images do not create an unrecorded coefficient.

Apply \st{0B73} on $T_{\mathrm{red}}$. The rational equivalence in
its proof lies on the intersection of both zero schemes; in particular
its target is precisely the support in (3). Reduction and the naturality
just proved identify the successive targets used here with those of
that lemma.

To prove (4), work on an integral cycle $[W]$. If neither restricted
section is zero, divisors of their tensor product add as cycles. Locally
this is $\ord(fg)=\ord(f)+\ord(g)$; at a one-dimensional local domain
one can see the length equality from
$0\to A/(f)\xrightarrow{\,g\,}A/(fg)\to A/(g)\to0$.
If one or both sections vanish identically on $W$, then $W\subset B$.
The product contributes $c_1((\cL\otimes\cM)|_W)[W]$; each
nonzero restricted section represents its own first Chern class on
$W$. Additivity of that class, \st{02SP}, proves equality already
in $\CH(W)$, and hence in the required target. Linearity proves
\eqref{ex68:support:eq:tensor}. Finally \st{02T9} identifies the push into $T$
with the first Chern operation, and the coherent assertion uses the actual annihilator scheme
\(Z=V(\operatorname{Ann}\mathcal F)\), with \(|Z|\subset T\).
Keep the full coherent module on \(Z\). Tensor the asserted injection
with the invertible line so that \st{02TB}(2), with \(k=r-1\),
applies to the actual module and its restriction to the zero scheme.
This restriction is exactly
\(\mathcal F/s(\mathcal F\otimes\cL^{-1})\).
The closed inclusion \(Z_{\mathrm{red}}\to Z\) and its base-changed
zero scheme give the canonical identifications of the Chow groups:
the cycle and rational-function generators are unchanged.
Apply \st{02TA} to these inclusions and then apply \st{02TA} to the proper closed
map \(Z_{\mathrm{red}}\to T_{\mathrm{red}}\) with its actual zero
support. The coherent cycles are computed on the full modules
before these reduction identifications, so all lengths are retained.
Injectivity remains essential.
\end{proof}

Standing context 1 · exact TeX
Let $R$ be a DVR, $S=\operatorname{Spec}R$, $\eta$ its generic point,
and $k$ its residue field. For analytic assertions assume that $R$ is
the valuation ring of a complete nontrivially discretely valued field
$K$ with its fixed absolute value. Use the dimension function
$\delta(\eta)=1$, $\delta(s)=0$ at the closed point. A DVR is
Noetherian and Cohen--Macaulay: its uniformizer is a regular sequence
of length one, and its only other localization is its fraction field.
Thus \st{00NM} gives the universally catenary hypothesis of
\st{02QL}. On a finite-type $S$-scheme, dimensions below are
$\delta$-dimensions. In particular, a horizontal integral model of
relative dimension $d$ has dimension $d+1$, and a vertical integral
$k$-scheme has its usual dimension.

For a closed subset $T$ of a finite-type $S$-scheme $\cX$ set
\[
                  C_r(\cX;T)=\CH_r(T_{\mathrm{red}}).
\]
Rational equivalences use integral subschemes \emph{inside $T$}.
This group is not defined as the image in $\CH_r(\cX)$. Reduction
identifies Chow groups: integral subschemes, their rational functions,
and their divisor relations are unchanged. This observation does not
replace the fundamental cycle of a nonreduced scheme by that of its
reduction; its generic lengths remain coefficients of the cycle.
We freely use a scheme-theoretic intersection to construct a cycle
and the canonical reduction identification to place its class in the
group on its closed support.

Proper pushforward uses the full function-field degree on an integral
cycle whose image has the same dimension, and zero when the image has
smaller dimension (\st{02R4}). It descends to Chow groups by
\st{02S2}. Its composition law follows on prime cycles from the tower
law for field degrees; a dimension decrease remains a decrease under
further pushforward. All field degrees include their inseparable parts.
If $T$ is vertical and proper over $R$, then $T_{\mathrm{red}}$ is
proper over $k$. Its zero-cycle degree is pushforward to
$\operatorname{Spec}k$ (\st{0AZ1}); on closed points it is
$\sum n_x[\kappa(x):k]$ (\st{0AZ2}). Hence proper pushforward
preserves this degree while retaining the prescribed vertical support.
Standing context 2 · exact TeX
For an invertible sheaf $\cL$ and a global section $s$ on $\cX$,
let $D=Z(s)$. A section here is allowed to be zero or a zero-divisor;
``global section'' does not mean a regular section in the
non-zero-divisor sense. Apply the Gysin construction of \st{02T8}
on $T_{\mathrm{red}}$ and then identify its target with the reduced
support. Write the resulting operation as
\[
 G_{(\cL,s),T}:C_r(\cX;T)\longrightarrow
                         C_{r-1}(\cX;T\cap|D|).
\]
