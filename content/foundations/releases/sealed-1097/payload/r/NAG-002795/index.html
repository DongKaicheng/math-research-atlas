Uniform tube and boundary defect; F8RC-T

I Descent of the polarized mixed tower

Open in the English reader · PDF page 2115

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{lemma}[Uniform tube and boundary defect; F8RC-T]
\label{lem:tube}
Let \(V=\cM(A)\) be affinoid, \(f\in A\), and let the nonempty actual
zero scheme \(D=\cM(A/(f))\) have dimension at most \(d\). For any unit
tuple \(g\), put \(P_0=t_g(D)\). There are \(\rho>0\) and a finite
integral affine polyhedral set \(\Delta\subset P_0\), with
\(\dim\Delta\leq d-1\), such that
\begin{align}
 t_g(\{|f|<\rho\})&\subset P_0,\label{fnd:nag002795:whole-ordinary-tube}\\
 t_g(\partial_KV\cap\{|f|<\rho\})&\subset\Delta.\label{eq:boundarytube}
\end{align}
Here \(\Delta=\varnothing\) if \(d=0\). If \(D\) is empty, a sufficiently
small tube is empty. The same assertions hold over any specified complete
base field and with arbitrary radii.
\end{lemma}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
By \tagg{NAG-002358}, \(P_0\) is a compact finite integral affine
polyhedral set of dimension at most \(d\). The inverse image of each
compact cell is an actual affinoid Laurent domain: integral affine
inequalities become bounds on monomials in the units \(g_i\), with
positive real radii. Their finite union \(W_0=t_g^{-1}(P_0)\) is an
analytic domain, obtained by gluing its affinoid pieces along their
actual intersections. It contains the closed subspace \(D\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:neighbourhood} gives an ordinary neighbourhood \(O\) of
\(D\) contained in \(W_0\). On the compact complement \(V\setminus O\),
\(|f|\) has a positive minimum, if the complement is nonempty. This
proves \eqref{fnd:nag002795:whole-ordinary-tube} for a sufficiently small positive \(\rho\).

Use \tagg{NAG-002356} to write the entire absolute boundary as a finite
union of actual compact analytic fibres \(V_j\) over fields \(H_j\),
where \(d(H_j/K)=1\) and the maps \(V_j\to V\) preserve completed
residue fields. Let \(D_j\) be the actual zero scheme of \(f|_{V_j}\).
Its points are exactly \(D\cap V_j\). For such a point \(z\), the tower
formula and the maximum dimension bound give
\[
 d_{H_j}(z)=d_K(z)-1\leq d-1.
\]
Consequently \(\dim_{H_j}D_j\leq d-1\), by \tagg{NAG-001591}. If
\(d=0\), no such point exists. Each nonempty \(D_j\) has a finite
polyhedral image \(\Delta_j=t_g(D_j)\) of dimension at most \(d-1\),
by \tagg{NAG-002358} over \(H_j\).

Apply the first paragraph over \(H_j\), with \(V_j,D_j\), to get
\(t_g(V_j\cap\{|f|<\rho_j\})\subset\Delta_j\). If \(D_j\) is empty,
compactness gives a positive minimum of \(|f|\) on \(V_j\), so its
small tube is empty. Shrink \(\rho\) to work for all finitely many
\(j\), and take \(\Delta=\bigcup_j\Delta_j\). The maps preserve the
absolute values of the original units, so this is a subset of \(P_0\).
It gives \eqref{eq:boundarytube} for the actual absolute boundary,
not merely for a polyhedral boundary of a chosen chart.
\end{proof}
