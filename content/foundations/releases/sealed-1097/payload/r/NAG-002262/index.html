Integral Galois descent for unramified coefficients

4 Analytic curves and skeleta

Open in the English reader · PDF page 761

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{lemma}[Integral Galois descent for unramified coefficients]
\label{v036:f5:unramifiedcoefficients}
Suppose $l/k$ is finite Galois, take its Galois lift $L/K$, and
write $O'=L^\circ$ and $G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$.
Then $O'/O$ is finite free and \textup{\'etale}, and
\[
 O'\otimes_O O'\xrightarrow{\sim}\prod_{g\in G}O',
 \qquad a\otimes b\longmapsto(a\,g(b))_g.
\]
For every $O$-module $M$, the coefficient inclusion identifies
\[
                  M=(M\otimes_O O')^G.
\]
If $A_0$ is an admissible formal $O$-algebra, its coefficient
extension is the complete admissible algebra
$A_0\otimes_O O'$. Its generic algebra is
$A_0[1/\pi]\widehat\otimes_K L$.
\end{lemma}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Write $O'=O[\alpha]=O[T]/(P)$ as in
Lemma~\ref{cand:unramifiedmodelbasechange}, with
$1,\alpha,\ldots,\alpha^{d-1}$ an orthonormal $O$-basis.
The polynomial $P$ is monic, and $P'(\alpha)$ has nonzero
reduction. For a square-zero ideal, a lift $b$ of a root is
corrected uniquely to a root by $b-P(b)/P'(b)$; successive
square-zero quotients give the same assertion for a nilpotent
ideal. Thus the finite presentation is formally \textup{\'etale},
hence \textup{\'etale} by \cite[Tag 00UR]{StacksAlg}.
Lemma~\ref{cand:galoisresidueextensionlift}
gives all its roots $g(\alpha)$ in $O'$, with pairwise different
residues. Evaluation on these roots is an isomorphism: its matrix
on $1,T,\ldots,T^{d-1}$ has Vandermonde determinant
$\prod_{g<h}(h(\alpha)-g(\alpha))\in(O')^\times$.
This proves the tensor-product assertion, as well as faithful
flatness.

The fixed module assertion holds without dividing by $d$.
Here is an explicit descent argument. Let
$z=\sum_i m_i\otimes b_i\in M\otimes_O O'$ be invariant.
Under the displayed product isomorphism, the two elements
\[
 \sum_i m_i\otimes b_i\otimes1,
 \qquad \sum_i m_i\otimes1\otimes b_i
 \quad\text{in }M\otimes_O O'\otimes_O O'
\]
have equal $g$-coordinates for every $g$, so are equal.
Apply the $O$-linear coefficient map $\lambda:O'\to O$
which takes the coefficient of $1$ in the indicated basis;
it satisfies $\lambda(1)=1$. Applying $\lambda$ on the first
coefficient factor gives
$z=(\sum_i\lambda(b_i)m_i)\otimes1$.
Injectivity of $m\mapsto m\otimes1$ follows from the same
coefficient map. This proves the equality of modules.

A finite free sum commutes with $\pi$-adic completion.
The extended ring is $\pi$-torsion-free, and its finite
presentation follows by extending an admissible presentation
of $A_0$.
For the generic algebra put $A=A_0[1/\pi]$. Inverting $\pi$
identifies $(A_0\otimes_O O')[1/\pi]$ with $A\otimes_K L$ by
the coefficient maps. In Proposition~\ref{fnd:generalizedfinitebasechange}
take the finite algebra to be $L$ over $K$ and the target to be
$A$. The actual absolute value on $L$ is its finite-module
maximum norm in the displayed basis. The resulting maximum
norm on $A\otimes_K L$ and the defining quotient norm of
$(A_0\otimes_O O')[1/\pi]$ given by
Proposition~\ref{fnd:affineformalpresentations} have the same
closed unit ball $A_0\otimes_O O'$. Scaling by powers of
$\pi$ compares the norms in both directions with constant
$|\pi|^{-1}$, giving the required bounded isomorphism with
$A\widehat\otimes_K L$. All these identifications
preserve the chosen inclusion of $K$ and its absolute value.
\end{proof}
