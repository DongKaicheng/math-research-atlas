Simultaneous collars after one fixed finite cover

I Descent of the polarized mixed tower

Open in the English reader · PDF page 2107

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{theorem}[Simultaneous collars after one fixed finite cover]
\label{psc:simultaneous}
Use Definition~\ref{psc:data}. Suppose that
\[
 \pi:X=\mathcal M(B)\longrightarrow Y_\varepsilon
\]
is norm-admissible finite, that \(X\) is nonempty and pure of
dimension \(d\), and that \(S=\pi^{-1}(\eta)\) is nonempty.
Let \(f=\pi^*g\) and let \(M\) be any finite \(B\)-module.

For \emph{any two distinct positive arrays} \(a,a'\), choose
boxes satisfying (2), separately for the two arrays. Then
\[
 C_a=(Q_at_f)^{-1}(P_a),\qquad
 C_{a'}=(Q_{a'}t_f)^{-1}(P_{a'})
\]
are saturated norm-admissible finite collars in the same \(X\).
Their entire central analytic Gauss fibres are the same finite
point set \(S\subset t_f^{-1}(0)\).
Every \(x\in S\) is absolutely interior and has graded moment
rank \(d\).

The full pointed fan at every \(x\in S\) is
\[
                       F=F_1\times\cdots\times F_p\times\mathbb R^k,
 \tag{7}\label{psc:productfan}
\]
where \(F_\ell\) is (5). The same rational subdivision of
\(\mathbb R^k\) may be used for both projections.
On each top cone \(\sigma\) the projections are injective on its
span, and their saturated-lattice indices are
\[
 e_{a,\sigma}=[\mathbb Z^d:Q_aN_\sigma]
       =\prod_{\ell=1}^p
          \begin{cases}
             a_{\ell j},&\sigma\hbox{ uses the puncture ray }j,\\
             A_\ell,&\sigma\hbox{ uses the last ray}.
           \end{cases}
 \qquad N_\sigma=\mathbb Z^n\cap\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\sigma .
 \tag{8}\label{psc:index}
\]
Thus these data satisfy all hypotheses of the independently
reviewed definition \Dep{mso:setup} and theorem
\Dep{mso:sectorcomparison}, including its common entire Gauss
set and saturation requirements.
If some \(m_\ell\geq2\), \(F\) is not a \(d\)-plane.
\end{theorem}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Write \(Y_a=q_a^{-1}(\mathbb T_{P_a})\).
Lemma~\ref{psc:basecollars} gives \(Y_a\subset Y_\varepsilon\)
as the full inverse image, with its actual finite algebra.
Domain base change and finite base change,
\Dep{fnd:generalizeddomainbasechange} and
\Dep{fnd:generalizedfinitebasechange}, identify
\[
 C_a=\pi^{-1}(Y_a),\qquad
 \mathcal O(C_a)=
       B\otimes_{\mathcal O(Y_\varepsilon)}\mathcal O(Y_a).
\]
The tensor is already complete with its canonical finite norm.
The map \(C_a\to Y_a\) is finite, as is \(Y_a\to\mathbb T_{P_a}\).
Composing finite free quotient presentations proves finiteness
with the canonical norm for the composite. This construction
also proves the displayed saturation equality for all of \(X\).
The same argument applies to \(a'\).

By (3), a point of \(C_a\) maps to the central target Gauss point
if and only if its image under \(\pi\) is \(\eta\).
The entire fibre is therefore exactly \(S\), independently of
\(a\). Its finiteness follows from the actual finite-dimensional
algebra over \(\mathcal H(\eta)\); all local factors are kept.
The map \(\pi\) is everywhere relatively interior by
\Dep{fnd:generalizedfinitealgebras}.
Absolute interiority of \(\eta\) and the composition inclusion
\Dep{fnd:affinoidinteriorcalculus} give absolute interiority
of every \(x\in S\).

We identify the fan before transferring it to \(X\).
The norm calculation in the preceding lemma gives global
containment \(t_g(Z^{\rm an})\subset F\).
Every vector of \(F\) sufficiently near \(0\) is realized as
follows. For a puncture ray of size \(s\geq0\), use the Gauss
norm in \(t_\ell-c_{\ell j}\) of radius \(e^{-s}\).
For the last ray use the Gauss norm in \(t_\ell\) of radius
\(e^s\). Give each \(z_b\) its prescribed Gauss radius.
Take the simultaneous Gauss norm in these \(d\) independent
variables. These points have precisely the stated moments.
As all parameters tend to zero, every such family tends to
\(\eta\). Indeed at radius \(1\) translation by an element of
\(K^\circ\) preserves the unit Gauss norm, and polynomial
Gauss evaluations are continuous in the radii. The same is
true for the inverses of the puncture coordinates, hence,
by uniform approximation, for the affinoid functions on a
fixed surrounding domain. There are only finitely many
choices of rays. Every ordinary neighbourhood of \(\eta\)
therefore realizes every sufficiently small vector in \(F\).
The frozen full-stabilization theorem identifies its pointed
fan with \(F\).

For \(x\in S\), the isometric field embedding
\(\mathcal H(\eta)\hookrightarrow\mathcal H(x)\)
induces an injective map of graded residue fields.
The graded relations of the initial forms of \(f(x)\) over
\(\widetilde K^{\rm gr}\) are consequently \emph{exactly}
the relations of \(g(\eta)\), not a larger ideal.
The initial forms of \(t_\ell-c_{\ell1}\) and \(z_b\) are
algebraically independent, and all other initial forms are
obtained from them by the displayed translations.
Thus the graded moment rank is \(d\).
In \Dep{v038:pointed:gradedfinitebasis} the fan is defined from
precisely this homogeneous relation ideal.
Together with \Dep{v038:pointed:fullstabilization}, this proves
that every \(C_x\) equals \(F\), even when \(X\) is nonreduced.

For a top cone, its saturated tangent lattice is the direct
sum of the primitive ray lattice of each block and the full
\(\mathbb Z^k\). Each primitive block vector maps to
\(a_{\ell j}\) or \(-A_\ell\) times the corresponding target
basis vector. The matrix is diagonal, with the identity on
the last \(k\) directions. This proves injectivity and (8).
If \(m_\ell\geq2\), the span of \(F_\ell\) has dimension
\(m_\ell>1\), while that fan has dimension one.
Thus the span of \(F\) has dimension greater than \(d\), and
\(F\) is not a \(d\)-plane.

We have verified, individually, purity, actual units and
module, both saturated finite collars, the same full finite
Gauss set, absolute interiority, graded rank, and simultaneous
genericity on every top cone. These are exactly the hypotheses
of the reviewed sector comparison.
\end{proof}
