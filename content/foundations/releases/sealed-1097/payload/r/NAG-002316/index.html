Realizing an unramified marking on the original model

4 Analytic curves and skeleta

Open in the English reader · PDF page 781

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{theorem}[Realizing an unramified marking on the original model]
\label{v037:f5:unramifiedrefinement}
Let $\mathcal X$ be a semistable algebraic model of $C$,
and let $D$ be an unramified marking datum. A finite
sequence of algebraic blowups supported in the special
fibre gives a semistable algebraic model
$\mathcal X'\to\mathcal X$ in which $D_K$ extends to a
closed immersion of $D$ into the relative smooth locus.
Every original component vertex is retained.
\end{theorem}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Choose a finite unramified Galois extension $L/K$ which
splits the nodes of $\mathcal X$ and the finite
\textup{\'etale} scheme $D_s$. This can be done in the
construction of Theorem~\ref{cand:simultaneousnodalsplitting}:
enlarge its chosen finite Galois residue extension to contain
the splitting fields of the finitely many separable residue
fields of $D_s$, and then take the lift of
Lemma~\ref{cand:galoisresidueextensionlift}. The same
pointed-chart construction works over this enlarged lift.
Write $O'=L^\circ$ and $G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$.
This lift satisfies $[L:K]=[\widetilde L:k]$ and
$|L^\times|=|K^\times|$, with the given absolute value.
The residue fields in this choice are finite separable by
\cite[Tag 02GL]{StacksAlg}.

Every rational point of $D_{\widetilde L}$ extends to a
section of $D_{O'}$ by
Lemma~\ref{cand:completevaluationhenselian}. These sections
are open and closed, since $D_{O'}$ is finite and
\textup{\'etale}. They are disjoint: two distinct sections
cannot meet generically, since their equalizer is open and
closed in the connected local spectrum and their closed
points differ. Their complement is finite over $O'$ and
has empty closed fibre. Its finite coordinate algebra $M$
satisfies $M/\mathfrak m_{O'}M=0$, so it is zero by
\cite[Tag 00DV(2)]{StacksAlg}. Thus $D_{O'}$ is
a disjoint union of copies of $\operatorname{Spec}O'$, and
$D_L$ gives a finite $G$-invariant set of distinct
$L$-rational points of $C_L$.

Apply Theorem~\ref{cand:equivariantmarkedrefinement} to
$\mathcal X_{O'}$ and this set. Its proof gives a finite
sequence of blowups of $G$-invariant finite-type ideals.
Its Hausdorff generic-fibre hypothesis follows from
Theorem~\ref{cand:projectiveformalcompletion} and
Proposition~\ref{fnd:analytificationprojectivecompact}.
Each ideal is the finite product of the intrinsic ideals
of the nodal or smooth marked clusters. Their support is
a finite set of closed special points, and they contain a
power of the original $\pi$. At a nodal cluster, the actual
charts are those of
Lemma~\ref{cand:symmetricnodeblowup}; at a smooth cluster
they are those of Lemma~\ref{cand:smoothtubeinsertion}.
In particular these are actual flat nodal blowups over
$O'$, including when the value group is nondiscrete and
when a node has both branches on the same component.

Descend the first ideal by Lemma~\ref{v037:f5:idealdescent}
and blow it up over $O$. Its coefficient extension is the
first prescribed blowup, with the same $G$-action. Repeat
on the resulting original-field scheme. Induction descends
the entire sequence. Every descended blowup is projective
by \cite[Tag 02NS]{StacksAlg}, and its composite with the
original projective model is projective by
\cite[Tag 0C4P]{StacksAlg}. Its generic fibre is unchanged
because its centre contains a power of $\pi$. The nodal
relative-curve property, including flatness and local
finite presentation, descends from $O'$ by
\cite[Tag 0C5E]{StacksAlg}. Projectivity gives the required
quasi-compactness and separatedness, hence finite presentation.
The geometric special fibre remains connected: the displayed
local charts replace a smooth point by an attached
projective line, or a node by a chain of one or two
projective lines. These replacements preserve connectedness
after every residue-field extension. This proves that all
descended stages are semistable algebraic models.

On the last coefficient extension, the marked sections give
a $G$-invariant closed immersion
$D_{O'}\hookrightarrow\mathcal X'_{O'}$. Its ideal is of
finite type: locally at each marked point it is the ideal
of a section in a smooth relative curve, as proved in
Lemma~\ref{cand:smoothmarkcluster}; the sections are disjoint.
Descend this ideal by Lemma~\ref{v037:f5:idealdescent}, and
write $Z\hookrightarrow\mathcal X'$ for the resulting
closed subscheme. The morphism $Z/O$ is finite by
\cite[Tag 02LA]{StacksAlg}, since its coefficient extension
is $D_{O'}$. The equivariant isomorphism
$Z_{O'}\simeq D_{O'}$ identifies their finite coordinate
algebras. Taking invariants and using
Lemma~\ref{v036:f5:unramifiedcoefficients} gives an
$O$-isomorphism $Z\simeq D$, compatible with the generic
marking. Its image is in the relative smooth locus.
Indeed, the cover $\mathcal X'_{O'}\to\mathcal X'$ is
\textup{\'etale}. Around every marked point upstairs the
structural map to $\operatorname{Spec}O'$ is smooth, and
$\operatorname{Spec}O'\to\operatorname{Spec}O$ is
\textup{\'etale}. Smooth locality on the source,
\cite[Tag 036U]{StacksAlg}, first gives smoothness of the
composite and then descends it along this cover at the
marked image. Finally Theorem~\ref{v037:f5:algebraicdomination}
applied to $\mathcal X'\to\mathcal X$ gives retention of
all original component vertices.
\end{proof}
Standing context 1 · exact TeX
Let $K$ be complete and nontrivially real-valued, with its specified
absolute value. Put $O=K^\circ$, $\mathfrak m=K^{\circ\circ}$,
and $k=\widetilde K$, and fix $0\ne\pi\in\mathfrak m$.
Let $C/K$ be a smooth projective geometrically connected curve.
Semistable algebraic models and finite \textup{\'etale} smooth
markings have the meaning of Definition~\ref{v036:f5:modelclass}.
Every generic-fibre identification in this section is an
identification with this fixed curve over the original field.
