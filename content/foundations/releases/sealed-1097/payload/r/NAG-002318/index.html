Common original-field semistable models

4 Analytic curves and skeleta

Open in the English reader · PDF page 784

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{theorem}[Common original-field semistable models]
\label{v037:f5:commondomination}
Any finite nonempty family of semistable algebraic models
of $C$ has a semistable algebraic model dominating all of
them by morphisms inducing the identity on $C$.
If the models are marked by the same unramified marking
datum $D$, the common model and all the morphisms may be
chosen to preserve that marking.
\end{theorem}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
First consider two models $\mathcal X$ and $\mathcal Y$.
Lemma~\ref{v036:f5:multisections} gives a finite
\textup{\'etale} smooth marking $A$ on $\mathcal Y$ with
at least three geometric points on every geometric special
component, all distinct and away from the nodes. If a
marking $D$ is already present, choose $A$ disjoint from it.
Apply Theorem~\ref{v037:f5:unramifiedrefinement} to
$\mathcal X$ and the generic marking datum $A$, or
$A\amalg D$ in the marked case. This gives a model
$\mathcal Z\to\mathcal X$ where these markings have
pairwise distinct smooth specializations.

Compare over the isometric algebraically closed field
$\Omega$ of Lemma~\ref{v036:f5:comparisonfield}.
At a component vertex $w$ of $\mathcal Y_{\Omega^\circ}$,
choose three of the auxiliary marked points on its
geometric component. Their distinct smooth specializations
give three disjoint open-disc tubes, each with closure
equal to that disc together with $w$, by
Proposition~\ref{v035:formal:tubeends}. They contain the
chosen generic marked points. Lemma~\ref{v037:f5:threebranches},
applied to $\mathcal Z_{\Omega^\circ}$, makes $w$ a
component vertex there. Proposition~\ref{v036:f5:vertexarithmetic}
then gives the original-field inclusion
$V(\mathcal Y)\subset V(\mathcal Z)$.
Theorem~\ref{v037:f5:algebraicdomination} produces the
actual algebraic morphism $\mathcal Z\to\mathcal Y$.
In the marked case both morphisms respect $D$ by
Corollary~\ref{v037:f5:intrinsicuniqueness}.

Induct on the finite number of models, replacing the first
two by their common model. Each new common model dominates
the preceding one, so composition retains every morphism
already constructed, as well as the common marking.
\end{proof}
Standing context 1 · exact TeX
Let $K$ be complete and nontrivially real-valued, with its specified
absolute value. Put $O=K^\circ$, $\mathfrak m=K^{\circ\circ}$,
and $k=\widetilde K$, and fix $0\ne\pi\in\mathfrak m$.
Let $C/K$ be a smooth projective geometrically connected curve.
Semistable algebraic models and finite \textup{\'etale} smooth
markings have the meaning of Definition~\ref{v036:f5:modelclass}.
Every generic-fibre identification in this section is an
identification with this fixed curve over the original field.
