Formal smooth and nodal tube closures

4 Analytic curves and skeleta

Open in the English reader · PDF page 746

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{proposition}[Formal smooth and nodal tube closures]
\label{v035:formal:tubeends}
Let $\mathfrak X_s$ be reduced and pure one-dimensional, and
suppose $X=\mathfrak X_\eta$ is Hausdorff. Let
$q\in\mathfrak X_s(k)$ admit a pointed diagram
\[
 (\mathfrak X,q)\ \longleftarrow\ (\mathfrak U,u)
                   \ \longrightarrow\ (\mathfrak S,o)
\]
of adic, topologically finitely presented, formally \'etale
maps, with $\mathfrak U$ affine and $u$ rational over $k$.
Assume $\mathfrak S$ is either
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\operatorname{Spf}O\langle T\rangle,
       &&o=(\overline T),\\
 \text{or}\quad
 &\operatorname{Spf}O\langle S,T\rangle/(ST-a),
       &&o=(\overline S,\overline T),\quad 0<|a|<1.
 \end{aligned}
\]
In the smooth case $]q[$ is an open unit disc. Its closure
is the disc together with the component point above the
unique residual component through $q$, and is its one-point
compactification. In the nodal case $]q[$ is the annulus
$|a|<|S|<1$. Its two radial ends attach to the component
points of the corresponding two branches through $q$.
Its closure is obtained by adjoining these ends; the two
component points may coincide. Every neighbourhood of an
attached endpoint in this closure contains the full
corresponding radial tail.
\end{proposition}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Reduction of the displayed Jacobian presentations modulo
$\mathfrak m$ gives ordinary \'etale maps on special fibres.
Proposition~\ref{v035:formal:etaletubes}, applied to both
arrows, identifies their entire selected tubes. On the
standard chart, Proposition~\ref{fnd:formalstratumtubes}
computes them as $|T|<1$ and $|a|<|S|<1$, respectively.

First work in the compact Hausdorff generic fibre of
$\mathfrak U$. Its special fibre is smooth or nodal near
$u$. Shrinking about $u$ preserves the tube and permits us
to assume it is reduced and pure one-dimensional. In the
smooth case exactly one component passes through $u$:
the completion of the local special-fibre ring is $k[[T]]$
by \cite[Tag 0AGX]{StacksAlg}, and the ring injects into
this domain by \cite[Tag 00MC]{StacksAlg}. In the nodal case
Lemma~\ref{cand:pointednodalbranches} gives exactly two
components through $u$, corresponding to $T=0$ and $S=0$.
Lemma~\ref{v035:formal:tubeboundary} restricts the boundary
to their component points.

The open unit disc is not compact, since $|T|$ has
supremum one and never takes that value. Thus its compact
closure must add the unique possible boundary point.
A compact Hausdorff space obtained from an open locally
compact space by adding one point is its one-point
compactification. Its endpoint neighbourhoods contain
the complements of the closed discs $|T|\le r$, $r<1$:
every compact subset of the open disc has $\max|T|<1$.

For the nodal chart, write $v_T$ for the component point
of the branch $T=0$, and $v_S$ for that of $S=0$.
Their reductions give
\[
 |S(v_T)|=1,\qquad |T(v_S)|=1,\qquad |S(v_S)|=|a|.
\]
A net of points of the tube with $|S|\to1$ has a cluster
point in its compact closure. Continuity of $|S|$ and the
boundary inclusion force every cluster point to be $v_T$.
It therefore converges to $v_T$. The same argument treats
$|S|\to|a|$ and $v_S$. Applying this to Gauss points proves
that both endpoints occur. If an open neighbourhood of
$v_T$ contained no full outer tail, its compact complement
would contain a net with $|S|\to1$, a contradiction. The
inner tail is treated in the same way. Equivalently, the
complement has maximum $|S|<1$ or minimum $|S|>|a|$.

An \'etale map of special-fibre curves sends component
generic points to component generic points, by the
going-down argument in
Lemma~\ref{cand:pointednodalbranches}. All components
through $q$ occur this way: the local map at $u$ over $q$
is faithfully flat, and going down, \cite[Tag 00HS]{StacksAlg},
lifts each minimal prime below the prime of $u$. Functoriality of
specialization and uniqueness of component points identify
the analytic images of the endpoints. The image of the
compact tube closure in Hausdorff $X$ is closed and contains
$]q[$. Conversely every point in that image is in
$\overline{]q[}$, by continuity and density of the tube in
its closure. The map is an isomorphism on the open tube,
and an endpoint cannot map into it because its
specialization is generic. Thus the only possible
identification is between the two nodal endpoints.
The endpoint-tail assertions descend through this
compact-to-Hausdorff quotient.
\end{proof}
Standing context 1 · exact TeX
Let $K$ be complete and nontrivially valued, with its given absolute
value. Write $O=K^\circ$, $\mathfrak m=K^{\circ\circ}$ and
$k=O/\mathfrak m$, and choose $\pi\in O$ with $0<|\pi|<1$.
All formal schemes in this section are admissible and topologically
finitely presented over $O$. They carry the $\pi$-adic topology.
For a special-fibre point $q$, write $]q[$ for its entire inverse
image under specialization.
