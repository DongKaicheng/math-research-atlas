Integral Gauss-chart recognition of model vertices

4 Analytic curves and skeleta

Open in the English reader · PDF page 802

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{theorem}[Integral Gauss-chart recognition of model vertices]
\label{v037:f5:gausschartrecognition}
Let $C/K$ be smooth, projective and geometrically connected.
Every component vertex of a semistable algebraic model of $C$
over the original $O$, in the sense of
Definition~\ref{v036:f5:modelclass}, is the component point of
an integral \textup{\'etale} Gauss chart whose generic fibre is
an actual affinoid domain of $C^{\an}$. The special fibre of
this chart may be chosen integral.

Consequently, if $C$ admits one such original-field model,
the following conditions on $v\in C^{\an}$ are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $v$ is a component vertex of a semistable algebraic
model over $O$;
\item $v$ is a component point of an integral
\textup{\'etale} Gauss chart, as in
Definition~\ref{v037:f5:integralgausschart}, whose generic
fibre is an actual affinoid domain of $C^{\an}$;
\item $v$ has an unramified branch certificate of
Definition~\ref{v037:f5:branchcertificate}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Let $\mathcal C$ be the given model and let $\eta_E$ be the
generic point of its special component $E$. The special nodal
curve is smooth at every component generic point.
Flatness, finite presentation, and
\cite[Tag 01V9]{StacksAlg} therefore make the family smooth
at $\eta_E$. By \cite[Tag 054L]{StacksAlg}
there is an affine open neighbourhood
$\mathcal V=\operatorname{Spec}B$ of $\eta_E$ with an
\textup{\'etale} coordinate map
$\mathcal V\to\mathbf A^1_O$.
The smooth family has relative dimension one, so the
affine-space dimension in that lemma is one.
Shrink $\mathcal V$ by a principal open so that its special
fibre is integral. To do so, in the reduced finite-type
$k$-algebra $B/K^{\circ\circ}B$, there are only finitely
many minimal primes by \cite[Tags 00FO and 00FR]{StacksAlg}.
Choose, for every minimal
prime other than that of $E$, an element in that prime but
not in the prime of $E$. Their product vanishes on all
other components and is nonzero at $\eta_E$.
Lift it to $B$ and invert it. The remaining special fibre
is a nonempty open of $E$. Its reduced coordinate ring
has a unique minimal prime, which is zero by
\cite[Tag 00E0]{StacksAlg}, so it is a domain.
The coordinate map remains \textup{\'etale}.

Write $\bar B=B/K^{\circ\circ}B$ after this shrink, and
write $t$ for the residual coordinate. The map
$\operatorname{Spec}\bar B\to\mathbf A^1_k$ is
\textup{\'etale} by \cite[Tag 02GO]{StacksAlg}; its image
is nonempty open by \cite[Tag 03WT]{StacksAlg}.
Hence $k[t]\to\bar B$ is injective. Its generic fibre is
a nonzero domain \textup{\'etale} of finite type over
$k(t)$, so \cite[Tag 02GL]{StacksAlg} makes it a finite
separable field. That field is $k(E)$.

Choose finitely many $k[t]$-algebra generators of $\bar B$.
Each satisfies a monic polynomial over $k(t)$. Clearing
the finitely many coefficient denominators gives one
nonzero $\bar g\in k[t]$ for which
\[
                 \bar B[1/\bar g]
       \quad\hbox{is finite over}\quad k[t,1/\bar g].
\]
Indeed, each chosen generator is now integral, and the
monic relations reduce their powers to finitely many
bounded-exponent monomials. Choose a lift $g\in O[T]$.
The algebra $B[1/g(T)]$ remains finitely presented and flat
over $O$, and is \textup{\'etale} of finite presentation
over $O[T,1/g(T)]$.

Let $A_0$ be its actual $\pi$-adic completion, and set
\[
              R_0=O\langle T,Z\rangle/(g(T)Z-1).
\]
Lemma~\ref{cand:affineflatformalcompletion} gives
admissibility, the actual restricted presentation, and
unchanged reductions modulo every $\pi^n$; the base
completion is $R_0$ by
Proposition~\ref{fnd:formalprincipalopenflat}.
The relative algebraic presentation gives a continuous
surjection $R_0\langle Y_1,\ldots,Y_s\rangle\to A_0$.
Because $\pi\in K^{\circ\circ}$, its reduction modulo
$K^{\circ\circ}$ is exactly $\bar B[1/\bar g]$.
Thus the residual-finiteness hypothesis of
Lemma~\ref{v037:f5:residualfiniteintegral} holds.
Its reduction modulo $\pi$ is the base change of the
ordinary \textup{\'etale} algebraic coordinate map, and
is \textup{\'etale} by \cite[Tag 02GO]{StacksAlg}.
Lemma~\ref{v037:f5:finiteetaleintegrallift} proves that
$A_0$ is finite projective and \textup{\'etale} over $R_0$.
Its nonempty integral special fibre is the chosen open of $E$.

The completion of $\mathcal C$ is an actual formal model
of $C^{\an}$ by
Theorem~\ref{cand:projectiveformalcompletion}.
The completed affine open just constructed has generic
fibre $U=\mathcal M(A_0[1/\pi])$, an actual affinoid
domain of $C^{\an}$; completion and restriction agree by
Lemma~\ref{cand:affineflatformalcompletion} and
Proposition~\ref{fnd:formalglobalgenericfaithful}.
Its special generic point is still $\eta_E$.
The component-point uniqueness and restriction statement
of Proposition~\ref{cand:reducedmodelcomponentpoints}
therefore identify its unique component point with the
original vertex $v_E$. This proves the first assertion.

It gives (1)$\Rightarrow$(2).
Theorem~\ref{v037:f5:gausschartcertificate} gives
(2)$\Rightarrow$(3), and
Theorem~\ref{v037:f5:vertexcertificate} gives
(3)$\Rightarrow$(1) under the stated existence hypothesis.
All constructions use $O$, its chosen $\pi$, and its
specified absolute value throughout.
\end{proof}
