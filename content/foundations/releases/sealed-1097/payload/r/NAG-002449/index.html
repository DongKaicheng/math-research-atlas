\begin{lemma}[Independent initial forms survive Gauss normalization]
\label{v038:pointed:gaussnormalization}
Let $V=\mathcal M(A)$ be a generalized $K$-affinoid space,
$x\in V$, and let $f_1,\ldots,f_s\in A^\times$ have
algebraically independent initial forms in $F_x$ over $k$.
Put $r_i=|f_i(x)|$. Give $K(S_1,\ldots,S_s)$ the
weighted Gauss absolute value with $|S_i|=r_i$ and
write $L$ for its completion. There exists an analytic
point $x_L\in V_L$ above $x$ with
\[
 \widetilde{f_1/S_1}(x_L),\ldots,
 \widetilde{f_s/S_s}(x_L)
\]
algebraically independent over $\widetilde L$.
Moreover $x\in\operatorname{Int}(V/K)$ implies
$x_L\in\operatorname{Int}(V_L/L)$.
\end{lemma}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Put $H=\mathcal H(x)$ and let $E$ be the completion of
$H(S_1,\ldots,S_s)$ for the same weighted Gauss norm.
The given norm on $K(S_1,\ldots,S_s)$ is its restriction,
so $L$ embeds isometrically in $E$. Evaluation at $x$
gives a bounded map $A\to H\subset E$. Together with
$L\to E$ it induces a bounded map
$A\widehat\otimes_K L\to E$ and hence a point $x_L$
above $x$.

Write $u_i=\widetilde{f_i(x)}$ and
$s_i=\widetilde{S_i}$. The defining Gauss norm says
that the graded Laurent polynomial ring
$F_x[s_1^{\pm1},\ldots,s_s^{\pm1}]$ embeds in
$\widetilde E^{\rm gr}$: a nonzero finite sum has a
maximal Gauss term and its initial form does not vanish.
Here polynomial independence holds even when two
weighted monomials have the same numerical degree,
because the Gauss norm does not cancel distinct
monomials. The $u_i$ are independent over $k$ by
assumption, while the $s_i$ are independent over the
entire $F_x$. Consequently the $u_i/s_i$ are
algebraically independent over the ordinary fraction
field of $k[s_1^{\pm1},\ldots,s_s^{\pm1}]$:
any polynomial relation, after clearing denominators
and powers of the $s_i$, would be a polynomial
relation in the $u_i$ with coefficients in
$k[s_1^{\pm1},\ldots,s_s^{\pm1}]$; independence of
the $u_i$, followed by independence of the $s_i$,
makes each coefficient zero. The degree-one residue
field $\widetilde L$ is a subfield of this fraction
field, as follows directly by reducing fractions for
the weighted Gauss norm. Thus the displayed normalized
initial forms are independent over $\widetilde L$.
The inclusion of the graded residue field of
$\mathcal H(x_L)$ in that of $E$ preserves such
independence.

Finally, base-change preservation of relative interior
in Proposition~\ref{fnd:affinoidinteriorcalculus},
applied over the point $\mathcal M(K)$, gives the last
assertion. The argument did not change the originally
specified absolute value on $K$.
\end{proof}
