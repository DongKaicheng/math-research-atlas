Weights of finite modules on an interior flat germ

5 Tropicalization, multiplicities and balancing

Open in the English reader · PDF page 988

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{theorem}[Weights of finite modules on an interior flat germ]
\label{v037:degree:moduleweight}
Let $Y=\mathcal M(A)$ be nonempty and pure of analytic
dimension $d$. Let a unit moment tuple $f$ define
$t:Y\to\mathbb R^n$, and let $w\in t(Y)$. Suppose that
the entire fibre $F_w=t^{-1}(w)$ lies in
$\operatorname{Int}(Y/K)$ and that, for some neighborhood $W$
of $w$ and a saturated rank-$d$ lattice $N\subset\mathbb Z^n$,
\[
                         t(Y)\cap W\subset w+N_{\mathbb R}.
\]
For $\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Min}(A)$, put
$Y_{\mathfrak p}=\mathcal M(A/\mathfrak p)$. Set
$m_{\mathfrak p}(w)=0$ if its moment fibre at $w$ is empty;
otherwise this fibre satisfies the same flat-germ hypotheses,
and let $m_{\mathfrak p}(w)$ be its positive intrinsic weight.

Let $M$ be a finite $A$-module. For any integral
$Q:\mathbb Z^n\to\mathbb Z^d$ injective on $N$, let $D_w$
be the actual algebra of $F_w$, let $R_Q$ be the polytorus
algebra at $Qw$, and let $H_Q$ be its Gauss field. Put
\[
 E_Q=D_w\widehat\otimes_{R_Q}H_Q,\qquad
 e_Q=[\mathbb Z^d:Q(N)],\qquad
 \delta_Q(M,w)=\dim_{H_Q}(M\otimes_AE_Q).
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
\label{v037:degree:eqmoduleweight}
 \frac{\delta_Q(M,w)}{e_Q}
       =\sum_{\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Min}(A)}
                    \beta_{\mathfrak p}(M)m_{\mathfrak p}(w)
       \in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}.
\end{equation}
In particular this integer, denoted $m_{M,f}(w)$, is independent
of $Q$ and is additive in short exact sequences of finite
$A$-modules. For $M=A$ it is the intrinsic weight of $Y$:
\[
 m_{Y,f}(w)=
 \sum_{\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Min}(A)}
       \operatorname{length}(A_{\mathfrak p})m_{\mathfrak p}(w).
\]
These module weights are unchanged by restriction to an
affinoid domain containing the whole fibre, whenever the
two underlying pure-dimensional germs satisfy the stated
flat-germ hypotheses.
\end{theorem}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{v036:flatgermweight} gives every finite map
$R_Q\to D_w$, with its canonical norm and actual Gauss fibre.
Apply Proposition~\ref{v037:degree:genericgauss} to the domain
$F_w\subset Y$.

The quotient $A\to A/\mathfrak p$ is finite with its canonical
norm. Its spectral map is everywhere interior relative to $Y$
by Theorem~\ref{fnd:generalizedfinitealgebras}. The composition
inclusion in Proposition~\ref{fnd:affinoidinteriorcalculus}
therefore makes every point over $F_w$ interior over $K$.
The affinoid $Y_{\mathfrak p}$ is pure of analytic dimension
$d$ by the assumed purity of $A$, and its moment image is
contained in that of $Y$. If its fibre at $w$ is nonempty,
Theorem~\ref{v036:flatgermweight} applies with the same lattice
$N$.

The actual domain base-change theorem
\ref{fnd:generalizeddomainbasechange} and finite tensor comparison
\ref{fnd:generalizedfinitebasechange} identify its full fibre
algebra with $D_w/\mathfrak pD_w$. This is finite over $R_Q$.
Its actual Gauss algebra is
\[
 (D_w/\mathfrak pD_w)\widehat\otimes_{R_Q}H_Q
                         =E_Q/\mathfrak pE_Q.
\]
For a nonempty fibre its dimension is
$e_Qm_{\mathfrak p}(w)$ by the flat-germ weight theorem.
For an empty fibre the actual algebra is zero, by
Theorem~\ref{fnd:generalizedpointunitcriterion}, and the same
identity holds with $m_{\mathfrak p}(w)=0$.
Substitution in
\eqref{v037:degree:eqgenericmeasurement} proves
\eqref{v037:degree:eqmoduleweight}.

All tensors in this argument also have their canonical
analytic meaning for $M$. The finite module
$M\otimes_AD_w$ has its complete canonical norm by
Proposition~\ref{fnd:generalizedfinitetensor}. A quotient of
a finite sum of copies of the finite canonical $R_Q$-module
$D_w$ gives its canonical finite-$R_Q$-module topology:
compose the two finite free quotient presentations.
Applying the same tensor comparison over $R_Q$ shows that
$M\otimes_AE_Q$ is already complete. Thus no separated
completion discards a submodule in the length calculation.

Flatness of $A\to E_Q$ makes its tensor functor exact, proving
additivity. For $M=A$ the numerator is the ordinary Gauss
degree of $D_w$, so the formula gives exactly $m_{Y,f}(w)$.

Finally let $A\to A_V$ represent a domain containing $F_w$,
and replace $M$ by $M\otimes_AA_V$. The actual full fibre
algebra is still $D_w$, by
Theorem~\ref{fnd:generalizeddomainbasechange}. The saturated
tangent lattice is unchanged by
Theorem~\ref{v036:flatgermlocalconstancy}. For a common $Q$,
associativity gives
\[
 (M\otimes_AA_V)\otimes_{A_V}E_Q=M\otimes_AE_Q.
\]
Both the Gauss dimension and its lattice divisor are the same,
which proves the restriction assertion.
\end{proof}
