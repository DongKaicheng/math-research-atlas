NAG-002851 · Accepted whole

Weak convergence does not control the logarithm

G Stable inertia and original-field models

Open in the English reader · PDF page 2008

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{proposition}[Weak convergence does not control the logarithm]
\label{f9ml:weakcounterexample}
For every $d\geq2$ and $K$ as in \hyperref[f9ml:finite-model-data]{the standing finite-model data} there are genuine semipositive
product-model measures $\mu_j$ of mixed mass one on
$X=(\mathbf P^1_K)^d$ and a fixed model-metrized line $(M,h)$ with a
nonzero regular section $s$ such that
\[
 \mu_j\Longrightarrow\delta_o,\qquad
 \int-\log\|s\|_h\,d\mu_j=j\lambda\longrightarrow+\infty,
 \qquad o\in Z(s).
\]
Thus finite-model integrability and weak measure convergence, even with
an unchanged metric on $M$, do not imply logarithmic integrability of the
limit. This does not contradict convergence theorems that also require
uniform convergence of the semipositive metrics defining the measures.
\end{proposition}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
For each $j\geq1$ use the model $(\mathbf P^1_R)^d$ with generic-fibre
identification $T_i=\pi^jS_i$, and the model lines
$\operatorname{pr}_i^*\cO(1)$. They are semipositive: on any special-fibre
curve their degrees are nonnegative, since they are generated by global
sections. The special fibre has one reduced component, $b=1$, and its
mixed degree is one, by intersecting the $d$ coordinate divisors at one
$k$-rational product point. Its model measure is therefore
$\mu_j=\delta_{\xi_j}$, where
\[
 \left|\sum_{m\in\mathbf N^d}a_mT^m\right|(\xi_j)
                  =\max_m|a_m|\,|\pi|^{j(m_1+\cdots+m_d)}.
\]
Gauss multiplicativity gives the point, and its polynomial evaluations
converge to $|a_0|$, the evaluations at the origin $o$.
The evaluation topology in \hyperref[fnd:affineanalytificationconstruction]{\texttt{NAG-001707}} gives $\xi_j\to o$ in the
common affine chart and thus in $X^\an$. Consequently the Dirac masses
converge against every continuous analytic test, not just radial tests.

Use $M=\operatorname{pr}_1^*\cO(\infty)$ with its standard model
metric and its global section $s=T_1$ relative to the frame $1$ on
the finite chart. On that chart
\[
             \|s(x)\|_h=\frac{|T_1(x)|}{\max(1,|T_1(x)|)}.
\]
This extends as a regular section nonzero at infinity, with zero divisor
$\{T_1=0\}$. At $\xi_j$ the norm is $|\pi|^j$, so the integral is
$j\lambda$. At $o$ it is zero and its negative logarithm is infinite.
For fixed $R$, the tails are $(j\lambda-R)_+$ and are unbounded in
$j$. The curvature metrics defining $\mu_j$ are moving; their uniform
convergence is not claimed.
\end{proof}
