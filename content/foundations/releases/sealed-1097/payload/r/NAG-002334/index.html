Unramified closed points from separable residual points

4 Analytic curves and skeleta

Open in the English reader · PDF page 796

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{lemma}[Unramified closed points from separable residual points]
\label{v037:f5:gausschartpointlifts}
Suppose $U$ is identified with an actual affinoid domain
of the analytification of a smooth projective geometrically
connected curve $C/K$. Every closed point $q\in Y$ with
$l=\kappa(q)$ finite separable over $k$ lifts to an
$O$-homomorphism
\[
                         A_0\longrightarrow L^\circ,
\]
where $L/K$ is a finite unramified lift of $l/k$.
Its generic analytic point is the analytification of a
closed point of $C$ with residue field exactly $L$.
Distinct residual points give distinct closed points of $C$.
The disjoint union of the corresponding
$\operatorname{Spec}L^\circ$ for any finite such list is
an unramified marking datum of
Definition~\ref{v037:f5:unramifieddatum}.
\end{lemma}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Choose $L/K$ by Lemma~\ref{cand:residueextensionlift}.
Its valuation ring has the monic presentation
$O[\alpha]=O[S]/(P)$ and the orthonormal basis
$1,\alpha,\ldots,\alpha^{[l:k]-1}$ of
Lemma~\ref{cand:unramifiedmodelbasechange}.
Since $P'(\alpha)$ is a unit, across a square-zero ideal
the unique correction of a lifted root $b$ is
$b-P(b)/P'(b)$. This proves formal \textup{\'etaleness};
the finite presentation is \textup{\'etale} by
\cite[Tag 00UR]{StacksAlg}. Thus $L^\circ/O$ is finite
free and \textup{\'etale}, with the original extending norm.

Choose $c\in L^\circ$ lifting the image of $t$ at $q$.
The value $g(c)$ is a unit because $\bar g(q)\ne0$.
Substitution $T\mapsto c$, $Z\mapsto g(c)^{-1}$ gives
$R_0\to L^\circ$. The finite \textup{\'etale} algebra
$A_0\otimes_{R_0}L^\circ$ has the rational special point
defined by $q$. Lemma~\ref{cand:completevaluationhenselian}
provides a section through it, and hence the stated map.
It is continuous for the $\pi$-adic topology. Its generic
map $A\to L$ is bounded by the integral-presentation norm
of Proposition~\ref{fnd:affineformalpresentations}.

Let $x\in U\subset C^{\an}$ be the resulting point.
On an affine algebraic neighbourhood of its image, the
mapping property of
Theorem~\ref{fnd:affineanalytificationconstruction}
gives a homomorphism from its coordinate ring to $L$.
Its image is a finite-dimensional domain over $K$, hence
a field: the minimal polynomial of any nonzero element
expresses its inverse as a polynomial in that element.
Thus the corresponding point $p\in C$ is closed and
$\kappa(p)\subseteq L$. Proposition
\ref{fnd:analytificationvaluedfibres} and completeness
of finite extensions in Lemma~\ref{fnd:finiteextensionnorms}
identify $\mathcal H(x)$ with $\kappa(p)$.

Choose $a\in A_0$ whose residue at $q$ is a primitive
generator $\beta$ of $l/k$, using \cite[Tag 030N]{StacksAlg}.
Write $b$ for its value in $L^\circ$ and $e=[l:k]$.
The change-of-basis matrix from
$1,\alpha,\ldots,\alpha^{e-1}$ to
$1,b,\ldots,b^{e-1}$ has invertible reduction, since both
residue lists are bases of $l/k$. Its determinant is a
unit, so $1,b,\ldots,b^{e-1}$ is an $O$-basis of $L^\circ$.
Evaluation of analytic functions takes values in
$\mathcal H(x)$, with the same values under its canonical
embedding in $L$, by Proposition~\ref{new:residueuniversal}.
Consequently $b\in\kappa(p)$ and $K(b)=L$, proving
$\kappa(p)=L$.

Two different points $q$ have different analytic lifts,
because specialization is a function. A finite residue
field over the complete field $K$ has only its unique
extending absolute value, so one closed algebraic point
cannot give those two different analytic points.
The resulting maps $\operatorname{Spec}L\to C$ are
therefore distinct closed immersions. Their finite
disjoint union is a closed immersion, while their integral
coefficient schemes are finite \textup{\'etale} over $O$.
This is exactly the required marking datum.
\end{proof}
