A potential smooth model with an original-field obstruction

4 Analytic curves and skeleta

Open in the English reader · PDF page 826

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{theorem}[A potential smooth model with an original-field obstruction]
\label{v038:f5:cubicramificationobstruction}
Assume $\operatorname{char}k\notin\{2,3\}$, and choose
$a\in K$ with $0<|a|<1$. Let $C_a$ be the projective curve
\[
                       y^2z=x^3+a z^3.
\]
It is smooth and geometrically connected. If
\[
          |a|^{1/2}\notin|K^\times|
                 \quad\text{or}\quad
          |a|^{1/3}\notin|K^\times|,
\]
then $C_a$ has no semistable algebraic model over the
original valuation ring $O$.

Nevertheless, if $b\in\Omega$ satisfies $b^6=a$ and
$L=K(b)$, the curve has the smooth projective model
\[
                \mathcal E/L^\circ:\quad Y^2Z=X^3+Z^3,
                \qquad
                [x:y:z]=[b^2X:b^3Y:Z].
\]
The unique component vertex of
$\mathcal E_{\Omega^\circ}$ is fixed by every isometric
$K$-automorphism of $\Omega$.
In particular, for $K=\mathbf Q_p$, $p\geq5$, with the
specified absolute value $|p|=p^{-1}$, the choice $a=p$
gives the stated nonexistence over $\mathbf Z_p$.
\end{theorem}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
We first verify the actual model and its special curve.
The integers $2$ and $3$ are units in $O$, and hence in
$L^\circ$. The two affine charts $Z\ne0$ and $Y\ne0$
cover $\mathcal E$: the equation rules out a projective
point with $Y=Z=0$. Their coordinate rings are
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 L^\circ[X,Y]/(Y^2-X^3-1),\\
 L^\circ[u,v]/(v-u^3-v^3).
 \end{gathered}
\]
Each is free over the polynomial algebra in the first
variable, since its equation is monic, up to a unit, in
the second. They are therefore flat over $L^\circ$.
They have finite algebra presentations; the projective
closed embedding makes the resulting morphism separated
and quasi-compact, so it is finitely presented.

On the first chart, $Y^2-X^3=1$ makes $D(X)$ and $D(Y)$
a cover; the corresponding partial derivatives
$-3X^2$ and $2Y$ are units. On the second chart, a point
with $v\ne0$ belongs to the first chart, whereas a point
with $v=0$ has $1-3v^2$ invertible. These are actual
standard smooth presentations by
\cite[Tag 01V9(6)]{StacksAlg}. Thus $\mathcal E/L^\circ$
is smooth of relative dimension one.

Over any extension field of $\widetilde L$, the polynomial
$X^3+1$ is not a square in the rational function field
in $X$: its order at infinity is $-3$, whereas the order
of a square is even. Therefore $Y^2-X^3-1$ is irreducible.
Its quotient ring injects into the corresponding quadratic
field over that rational function field, by its monic
degree-two basis, so the affine chart is integral.
The only point of its projective complement is
$[0:1:0]$. Every fibre is smooth of relative dimension
one; in particular this point is on a one-dimensional
component and cannot be an additional component by itself.
The affine chart is consequently dense, and the fibre
is connected. It is geometrically reduced and regular
by \cite[Tag 056T]{StacksAlg}. This verifies the exact
semistable-model conditions, including geometric
connectedness, over $L^\circ$.

The same calculation with $a\ne0$ over any extension of
$K$ proves that $C_a$ is smooth and geometrically
connected: the affine chart has equation
$y^2=x^3+a$, with partial derivatives $2y$ and $-3x^2$,
which cannot both vanish on it; the unique point at
infinity has a unit partial derivative in the chart
$y\ne0$. Its affine polynomial is irreducible by the
same odd-order calculation.
The root $b$ exists in the chosen algebraically closed
$\Omega$. The extension $L/K$ has its actual degree
$[K(b):K]\leq6$ and is complete with the extending
absolute value by Lemma~\ref{fnd:finiteextensionnorms}.
The displayed coordinate change identifies the generic
fibre of $\mathcal E$ with $C_{a,L}$, and
$|b|=|a|^{1/6}$ for this unchanged extending value.

Write $E$ for the special curve after extension to
$\overline k$. Its function field is not rational.
Here is a direct verification. On the affine chart
the differential
\[
                              \omega=\frac{dX}{2Y}
\]
extends across a zero of $Y$ as $dY/(3X^2)$, since
$2Y\,dY=3X^2\,dX$ and $X\ne0$ there. Near infinity
put $u=X/Y$ and $v=Z/Y$, so $v=u^3+v^3$.
The affine coordinates are $u/v$ and $1/v$; differentiating
the equation gives
\[
 (1-3v^2)\,dv=3u^2\,du,\qquad
 \omega=\frac{v\,du-u\,dv}{2v}
                          =-\frac{du}{1-3v^2}.
\]
This is regular at infinity. The differential is nonzero:
on the affine function field the derivation
$\delta(X)=2Y$, $\delta(Y)=3X^2$ respects the equation
and evaluates $\omega$ to $1$.

If $\overline k(E)$ were $\overline k(t)$,
\cite[Tag 0BY1]{StacksAlg}, applied to the smooth
projective curve $E$ and $\mathbf P^1_{\overline k}$,
would identify these curves. But a regular differential
on $\mathbf P^1$ has the form $f(t)\,dt$ on its affine
line, with $f\in\overline k[t]$; at $s=t^{-1}$ it is
$-f(s^{-1})s^{-2}\,ds$, which is regular at $s=0$
only when $f=0$. The nonzero $\omega$ is a contradiction.

Complete $\mathcal E_{\Omega^\circ}$ along any fixed
$0\ne\pi\in K^{\circ\circ}$.
Theorem~\ref{cand:projectiveformalcompletion} identifies
its actual analytic generic fibre with $C_{a,\Omega}^{\an}$.
Proposition~\ref{cand:reducedmodelcomponentpoints} supplies
the unique component vertex $w$ over the generic point
of $E$. Put $v=p(w)$.
The reduction identification in
Proposition~\ref{cand:componentvaluedresiduefield}
shows that the affine functions $X$ and $Y$ have
norm one at $w$, since both have nonzero residues at
the generic point of $E$. Hence the original rational
functions $x/z$ and $y/z$ satisfy
\[
             |(x/z)(v)|=|a|^{1/3},\qquad
             |(y/z)(v)|=|a|^{1/2}.
\]
They are nonzero elements of $\mathcal H(v)$, so either
failure in the hypothesis makes its value group strictly
larger than $|K^\times|$.
Proposition~\ref{v038:f5:essentialcomponentobstruction}
now rules out every original-field semistable model.

Finally, let $\sigma$ be an isometric $K$-automorphism
of $\Omega$. Then $\sigma(b)=\zeta b$ with
$\zeta^6=1$, and $|\zeta|=1$.
The canonical semilinear action on $C_{a,\Omega}$
transforms the displayed integral coordinates by
\[
                    X\longmapsto\zeta^{-2}X,
                    \qquad Y\longmapsto\zeta^{-3}Y,
                    \qquad Z\longmapsto Z.
\]
These substitutions and their inverses preserve the
equation over $\Omega^\circ$, so give an actual
semilinear automorphism of the smooth model. Its unique
special component, and thus its component vertex $w$,
is preserved. For $K=\mathbf Q_p$ and $a=p$, the two
displayed values are $p^{-1/3}$ and $p^{-1/2}$, neither
of which lies in $|\mathbf Q_p^\times|=p^{\mathbf Z}$.
Throughout, the absolute value was extended and never
rescaled.
\end{proof}
