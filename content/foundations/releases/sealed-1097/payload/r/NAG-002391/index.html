Integration and closedness of a local balanced cycle

5 Tropicalization, multiplicities and balancing

Open in the English reader · PDF page 1018

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{theorem}[Integration and closedness of a local balanced cycle]
\label{v037:integration:balancedcurrent}
Let $C$ be a local weighted integral $d$-cycle on an open
subset $U\subset N_{\mathbb R}$ in the sense of
Definition~\ref{v037:analytic:localcycledefinition}.
For smooth superforms with compact support on $C$, the
cell formula
\[
                         I_C(\alpha)=\sum_\sigma m_\sigma
                                                 \int_\sigma\alpha
\]
defines a linear functional in bidegree $(d,d)$, independent
of finite local presentations and subdivisions. It is
nonnegative on forms whose tangential top coefficients are
nonnegative whenever all weights are nonnegative.
If $C$ is balanced, then
\[
                      I_C(d'\beta)=0,
                     \qquad I_C(d''\gamma)=0
\]
for compactly supported forms of bidegrees $(d-1,d)$ and
$(d,d-1)$, respectively. For $d=0$ the functional is the
weighted sum of values and both differential assertions are
empty.

If an integral affine map $a$ is proper on the support of
$C$, has finite source presentations near the target support,
and defines the lattice-index pushforward $a_*C$, then
\[
                            I_C(a^*\alpha)=I_{a_*C}(\alpha)
\]
for every smooth $\alpha$ with compact ambient support in the target
open set.
\end{theorem}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Let $K$ be the support of the form on $C$. Around each point
of this compact set choose a finite presentation and a
smaller closed rectangular box contained in its open domain.
Finitely many box interiors cover $K$. Intersect the finitely
many presented cells with these boxes and take a common
hyperplane refinement. On overlaps their weights agree by
the definition of a local cycle. Count each resulting cell
once with that weight. This gives a finite polyhedral
description on an open neighbourhood of $K$ in the support.
Cells or artificial faces outside that neighbourhood meet
no nonzero tangential coefficient of the form. The integral
may therefore be computed on this finite description.

Two such descriptions have a common refinement near $K$.
The first assertion of
Lemma~\ref{v037:integration:polyhedralcalculus} proves that
the two sums coincide. Linearity and nonnegativity follow
from the corresponding inequalities for real grid sums.

For $\beta$, use such a finite description around its support.
Any artificial exterior facet lies outside that support; all
the adjacent tangential coefficients of $\beta$ vanish in
a relative neighbourhood, so their boundary contractions
vanish there as well. On a genuine $(d-1)$-face $\tau$,
formula~\eqref{v037:eq:superstokes} adds the normal lifts
with their weights. Its contribution is
\[
 (-1)^{d(d+1)/2}\int_\tau
   \beta(v_1',\ldots,v_{d-1}';v_1'',\ldots,v_{d-1}'',
                    (\sum_{\sigma\supset\tau}
                             m_\sigma u_{\sigma/\tau})'')
                                                    \,d\mu_\tau.
\]
Balancing puts the vector in the last slot in
$(N_\tau)_{\mathbb R}$. The double-primed factor alternates,
so this expression is zero. Summing the finitely many faces
gives $I_C(d'\beta)=0$.
The identities $d''=Jd'J$ and $J\omega_{N_\sigma}=(-1)^d
\omega_{N_\sigma}$ give the assertion for $\gamma$ by
exchanging the two exterior factors. This verifies both
closedness statements with the stated normalization.

For the projection formula, properness makes the support of
the pullback on $C$ compact. Choose finite source and target
presentations near these compact sets and refine along all
cell images. On a source $d$-cell of full rank, the affine
map is injective on its affine span. The change-of-variables
formula of Lemma~\ref{v037:integration:polyhedralcalculus}
multiplies its contribution by
$[N_\tau:a(N_\sigma)]$. On a source cell of rank less than
$d$ the pullback vanishes. Grouping the remaining finitely
many contributions over each target cell gives exactly its
lattice-index pushforward weight. This is the asserted
identity.
\end{proof}
