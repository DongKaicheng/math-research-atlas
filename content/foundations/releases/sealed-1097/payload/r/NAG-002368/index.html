Finite neighborhoods of a relative interior level

5 Tropicalization, multiplicities and balancing

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{theorem}[Finite neighborhoods of a relative interior level]
\label{v037:degree:finitelevelneighborhood}
Let $p:X=\mathcal M(B)\to Y=\mathcal M(A)$ be any affinoid
morphism, and let a unit moment tuple on $Y$ define
$t_Y:Y\to\mathbb R^{n'}$. Fix $w'\in t_Y(Y)$ and suppose
\[
                   p^{-1}(t_Y^{-1}(w'))\subset\operatorname{Int}(X/Y).
\]
There is a closed rectangular box $P$ about $w'$, with
$w'$ in its interior, for which
\[
 Y_0=t_Y^{-1}(P),\qquad X_0=p^{-1}(Y_0),\qquad
                              p_0:X_0\longrightarrow Y_0
\]
are actual affinoid domains and a finite morphism with
the canonical module Banach structure. The box may be
chosen inside any prescribed neighborhood of $w'$.

Suppose in addition that $X$ and $Y$ are pure of analytic
dimension $d$, that $w'$ is a whole interior flat germ of
$t_Y$ with saturated lattice $N_Y$, and that unit moment
coordinates on $X$ and an integral lattice map $a$ satisfy
the character identity $t_Y\circ p=a\circ t_X$. Suppose
\[
                 a^{-1}(w')\cap t_X(X)=\{w_1,\ldots,w_s\}
\]
is finite and nonempty, and that the local source image
at each $w_i$ lies in $w_i+(N_i)_{\mathbb R}$ for a
saturated rank-$d$ lattice $N_i$. Write $A_0$ and $B_0$
for the actual algebras of $Y_0$ and $X_0$. For a sufficiently
small box as above, all the original source weights are
defined and
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_i m_{X,f_X}(w_i)[N_Y:a(N_i)]
   &=m_{B_0,f_Y}(w')\\
   &=\sum_{\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Min}(A_0)}
              \operatorname{length}_{(A_0)_{\mathfrak p}}
                              ((B_0)_{\mathfrak p})
                     m_{(Y_0)_{\mathfrak p},f_Y}(w').
\end{aligned}
\]
The component weight is zero for an empty fibre. The resulting
integer is independent of the chosen box. If the displayed
generic module lengths are $\Delta$ times those of $A_0$
on all components meeting the fibre, then the middle term
is $\Delta m_{Y,f_Y}(w')$.
\end{theorem}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{fnd:affinoidinteriorcalculus}, the
relative boundary
$Z=X\setminus\operatorname{Int}(X/Y)$ is closed.
It is compact by Proposition~\ref{fnd:boundedspectrumcompact}.
The compact set $(t_Y\circ p)(Z)$ does not contain $w'$,
so choose a closed box $P$ about $w'$ avoiding it and
inside the prescribed neighborhood. The simultaneous
coordinate bounds give the actual domain $Y_0$ by
Theorem~\ref{fnd:generalizedrationaluniversal}.
Theorem~\ref{fnd:generalizeddomainbasechange} gives the
actual domain $X_0$, with algebra
$B\widehat\otimes_AA_0$.

Every point of $X_0$ comes from
$\operatorname{Int}(X/Y)$. Base change of relative interior
in Proposition~\ref{fnd:affinoidinteriorcalculus} makes
$p_0$ everywhere interior. Theorem
\ref{fnd:affinoidboundarylessfinite} proves that $B_0$
is finite over $A_0$ and has its canonical module norm.
This proves the first assertion over the original field
and absolute value.

For the remaining assertions choose $P$ also inside the
target flat-germ neighborhood. Purity of $X_0$ and $Y_0$
follows from Theorem~\ref{v036:analytic:domainpurity}.
The whole target fibre is contained in $Y_0$; all its
defining inequalities are strict at that fibre.
The relative domain presentation and the composition
inclusion \ref{fnd:affinoidinteriorcalculus} therefore make
it interior over $K$ in $Y_0$. Its weight is the original
one by Theorem~\ref{v036:flatgermlocalconstancy}.

Each entire original source fibre at $w_i$ is contained
in $X_0$, because $a(w_i)=w'$. It is interior over $K$
in $X$ by the assumed relative interior and the target
absolute interior. It is also interior in $X_0$ by the
finite relative interior of $p_0$ and composition.
The local flat containment persists under restriction.
Theorem~\ref{v036:flatgermweight} defines these weights;
Theorem~\ref{v036:flatgermlocalconstancy} identifies their
values before and after restriction. The complete set
of source moment values over $w'$ is unchanged.
Theorem~\ref{v037:degree:finitecyclepushforward}, applied
to the actual finite map $p_0$, now gives the displayed
formula and the assertion involving $\Delta$.

For independence of the box, intersect any two sufficiently
small boxes. Their intersection is again a closed box
with $w'$ in its interior. The associated finite maps
restrict to the same actual map on this intersection,
by Theorem~\ref{fnd:generalizeddomainbasechange} and
Proposition~\ref{fnd:generalizedfinitebasechange}.
The finite module over either target restricts to this
same module, and the intersection contains the whole
target moment fibre. The restriction assertion in
Theorem~\ref{v037:degree:moduleweight} proves equality of
the two middle terms. This proves the claimed independence.
\end{proof}
