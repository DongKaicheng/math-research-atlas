Protection by elliptic tails

4 Analytic curves and skeleta

Open in the English reader · PDF page 756

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{lemma}[Protection by elliptic tails]
\label{v035:f5:elliptictails}
Let $\mathcal C$ be a split nodal algebraic model with vertex set
$W$, and let $V\subset W$ be a semistable vertex set of $X$.
There is a proper flat finitely presented prestable family
$\mathcal Y_0/O$ obtained by attaching three smooth elliptic tails
at distinct smooth points on every component indexed by $V$.
Its genus is $g(C)+3|V|$, its generic fibre is stable, and its
stabilization $c:\mathcal Y_0\to\mathcal Y$ contracts precisely
the original special components indexed by $W\setminus V$.
The stable family $\mathcal Y$ is projective over $O$.
\end{lemma}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
First choose a smooth projective elliptic $O$-curve $\mathcal E$
with a smooth section. If $\operatorname{char}k\ne2$, take the
projective completion of
\[
                 y^2=x(x-1)(x-\lambda),
          \qquad \overline\lambda\notin\{0,1\}.
\]
Its fibres are smooth by the distinct roots and the unit $2$.
If $\operatorname{char}k=2$, take $y^2+y=x^3$. The special fibre
has $y$-derivative one. In mixed characteristic a singular affine
point would have $x=0$ and $y=-1/2$, which does not satisfy the
equation; in equal characteristic the derivative is again one.
The point at infinity is smooth in both equations.
Lemma~\ref{cand:integralplanecubicfamily} supplies flatness,
finite presentation, and projectivity. Each fibre is a smooth plane
cubic, so its global functions are the constants and its genus is one by
\cite[Tag 0BYD]{StacksAlg}. Use the infinity section.

Choose three distinct smooth $k$-points on each special component
indexed by $V$, outside its nodes. Such points exist because a
nonempty smooth open of a curve over the algebraically closed field
$k$ has infinitely many closed points: its closed points are dense
and rational by \cite[Tag 00FV]{StacksAlg}, and finitely many closed
points cannot be dense in a curve. By
Corollary~\ref{cand:smoothpointpolydisctube}, each chosen tube contains
a $K$-point. The valuative criterion
\cite[Tag 0A40]{StacksAlg} extends this point uniquely to an $O$-section;
its specialization is the chosen point. These sections are disjoint
and lie in the relative smooth locus: an intersection, or a closed
complement of the smooth locus meeting a section, would also meet
its special point. Attach a separate copy of $\mathcal E$ at each
section by Lemma~\ref{v035:f5:pinch}.

Both geometric fibres of $\mathcal C$ are connected of genus $g(C)$
by Lemma~\ref{v035:f5:connectedgenus}. On either fibre, over its
algebraically closed ground field $F$, one elliptic attachment gives
\[
 0\longrightarrow\mathcal O_{A\cup E}
 \longrightarrow\mathcal O_A\oplus\mathcal O_E
 \longrightarrow\mathcal O_p\longrightarrow0.
\]
The final map on global sections is the surjective difference
$F\oplus F\to F$. Its cohomology sequence gives
$H^0(A\cup E,\mathcal O)=F$ and
$H^1(A\cup E,\mathcal O)=H^1(A,\mathcal O)\oplus H^1(E,\mathcal O)$.
Thus the fibres remain connected and their genus is $g(C)+3|V|$.
The set $V$ is nonempty: otherwise connected compact $X$ would itself
be an open ball or annulus. Hence this genus is at least three.
The connected nodal-fibre criterion
\cite[Tags 0E6L, 0E6T]{StacksAlg} makes $\mathcal Y_0$ prestable.

Apply stabilization \cite[Tag 0E8A]{StacksAlg}. Its fibre maps
contract rational tails and then rational bridges as described in
\cite[Tag 0E7Q]{StacksAlg}. In a component $U$ of $X\setminus V$
containing an unwanted vertex, Lemma~\ref{v035:f5:finiteforest}
gives a finite tree of smooth rational components with one or two
labelled boundary ends. In a one-ended tree, successively remove
leaves away from the boundary end. These are rational tails and
remove the whole tree. In a two-ended tree, first remove all leaves
away from the unique path joining its ends; then contract the
remaining rational bridges. The two boundary ends remain distinct
branch labels if they attach to the same component in $V$, and
contracting the path creates a self-node on that component.
Every unwanted vertex is thereby removed. At each stage every
$V$-component retains its three auxiliary elliptic attachments, and
every elliptic component retains its one attachment. The remaining
components are stable. The uniqueness of stabilization proves that
exactly these components survive.

In the generic fibre the original smooth curve has at least three
attachments and each elliptic tail has one, so this fibre is already
stable. Its stabilization is therefore its identity. Finally
\cite[Tag 0E74]{StacksAlg} makes the relative dualizing line bundle
of $\mathcal Y$ ample over a neighborhood of the closed point of
$\operatorname{Spec}O$, hence over the whole base. By
\cite[Tags 0D32, 0B5N]{StacksAlg}, $\mathcal Y$ is a projective
scheme over this local base.
\end{proof}
Standing context 1 · exact TeX
For the rest of this section and the next, assume that $K$ is
algebraically closed. Let $C/K$ be a smooth connected projective curve
and put $X=C^{\an}$. A split nodal algebraic model means a projective,
flat, finitely presented $O$-model of $C$ with reduced nodal special
fibre and the split pointed algebraic charts of
Theorem~\ref{cand:pointednodalvaluationchart}. The smoothing parameters
at its nodes are nonzero. Theorems~\ref{cand:projectiveformalcompletion}
and~\ref{cand:splitmodelskeleton} identify its completed generic fibre
with $X$ and supply its embedded finite graph and retraction.
The space $X$ is compact Hausdorff by
Proposition~\ref{fnd:analytificationprojectivecompact}.
