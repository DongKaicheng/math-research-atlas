The topological coequalizer

Proper direct image on historical ordinary compact tests

Open in the English reader · PDF page 1956

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{lemma}[The topological coequalizer]\label{f9ds:topdescent}
The map $p:Y\to X$ is a closed surjection and hence a quotient map.
Every pair $y_1,y_2\in Y$ with $p(y_1)=p(y_2)$ lifts to a point of
$Y^{[2]}$ with projections $y_1,y_2$. Consequently pullback identifies
$C^0(X,\mathbb R)$ with
\[
 \{u\in C^0(Y,\mathbb R):q_1^*u=q_2^*u
                          \text{ on }|Y^{[2]}|\}.
\]
\end{lemma}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
At $x\in X$, put $H=\HH(x)$. The actual fibre algebra is
$C=H\otimes_KL$ by the finite-tensor and affinoid-fibre theorems.
It is a nonzero $n$-dimensional $H$-algebra. Each residue field of an
Artinian local factor is finite over the complete field $H$ and
therefore has the unique extending absolute value. It defines a point
of the fibre. Thus $p$ is surjective.

If $F$ is closed in $Y$ and $V$ is an affinoid neighbourhood in $X$,
then $F\cap p^{-1}(V)$ is closed in the compact affinoid $p^{-1}(V)$.
Its image is compact, hence closed in the Hausdorff space $V$.
That image is exactly $p(F)\cap V$. The ordinary interiors of such
$V$ cover $X$, so $p(F)$ is closed. A continuous closed surjection is
a quotient map.

For the pair assertion, let $E_i=\HH(y_i)$, a finite extension of $H$.
The nonzero finite-dimensional $H$-algebra $E_1\otimes_HE_2$ has a
field quotient $F'$. Both $E_i$ embed in $F'$. Uniqueness of the
extending absolute values makes these embeddings isometric. The
compatible evaluations to $F'$ are bounded; by the finite analytic
tensor universal property they define a point of $Y^{[2]}$ with the
required projections. This uses the entire tensor, whether or not
it is reduced.

The equalizer condition on $u$ now says exactly that $u$ is constant
on every fibre of $p$. It defines a unique function $v$ on $X$ with
$u=v\circ p$. For an open set $O\subset\mathbb R$, the preimage of
$v^{-1}(O)$ is $u^{-1}(O)$; the quotient property proves continuity
of $v$. The converse follows by composition.
\end{proof}
