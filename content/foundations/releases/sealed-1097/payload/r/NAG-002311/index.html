Algebraic domination from vertex inclusion

4 Analytic curves and skeleta

Open in the English reader · PDF page 778

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{theorem}[Algebraic domination from vertex inclusion]
\label{v037:f5:algebraicdomination}
For two semistable algebraic models $\mathcal X_1,\mathcal X_2$
of $C$, there is an $O$-morphism
\[
                       f:\mathcal X_1\longrightarrow\mathcal X_2
\]
inducing the identity on $C$ if and only if
$V(\mathcal X_2)\subset V(\mathcal X_1)$ in $C^{\an}$.
When it exists, it is unique and proper, and
$f_*\mathcal O_{\mathcal X_1}=\mathcal O_{\mathcal X_2}$.
\end{theorem}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
A morphism of the indicated models is proper by its closed
graph and the properness of the source over $O$. Its completion
induces the analytic identity, so
Lemma~\ref{v035:f5:dominationvertices} proves the necessary
vertex inclusion.

Suppose conversely that the inclusion holds. Form
$\mathcal G$ and its two projections as in
Lemma~\ref{v037:f5:graphmodel}. Both targets are normal and
integral by Proposition~\ref{v036:f5:genericnormalization}.
We show that $p_2$ is constant on every set-theoretic fibre
of $p_1$.

Choose the isometric comparison field $\Omega$ of
Lemma~\ref{v036:f5:comparisonfield}, with residue field
$\overline k$. The two base-changed target models are
semistable algebraic models over $\Omega^\circ$. Their nodes
have the split rational pointed charts of
Theorem~\ref{cand:simultaneousnodalsplitting}, since $\Omega$
is algebraically closed. Completing these charts gives the
pointedly semistable formal models of
Definition~\ref{v035:formal:modelclass}. By
Corollary~\ref{v035:formal:partition}, the fibres of their
specialization maps are their singleton component vertices
and the connected components of the complements of those
vertex sets. Proposition~\ref{v036:f5:vertexarithmetic}
identifies their vertex sets with the entire inverse images
of the original vertex sets, so
\[
        V(\mathcal X_{2,\Omega^\circ})
                    \subset V(\mathcal X_{1,\Omega^\circ}).
\]

Consequently $\operatorname{sp}_2$ is constant on every
fibre of $\operatorname{sp}_1$ on $C_\Omega^{\an}$.
For a component generic point the latter fibre is a
singleton. For a closed point it is a connected component
of the complement of the larger vertex set. It avoids the
smaller vertex set, and lies in one connected component of
its complement, which is one fibre of
$\operatorname{sp}_2$.

Let $z_1,z_2\in\mathcal G_{\overline k}$ have the same
$(p_1)_{\overline k}$-image. The surjective specialization
in Lemma~\ref{v037:f5:graphmodel} supplies
$x_1,x_2\in C_\Omega^{\an}$ with
$\operatorname{sp}_{\mathcal G}(x_j)=z_j$.
Functoriality gives
$\operatorname{sp}_1(x_1)=\operatorname{sp}_1(x_2)$.
The preceding constancy gives
$\operatorname{sp}_2(x_1)=\operatorname{sp}_2(x_2)$, hence
$(p_2)_{\overline k}(z_1)=(p_2)_{\overline k}(z_2)$.

Here is the descent of this actual fibre relation. A field
extension is flat by \cite[Tag 00HD]{StacksAlg}, since
the only ideals of a field are zero and the whole field;
its nonzero closed fibre makes it faithfully flat by
\cite[Tag 00HP]{StacksAlg}. If $t_1,t_2\in\mathcal G_s$
have the same $p_1$-image $y$, the tensor product
$\kappa(t_1)\otimes_{\kappa(y)}\kappa(t_2)$ is therefore
nonzero. A prime of it, supplied by
\cite[Tag 00E0(2)]{StacksAlg}, gives a point of
$\mathcal G_s\times_{\mathcal X_{1,s}}\mathcal G_s$
with projections $t_1,t_2$. Faithful flatness of
$\overline k/k$ and surjectivity on spectra,
\cite[Tag 00HQ]{StacksAlg}, lift this point to the
base-changed fibre product. Its two projections have the
same $(p_1)_{\overline k}$-image, and thus the same
$(p_2)_{\overline k}$-image. Projection back shows
$p_2(t_1)=p_2(t_2)$. On the generic fibre both projections
are identities. The generic and special fibres exhaust
the rank-one base, and their images cannot meet. This proves
the required constancy on every fibre of $p_1$.

Lemma~\ref{v036:f5:properquotient}, applied to the proper
birational map $p_1$ and its normal target, gives a unique
algebraic factorization $p_2=f\circ p_1$. It induces the
identity on $C$. Any two morphisms $\mathcal X_1\to
\mathcal X_2$ inducing that identity are equal: their
equalizer is closed because $\mathcal X_2/O$ is separated,
and contains the schematically dense generic fibre of the
flat integral source. Finally the same proper-quotient
lemma applied to $f$ gives
$f_*\mathcal O_{\mathcal X_1}=\mathcal O_{\mathcal X_2}$.
\end{proof}
