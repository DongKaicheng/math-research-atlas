Global finite-module tropical chains

5 Tropicalization, multiplicities and balancing

Open in the English reader · PDF page 1028

The broader finite character-compatible chain projection under general moment tuples remains open at the original scope. The three narrower selected clauses remain available. NAG-002462 uses only the literal-pullback case with the identity map of moment lattices via NAG-002419.

Historical statement · exact TeX
\begin{theorem}[Global finite-module tropical chains]
\label{v037:integration:globalchain}
Let $X=\mathcal M(A)$ be pure of analytic dimension $d$ over
a complete non-Archimedean field with its specified absolute
value, and let a unit tuple $f$ define $t_f:X\to\mathbb R^n$.
For every finite $A$-module $M$ there is a canonical finite
nonnegative integral $d$-dimensional polyhedral chain
\[
                              \mathcal T_f(M).
\]
Its support is contained in $t_f(X)$. On any common finite
subdivision of the actual image and $t_f(\partial_KX)$,
the weight of a $d$-cell is the module weight
$m_{M,f}(v)$ at any point of its relative interior. Cells
of dimension less than $d$ give no $d$-chain term.
The chain is additive in short exact sequences, independent
of subdivision, and balanced off $t_f(\partial_KX)$.
For $M=A$ every occurring $d$-cell has positive weight.

For a smooth $(d,d)$-superform $\alpha$ on an open
neighbourhood of the actual compact image, define
\[
                   \mathcal I_{M,f}(\alpha)
                              =\sum_\sigma m_\sigma\int_\sigma\alpha.
\]
This is independent of subdivision and is nonnegative when
the tangential top coefficients of $\alpha$ are nonnegative.
It agrees with $I_{M,f}$ of
Theorem~\ref{v037:integration:momenttests} for tests supported
in the whole interior weight domain.

Let $p:\mathcal M(B)\to\mathcal M(A)$ be finite, with both
spaces pure of analytic dimension $d$, and suppose moment
tuples satisfy $t_{f_A}\circ p=a\circ t_{f_B}$ for an
integral map $a$. For every finite $B$-module $M$,
\[
                  a_*\mathcal T_{f_B}(M)=\mathcal T_{f_A}(M),
                  \qquad
                  \mathcal I_{M,f_B}(a^*\alpha)
                               =\mathcal I_{M,f_A}(\alpha).
\]
On the right $M$ is its finite $A$-module. In particular,
adjoining analytic unit coordinates preserves these chains
under projection and preserves the corresponding integrals.
\end{theorem}

\noindent\textbf{The finite-cell functional.}\quad
Let \(X=\mathcal M(A)\) be a pure generalized affinoid of dimension \(d\) over the specified complete field, \(f\) a finite tuple of analytic units, and \(M\) a finite \(A\)-module. Use the canonical chain from the \hyperref[fnd:nag002405:global-finite-module-chain-weights]{finite-module chain and weights} and its weights \(m_\sigma\).
For a smooth $(d,d)$-superform $\alpha$ on an open
neighbourhood of the actual compact image, define
\[
                   \mathcal I_{M,f}(\alpha)
                              =\sum_\sigma m_\sigma\int_\sigma\alpha.
\]
This is independent of subdivision and is nonnegative when
the tangential top coefficients of $\alpha$ are nonnegative.
\par
\begin{proof}
All cells with nonzero weights are compact polytopes:
the actual moment image is \hyperref[fnd:boundedspectrumcompact]{compact}, and the complex is
finite. Consequently the integral is a finite sum of
the integrals constructed in
Lemma~\ref{v037:integration:polyhedralcalculus}.
That lemma gives subdivision independence and positivity.
\end{proof}

Statement · exact TeX
Let $X=\mathcal M(A)$ be pure of analytic dimension $d$ over
a complete non-Archimedean field with its specified absolute
value, and let a unit tuple $f$ define $t_f:X\to\mathbb R^n$.
For every finite $A$-module $M$ there is a canonical finite

\par
Selected scope proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{v037:globaltrop:finiteimage} gives a finite
polyhedral image of dimension at most $d$ and a finite
boundary image of dimension at most $d-1$. Choose one
finite common subdivision with the boundary image as a
subcomplex. At a point in the relative interior of a
$d$-cell, the whole moment fibre is therefore interior,
and the local image is contained in that cell's affine
$d$-plane. Theorem~\ref{v037:degree:moduleweight} defines
the nonnegative integer weight there. It is locally
constant, by Theorem~\ref{v037:degree:modulecycle} on
small convex neighbourhoods with the actual local fan
presentations of Theorem~\ref{v037:analytic:interiorlocalstar}.
The relative interior of the cell is convex, so the
weight is constant there. For the structure module it
is positive by Theorem~\ref{v036:flatgermweight}.

On a common subdivision of two choices, the generic
weights compute the same whole-fibre module invariant.
Thus their chains agree. Additivity follows from the
exact-sequence formula of
Theorem~\ref{v037:degree:moduleweight}.
\end{proof}

\noindent\textbf{Adjoining unit coordinates on the same affinoid.}\quad
With the same pure generalized affinoid \(X=\mathcal M(A)\), finite \(A\)-module \(M\), and finite unit tuple \(f\), let \(g\) be another finite unit tuple on \(X\). Let \(a\) be the projection from the combined coordinates to the \(f\)-coordinates. For the canonical finite chains and finite-cell functionals, and every smooth top superform \(\alpha\) near the actual \(f\)-moment image,
\[
a_*\mathcal T_{(f,g)}(M)=\mathcal T_f(M),\qquad
\mathcal I_{M,(f,g)}(a^*\alpha)=\mathcal I_{M,f}(\alpha).
\]
\par
\begin{proof}
Take the finite map \(p=\mathrm{id}_X\), with \(f_B=(f,g)\) and \(f_A=f\). Each target unit is the corresponding coordinate of the joint tuple. In the following calculation the two chains and integrals are given by the \hyperref[fnd:nag002405:global-finite-module-chain-weights]{finite-module chain and weights} and the \hyperref[fnd:nag002405:finite-cell-functional]{finite-cell functional}.
For this finite morphism, take finite global subdivisions
of the two actual images and their boundary images by \hyperref[v037:globaltrop:finiteimage]{finite polyhedrality}.
Refine along the images of all source cells, using the
finite linear elimination and hyperplane construction in
\hyperref[cand:balancedpolyhedralpushforward]{the finite refinement calculation}.
Outside a finite union of polyhedra of dimension at most
$d-1$ in each target $d$-cell, the target whole fibre is
interior, and every source weight above it lies in a
full-rank $d$-cell. The entire inverse weight set is
finite, with at most one point per such source cell.
For this finite identity, \hyperref[fnd:generalizedfinitealgebras]{relative interior} and
\hyperref[fnd:affinoidinteriorcalculus]{composition} give absolute interior at every point of those source fibres.

The \hyperref[v037:analytic:interiorlocalstar]{local star of a whole interior fibre} supplies pure local images near the target weight and at each source weight above it.
\hyperref[v037:analytic:finitepolyhedralpatching]{Finite patching} retains the complete source inverse support in a finite pure presentation on a small convex target neighbourhood in the target local image presentation. Theorem~\ref{v037:degree:modulefunctoriality}
then identifies the finite lattice-index sum with the
target weight of $M$ as an $A$-module. Its empty-inverse-
image case gives zero on both sides. Thus the generic
weight of every target $d$-cell is exactly its pushforward
weight. The resulting equality of finite chains is
$a_*\mathcal T_{f_B}(M)=\mathcal T_{f_A}(M)$.
This proves the chain formula for the coordinate projection on the same affinoid.

The full actual source support is compact, so this
pushforward is proper. On each full-rank source cell,
Lemma~\ref{v037:integration:polyhedralcalculus} gives the
factor $[N_\tau:a(N_\sigma)]$ in the integral. On a
rank-dropping cell the top-degree pullback is zero.
Grouping these finite cell integrals by their target
cells proves the integral identity. No ambient support
cutoff is needed here, because all the integration
polytopes are compact. This proves the integral formula on the same affinoid.
\end{proof}
