Vertices under a formal domination

4 Analytic curves and skeleta

Open in the English reader · PDF page 751

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{lemma}[Vertices under a formal domination]
\label{v035:f5:dominationvertices}
Let $\mathfrak X_1,\mathfrak X_2$ be admissible formal models of an
analytic space $X$, with reduced pure one-dimensional special fibres
of finite type over $k$. If a formal morphism
$f:\mathfrak X_1\to\mathfrak X_2$ induces the identity on $X$, then
\[
                         V(\mathfrak X_2)\subset V(\mathfrak X_1).
\]
\end{lemma}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Let $v\in V(\mathfrak X_2)$, with
$\eta=\operatorname{sp}_2(v)$ a component generic point. Put
$p=\operatorname{sp}_1(v)$. Functoriality of specialization gives
$f_s(p)=\eta$. If $p$ were closed, its residue field would be finite
over $k$ by \cite[Tag 00FV]{StacksAlg}, whereas the residue field
of $\eta$ has transcendence degree one by
\cite[Tag 0A21(6)]{StacksAlg}. The induced injective $k$-homomorphism
$\kappa(\eta)\to\kappa(p)$ is impossible. Thus $p$ is a component
generic point, and the singleton-fibre assertion of
Proposition~\ref{cand:reducedmodelcomponentpoints} says that its
component vertex is precisely $v$.
\end{proof}
Standing context 1 · exact TeX
Let $K$ be a complete nontrivially real-valued non-Archimedean field,
with its specified absolute value. Write $O=K^\circ$,
$\mathfrak m=K^{\circ\circ}$, and $k=O/\mathfrak m$, and choose
$\pi\in O$ with $0<|\pi|<1$. All formal schemes below are
$\pi$-adic. An admissible formal algebra is understood to be
$\pi$-torsion-free and topologically finitely presented over $O$.
For a formal open $\mathfrak U\subset\mathfrak X$, put
$D_{\mathfrak U}=\operatorname{sp}^{-1}(\mathfrak U)$, with its
analytic $G$-domain structure.
