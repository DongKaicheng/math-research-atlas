\begin{lemma}[Interior germs of degree \(d\) have generic support]
\label{f7v38:generic}
Let \(A\) be integral and \(V=\mathcal M(A)\) pure of analytic dimension
\(d\geq1\). Let \(x\in\Int(V/K)\), and let \(\beta=t_f^*\alpha\)
be a single global moment representative of bidegree \((d-1,d)\).
If \(\beta_x\ne0\) in the ordinary stalk, then \(s_f(x)=d\) and
\(\ker(x)=0\).
\end{lemma}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Apply \eqref{eq:pointed} on \(V\). Taking \(U=W_0\) and then a
smaller ordinary affinoid neighborhood of \(x\) whose moment image
lies in \(B_{\varepsilon_{W_0}}(w)\) gives a neighborhood whose
entire actual image is contained in the \(s_f(x)\)-dimensional
fan. Such a neighborhood exists by continuity of \(t_f\) and
the ordinary affinoid-neighborhood basis used in
\src{v037:forms:germsheaf}. If \(s_f(x)<d\), the second alternating
degree \(d\) forces every tangential restriction of \(\alpha\)
on this image to vanish. The ordinary germ definition
\src{v037:forms:ordinarysmoothgerm} would then give \(\beta_x=0\).
Thus \(s_f(x)\geq d\). The finite-image dimension bound
\src{v037:globaltrop:finiteimage}, applied to \(V\), and the
equality \eqref{eq:pointed} imply \(s_f(x)\leq d\).

Suppose that \(0\ne a\in\ker(x)\). The actual closed subspace
\[
 Z=\mathcal M(A/(a))
\]
is nonempty because it contains the same point \(x\).
The ideal \((a)\) is contained in no minimal prime of the integral
ring \(A\). Therefore \src{v037:degree:exceptionalimage} gives
\(\dim_K Z\leq d-1\). The map \(Z\to V\) is finite with its
canonical quotient norm, hence everywhere relatively interior by
\src{fnd:generalizedfinitealgebras}. Since \(x\in\Int(V/K)\),
the composition inclusion
\src{fnd:affinoidinteriorcalculus} gives \(x\in\Int(Z/K)\).

The completed residue field at this point of \(Z\) is the same
\(\mathcal H(x)\): the quotient is by an ideal in the evaluation
kernel. The restricted units \(f_i|_Z\) have exactly the same
values and initial forms. Applying \src{NAG-002455} on \(Z\)
therefore gives a pointed fan of dimension the same number \(s_f(x)\).
On the other hand, the entire actual image \(t_f(Z)\) has dimension
at most \(d-1\), by the same exceptional-image theorem. Equality
\eqref{eq:pointed} on \(Z\) implies \(s_f(x)\leq d-1\), contradicting
\(s_f(x)=d\). Thus \(\ker(x)=0\).
\end{proof}
