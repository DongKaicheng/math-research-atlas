Connectedness and the genus of the special fibre

4 Analytic curves and skeleta

Open in the English reader · PDF page 752

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{lemma}[Connectedness and the genus of the special fibre]
\label{v035:f5:connectedgenus}
The space $X$ is connected. For every split nodal algebraic model
$\mathcal C$ of $C$, its special fibre is connected and has arithmetic
genus $g(C)$.
\end{lemma}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Smoothness over the normal field $K$ makes $C$ normal by
\cite[Tag 033C]{StacksAlg}. Its irreducible components cannot meet,
so connectedness makes it integral. Its ring of global functions is
a finite field extension of the algebraically closed field $K$
by \cite[Tag 04L2(1)]{StacksAlg}. Together with
Corollary~\ref{fnd:projectiveschemegaga},
this gives
\[
                  H^0(X_G,\mathcal O_X)=H^0(C,\mathcal O_C)=K.
\]
A nontrivial clopen decomposition of $X$ would give a nonconstant
idempotent by gluing the functions zero and one on its two members.
The open cover is an admissible analytic cover, so this contradicts
the displayed equality. Hence $X$ is connected.

Its continuous retraction onto $\Sigma(\mathcal C)$ has connected
image. The vertices and edges of this graph are the special components
and their nodes, with their actual incidences. Thus the dual graph is
connected, and so is the union of the special components. The fields
$K$ and $k$ are algebraically closed; these are the two geometric
fibres of the rank-one valuation base. They are reduced, connected
curves. By \cite[Tag 0E6L]{StacksAlg},
$R^1f_*\mathcal O_{\mathcal C}$ is finite locally free and commutes
with base change. Its rank is constant on $\operatorname{Spec}O$,
and its generic rank is $g(C)$. This proves the genus assertion.
\end{proof}
