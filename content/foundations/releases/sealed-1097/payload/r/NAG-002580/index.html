Extension with a compact collar

Chapter 8 supplement: Interior Stokes with coherent coefficients

Open in the English reader · PDF page 1791

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{lemma}[Extension with a compact collar]
\label{f7gg:zeroextension}
Let \(W\subseteq X\) be an affinoid domain in a good
Hausdorff space, and let \(\theta\in\A^{p,q}(W)\) have support
in a compact set \(C\subseteq\Int_{\rm top}(W,X)\).
There is a unique extension \(e_!\theta\) on \(X\) with support
contained in \(C\) and restriction \(\theta\) on \(W\). It is zero
outside \(W\) and satisfies
\[
 d'e_!\theta=e_!d'\theta,\qquad
 d''e_!\theta=e_!d''\theta,\qquad
 Je_!\theta=e_!J\theta.
\]
All displayed supports lie in \(C\).
If \(X\) is pure of dimension \(d\), \(\mathcal F\) is coherent,
and \(\theta\) has bidegree \((d,d)\), then
\[
 \int_{X,\mathcal F}e_!\theta
             =\int_{W,\mathcal F|_{W_G}}\theta.
\]
\end{lemma}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Use the open cover
\(\Int_{\rm top}(W,X)\), \(X\setminus C\), and glue
\(\theta\) to zero. On their overlap \(\theta\) has zero
ordinary germs. Every differential and \(J\) is local on the
ordinary sheaf and preserves the zero section, by
\src{v037:forms:germsheaf}; hence the same gluing proves
the three commutations.
At a point of \(W\) outside this ordinary interior, the compact
set \(C\) is absent from a neighborhood, so the resulting
restriction still agrees with \(\theta\).
Uniqueness follows from the same open cover.
The integral equality is compact-neighborhood independence in
\src{v037:forms:compactintegral}. No distributional boundary
derivative is introduced: the section is already zero on
a neighborhood of every newly crossed boundary point.
\end{proof}
