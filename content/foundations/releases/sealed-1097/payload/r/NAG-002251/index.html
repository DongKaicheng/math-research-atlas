Faithful analytic coordinates

5 Tropicalization, multiplicities and balancing

Open in the English reader · PDF page 931

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{theorem}[Faithful analytic coordinates]
\label{v035:faithfullocal}
There are an ordinary open analytic neighbourhood $V$ of
$\Sigma$ in $Y$ and an analytic map
$\varphi:V\to(\mathbf G_{m,K}^{N})^{\an}$ whose tropicalization
is a homeomorphism on $\Sigma$ onto its image. On every cell
of a finite subdivision it is integral affine and induces
an isomorphism onto the saturated lattice of its image.
\end{theorem}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Include the units $X_0,\ldots,X_r$, whose product is $a$;
their valuations distinguish the base weights. For each
$I$ and $\eta\in Q_I$, choose $\bar h_\eta$ in the
special-fibre algebra that belongs to every other prime
in $Q_I$ and does not belong to $\eta$. Indeed these primes
are incomparable, since they lie in the same zero-dimensional
\etale fibre; multiplying elements of $\eta'\setminus\eta$
for $\eta'\ne\eta$ gives the required element. Omit the
construction when $Q_I$ has fewer than two members.
Lift $\bar h_\eta$ to $h_\eta\in B_0$ and choose
$0<|c_\eta|<1$ by Lemma~\ref{v035:faithfulscalarshifts} so
that $g_\eta=h_\eta+c_\eta$ is nonzero on $\Sigma$.
Specialization gives, for $w\in\operatorname{relint}\Delta_I$,
\[
 |g_\eta(x_{\eta,w})|=1,\qquad
 0<|g_\eta(x_{\eta',w})|<1\quad(\eta'\ne\eta).
\]
Thus this finite list separates points over the same weight.

Let $V$ be the complement of the zero loci of the $g_\eta$.
It is an ordinary open neighbourhood of $\Sigma$. Each
$g_\eta$ is an analytic unit on $V$: near any point choose
a positive lower bound for its norm and use the inverse
on the corresponding rational domain, as in
Theorem~\ref{fnd:generalizedrationaluniversal}. These units
and the $X_i$ define $\varphi$. Its tropicalization is
continuous and injective on $\Sigma$, hence is a homeomorphism
onto its image by compactness.

Apply Proposition~\ref{v035:faithfulanalyticpl} to the finite
list. The union of its hyperplanes gives a common finite
subdivision, compatible with all coordinate faces and their
identifications. On a subcell the linear map is, after
eliminating one standard coordinate, the graph
$n\mapsto(n,Ln)$ of an integral map. Projection to the first
coordinates is an integral left inverse. If a point of its
real image belongs to the ambient integer lattice, its first
coordinates belong to the subcell's saturated tangent lattice;
the entire point is then the corresponding integral graph
point. This proves the claimed lattice equality.
\end{proof}
Standing context 1 · exact TeX
Let $K$ be complete and nontrivially valued, with its specified
real-valued absolute value. Put $O=K^\circ$, $k=\widetilde K$,
$v=-\log|\cdot|$, and $\Gamma=v(K^\times)$.
Consider a topologically finitely presented formally \etale map
of admissible algebras
\[
 A_0=O\langle X_0,\ldots,X_r,T_1,\ldots,T_q\rangle/
              (X_0\cdots X_r-a)\longrightarrow B_0,
 \qquad 0<|a|<1.
\]
Write $B=B_0\otimes_O K$, $Y=\mathcal M(B)$, and
$\Sigma\subset Y$ for the inverse image of the standard skeleton.
The notation $\Delta_I$, $Q_I$, and $x_{\eta,w}$ is that of
Proposition~\ref{cand:actualskeletalfibrestrata} and
Theorem~\ref{cand:etaleskeletonfinitefaces}. In particular, $\Sigma$
is a compact finite complex, and a point over the relative interior
of $\Delta_I$ is specified by $w$ and $\eta\in Q_I$.
A finite piecewise $\Gamma$-integral-affine function has integral
linear parts and constants in $\Gamma$ on a finite subdivision
defined by integral linear inequalities with constants in $\Gamma$.
