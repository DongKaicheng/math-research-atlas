Pushforward after adding analytic unit coordinates

5 Tropicalization, multiplicities and balancing

Open in the English reader · PDF page 1009

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{theorem}[Pushforward after adding analytic unit coordinates]
\label{v037:analytic:momentaugmentation}
In the setting above let $g_1,\ldots,g_m\in B^\times$ and put
\[
 \widehat t=(t,(-\log|g_j|)_j):X\longrightarrow\mathbb R^{n+m},
 \qquad \pi:\mathbb Z^{n+m}\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^n.
\]
Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb R^n$ be open and convex and avoid
$t(\partial_KX)$. Suppose finite pure $d$-dimensional integral
affine complexes $\mathcal C$ and $\widehat{\mathcal C}$ satisfy
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 t(X)\cap\Omega&=|\mathcal C|\cap\Omega,\\
 \widehat t(X)\cap\pi^{-1}(\Omega)
       &=|\widehat{\mathcal C}|\cap\pi^{-1}(\Omega).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Equip both with their analytic weights from
Theorem~\ref{v037:analytic:polyhedralcycle}. Then on $\Omega$
\[
                  \pi_*[\widehat{\mathcal C}]=[\mathcal C].
\]
More explicitly, outside a finite union of polyhedra of dimension
at most $d-1$, each $v\in|\mathcal C|\cap\Omega$ lies in the
relative interior of a $d$-cell $\sigma$, its full inverse image
in $\widehat t(X)$ is finite, and
\begin{equation}
\label{v037:eq:augmentationweight}
 m_\sigma=
 \sum_{\substack{u\in\widehat t(X)\\\pi(u)=v}}
       m_{\widehat\sigma(u)}
             [N_\sigma:\pi(N_{\widehat\sigma(u)})].
\end{equation}
Here $u$ lies in the relative interior of a unique $d$-cell
$\widehat\sigma(u)$, and $\pi$ is injective on its tangent space.
The equality uses all source moment fibres over $v$.
\end{theorem}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
The set $\pi^{-1}(\Omega)$ is open and convex and misses
$\widehat t(\partial_KX)$. Thus the indicated weights exist on
both complexes and are balanced by
Theorem~\ref{v037:analytic:polyhedralcycle}.

Remove from $\Omega$ the cells of $\mathcal C$ of dimension
less than $d$, the images of all cells of
$\widehat{\mathcal C}$ of dimension less than $d$, and the
images of those $d$-cells on whose tangent space $\pi$ has rank
less than $d$. Each removed set is contained in a finite union
of affine subspaces of dimension at most $d-1$. The images are
polyhedra: eliminating one real coordinate from a finite list
of linear inequalities replaces the upper and lower bounds for
that coordinate by their pairwise comparisons, and preserves
the inequalities not involving it; iterating gives a finite
inequality presentation of a linear image. Rational coefficients
remain rational. This also supplies a finite polyhedral
refinement adapted to all these images, by cutting along the
finitely many resulting supporting hyperplanes.

The complement is dense in the relative interior of every
$d$-cell meeting $\Omega$. To verify this directly, in its affine
span choose a line direction outside the finitely many proper
linear subspaces parallel to the excluded affine subspaces.
A line in that direction meets each of them at at most one
point. Arbitrarily short segments therefore contain points of
the complement. Such a direction exists, for example by the
finite-polynomial argument in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{v037:analytic:wallbalance}.

Fix $v$ in this complement and in the target support. Every
$u\in\widehat t(X)$ above $v$ is in
$\pi^{-1}(\Omega)$, so it lies in the relative interior of a
unique $d$-cell of $\widehat{\mathcal C}$, and $\pi$ has full
rank on that cell. The affine restriction is injective, so there
is at most one such point for each of the finitely many cells.
There is at least one, since $t=\pi\circ\widehat t$. This proves
finiteness and nonemptiness of the full inverse image.

Near $v$ the target image is contained in its affine $d$-plane,
and near every $u$ the joint image is contained in its affine
$d$-plane. The whole target fibre is interior because
$v\notin t(\partial_KX)$; the whole source fibres are contained
in it. Apply Theorem~\ref{v036:analyticgeometricpushforward} to
the identity morphism $X\to X$, with source moment tuple $(f,g)$,
target moment tuple $f$, and lattice map $\pi$. Its algebra is
finite free of rank one over itself, with its original Banach
structure. All its full-fibre hypotheses have just been checked.
The resulting formula is exactly
\eqref{v037:eq:augmentationweight}.

The restriction of $\pi$ to the actual joint support over
$\Omega$ is proper: above a compact subset of $\Omega$ its
inverse image is a closed subset of the compact set
$\widehat t(X)$. On the finite refinement constructed above,
the weight defining $\pi_*$ at a generic point of a target
$d$-cell is the finite sum in
\eqref{v037:eq:augmentationweight}; cells with smaller-dimensional
image contribute no $d$-weight. The formula agrees with the
analytic target weight on a dense subset of every such cell.
Both are constant there, so the weighted cycles are equal.
\end{proof}
