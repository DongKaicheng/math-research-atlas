Statement · exact TeX
\begin{proposition}[Strict principal zero purity; F8ZP-P]
\label{prop:strictprincipal}
Let $A$ be a nonzero strict $K$-affinoid algebra pure of dimension
$n\geq1$. Suppose $f$ avoids every minimal prime of $A$. Then $A/fA$
is zero or every one of its minimal-prime quotients has Krull dimension
$n-1$. For every prime $\mathfrak q$ minimal over $fA$ and every
$\mathfrak p\in\Min(A)$ contained in $\mathfrak q$,
\[
       \htp_{A/\mathfrak p}(\mathfrak q/\mathfrak p)=1.
\]
If instead $n=0$, such an $f$ is a unit. These conclusions apply in
particular when $f$ is a non-zero-divisor.
\end{proposition}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
If $f$ is a unit, the quotient is zero. Otherwise choose
$\mathfrak q\in\Min_A(fA)$ and a minimal prime
$\mathfrak p\subseteq\mathfrak q$. The image of $f$ in the Noetherian
domain $A/\mathfrak p$ is nonzero. The prime
$\mathfrak q/\mathfrak p$ is minimal over this element: a smaller prime
containing it would lift to a prime strictly below $\mathfrak q$
containing $f$. Tag 00KV applies and bounds its height by one.
It is not the zero prime, because it contains that nonzero element.
Its height is therefore exactly one. Equation~\eqref{eq:dimension}
gives $\dim(A/\mathfrak q)=n-1$ for every such $\mathfrak q$.
These are exactly the minimal-prime quotients of $A/fA$.

When $n=0$, the same reasoning would give height one in a
zero-dimensional component, an impossibility. Thus no prime can
contain $f$, and $f$ is a unit. Lemma~\ref{lem:avoid} proves the final
clause. Components on which $f$ is a unit simply contribute no zero
component; no assumption that $f$ is a nonunit on every component was
made. Nilpotents and embedded primes have not been removed.
\end{proof}
