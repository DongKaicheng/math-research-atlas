Statement · exact TeX
\begin{proposition}[Quotient independence and full Cartier gluing; F8MG-Q]
\label{prop:quotient}
For a regular meromorphic \(f\) on \(U\), the local native pairings
\(L_{a/b}=L_a-L_b\) define a unique linear pairing
\(L_f:\A^{n,n}_{U,c}\to\R\). It is additive under multiplication of
regular meromorphic functions. The local full Cartier cycles
\(\divis(a)-\divis(b)\) glue to \(\divis(f)\), and their native
pairings on compact ordinary \((n-1,n-1)\)-tests glue independently
of presentations. The cycle assertion uses R-ZP and the exact frozen
restricted Cartier comparison; the logarithmic pairing does not need
R-SPL or a new measure representation.
\end{proposition}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
For a compact top test in one quotient neighbourhood, frozen
\tagg{NAG-002806} makes its closed support disjoint from the zeros
of both \(a\) and \(b\). On that support both logarithms are bounded,
ordinary smooth functions, and their products with the test have
well-defined native compact integrals, extended by zero where the test
vanishes. If \(a/b=c/d\), refine to actual common affinoid
neighbourhoods. Localization at regular elements is injective, so
\(ad=bc\) there. The test vanishes near the zeros of all four
factors, and on the remaining support
\(\log|a|-\log|b|=\log|c|-\log|d|\). Thus the pairings agree.
This also proves logarithmic additivity for a product, after adjoining
its finitely many regular factors.

For a general compact top test \(\alpha\), take a finite ordinary
partition \(\rho_j\) subordinate to quotient neighbourhoods, summing
to one near \(\Supp\alpha\), and set
\(L_f(\alpha)=\sum_j(L_{a_j}-L_{b_j})(\rho_j\alpha)\).
Two choices are compared by the finite product partition
\(\rho_j\rho'_k\). Its supports lie on actual common neighbourhoods,
where the preceding equality applies. Their sums equal the original
test as ordinary sections. This proves independence and linearity;
no countable partition or limiting interchange is used.

For cycles, R-ZP makes every numerator, denominator, and regular
cross-product zero space empty or pure of one common dimension
\(n-1\) on each common affinoid. The antichain hypothesis of
\tagg{NAG-002772} is then satisfied: a strict containment between
two component primes of that analytic dimension would contradict
\tagg{NAG-002374}. Its scoped product additivity applied to
\(ad=bc\) gives equality of the divisor differences, including
all generic lengths. Domain compatibility and ordinary gluing in that
same theorem give the cycle; \tagg{NAG-002778} supplies its native
pairings with the full coherent coefficients. It does not replace
these by reduced supports or count geometric points. The compact
partition argument proves the asserted pairing independence.
\end{proof}
