Finite pushforward on compact analytic domains

5 Tropicalization, multiplicities and balancing

Open in the English reader · PDF page 1040

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{theorem}[Finite pushforward on compact analytic domains]
\label{v037:integration:compactfinitepushforward}
Let $p:Y\to X$ be a norm-admissible finite morphism of
good Hausdorff analytic spaces pure of the same analytic
dimension $d$, over a complete field with its specified
real-valued absolute value. Let $Q\subseteq X$ be a finite
union of affinoid domains, and put $R=p^{-1}(Q)$.
Let $\mathcal F$ be coherent on $Y_G$.
Then $R$ is a finite union of affinoid domains of $Y$,
and both $Q$ and $R$ are compact analytic domains.
Neither is required to be good.

Pulling back the unit tuples defines a pullback of finite
moment-chart data on $Q$ to such data on $R$.
For every top-degree datum $\alpha$,
\begin{equation}
\label{v037:integration:eqcompactfinitepushforward}
 \mathcal I_{R,\mathcal F|_R}(p^*\alpha)
       =\mathcal I_{Q,(p_*\mathcal F)|_Q}(\alpha).
\end{equation}
Successive applications agree for composable finite
morphisms between spaces of the same pure analytic
dimension. If $(p_*\mathcal F)|_Q$ is locally free of
constant rank $\rho$, then
\[
 \mathcal I_{R,\mathcal F|_R}(p^*\alpha)
                   =\rho\,\mathcal I_{Q,\mathcal O_Q}(\alpha).
\]
All conclusions allow trivial valuation, arbitrary
positive affinoid radii, and nonreduced spaces and modules.
\end{theorem}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Choose a finite affinoid-domain cover $(V_i)$ of $Q$.
Each $V_i$ is an affinoid domain of $X$ by the domain
transitivity of
Proposition~\ref{fnd:generalnetdomaincalculus}.
Finiteness of $p$ makes $W_i=p^{-1}(V_i)$ an actual
affinoid domain of $Y$, whose algebra is a finite
canonical module over $\mathcal O(V_i)$.
These domains cover $R$. The finite-union assertion
of Theorem~\ref{v037:integration:compactintegral}
therefore gives the indicated compact analytic-domain
structures. By
Lemma~\ref{v037:integration:separatedintersections},
every finite intersection $V_I$ is affinoid, and
\[
                       W_I=\bigcap_{i\in I}W_i=p^{-1}(V_I)
\]
is its full finite affinoid inverse image.
Every nonempty such domain is pure of dimension $d$
by Lemma~\ref{v037:integration:puritylocality}.

Suppose that $(f_i,\alpha_i)$ presents the given
chart data on the $V_i$. Its proposed pullback has
tuple $p^*f_i$ and the same ambient superform $\alpha_i$
on $W_i$. Units remain units. On $W_i\cap W_j$ the
actual joint moment image is contained in the joint
moment image on $V_i\cap V_j$. Both are finite
polyhedral sets by Theorem~\ref{v037:globaltrop:finiteimage}.
Refine their complexes by their finitely many defining
hyperplanes, using
Lemma~\ref{cand:polyhedralrefinementproduct}.
Each cell of the source image lies in a cell of the
target image, and its tangent space is a subspace of
that cell's tangent space. Tangential equality on
the target consequently restricts to tangential
equality on the source. This proves compatibility
of the pulled-back data in every bidegree.
The same argument applies to a common refinement
of two representatives. Inverse images of its domains
are actual finite affinoid domains, and pullback
commutes with adjoining units. Thus the pullback
depends only on the equivalence class of the data.

Proposition~\ref{fnd:finitecoherentdirectimagebasechange}
makes $p_*\mathcal F$ coherent and identifies, for
each nonempty $I$,
\[
 \Gamma(V_I,p_*\mathcal F)=\Gamma(W_I,\mathcal F).
\]
Write $A_I=\mathcal O(V_I)$, $B_I=\mathcal O(W_I)$
and $M_I=\Gamma(W_I,\mathcal F)$.
The module $M_I$ is finite over $B_I$ by
Theorem~\ref{fnd:fullgkiehltheoremb}, and finite over
$A_I$ because $B_I$ is finite over $A_I$.
The cited direct-image theorem gives precisely this
restriction of scalars, with its compatible canonical
norms and domain restrictions.

Assume first that $W_I$ is nonempty and put $f=f_i|_{V_I}$.
The two tuples are $f$ and its literal pullback $p^*f$ on
the full inverse domain. The character identity therefore holds
with the identity map on the moment lattice.

By Theorem~\ref{v037:globaltrop:finiteimage}, both actual moment
images are finite polyhedral sets. The absolute boundary image of
$V_I$ has dimension at most $d-1$ and is empty if $d=0$.
Use Lemma~\ref{cand:polyhedralrefinementproduct} to subdivide the two
images and that boundary image together. At a relative-interior
point $w$ of a target $d$-cell, choose a small convex open
neighbourhood in moment space disjoint from the boundary image
and the other cells. The target image is presented there by the
given cell. If the source image contains $w$, it contains that
cell and has the same local presentation. The whole target fibre
is absolutely interior there. Finiteness gives relative interior
everywhere on the actual source affinoid by
Theorem~\ref{fnd:generalizedfinitealgebras}, so
Proposition~\ref{fnd:affinoidinteriorcalculus} gives absolute
interior of the whole source fibre there. With these pure local
presentations, Theorem~\ref{v037:degree:modulefunctoriality} compares
the same finite module $M_I$ over $B_I$ and $A_I$. The index
on the common saturated tangent lattice is one.

If the source image misses $w$, its compactness lets us avoid
it on a neighbourhood of $w$. For the entire target simultaneous
level algebra $D_w$, the inverse level algebra
$B_I\otimes_{A_I}D_w$ is the actual finite pullback by
Theorems~\ref{fnd:generalizedfinitebasechange}
and~\ref{fnd:generalizeddomainbasechange}. It is zero by
Theorem~\ref{fnd:generalizedpointunitcriterion}, since its spectrum
is empty. Thus $M_I\otimes_{A_I}D_w=0$, and further tensoring
with the transverse Gauss field gives zero. The target module
weight is zero by Theorem~\ref{v037:degree:moduleweight}, in
agreement with the empty source coefficient.

These generic coefficients determine the
\hyperref[fnd:nag002405:global-finite-module-chain-weights]{closed finite-module chains}
on the common subdivision. Pairing the same ambient form with
these equal chains by the
\hyperref[fnd:nag002405:finite-cell-functional]{finite-cell integral}
gives
\[
 \mathcal I_{M_I,p^*f_i|_{W_I}}(\alpha_i)
       =\mathcal I_{M_I,f_i|_{V_I}}(\alpha_i),
\]
where the right side regards $M_I$ as an $A_I$-module.
An empty inverse image has zero module on both sides.
Multiply by $(-1)^{|I|+1}$ and sum over the nonempty
index sets. The two finite sums are exactly the
compact chart integrals in
Theorem~\ref{v037:integration:compactintegral}, including
its non-good-domain variant. This proves
\eqref{v037:integration:eqcompactfinitepushforward}.

For a further finite map $q:Z\to Y$, the inverse
image of every $W_i$ is again the full finite
affinoid inverse image. Apply the same equality
first to $q$ and then to $p$.
Pullback of unit functions is associative, and
\[
                   p_*(q_*\mathcal G)=(p\circ q)_*\mathcal G
\]
on sections of every analytic domain, since both
sides take sections on its full inverse image.
These identities prove the stated compatibility
for successive applications.

Finally suppose the indicated direct image is
locally free of constant rank $\rho$ on $Q_G$.
Refine its local trivializing cover by affinoid
domains and intersect them with the presenting
chart domains. The quasi-net and compactness
assertions of Lemma~\ref{fnd:quasinetcalculus}
give a finite such refinement. The compact integral
is unchanged by this refinement. On every new $V_I$
its finite module $N_I$ is free of rank $\rho$.
At each minimal prime
$\mathfrak p$ of $A_I$,
$\operatorname{length}((N_I)_{\mathfrak p})
=\rho\operatorname{length}((A_I)_{\mathfrak p})$.
The exact module-weight formula of
Theorem~\ref{v037:degree:moduleweight} therefore
multiplies every top-dimensional global-chain
coefficient by $\rho$. Lower-dimensional cells
give no top-dimensional term. Applying the
\hyperref[fnd:nag002405:finite-cell-functional]{finite-cell integral}
to the scaled
\hyperref[fnd:nag002405:global-finite-module-chain-weights]{finite-module chain}
gives
\[
 \mathcal I_{N_I,f_i}(\alpha_i)
                     =\rho\,\mathcal I_{A_I,f_i}(\alpha_i).
\]
The same finite cover sum proves the last formula.
In particular the argument preserves all generic
Artinian lengths before multiplying by $\rho$.
\end{proof}
