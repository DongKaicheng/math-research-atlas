The specialization partition of a formal curve

4 Analytic curves and skeleta

Open in the English reader · PDF page 748

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{corollary}[The specialization partition of a formal curve]
\label{v035:formal:partition}
For a pointedly semistable formal model, its vertex set is
a finite semistable set of type-II points. Its complement
is the disjoint union of the smooth
open-disc tubes and finitely many nodal open-annulus tubes.
These are exactly its connected components. The boundary
of each tube is exactly the set of component points of
the residual components passing through its special point.
\end{corollary}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
The vertex set is finite by
Proposition~\ref{cand:reducedmodelcomponentpoints}.
Proposition~\ref{cand:componentvaluedresiduefield}, applied
on a formal affine neighbourhood of each component generic
point, gives residual transcendence degree one and relative
rational value-group rank zero. Thus each vertex is of
type II under the intrinsic convention just stated.
Every point of the finite-type special-fibre curve is
either closed or a component generic point. The tubes
therefore give the asserted disjoint partition, and
Proposition~\ref{v035:formal:tubeends} identifies their
analytic structures and boundaries. They are open in
$C^{\an}$ and clopen in the complement of the vertex set.
Each is connected, so they are precisely the connected
components. The nodal closed points are finite. Put \(D=\mathfrak X_s\).
It is quasi-compact and locally of finite type over \(k\), hence
Noetherian by a finite affine cover and \cite[Tag 00FN]{StacksAlg}.
For a point \(q\) of \(D\), put
\[
 e(q)=\dim_{\kappa(q)}
      \bigl((\Omega_{D/k})_q\otimes_{\mathcal O_{D,q}}\kappa(q)\bigr).
\]
On an affine chart a finite presentation of the coordinate algebra
gives a finite presentation of \(\Omega_{D/k}\) by differentiating
the defining relations. With \(n\geq 1\) generators of the module,
the locus \(e(q)\geq 2\) is cut out by the \((n-1)\)-minors of the
relation matrix. For \(n=1\) the size-zero minor is \(1\), and for
\(n=0\) the locus is empty. Thus these tests define a closed subset
\(N\subset D\).

Reduce a supplied pointed diagram modulo \(\mathfrak m=K^{\circ\circ}\).
On the affine opens of this diagram both residual maps are of finite
presentation and retain the unique square-zero lifting property.
Indeed the targets of the lifting test over \(k\) are discrete and
annihilated by \(\mathfrak m\), so a lift for the formal map factors
through the special fibre. The two residual maps are therefore
\'etale by \cite[Tag 00UR]{StacksAlg}. For either residual affine
map \(A\to B\), differentials over \(k\) pull back. To see this, for a
\(B\)-module \(M\) and a \(k\)-derivation \(d:A\to M\), give
\(B\oplus M\), with square-zero summand \(M\), the \(A\)-algebra
structure \(a\mapsto(a|_B,d(a))\). The identity map to the quotient
\(B\) has a unique \(A\)-algebra lift from \(B\), whose second
coordinate is the unique extending \(k\)-derivation. This gives
\(\Omega_{B/k}=B\otimes_A\Omega_{A/k}\). Dimensions of residue
fibres are therefore unchanged along either map.

On \(k[T]\) the relative differential module has one free generator.
On \(k[S,T]/(ST)\) the differential presentation has two generators
and the single relation \(T\,dS+S\,dT\), so its rank-at-least-two
locus is the origin. The pointed diagrams thus identify the closed
points of \(N\) with the nodal closed points. At a point \(u\) over a
nodal closed \(q\), the ideal \((S,T)\) generates the maximal ideal
of \(\mathcal O_{\mathfrak U_s,u}\), by
\cite[Tag 02GU(1) and (6)]{StacksAlg} for the right residual map.
Consequently the inverse image of the rank locus in
\(\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal O_{\mathfrak U_s,u}\) consists only of
its closed point. The left residual map
\(\mathcal O_{D,q}\to\mathcal O_{\mathfrak U_s,u}\) is a flat local map
by \cite[Tag 02GU(1) and (2)]{StacksAlg}. Its map on spectra is
surjective by \cite[Tag 00HQ(3) and (2)]{StacksAlg}, since the
closed point is hit. Pullback of the residue-rank test now shows that
\(q\) has no proper generalization in \(N\).

Every point of the closed Noetherian subspace \(N\) specializes
to a closed point there: choose a minimal nonempty closed subset
of its closure and a point of that subset. As \(N\) is closed in
\(D\), such a point is also closed in \(D\). It follows that every
point of \(N\) is closed. Each irreducible component then consists
of its single generic point, and there are finitely many irreducible
components. Thus only finitely many nodal annular tubes occur.
\end{proof}
