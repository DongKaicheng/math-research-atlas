\begin{proposition}[Fixed-test logarithmic integrability; F8RC-L]
\label{prop:log}
Let \(X\) be good, Hausdorff, and pure of dimension \(n\), and let
\(f\) be a regular analytic function on an open neighbourhood of the
compact support of an ordinary smooth form \(\beta\). If one of its
bidegrees is \(n\), then \(\beta\) vanishes on an ordinary
neighbourhood of \(\{f=0\}\). In particular, for an ordinary compact
\((n,n)\)-test \(\alpha\), the pairing
\[
 L_f(\alpha)=\int_{X\setminus\{f=0\}}\log|f|\,\alpha
\]
is well-defined by the frozen ordinary compact integral. For every
fixed such test, bounded continuous truncations agreeing with
\(\log|f|\) above \(-R\) give the same value for all sufficiently
large \(R\). If \(\eta\) is ordinary smooth of bidegree
\((n-1,n-1)\), then \(d''\eta\) and \(\dd\eta\) vanish near
\(\{f=0\}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Work on the open set where \(f\) is given. At a zero outside
\(\operatorname{supp}\beta\) the form already vanishes nearby.
At a zero in the support, choose a common ordinary affinoid
neighbourhood \(V\) on which \(f\) is regular and \(\beta\) has one
unit-moment representative \(t_g^*\gamma\), using the \hyperref[v037:forms:germsheaf]{ordinary moment sheaf}.
The affinoid is pure of dimension \(n\) by \hyperref[v037:integration:puritylocality]{domain purity}.
Regularity \hyperref[prop:length]{avoids every minimal prime}. Apply \hyperref[v037:degree:exceptionalimage]{dimension drop} to the nonzero image of \(f\) on each integral ambient component.
The component zero spaces cover the support of the full actual zero
quotient \(D=\mathcal M(\mathcal O(V)/(f))\), so its dimension is at most
\(n-1\); for \(n=0\) it is empty.
The \hyperref[fnd:nag002795:ordinary-image-tube]{ordinary moment-image tube} for this actual quotient
puts the \(g\)-image of a sufficiently small strict sublevel of
\(|f|\) in \(t_g(D)\), of dimension at most \(n-1\) when nonempty.
Intersect that sublevel with the ordinary interior of \(V\) in \(X\). The restriction of
\(\gamma\) to every face is zero when one exterior degree is \(n\).
Thus its ordinary pullback is zero on this neighbourhood. This is
an assertion about the actual ordinary germ and its representative,
not just about a vanishing integral. It is local at every point of
the zero scheme.

For compactly supported \(\alpha\), its closed support is therefore
disjoint from the zero scheme. On this compact support \(|f|\) has
a positive lower bound and a finite upper bound. The logarithm is
bounded there and is an ordinary smooth function where \(f\) is a
unit. Adjoin the unit \(f\) to the chart tuple on the unit locus near the compact support. Multiplication gives a compact ordinary form, extended by zero
across the neighbourhood where \(\alpha\) is zero. Its integral is
the displayed value by the \hyperref[v037:forms:compactintegral]{ordinary compact integral}.
Choose \(R\) so that \(\log|f|>-R\) on this support. Then the truncation
agrees with the smooth unit logarithm on a neighbourhood of the
support. The product agrees as an ordinary compact form and is zero
where the fixed test is zero. The final assertions follow by applying the same argument
to \(d''\eta\) and \(\dd\eta\), whose bidegrees are \((n-1,n)\) and \((n,n)\), on the open set where they and \(f\) are defined. Neither \(S_1\) nor purity of the zero
scheme is needed for this proposition.
\end{proof}
