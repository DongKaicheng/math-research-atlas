The least contraction retaining specified vertices

4 Analytic curves and skeleta

Open in the English reader · PDF page 790

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{proposition}[The least contraction retaining specified vertices]
\label{v037:f5:leastcontraction}
Let $(\mathcal X,D)$ be a marked semistable algebraic model, with
vertex set $W$, and let $\varnothing\ne S\subset W$.
There is a marked semistable algebraic model $\mathcal X_S$ and
a morphism $\mathcal X\to\mathcal X_S$ retaining $S$ such that
every marked contraction $\mathcal X\to\mathcal Y$ retaining $S$
has a unique factorization
\[
                   \mathcal X\longrightarrow\mathcal Y
                                      \longrightarrow\mathcal X_S.
\]
Its geometric marked graph is the terminal graph of
Definition~\ref{v037:f5:protectedgraph}, with protected set equal
to the entire inverse image of $S$ and with $R$ the vertices whose
normalized residual component is $\mathbf P^1_{\overline k}$.
The construction uses finitely many actual contractions over $O$.
\end{proposition}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Use the actual coefficient extension to $\Omega$ from
Lemma~\ref{cand:algebraicmodelscalar} and the geometric marked dual graph of
Definition~\ref{v036:f5:modelclass}. Its vertex set is the entire
inverse image of $W$ by Proposition~\ref{v036:f5:vertexarithmetic}.
The normalized residual component at a retained geometric vertex
is unchanged under a model morphism: it is the intrinsic normal
proper curve of Theorem~\ref{cand:intrinsicresidualcurve}.
This makes the decoration $R$ independent of subsequent
contractions.

A geometric component is itself a smooth projective line exactly
when its normalization is a projective line and it has no
self-node. Here a self-node is precisely a loop of the dual graph.
Indeed the actual normalization squares of
Lemma~\ref{cand:normalizedmodelbranches} separate exactly the two
branches of each node. At a node joining different components,
the selected component has just one smooth branch: in a pointed
\textup{\'etale} chart it is one of the two axes. Smooth locality
on the source, \cite[Tag 036U]{StacksAlg}, descends this assertion
to that component. Away from the nodes normalization is already
an isomorphism, as in
Lemma~\ref{cand:smoothnodalnormalizationbasechange}.
Thus a component without self-nodes is its smooth normalization;
the assertion follows.

Let $A$ be the set of all currently deletable geometric vertices.
It is invariant under $\operatorname{Aut}(\overline k/k)$:
the action preserves incidence, loops, the marked-leg set, the
normalized component isomorphism class, and the protected set.
Lemma~\ref{v037:f5:geometriccomponentorbits} makes $A$ the entire
inverse image of a subset of original components.

Every vertex of the graph induced by $A$ has degree at most two
in the full graph. A connected component $T$ of it cannot contain
a cycle: all vertices of that cycle would already have full degree
two and no edge leaving it. It would be the whole connected graph,
contrary to the nonempty protected complement. Thus $T$ is a
tree. Put $a=|T|$. Counting its internal edges gives
\[
 b(T)+m(T)
   =\sum_{v\in T}\bigl(\deg_G(v)+m(v)\bigr)-2(a-1)
   \leq2.
\]
All its actual geometric components are smooth projective lines
by the preceding paragraph. Therefore the complement of $A$
satisfies the exact marked forest condition, and
Theorem~\ref{v036:f5:markedcontraction} constructs an actual
original-$O$ contraction deleting precisely $A$.
Its fibre description gives exactly the graph operations in
Lemma~\ref{v037:f5:pruningconfluence}, including the transfer of
a mark and the creation of a self-node from two coincident ends.
The whole marking remains the original finite \textup{\'etale}
$O$-scheme.

Repeat with all deletable vertices of the new model. Each nonempty
round removes original components and retains $S$, so after
finitely many rounds the process stops at a model $\mathcal X_S$
with no deletable geometric vertex. Each round is a forest
contraction and hence decomposes combinatorially into elementary
prunings by Lemma~\ref{v037:f5:pruningconfluence}.
That lemma identifies the resulting graph with its unique terminal
graph. Single geometric deletions in this combinatorial comparison
need not descend individually; every algebraic round used the
entire invariant forest and the original-field contraction theorem.

Now let $q:\mathcal X\to\mathcal Y$ be any marked contraction
retaining $S$. Proposition~\ref{v036:f5:forestnecessity} says that
its removed components satisfy the marked forest condition.
The target has the graph of that forest contraction: the exact
contraction of Theorem~\ref{v036:f5:markedcontraction} to the same
vertex set has the stated fibres and is identified with $q$ by
Proposition~\ref{v036:f5:contractionuniqueness}.
It is consequently a sequence of elementary prunings on the
geometric graph. Continuing to a terminal graph and applying
confluence shows that every retained vertex of $\mathcal X_S$
is a vertex of $\mathcal Y$. The entire-fibre identification
of Proposition~\ref{v036:f5:vertexarithmetic} gives the same
inclusion for original analytic vertices.
Theorem~\ref{v037:f5:algebraicdomination} now supplies the actual
map $\mathcal Y\to\mathcal X_S$, and
Corollary~\ref{v037:f5:intrinsicuniqueness} makes it marked.
Uniqueness of the morphisms inducing the generic identity proves
the factorization and its uniqueness.
\end{proof}
