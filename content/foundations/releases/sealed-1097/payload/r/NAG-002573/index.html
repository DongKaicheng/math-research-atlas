A proper finite polyhedral pushforward; F8GP-5

Proper direct image on historical ordinary compact tests

Open in the English reader · PDF page 1841

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{lemma}[A proper finite polyhedral pushforward; F8GP-5]
\label[lemma]{lem:proper}
The map \(F:P\to\R^{r+1}\) is proper. The chain \(C_0=F_*\mathcal P\)
has a finite polyhedral presentation, and for every real \(S\),
\[
 F_*\bigl(\mathcal P|_{\{t\circ F\leq S\}}\bigr)
                         =C_0|_{\{t\leq S\}}.
 \tag{3.3}
\]
\end{lemma}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
The \(v\)-coordinates range over one fixed compact box.
On the inverse image of a compact target set, its \(t\)-coordinate
has an upper bound \(S\). Equation (3.1) bounds each \(s_i\) above,
while \(s_i\geq-A_i\). Thus the inverse image is a closed subset
of a bounded box, hence compact. Positivity of every \(m_i\) is
used in these separate upper bounds.

There are only \(3^k\) product \(n\)-cells before subdivision.
The image of each is a polyhedron: eliminate the finitely many
linear inequalities, or use the injective affine map on its span
when the rank is \(n\). The finitely many image faces admit a
common finite polyhedral refinement. On a full-rank cell,
\(F\) is an affine isomorphism onto its image, with the fixed
finite index (2.1). Sum these indices over all cells above each
image cell. Rank-deficient cells contribute zero.
This constructs a finite nonnegative integral chain.

On a full-rank cell, intersecting its image with \(t\leq S\) is
exactly the image of its intersection with \(t\circ F\leq S\).
The tangent lattice and index are unchanged on surviving
\(n\)-dimensional pieces. If the whole cell is at \(t=S\), both
operations retain the whole cell. Rank-deficient pieces remain
rank-deficient after cutting. These facts prove (3.3) coefficient
by coefficient, after finite refinement.
\end{proof}
