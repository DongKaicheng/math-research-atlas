\begin{proposition}[Graded rank and central Gauss degree do not supply directions]
\label{bdg:rankobstruction}
The following two examples show why a boundary fan formula must be
separated from a central finite-neighbourhood assertion.

First, over a trivially valued complete $K$, choose a $K$-free polyradius
$r=(r_1,\ldots,r_d)$ and let
\[
 X_0=\mathcal M(K\{r^{-1}T,rT^{-1}\})=\mathcal M(K_r),\quad
 f=(T_1,\ldots,T_d),\quad w=-\log r.
\]
Then $X_0=\{x\}$, it is pure of analytic dimension $d$, and
$r_f(x)=d$, but $x\in\partial_KX_0$ and $C_{x,f}(K_r)=0$.
For every nonsingular integral $Q$ the actual projected Gauss algebra is
$K_r$ of degree $|\det Q|$ over its target Gauss field. Its quotient by
$[\mathbf Z^d:Q\mathbf Z^d]$ is $1$, although the weighted $d$-fan is zero.
With the redundant tuple $(T_1,\ldots,T_d,1)$, injectivity on every actual
$d$-face is vacuous: both the projection selecting the first $d$
coordinates and that selecting $(T_1,\ldots,T_{d-1},1)$ have matrix
rank $d$, but their projected graded ranks are $d$ and $d-1$.

Second, over a nontrivially valued $K$, choose $0<\rho<1$ in $|K^\times|$.
Let $X=\{\rho\leq|T|\leq1\}$, $X'=\{|T|=1\}$, and $f=T$.
At the unit Gauss point $\eta$, both sources have graded rank $1$ and the
same completed residue field. For $T\mapsto T^q$, $q\ne0$, their actual
Gauss local degree is $|q|$. Nevertheless
\[
 C_{\eta,f}(\mathcal O_X)=[\mathbf R_{\geq0}]\ \text{with weight }1,
 \qquad C_{\eta,f}(\mathcal O_{X'})=0.
 \tag{B5}\label{bdg:eqcut}
\]
The inclusion $X'\hookrightarrow X$ is an analytic-domain cut, not an
ordinary neighbourhood at $\eta$. Its map to the ambient torus is not
finite at $\eta$ in the sense of finite maps between ordinary analytic
neighbourhoods. Thus a finite analytic Gauss fibre and maximal graded
rank do not imply a central finite collar.
\end{proposition}
