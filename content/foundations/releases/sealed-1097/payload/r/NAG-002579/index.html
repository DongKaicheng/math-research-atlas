A compact bump with one moment presentation

Chapter 8 supplement: Interior Stokes with coherent coefficients

Open in the English reader · PDF page 1789

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{lemma}[A compact bump with one moment presentation]
\label{f7gg:bump}
Let \(X\) be good and Hausdorff, let \(V=\mathcal M(A)\)
be an ordinary affinoid neighborhood of \(x\), and suppose
\[
 x\in\Int(V/K),\qquad \ker(x)=\mathfrak p\in\Min(A).
\]
Given an ordinary open neighborhood \(O\) of \(x\) in \(X\),
there exist an affinoid domain \(W\subseteq V\), containing
an ordinary neighborhood of \(x\) in \(X\), analytic units
\(g\) on \(W\), and a smooth real function \(\Phi\) on their
moment space such that the scalar section \(b_W=t_g^*\Phi\)
has the following properties:
\[
\begin{gathered}
 0\leq b_W\leq1,\qquad b_W=1\text{ near }x,\\
 \supp_W(b_W)\subseteq C,\qquad
 C\text{ compact},\\
 C\subseteq O\cap\Int_{\rm top}(W,X)\cap\Int(W/K).
\end{gathered}
\]
It extends by zero to a global \(b\in\A_X^{0,0}(X)\).
The restriction of this extension to all of \(W\) is \(b_W\).
If a form is represented by one tuple on \(V\), it remains
represented by that tuple on \(W\).
\end{lemma}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Choose \(h\in A\setminus\mathfrak p\) lying in every other
minimal prime, using the same finite product construction;
take \(h=1\) if there are no other minimal primes.
Then \(|h(x)|>0\).
Choose \(0<\delta<|h(x)|\) and put
\[
                 V_0=\{y\in V:|h(y)|\geq\delta\}.
\]
This is an actual Laurent domain.
Every evaluation kernel on \(V_0\) contains \(\mathfrak p\):
it contains a minimal prime, and the other ones contain \(h\).
Thus every element of \(\mathfrak p\) has value zero
\emph{throughout} \(V_0\).

Choose a basic ordinary neighborhood \(G\) of \(x\) in \(V\),
given by finitely many strict upper bounds and positive
strict lower bounds on elements of \(A\), with
\[
 G\subseteq O\cap\Int_{\rm top}(V,X)\cap\Int(V/K).
\]
The evaluation-topology neighborhood basis and openness
of affinoid interior justify this choice.
If a function in these conditions vanishes at \(x\), it lies
in \(\mathfrak p\); its only possible condition is a positive
upper bound, automatically satisfied on \(V_0\).
Delete these conditions when working in \(V_0\).
Every remaining function is nonzero at \(x\).

Adjoin the condition \(|h|>\delta\) and positive lower
bounds smaller than the values at \(x\) for all remaining
functions. Adding finite upper bounds larger than their
values at \(x\), if necessary, gives a list \(g_1,\ldots,g_N\)
of elements of \(A\), nonzero at \(x\), and real intervals
\((l_j,u_j)\) with
\[
 0<l_j<|g_j(x)|<u_j<\infty.
\]
Their simultaneous strict locus \(G'\) contains \(x\),
lies in \(V_0\), and satisfies \(G'\subseteq G\).
For each \(j\) choose
\[
             l_j<L_j<|g_j(x)|<U_j<u_j.
\]
Set
\[
           W=\{y\in V:L_j\leq|g_j(y)|\leq U_j
                              \text{ for every }j\}.
\]
The actual rational-domain point and joint-presentation
theorems make \(W\) an affinoid domain. It is an ordinary
neighborhood of \(x\) in \(X\), since its inequalities
are strict at \(x\) and \(x\in\Int_{\rm top}(V,X)\).
Each \(g_j|_W\) is a unit, with inverse supplied by its
Laurent presentation. Moreover \(W\subseteq G\).

Choose \(0\leq\Phi_j\leq1\) smooth with compact support in
\((-\log U_j,-\log L_j)\), equal to \(1\) near
\(-\log|g_j(x)|\), and put \(\Phi=\prod_j\Phi_j\).
These real profiles are those constructed in
\src{v037:forms:smoothcutoff}.
The compact set
\[
                   C=t_g^{-1}(\supp\Phi)\subseteq W
\]
contains the support of \(b_W=t_g^*\Phi\).
Every point of \(C\) satisfies all defining inequalities
of \(W\) strictly. Thus \(C\subseteq\Int_{\rm top}(W,X)\):
use the strict locus in \(V\) and \(W\subseteq
\Int_{\rm top}(V,X)\).
Also \(C\subseteq\Int(V/K)\).
The domain-interior theorem and composition give
\(C\subseteq\Int(W/K)\).

Finally glue \(b_W\) on \(\Int_{\rm top}(W,X)\) to zero on
\(X\setminus C\). These are an ordinary open cover of \(X\)
and the sections agree on the overlap.
The ordinary sheaf theorem gives a global smooth moment
function \(b\). On \(W\setminus C\) both its restriction
and \(b_W\) are zero locally, so they agree on all of \(W\).
Compactness of \(C\) and Hausdorffness of \(X\) are essential
for this zero-extension argument.
\end{proof}
