Why a model profile cannot be treated piecewise

Historical statement · exact TeX
\begin{proposition}[Why a model profile cannot be treated piecewise]
\label{f9tm:modelobstruction}
A continuous function on $\mathbf R$ which is affine on a finite
polyhedral subdivision and is $C^1$ is affine. Consequently, for
$m>0$ it cannot also have the two different tail slopes $-m$ and $0$
in \eqref{eq:tails}. Thus the positive-degree finite piecewise-affine
toric model profiles do not lie in the smooth fixed-torus-profile
class above.

For the product model $(\mathbf P^1_{K^\circ})^d$ with the standard
model metrics on $L_i$, the profiles are
$\phi_i^{\rm mod}(t)=m_i\max(0,-t)$. The Chambert--Loir model-measure
formula in this explicit reduced model gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:modelmeasure}
 \mu_{\rm CL}^{\rm mod}=D\,\delta_{\zeta_0},\qquad
 \int u_F\,d\mu_{\rm CL}^{\rm mod}=D F(0)
 \quad\bigl(F\in C(\overline{\mathbf R}^{,d})\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $\zeta_0$ is the unit-radius Gauss point of $T^\an$.
In contrast, discarding the corners and using the ordinary Hessians
on the affine pieces gives the zero density. It fails to reproduce
\eqref{eq:modelmeasure}, even for a compactly supported smooth radial
test equal to one near the origin.
\end{proposition}
