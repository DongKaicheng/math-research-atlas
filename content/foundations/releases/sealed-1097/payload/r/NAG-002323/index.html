Intrinsic correspondence over the original field

4 Analytic curves and skeleta

Open in the English reader · PDF page 786

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{theorem}[Intrinsic correspondence over the original field]
\label{v037:f5:intrinsiccorrespondence}
Suppose $C$ admits at least one semistable algebraic
model over $O$, and fix an unramified marking datum $D$.
The component vertex map induces a bijection between
isomorphism classes of semistable algebraic models of $C$
carrying $D$ as a finite \textup{\'etale} smooth marking
and the finite nonempty vertex sets admissible for
$(C,D)$ in Definition~\ref{v037:f5:admissiblevertices}.
For two such models there is a morphism inducing the
identity on $C$ and respecting $D$ precisely when the
target vertex set is contained in the source vertex set;
that morphism is unique. Thus this bijection is an
equivalence with the partially ordered set under reverse
inclusion. Admissibility is independent of the chosen
comparison field.
\end{theorem}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Let $\mathcal Y$ be such a marked model. Its vertices have
certificates by Theorem~\ref{v037:f5:vertexcertificate}.
Proposition~\ref{v036:f5:vertexarithmetic} identifies their
entire inverse image with its geometric component vertices.
After extension to $\Omega$, the pointed charts and
Corollary~\ref{v035:formal:partition} make this a finite
semistable vertex set. Distinct marked sections have
distinct smooth specializations, so its open-disc tubes
contain at most one marked point and its nodal annular
tubes contain none. This proves admissibility.

Conversely, let $V$ be admissible. By
Theorem~\ref{v037:f5:vertexcertificate}, choose an
original-field semistable algebraic model whose vertex
set contains $V$. Refine it by
Theorem~\ref{v037:f5:unramifiedrefinement} to make $D$ a
disjoint smooth marking. Write $(\mathcal X,D)$ for the
resulting marked model and $W$ for its vertex set. It
still has $V\subset W$. Over $\Omega$, the entire
inverse images satisfy $\overline V\subset\overline W$
by Proposition~\ref{v036:f5:vertexarithmetic}.

Consider a connected component $T$ of the geometric
dual subgraph on the vertices outside $\overline V$.
Since $\overline V$ is semistable,
Lemma~\ref{v035:f5:finiteforest} identifies $T$ with the
part of the source skeleton in one component $U$ of
$C_\Omega^{\an}\setminus\overline V$. It is a finite
tree, and each of its geometric component curves itself
is a smooth $\mathbf P^1_{\overline k}$, hence has no
self-node. The number of its attaching
branches is one if $U$ is an open disc and two if $U$ is
an open annulus, with both branches counted even when
they attach to the same protected component.

Every marking whose specialization is on a component of
$T$ belongs to $U$. Indeed, its smooth $\mathcal X$
tube is connected, avoids $\overline V$, and has that
component vertex in its closure. Since this vertex lies
in open $U$, the tube meets $U$ and therefore lies in
that connected component of the complement. The marking
condition for $V$ consequently bounds the number of
marked legs on $T$ by one in the disc case and by zero
in the annular case. Thus $T$ satisfies precisely the
geometric marked forest condition of
Definition~\ref{v036:f5:forestcondition}.

Theorem~\ref{v036:f5:markedcontraction} now gives an
actual original-field marked model with component vertices
exactly $V$. Its generic identification is the original
one and its marking is isomorphic to the prescribed $D$.
Corollary~\ref{v037:f5:intrinsicuniqueness} proves
uniqueness of the marked model, and
Theorem~\ref{v037:f5:algebraicdomination}, together with
the marking assertion of that corollary, identifies all
its morphisms. This proves the correspondence and its
categorical assertion. Its characterization by original-field
models also proves independence of the comparison field.
\end{proof}
