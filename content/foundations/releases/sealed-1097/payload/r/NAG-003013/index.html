Boundary construction of the actual unit lattice

Proper direct image on historical ordinary compact tests

Open in the English reader · PDF page 1881

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{theorem}[Boundary construction of the actual unit lattice]\label{thm:lattice}
The group
\[
                         M_A=A^\times/K^\times
\]
is a lattice. More precisely, there is a normal integral proper $K$-variety $Y$ with function field $F$, a dense open $V=\Spec B\subset Y$ where $B$ is the normalization of $A$ in $F$, and a finite set $S$ of codimension-one points of $Y\setminus V$, such that
\[
 M_A\longrightarrow\Z^S,\qquad [u]\longmapsto
                  (\ord_D(u))_{D\in S}
 \tag{1}\label{eq:boundary}
\]
is injective. No saturation of a group generated by chosen coordinates is used in this construction.
\end{theorem}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Choose generators $a_1,\ldots,a_m$ of $A$. To construct a projective closure explicitly, take the homogeneous kernel of
\[
 K[X_0,\ldots,X_m]\longrightarrow A[T],\qquad
 X_0\longmapsto T,\quad X_i\longmapsto a_iT.
\]
The image is an integral graded algebra. Its Proj is an integral closed subscheme $\overline U$ of $\mathbf P^m_K$ whose $X_0\ne0$ chart is $U$; that chart is dense. Projectivity gives properness by \tagref{01WC}.

Normalize $\overline U$. By \tagref{0BXR}, the normalization $\nu:Y\to\overline U$ is finite and birational, with $Y$ a normal integral variety. It is proper: a finite ring map is integral by the determinant argument applied to multiplication on a finite generating module, so its associated finite morphism is universally closed by \tagref{01WM}; it is finite type and separated because its affine diagonal is induced by a surjective multiplication map. Composition with $\overline U\to\Spec K$ is proper by \tagref{01W3}. The inverse image $V$ of $U$ is the affine normalization $\Spec B$. Here normalization restricts to an affine open by taking the integral closure in $F$; localization commutes with this operation by clearing the denominators of a monic integral equation.

All these schemes are Noetherian by \tagref{00FN}. The closed set $Y\setminus V$ has finitely many irreducible components. Any codimension-one point in it is the generic point of one of those components, since $Y$ is integral and the complement misses its generic point. Thus $S$ is finite. At a codimension-one point $D$, the normal Noetherian local ring $\mathcal O_{Y,D}$ is a discrete valuation ring by \tagref{00PD}, and we use its order normalized by $\ord_D(\pi_D)=1$. Every unit of $A$ is a unit on $V$, so its order vanishes at every codimension-one point outside $S$. Constants have order zero everywhere. This defines (\ref{eq:boundary}).

If $[u]$ maps to zero, then $u$ and $u^{-1}$ belong to every codimension-one local ring of $Y$. On every affine open of $Y$, \tagref{031T}(2) identifies its normal coordinate ring with the intersection of its height-one localizations inside $F$. Thus $u$ and $u^{-1}$ are regular everywhere on $Y$.

A regular function $f$ on this proper integral $Y$ is constant. Indeed, the associated map $Y\to\mathbf A^1_K\subset\mathbf P^1_K$ is proper by \tagref{01W6}, since $\mathbf P^1_K$ is separated. Its image is a nonempty closed irreducible subset of $\mathbf P^1_K$ missing infinity, hence one closed point. Since $K$ is algebraically closed it is a $K$-point, and reducedness of $Y$ implies $f$ equals that scalar as a regular function. Applying this to $u$ proves injectivity. Lemma~\ref{lem:subgroup} now proves finite freeness.
\end{proof}
