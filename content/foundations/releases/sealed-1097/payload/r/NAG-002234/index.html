Pinching smooth sections over a local ring

4 Analytic curves and skeleta

Open in the English reader · PDF page 755

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{lemma}[Pinching smooth sections over a local ring]
\label{v035:f5:pinch}
Let $R$ be a local ring. Let $A$ and $B$ be flat finitely presented
$R$-algebras with augmentations to $R$. Their fibre product is a flat
finitely presented $R$-algebra and commutes with arbitrary base change.
If $A$ and $B$ are affine neighborhoods of sections in the smooth
loci of separated relative curves, they can be shrunk around those
sections so that $A\times_R B$ is \textup{\'etale} over
$R[u,v]/(uv)$.

Consequently, pinching smooth sections of two proper flat finitely
presented nodal $R$-curves produces a proper flat finitely presented
nodal $R$-curve, compatibly with arbitrary base change.
\end{lemma}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Translate finite algebra generators by their augmentation values and
write $A=R[x_1,\ldots,x_m]/I$ and $B=R[y_1,\ldots,y_n]/J$.
The relation ideals are finitely generated and have zero constant
terms. There is an isomorphism
\[
 A\times_R B
   =R[x_1,\ldots,x_m,y_1,\ldots,y_n]/(I,J,(x_i y_j)_{i,j}).
\]
Indeed, after removing cross products every polynomial is
represented as $c+f(x)+g(y)$ modulo $I,J$, with $f,g$ of zero
constant term; its image is zero precisely when $c=0$, $f\in I$,
and $g\in J$. As an $R$-module this ring is
$R\oplus\ker(A\to R)\oplus\ker(B\to R)$, hence is flat.
The split exact sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow A\times_R B\longrightarrow A\oplus B
     \xrightarrow{\epsilon_A-\epsilon_B}R\longrightarrow0
\]
proves arbitrary-base-change compatibility. The ring $A\oplus B$
is generated as an $A\times_R B$-module by $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$,
and the corresponding finite morphism is surjective.

For the local chart assertion, choose actual \textup{\'etale}
coordinates at the special points of the sections using
\cite[Tag 054L]{StacksAlg}, and subtract their section values.
The chosen affine neighborhoods contain the entire sections, since
an open subset of a local spectrum containing its closed point is
the whole spectrum. The zero fibre of an \textup{\'etale} coordinate
map is \textup{\'etale} and separated over $R$; its section is open
and closed by \cite[Tag 024T]{StacksAlg}. Remove its other components
and take a principal affine neighborhood of the section's special
point. We obtain
\[
 R[u]\longrightarrow A\text{ \textup{\'etale}},\quad A/uA=R,
 \qquad
 R[v]\longrightarrow B\text{ \textup{\'etale}},\quad B/vB=R.
\]
The algebra $D=A\otimes_R B$ is \textup{\'etale}, hence flat,
over $R[u,v]$. Tensoring
\[
 0\longrightarrow R[u,v]/(uv)
 \longrightarrow R[u,v]/(v)\oplus R[u,v]/(u)
 \longrightarrow R[u,v]/(u,v)\longrightarrow0
\]
with $D$ identifies its kernel with $A\times_R B$. Thus
$A\times_R B=D/(uv)$, \textup{\'etale} over $R[u,v]/(uv)$.

The scheme pushout along the two closed sections exists by
\cite[Tag 0E25]{StacksAlg}. Its affine-neighborhood hypothesis
holds because every fibre of a section map has at most one point.
Near the joined section its rings are precisely the fibre products
above; elsewhere the old curves are unchanged. These charts prove
flatness, local finite presentation, and nodality. The disjoint union
of the original curves maps finitely and surjectively to the pushout.
The pushout is separated by \cite[Tag 0E26]{StacksAlg} and universally
closed by \cite[Tag 03GN]{StacksAlg}, hence proper. Quasi-compactness
and separatedness promote local finite presentation to finite
presentation. The same charts and their localization maps commute
with arbitrary base change, proving the last assertion.
\end{proof}
