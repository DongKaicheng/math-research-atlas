Finite pushforward with full generic lengths

5 Tropicalization, multiplicities and balancing

Open in the English reader · PDF page 990

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{theorem}[Finite pushforward with full generic lengths]
\label{v037:degree:finitecyclepushforward}
Let $p:X=\mathcal M(B)\to Y=\mathcal M(A)$ be finite, with
the canonical finite-$A$-module norm on $B$. Suppose that
$X$ and $Y$ are pure of analytic dimension $d$. Let unit
moment tuples define $t_X:X\to\mathbb R^n$ and
$t_Y:Y\to\mathbb R^{n'}$, and let
$a:\mathbb Z^n\to\mathbb Z^{n'}$ be integral. Assume the
corresponding identity of characters, so that
$t_Y\circ p=a\circ t_X$.

Let $w'\in t_Y(Y)$ have its whole fibre in
$\operatorname{Int}(Y/K)$, and suppose that the local target
image is contained in $w'+(N_Y)_{\mathbb R}$ for a saturated
rank-$d$ lattice $N_Y$. Assume that
\[
                 a^{-1}(w')\cap t_X(X)=\{w_1,\ldots,w_s\}
\]
is finite and nonempty and that the local image at each $w_i$
is contained in $w_i+(N_i)_{\mathbb R}$ for a saturated
rank-$d$ lattice $N_i$.

For every $i$, the lattice $a(N_i)$ is contained in $N_Y$
with finite index. Write $m_{\mathfrak p}(w')$ for the
component weights of $Y$ in
Theorem~\ref{v037:degree:moduleweight}. Then
\begin{equation}
\label{v037:degree:eqcyclepushforward}
 \sum_{i=1}^s m_{X,f_X}(w_i)[N_Y:a(N_i)]
   =m_{B,f_Y}(w')
   =\sum_{\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Min}(A)}
                         \beta_{\mathfrak p}(B)m_{\mathfrak p}(w').
\end{equation}
The coefficients are the full generic pushforward lengths:
\[
 \beta_{\mathfrak p}(B)=
 \sum_{\substack{\mathfrak q\in\operatorname{Min}(B)\\
                 \mathfrak q\cap A=\mathfrak p}}
       \operatorname{length}_{B_{\mathfrak q}}(B_{\mathfrak q})
                          [\kappa(\mathfrak q):\kappa(\mathfrak p)].
\]

Consequently, if a positive rational number $\Delta$ satisfies
\[
 \beta_{\mathfrak p}(B)=
                \Delta\,\operatorname{length}(A_{\mathfrak p})
\]
for every component with nonempty moment fibre at $w'$, then
\[
             \Delta\,m_{Y,f_Y}(w')
                =\sum_{i=1}^s m_{X,f_X}(w_i)[N_Y:a(N_i)].
\]
For a reduced integral target, $\Delta$ is the positive integer
$\dim_{\operatorname{Frac}(A)}
(B\otimes_A\operatorname{Frac}(A))$. If $B$ is also integral,
it is $[\operatorname{Frac}(B):\operatorname{Frac}(A)]$.
\end{theorem}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Finite relative interior, Theorem~\ref{fnd:generalizedfinitealgebras},
and the composition inclusion
\ref{fnd:affinoidinteriorcalculus} put every whole source fibre
over $w'$ in $\operatorname{Int}(X/K)$.
Theorem~\ref{v036:flatgermweight} therefore defines all source
and target weights. Their local images fill relative
neighborhoods in the specified planes by
Theorem~\ref{v036:flatgermlocalconstancy}.
The equality of moment maps then gives
$a((N_i)_{\mathbb R})\subset(N_Y)_{\mathbb R}$.
Saturation gives $a(N_i)\subset N_Y$. A nonzero vector in
the kernel of $a|_{(N_i)_{\mathbb R}}$ would produce a small
interval of source moment weights above $w'$, contrary to
the stated finite set. Thus $a|_{N_i}$ is injective, and
its image has finite index in $N_Y$.

Choose an integral $Q:\mathbf Z^{n'}\to\mathbf Z^d$ injective
on $N_Y$ by Lemma~\ref{cand:elementarylatticesplitting}.
Inside the target flat-germ neighbourhood choose a closed box
$P=\prod_j[\alpha_j,\beta_j]$ with $w'$ in its interior.
Write $g_j=f_{Y,j}$ and $Y_0=t_Y^{-1}(P)$. The inequalities
\[
      |g_j|\leq e^{-\alpha_j},\qquad
      |g_j^{-1}|\leq e^{\beta_j}
\]
give a single rational presentation over $A$ with denominator $1$.
Theorems~\ref{fnd:generalizedrationalpoints}
and~\ref{fnd:generalizedrationaluniversal} identify its quotient,
with its quotient norm, as the affinoid domain algebra of $Y_0$.
At every $y$ in the full fibre $F'=t_Y^{-1}(w')$, the two
coordinates for each $j$ are strictly below their displayed
presentation radii. Thus $y\in\operatorname{Int}(Y_0/Y)$ by
Definition~\ref{fnd:relativeinterior}. Since
$F'\subset\operatorname{Int}(Y/K)$ by hypothesis,
Proposition~\ref{fnd:affinoidinteriorcalculus} gives
$F'\subset\operatorname{Int}(Y_0/K)$.

The image of $Y_0$ lies in $w'+(N_Y)_{\mathbf R}$, on which
$Q$ is injective. Its entire projected level at $Qw'$ is therefore
$F'$. For every norm-admissible affinoid test over $Y$, mapping
into $F'$ is equivalent to mapping into $Y_0$ and then into this
projected level. The rational domain universal property gives
inverse bounded maps between the two representing algebras.
Denote this algebra by $D'_{w'}$ with its original characters.

By Theorem~\ref{fnd:generalizeddomainbasechange},
$X_0=p^{-1}(Y_0)$ and $p^{-1}(F')$ are the actual pullbacks of
the two target domains. Proposition~\ref{fnd:generalizedfinitebasechange}
preserves the finite canonical module structure of $X_0\to Y_0$.
Theorem~\ref{fnd:generalizedfinitealgebras} and the composition
inclusion put every point above $F'$ in $\operatorname{Int}(X_0/K)$.
By the character identities, the whole $Qa$-level on $X_0$ at
$Qw'$ is $p^{-1}(F')$. Its source moment values are precisely the
$w_i$: every original source point with $t_X=w_i$ already maps
into $F'$. The same universal property identifies the projected
source level with the pulled-back domain over $X$, and each
exact $w_i$-domain in $X_0$ with the original whole domain in $X$.
Proposition~\ref{v035:analytic:projectedfibrefactors} now splits
the projected level algebra into the product of their actual algebras.
Consequently there are bounded algebra isomorphisms
\[
       \prod_iD_{w_i}\ \simeq\
       B\widehat\otimes_A D'_{w'}\ \simeq\ B\otimes_A D'_{w'} .
\]
The last isomorphism uses the given finite canonical norm and
Proposition~\ref{fnd:generalizedfinitebasechange}. The maps
respect the projected target characters, since they come from
restriction and pullback of the specified character identity.

Let $R_Q$ be the exact-radius target at $Qw'$ and $H_Q$
its Gauss field. All finite tensors are ordinary complete
canonical tensors by
Proposition~\ref{fnd:generalizedfinitebasechange}.
Tensoring the preceding product with $H_Q$ and taking
dimensions gives
\[
 \dim_{H_Q}(B\otimes_AE_Q)
                          =\sum_i\delta_{Qa}(w_i).
\]
Theorem~\ref{v037:degree:moduleweight} evaluates the left
side as $[\mathbb Z^d:Q(N_Y)]m_{B,f_Y}(w')$.
Theorem~\ref{v036:flatgermweight} evaluates each right-hand
term as
$[\mathbb Z^d:Qa(N_i)]m_{X,f_X}(w_i)$.
Counting representatives in
$Qa(N_i)\subset Q(N_Y)\subset\mathbb Z^d$ gives
\[
 [\mathbb Z^d:Qa(N_i)]
    =[\mathbb Z^d:Q(N_Y)]\,[N_Y:a(N_i)].
\]
Cancellation proves \eqref{v037:degree:eqcyclepushforward}.

For the coefficient formula, fix a minimal prime
$\mathfrak p$ of $A$. The ring $A_{\mathfrak p}$ is
Artinian by the first proposition. Its finite algebra
$B_{\mathfrak p}=B\otimes_AA_{\mathfrak p}$ has finite
length as an $A_{\mathfrak p}$-module, and hence is
Artinian as a ring. Decompose it into its local factors
by \cite[Tag 00JB]{StacksAlg}. They are $B_{\mathfrak q}$
for primes $\mathfrak q$ of $B$ over $\mathfrak p$.
Such primes are minimal in $B$: a smaller prime would
have the same contraction, contradicting incomparability
\cite[Tag 00GT]{StacksAlg} for the finite, hence integral,
map \cite[Tag 00GK]{StacksAlg}.
Every simple quotient in a composition series of
$B_{\mathfrak q}$ is $\kappa(\mathfrak q)$, whose length
over $A_{\mathfrak p}$ is its full finite dimension over
$\kappa(\mathfrak p)$. Adding these lengths gives the
asserted coefficient formula.

The target formula in Theorem~\ref{v037:degree:moduleweight}
now proves the assertion with $\Delta$. If $A$ is an
integral domain, its unique generic local ring is
$\operatorname{Frac}(A)$ and has length one. Thus
$\beta_{(0)}(B)$ is the displayed vector-space dimension.
It is positive because
\eqref{v037:degree:eqcyclepushforward} has a positive
left side. If $B$ is integral, this positivity first makes
$A\to B$ injective: a nonzero element of its kernel would
become invertible and annihilate the generic algebra.
Localizing the domain $B$ at the nonzero elements of $A$
is therefore injective. The resulting finite-dimensional
domain is a field and hence is precisely
$\operatorname{Frac}(B)$. This proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}
