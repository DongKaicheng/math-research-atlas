Proper moment pushforward with generic lengths

5 Tropicalization, multiplicities and balancing

Open in the English reader · PDF page 1072

The selected finite-collar comparison uses the literal pullback of the target moment tuple on the full inverse image, with the same module and identity map of moment lattices. The broad finite-map projection theorem remains a separate historical proof gap.

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{theorem}[Proper moment pushforward with generic lengths]
\label{v038:degree:propercycle}
Let $p:X\to Y$ be proper, where $X$ and $Y$ are good Hausdorff
and pure of the same analytic dimension $d$. Let $\mathcal F$
be coherent on $X_G$. For an affinoid domain $V\subseteq Y$
and a unit tuple $f$ on $V$, put $Z=p^{-1}(V)$ and
\[
              \mathcal P_{p,\mathcal F,f}
                       =\mathcal T_{Z,p^*f}(\mathcal F|_Z).
\]
This is a finite nonnegative integral $d$-chain. There is a
compact finite polyhedral set $E$ of dimension at most $d-1$
such that at every generic $d$-cell point $w\notin E$ it has
the following description. There is an actual finite
restriction
\[
 p_P:X_P=p^{-1}(V_P)\longrightarrow V_P=t_f^{-1}(P),
 \qquad w\in\operatorname{int}(P),
\]
with affinoid algebras $A_P,B_P$. For the finite $B_P$-module
$M_P=\Gamma(X_P,\mathcal F)$ its coefficient is
\begin{align*}
 m_{\mathcal P}(w)
   &=\sum_{\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Min}(A_P)}
       \operatorname{length}_{(A_P)_{\mathfrak p}}
                     ((M_P)_{\mathfrak p})
                  m_{\mathcal M(A_P/\mathfrak p),f}(w),\\
 \operatorname{length}_{(A_P)_{\mathfrak p}}
                     ((M_P)_{\mathfrak p})
   &=\sum_{\substack{\mathfrak q\in\operatorname{Min}(B_P)\\
                      \mathfrak q\cap A_P=\mathfrak p}}
       \operatorname{length}_{(B_P)_{\mathfrak q}}
                       ((M_P)_{\mathfrak q})
                    [\kappa(\mathfrak q):\kappa(\mathfrak p)].
\end{align*}
An empty component fibre contributes zero. Residue-field
degrees in this expression are full degrees, including their
inseparable factors. The coefficient and the chain do not
depend on the finite collar or the affinoid cover of $Z$.

If a global unit tuple $g$ on $Z$ and an integral map $a$
satisfy $t_f\circ p=a\circ t_g$, then
\[
                a_*\mathcal T_{Z,g}(\mathcal F|_Z)
                                          =\mathcal P_{p,\mathcal F,f}.
\]
In particular its generic coefficient is the full lattice-index
sum over all full-rank source cells above that target cell;
contracted cells contribute zero. For every smooth
$(d,d)$-superform $\alpha$ defined near $t_f(V)$,
\[
          \mathcal I_{Z,\mathcal F}(p^*t_f^*\alpha)
                             =\int_{\mathcal P_{p,\mathcal F,f}}\alpha.
\]
\end{theorem}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
The space $Z$ is compact Hausdorff and good as in
Corollary~\ref{v038:degree:properfinitecollar}. Its affinoid
domains are domains of $X$ and are pure of dimension $d$ by
Lemma~\ref{v037:integration:puritylocality}. Thus $Z$ is pure
of dimension $d$, and
Theorem~\ref{v038:degree:compactchain} defines the displayed
chain with its stated positivity and finiteness.

Choose the exceptional set in
Corollary~\ref{v038:degree:properfinitecollar}, and enlarge it
by $t_f(\partial_KV)$. On a finite common subdivision of the
actual image, the source-chain image and this exceptional
set, obtained by Lemma~\ref{cand:polyhedralrefinementproduct},
a generic point $w$ of a $d$-cell avoids the exceptional
set. Near it, the target image lies in the affine span of
that cell, with its saturated rank-$d$ tangent lattice.
The proper finite-collar theorem gives $p_P$; choose the box
small enough that $w$ satisfies its inequalities strictly.
Every point of the entire target fibre is absolute interior
in $V$ and interior in the domain $V_P$. Domain interior and
composition, Propositions~\ref{fnd:generaldomainordinaryinterior}
and~\ref{fnd:affinoidinteriorcalculus}, make it absolute
interior in $V_P$. Every point of its full inverse image is
absolute interior in $X_P$, because $p_P$ is finite.
The nonempty domains $V_P$ and $X_P$ are pure of dimension
$d$, and $M_P$ is finite over both $B_P$ and $A_P$ by
Proposition~\ref{fnd:finitecoherentdirectimagebasechange}.

The restriction statement in
Theorem~\ref{v038:degree:compactchain} identifies the source
chain near $w$ with that of $X_P$. Apply the \hyperref[v037:integration:compactfinitepushforward]{one-affinoid chain calculation in finite pushforward}
to \(p_P:X_P\to V_P\) and the single domain \(V_P\).
The finite collar gives norm-admissibility, and the coefficient
module is \(M_P\) on the full inverse image and the same module
regarded over \(A_P\) on the target. The source tuple is the
literal pullback of \(f|_{V_P}\); the map of moment lattices is
the identity. The calculation identifies the two finite chains,
with index one on each common saturated top tangent lattice and
zero target module weight where the source fibre is empty. The generic-length formula
of Theorem~\ref{v037:degree:moduleweight} is the first equality
in the statement. The Artinian local factor calculation of
Theorem~\ref{v037:degree:modulefunctoriality} is the second.
It uses the actual local module lengths, and its simple
factors have dimension the full field degree over the target
residue field. Thus neither nilpotents nor inseparability
have been removed.

Two collars compare on a smaller moment box retaining the
whole target fibre and its entire inverse image. Finite
base change identifies their restricted algebras and modules
with the algebra and module of that same actual inverse
image. The whole-fibre module restriction assertion of
Theorem~\ref{v037:degree:moduleweight} gives the same
coefficient. Cover independence also follows from the
already canonical compact chain.

The lattice pushforward is the last assertion of
Theorem~\ref{v038:degree:compactchain} on $Z$. It requires
only the displayed equality of moment maps; the actual
joint-character graph argument is included in its affinoid
prerequisite. A common finite refinement of the compact
source image gives the finite full-rank lattice-index sum
on each generic target cell. Its support is proper, and
rank-dropping source cells have zero top-degree contribution.
Finally the compact-chain integral identity gives the
last display directly. Every construction used the complete
inverse image $Z$, and every finite collar retained its
entire inverse image over the target box.
\end{proof}
