Unramified invariance of semistable-model existence

4 Analytic curves and skeleta

Open in the English reader · PDF page 823

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{theorem}[Unramified invariance of semistable-model existence]
\label{v038:f5:unramifiedexistence}
Let $C/K$ be smooth, projective and geometrically
connected, let $D$ be an unramified marking datum, and
let $L/K$ be any finite unramified extension.
Then $(C,D)$ has a marked semistable algebraic model
over the original $O$ if and only if $(C_L,D_{L^\circ})$
has one over $L^\circ$.

In the Galois case, given any unmarked semistable
algebraic model $\mathcal Y/L^\circ$, one may choose
the descended model so that its base change to
$L^\circ$ dominates every Galois conjugate of
$\mathcal Y$ and carries the prescribed marking.
No bound on the genus or assumption on the residue
characteristic or discreteness of the valuation is
required.
\end{theorem}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Base change of a projective model is projective by
base change of its closed projective embedding.
Finite presentation is preserved by the same finite
algebra presentations, and nodality and flatness are
preserved by \cite[Tag 0C5B]{StacksAlg}.
The special fibre is the corresponding residue-field
extension, as in Lemma~\ref{cand:algebraicmodelscalar};
geometric connectedness and reducedness are preserved
by their definitions. Closed immersions and finite
\textup{\'etale} markings are preserved by base change,
the latter by \cite[Tag 02GO]{StacksAlg}; smoothness
along them follows by base change of the standard
smooth presentations in \cite[Tag 01V9]{StacksAlg}.
This proves the forward implication.

First assume that $L/K$ is Galois, with group $G$.
Let $\mathcal Y/L^\circ$ be a semistable algebraic
model, initially without a marking. In $(C_L)^{\an}$ put
\[
                       S=\bigcup_{g\in G}gV(\mathcal Y).
\]
This is a finite nonempty invariant set of component
vertices of the conjugate models. Each has an
unramified branch certificate by
Theorem~\ref{v037:f5:vertexcertificate}, applied over
$L$, where the model $\mathcal Y$ already supplies
the requisite existence hypothesis.
Theorem~\ref{v037:f5:leastsemistablehull} gives the
least model $\mathcal M_{D_{O'}}(S)$ marked by
$D_{O'}$, where $O'=L^\circ$.
Its vertex set contains each $gV(\mathcal Y)$.
Theorem~\ref{v037:f5:algebraicdomination} therefore
gives actual generic-identity morphisms from it to
all conjugate models.

The canonical semilinear action of $G$ on $(C_L,D_{O'})$
preserves $S$. The equivariance assertion of the same
least-hull theorem extends it uniquely to the actual
algebraic model $\mathcal M_{D_{O'}}(S)$, with the
group law and the specified action on the marking.
Proposition~\ref{v038:f5:unramifiedeffectivemodel}
descends this model to the original $O$. This proves
both the reverse implication and the domination
assertion in the Galois case.

For a general finite unramified $L/K$, write
$l=\widetilde L$. Choose a finite Galois extension
$l'/k$ containing $l$. Concretely, take a primitive
element of the finite separable extension $l/k$ by
\cite[Tag 030N]{StacksAlg}, form the splitting field
of its separable minimal polynomial by
\cite[Tag 09HU]{StacksAlg}, and use
\cite[Tag 0EXM]{StacksAlg} for its Galois property.
The field is finite because it is generated by
finitely many algebraic roots.
Lemma~\ref{cand:galoisresidueextensionlift} supplies
its finite unramified Galois lift $L'/K$.

There is an actual isometric $K$-embedding $L\hookrightarrow L'$.
Indeed, the construction of
Lemma~\ref{cand:residueextensionlift} presents
$L=K(\alpha)$ by a monic minimal polynomial
$P\in O[T]$ with separable irreducible reduction,
and identifies $\bar\alpha\in l$ with its residue
generator. Its image in $l'$ is a simple root of
$\bar P$. The simple-root construction in
Lemma~\ref{cand:completevaluationhenselian}, applied
inside the complete field $L'$, lifts it to a root
$\alpha'\in(L')^\circ$ of $P$.
Sending $\alpha$ to $\alpha'$ embeds $L$ in $L'$;
uniqueness of the extending absolute value in
Lemma~\ref{fnd:finiteextensionnorms} makes this
embedding isometric. It consequently embeds the
actual valuation rings.

Base change the given model over $L^\circ$ to
$(L')^\circ$, using the properties verified in the
first paragraph. Apply the Galois case to $L'/K$.
The resulting marked model is over the original
valuation ring with the original generic curve and
marking. All absolute values throughout are the
specified extending values; none has been rescaled.
\end{proof}
