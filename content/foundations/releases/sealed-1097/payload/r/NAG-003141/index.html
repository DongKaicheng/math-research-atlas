A unit logarithm need not annihilate a positive current

G Stable inertia and original-field models

Open in the English reader · PDF page 2029

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{proposition}[A unit logarithm need not annihilate a positive current]\label{f9cp:counterexample}
For every $n\geq2$, let $z_1,\ldots,z_n$ be the coordinates on the analytic split torus over $K$, write $x_j=-\log|z_j|$ and $t=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$, and set
\[
 \Omega=(1,2)\times(0,1)^{n-1},\qquad
 U=t^{-1}(\Omega),\qquad h=x_1=\log|z_1^{-1}|.
\]
Then
\[
                  T(\eta)=\int_{U,\mathcal O_U}x_1\eta,
                  \qquad \eta\in\E^{n,n}(U),
\]
is a positive native current with an order-zero bound on every fixed coefficient stage. Nevertheless $\DD h=0$ and $\DD(hT)\ne0$. Thus positivity and these order-zero bounds do not suffice for harmonic frame invariance.
\end{proposition}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Closed polyannuli with boxes inside $\Omega$ are ordinary affinoid neighborhoods in $U$. They are integral and have analytic dimension $n$ by \NAG{002294}; The affine analytification construction \NAG{001707} and separated-space theorem \NAG{001713} make the ambient torus good and Hausdorff. Thus $U$ is good, Hausdorff and pure of dimension $n$. For the global units $g=z_1^{-1}$ and $h_0=1$, the zero set in \NAG{002480} is empty. Its retraction condition is therefore vacuous. That frozen theorem constructs exactly $T$ and its fixed-stage order-zero estimates, on all native top tests. Since $x_1>0$, positivity follows from \NAG{002425}. The unit calculation in Theorem~\ref{f9cp:harmonic} gives $\DD h=0$ as a smooth form; its current conclusion is unavailable without closedness of $T$.

Choose a nonnegative nonzero $\psi\in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ and use the native test
\[
 \alpha=\psi(x)\bigwedge_{j=2}^n(d'x_j\wedge d''x_j)
                         \in\E^{n-1,n-1}(U).
\]
Its support is compact: the inverse image of $\supp\psi$ is closed in a closed polyannulus whose box is compactly contained in $\Omega$. For the coordinate moment map the actual whole-fibre Gauss module is the Gauss field itself, and the tangent lattice map is the identity. Thus its weight is one by \NAG{002362}, consistently with the degree-one identity case of \NAG{002294}. The global chain formula \NAG{002405} and the lattice convention identify coordinate top integrals with ordinary real integrals of their coefficients, without a factorial or a valuation-normalization factor.

Only the $x_1$ derivative survives in $\DD\alpha$. Therefore Theorem~\ref{f9cp:operators} gives
\[
 (\DD(hT))(\alpha)
   =\int_\Omega x_1^2\,\partial_1^2\psi\,dx_1\cdots dx_n
   =2\int_\Omega\psi\,dx_1\cdots dx_n>0.
\]
The second equality is the one-variable fundamental theorem applied twice, with vanishing boundary terms because $\psi$ is compactly supported; iterated integration is the elementary calculation proved in \NAG{002389}. No analytic Stokes or Poincar\'e--Lelong theorem enters this computation. In particular $T$ cannot be closed, by Theorem~\ref{f9cp:harmonic}.
\end{proof}
