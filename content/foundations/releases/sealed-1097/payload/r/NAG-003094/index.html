Strong truncation in the frozen retraction scope

I Descent of the polarized mixed tower

Open in the English reader · PDF page 2096

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{theorem}[Strong truncation in the frozen retraction scope]\label{thm:log}
Under the preceding exact hypotheses, $T_M$ and $T$ are continuous for
$\tau_a$, and $T_M=T$ on each $\tau_a$-bounded test set for all
sufficiently large real $M$.

More quantitatively, every cutoff presentation admits constants
$C_i,H_{i,g},H_{i,h}\geq0$, with $H_i=\max(H_{i,g},H_{i,h})$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:logtail}
 |D_M Q_i\eta|\leq A_i(M)p_i(\eta),\qquad
 A_i(M)\leq C_i\bigl((H_{i,g}-M)_++(H_{i,h}-M)_+\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $p_i(\eta)=\max_{I,J}\sup_{t_{f_i}(V_i)}|\eta_{I,J}|$.
For each $a>0$, the weights
\[
 w_i=1+C_i(H_{i,g}+H_{i,h})e^{aH_i}
\]
define a continuous gauge $P_a$ by \eqref{eq:gauge} and give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:logstrong}
 \sup_{\alpha\in B}|(T_M-T)(\alpha)|
       \leq e^{-aM}\sup_{\alpha\in B}P_a(\alpha)
\end{equation}
on every $\tau_a$-bounded set $B$.
All coherent-cycle weights, lattice volumes, cutoff coefficients,
pullback minors and finite inclusion--exclusion terms enter $C_i$.
\end{theorem}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
The frozen \tagref{NAG-002480} supplies $T$, its continuity, and a compact
$K_i\subset U\setminus Z$ containing the supports of all $Q_i\eta$.
It also supplies eventual equality on the whole presentation, uniformly
in its unrestricted coefficients. Multiplication by the fixed smooth
$u_M$ and the finite native cell formula give continuity of $T_MQ_i$;
hence $T_M$ is continuous by finality. Theorem~\ref{thm:stationarity}
already proves uniform eventual equality on bounded sets.

For the explicit constants, choose a fixed smooth cutoff equal to one
near $K_i$ and supported where $g,h$ are units. Use finitely many actual
joint moment charts there, adjoining $f_i,g,h$ and representations of
this cutoff and $\chi_i$. The native finite cell formula has the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cellerror}
 D_MQ_i\eta=\sum_{\sigma,I,J}\epsilon_\sigma m_\sigma
 \int_\sigma b_{\sigma,I,J}(w)\eta_{I,J}(P_\sigma w)
       \bigl(\delta_M(l_{g,\sigma}(w))
                   -\delta_M(l_{h,\sigma}(w))\bigr)\,d\lambda_\sigma,
\end{equation}
where $\delta_M(t)=\lambda_M(t)-t$, the cells are fixed and compact,
$\epsilon_\sigma\in\{1,-1\}$ are the finite cover signs,
$m_\sigma$ are the actual integer coherent-cycle multiplicities,
$\lambda_\sigma$ is lattice-normalized volume, and the smooth fixed
coefficients $b$ include the covector pullback minors.
The maps $P_\sigma$ are the coefficient-coordinate projections, with
images in $t_{f_i}(V_i)$, and $l_{g,\sigma},l_{h,\sigma}$ are the finite
affine logarithms of the adjoined unit coordinates.
The formula uses only the frozen native integration leaves
\tagref{NAG-002389}, \tagref{NAG-002405}, \tagref{NAG-002416} and
\tagref{NAG-002425}, on the fixed zero-avoiding support.

Writing $s=t+M$ shows directly that
\[
 0\leq\delta_M(t)\leq(-t-M)_+.
\]
Indeed it vanishes for $s\geq0$, and for $s<0$ it is
$(-s)(1-\rho(s+1))$. Define
\begin{align*}
 A_i(M)&=\sum_{\sigma,I,J}|m_\sigma|
 \int_\sigma |b_{\sigma,I,J}|
 \bigl((-l_{g,\sigma}-M)_++(-l_{h,\sigma}-M)_+\bigr)d\lambda_\sigma,\\
 C_i&=\sum_{\sigma,I,J}|m_\sigma|
                  \int_\sigma |b_{\sigma,I,J}|d\lambda_\sigma,\qquad
 H_{i,a}=\max_{\sigma,w\in\sigma}(-l_{a,\sigma}(w))_+
 \quad(a=g,h).
\end{align*}
Empty sums have $C_i=H_{i,g}=H_{i,h}=0$.
Every integrand in these definitions is continuous on a compact cell.
Thus these are legitimate frozen real integrals; no measure extension
or cancellation of total variations is needed. Taking absolute values
term by term in \eqref{eq:cellerror} proves \eqref{eq:logtail}.
If $M>H_i$ the bound is zero. If $M\leq H_i$, then
\[
 A_i(M)e^{aM}\leq C_i(H_{i,g}+H_{i,h})e^{aH_i}<w_i.
\]
Proposition~\ref{prop:gauge} with $\varepsilon(M)=e^{-aM}$ gives
\eqref{eq:logstrong}. The constants use actual multiplicities rather
than an unweighted support or a claimed uniform comparison of charts.
\end{proof}
