Ambient regular representatives; F8NM-R

F Descent of an algebraized polarized tower

Open in the English reader · PDF page 2001

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{proposition}[Ambient regular representatives; F8NM-R]\label{fnd:nag003099:regular-quotient-representatives}
Ambient \(S_1\) is unnecessary for local regular numerator and denominator
representatives of a unit in the total quotient sheaf. Such
representatives remain regular after every actual affinoid restriction,
and their pullbacks to the reduced components are nonzero and regular.
Equality of two representations \(a/b=c/d\) gives \(ad=bc\) after
an actual common ordinary refinement. Componentwise nonvanishing alone
is not a substitute for ambient regularity.
\end{proposition}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
For a ring \(A\), localization at its regular elements is injective.
If \(a/b\) is a unit, write its inverse as \(c/d\), where \(b,d\)
are regular. Equality in the localization gives \(ac=bd\) in \(A\).
The product \(bd\) is regular. If \(az=0\), then \(bdz=acz=0\),
so \(z=0\). Thus \(a\) is regular; the same argument applies to
\(c\). No associated-prime purity hypothesis is used.

The good-space construction at the end of the proof of frozen
\hyperref[fnd:nag002772:ordinary-quotient-shrinking]{NAG-002772} takes these quotients on ordinary neighbourhoods.
When injectivity is first known at a germ, the multiplication kernel is
coherent and vanishes after shrinking. A finite collection of numerators,
denominators, inverse representatives and equalities can be handled on
one smaller neighbourhood. This is the exact coherent shrinking scope
of that input, not a claim about arbitrary meromorphic objects on a
nongood space. Affinoid-domain restriction is flat
(\tagg{NAG-001832}), so it preserves the regular multiplication maps.

A regular element avoids every minimal prime, as checked in reviewed
\hyperref[prop:length]{F8CR-L}. Its image in the domain \(A/\mathfrak q_i\) is therefore
nonzero and regular. \hyperref[fnd:nag002966:component-support]{F8CR-S} and flat actual restrictions supply the
corresponding analytic component regularity. The same injection of
regular localization proves the cross-product identity. The failure
of the last proposed weakening is shown in \hyperref[fnd:nag003099:algebraic-obstruction]{the algebraic example below}.
\end{proof}
