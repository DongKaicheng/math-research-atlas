Statement · exact TeX
\begin{lemma}[Pinching along finite \texorpdfstring{\'etale}{etale} markings]
\label{v036:f5:multisectionpinch}
Let $Z$ be finite \textup{\'etale} over $O$, and let
$Z\hookrightarrow\mathcal A$, $Z\hookrightarrow\mathcal B$
be closed immersions into the smooth loci of proper flat
finitely presented nodal $O$-curves. Their pushout is a
proper flat finitely presented nodal $O$-curve. Its formation
commutes with arbitrary base change on $O$.
\end{lemma}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
The pushout exists by \cite[Tag 0E25]{StacksAlg}: each fibre
of either closed immersion contains at most one point, so
the required affine-neighborhood condition holds. On compatible
affine neighborhoods its ring is $A\times_C B$, where both
maps onto the coordinate ring $C$ of the corresponding open
in $Z$ are surjective. This ring $C$ is flat over $O$.
There is an exact sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow A\times_C B\longrightarrow A\oplus B
       \xrightarrow{a-b}C\longrightarrow0.
\]
% Proposed detached explanatory proof at the signed flat-cokernel site.
Let $P=A\times_C B$ and $Q=A\oplus B$, so that
$0\to P\to Q\to C\to0$ is the displayed sequence of
ordinary $O$-modules. For an arbitrary $O$-algebra $T$,
choose a surjection from a free $O$-module $F$ onto its
underlying module and write $R$ for the kernel. If a class
in $P\otimes_O T$ maps to zero, lift it to $p_F$ in
$P\otimes_O F$. The image of $p_F$ in $Q\otimes_O F$
comes from an element $q_R$ of $Q\otimes_O R$. The image
of $q_R$ in $C\otimes_O R$ maps to zero in $C\otimes_O F$,
so is zero because $C$ is flat. Lift $q_R$ to $P\otimes_O R$.
These lifts exist because tensoring a module quotient is
the quotient of the tensor product by the image of the
relations, by the defining balanced maps. Tensoring the
inclusion $P\to Q$ with the free module $F$ is a direct
sum of copies of that inclusion. It follows that $p_F$
is the image of the lift in $P\otimes_O R$, so the original
class vanishes in $P\otimes_O T$. The same quotient property
identifies the image with the kernel of
$(A\otimes_O T)\oplus(B\otimes_O T)\to C\otimes_O T$.
The resulting isomorphism with the fibre product is an
algebra map by its two coordinate maps.
Thus these affine pushouts and their
localizations commute with every base change.

Choose a finite Galois extension of $k$ splitting the finite
\textup{\'etale} scheme $Z_s$, and take its unramified lift
by Lemma~\ref{cand:galoisresidueextensionlift}. Its integral
coefficient extension is faithfully flat by
Lemma~\ref{v036:f5:unramifiedcoefficients}.
Over its valuation ring $O'$, every rational point of $Z_s$
lifts to an open-and-closed section of the finite
\textup{\'etale} scheme $Z_{O'}$, by
Lemma~\ref{cand:completevaluationhenselian}. These sections
exhaust it: their complement is finite over $O'$ and has
empty special fibre, hence is empty. The base-changed pushout
is therefore the successive pinching of disjoint smooth
sections. Lemma~\ref{v035:f5:pinch} proves it flat, finitely
presented and nodal. These properties descend along the
finite faithfully flat cover: use
\cite[Tags 02L0, 0C5E]{StacksAlg}, the latter including
flatness and relative dimension one.

Both old curves map by closed immersions into the pushout,
as is also clear from the two surjective projections of
$A\times_C B$. Their disjoint union is a finite surjective
cover. The pushout is separated by \cite[Tag 0E26]{StacksAlg}
and universally closed over $O$ by \cite[Tag 03GN]{StacksAlg}.
Together with the finite presentation just established, these
properties give properness. The affine kernel calculation
already proves the stated base-change compatibility.
\end{proof}
