Actual refinements and zero-space purity; F8ZP-R

Proper direct image on historical ordinary compact tests

Open in the English reader · PDF page 1972

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{theorem}[Actual refinements and zero-space purity; F8ZP-R]
\label{f8:zero-purity-restriction}
Let $X$ be a good Hausdorff $K$-analytic space pure of analytic dimension
$n\geq1$, let $U\subseteq X$ be open, and let $f\in\OO_{U_G}(U)$ act
injectively on $\OO_{U_G}$. Let $D=V(f)\hookrightarrow U$ be the actual
principal closed analytic subspace. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
\item $D$ is empty or pure of analytic dimension $n-1$.
\item For every actual affinoid domain $V=\M(B)\subseteq U$, including
a domain that is not an ordinary neighbourhood in $U$, its restricted
zero space is the full quotient $D\cap V=\M(B/fB)$, empty or pure of
analytic dimension $n-1$.
\item These conclusions hold on every nonempty finite intersection
$V_I=\bigcap_{i\in I}V_i$ of actual affinoid domains in $U$, and on
further actual affinoid-domain refinements of $V_I$.
\end{enumerate}
The same conclusion holds for an already given effective Cartier
closed subspace locally defined by such regular sections.
\end{theorem}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
If $V$ is nonempty, \tagg{NAG-002413} makes $B$ pure of analytic
dimension $n$. The kernel sheaf is formed on sections: kernels of compatible
sections glue uniquely. Thus a section on the $G$-domain $V$ killed
by $f$ belongs to this zero kernel sheaf. This gives an injection
$B\xrightarrow{f}B$. Theorem~\ref{f8:zero-purity-general} applies to this ring and section.
The defining quotient is actual by \tagg{NAG-001562}. Its restriction
from an affinoid chart is the principal quotient just written, by
\tagg{NAG-001846} and the finite tensor comparison
\tagg{NAG-001565}. This proves (ii), with the zero-algebra convention
covering empty domains.

For clarity, on a further actual affinoid domain $W=\M(C)\subseteq V$
these same interfaces give bounded canonical identifications
\begin{equation}\label{eq:f8:zero-purity-domain-restriction}
 C\widehat\otimes_B(B/fB)
       \simeq C\otimes_B(B/fB)
       \simeq C/fC.
\end{equation}
The first tensor has a finite canonical second factor. It is not an
assertion about arbitrary Banach modules. Moreover the restriction
$B\to C$ is flat by \mbox{\hyperref[fnd:nag001832:ordinary-single-domain-flatness]{ordinary single-domain flatness}}, so $f$ remains a non-zero-divisor
in $C$ even if regularity was originally specified on $B$ alone.
The quotient in \eqref{eq:f8:zero-purity-domain-restriction} is the actual inverse-image
domain in $D\cap V$, with its nilpotents. Both purity of $C$ and purity
of its zero quotient follow from the statements already applied.

The intersections $V_I$ are actual affinoid domains by
\tagg{NAG-002411}; hence (ii) and \eqref{eq:f8:zero-purity-domain-restriction} apply to them
and to their refinements. This proves (iii). Each point of $D$ has an
ordinary affinoid neighbourhood obtained by intersecting $D$ with an
affinoid neighbourhood in $U$. Assertion (ii) proves purity on these
neighbourhoods, which is exactly the good-space definition. Thus (i)
follows. In the effective Cartier variant apply the same argument on
its defining open charts. On overlaps two local equations differ by
a unit, hence define the same ideal and actual quotient. Purity is
local in these neighbourhoods, so the conclusions agree on overlaps.
No global irreducible-component matching is required.
\end{proof}
