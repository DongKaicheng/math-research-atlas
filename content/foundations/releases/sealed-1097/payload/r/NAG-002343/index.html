An unramified Gauss germ gives an actual integral chart

4 Analytic curves and skeleta

Open in the English reader · PDF page 805

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{proposition}[An unramified Gauss germ gives an actual integral chart]
\label{v037:f5:gaussgermintegralchart}
Let $X$ be a good $K$-analytic space, let $v\in X$, and suppose
that an analytic morphism from an ordinary neighbourhood of $v$
to the analytic affine line is \textup{\'etale} at $v$ and sends
$v$ to $\eta$. Put $L=\mathcal H(v)$, with the induced map
$H\to L$. Suppose that $L/H$ is finite, that
$l=\widetilde L$ is finite separable over $k(t)$, and that
\[
                           [L:H]=[l:k(t)].
\]
Then $v$ belongs to an actual affinoid domain of $X$ carrying
an integral \textup{\'etale} Gauss chart. Its special fibre is
integral and $v$ is its unique component point.
\end{proposition}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Choose a primitive element $\alpha$ of $l/k(t)$ by
\cite[Tag 030N]{StacksAlg}, and let $\bar P\in k(t)[S]$
be its monic minimal polynomial of degree $f$.
Choose $g_0\in O[T]$ with nonzero reduction so that all
coefficients of $\bar P$ lie in $k[t,1/\bar g_0]$.
Lift them to $O[T,1/g_0]$, obtaining a monic polynomial $P$.
On the free module $O[T,1/g_0][S]/(P)$ with its monic
remainder basis, let $\Delta\in O[T,1/g_0]$ be the
determinant of multiplication by $P'$.
Its reduction is nonzero in $k(t)$, because $\bar P$ is
separable. Write $\Delta=a(T)/g_0(T)^m$ with $a\in O[T]$;
then $\bar a\ne0$. Put $g=g_0a$.
The adjugate matrix makes $P'$ a unit in
\[
       B^{\mathrm{alg}}_0=O[T,1/g][S]/(P).
\]
For an arbitrary square-zero ideal, a lifted root is corrected
uniquely by $b\mapsto b-P(b)/P'(b)$. Thus this finite free
algebra is formally \textup{\'etale}, and is ordinarily
\textup{\'etale} by \cite[Tag 00UR]{StacksAlg}.

Let
\[
 R_0=O\langle T,Z\rangle/(g(T)Z-1),\qquad
 B_0=R_0[S]/(P).
\]
The algebra $B^{\mathrm{alg}}_0$ is flat over $O$:
coefficientwise injectivity in the ideal criterion
\cite[Tag 00HD]{StacksAlg} makes $O[T]$ flat over $O$;
localization is flat by \cite[Tag 00HT]{StacksAlg}, and
the final extension is finite free.
The actual completion calculation in
Lemma~\ref{cand:affineflatformalcompletion} identifies $B_0$
with the completion of $B^{\mathrm{alg}}_0$, coefficient by
coefficient in its finite free monic basis. It is admissible,
finite free and \textup{\'etale} over $R_0$.
Its special algebra is finite free over $k[t,1/\bar g]$
and injects into its generic fibre $l$, so it is a domain.
Consequently Proposition~\ref{v037:f5:integralgaussdegree}
gives exactly one point $w$ over $\eta$ in
$B=\mathcal M(B_0[1/\pi])$, with
$[\mathcal H(w):H]=f$ and residual extension $l/k(t)$.
Here $0\ne\pi\in K^{\circ\circ}$ is any choice used for
the completion.

The residue element $\alpha\in\widetilde L$ is a simple
root of $\bar P$. The complete-valuation henselian lemma
\ref{cand:completevaluationhenselian} lifts it to a root
of $P$ in $L^\circ$. It gives an $H$-homomorphism
$\mathcal H(w)=H[S]/(P)\to L$.
Both fields have degree $f$ over $H$, so it is an isomorphism;
it is isometric by Lemma~\ref{fnd:finiteextensionnorms}.

Use the full algebraic generic cover
\[
 \operatorname{Spec}B^{\mathrm{alg}}_0[1/\pi]
                       \longrightarrow\operatorname{Spec}K[T,1/g].
\]
Its analytification $\mathcal B$ is \textup{\'etale} over
the ordinary open $g\ne0$ in the analytic line by
Proposition~\ref{fnd:analytificationzariskiopens} and
Theorem~\ref{fnd:analytificationetalepreservation}.
Theorem~\ref{fnd:analytificationfiniteclosed} identifies $B$
with its entire inverse image of the affinoid domain $D_g$.
In particular its entire fibre at $\eta$ is $\{w\}$.

By the ordinary-open local definition of an analytic
\textup{\'etale} map, the map near $v$ restricts to a finite
flat map with zero relative differentials. Finite branch
separation in Lemma~\ref{fnd:finiteanalyticbranchseparation}
then gives a finite \textup{\'etale} germ with fibre field $L$.
The completed-residue-field
equivalence of Theorem~\ref{fnd:goodetalegermresidueequivalence}
therefore gives an isomorphism over the analytic line between
ordinary neighbourhoods of $v$ in $X$ and of $w$ in
$\mathcal B$. Write $W$ for the latter neighbourhood.

By Proposition~\ref{new:affinoidcompact}, the compact set
$B\setminus W$ has closed image in the Hausdorff affinoid
$D_g$. Its image avoids $\eta$ because
the full fibre is the singleton $w\in W$.
Lemma~\ref{v037:f5:gaussprincipalshrink} supplies a polynomial
$h\in O[T]$ with nonzero reduction such that the domain
$D_{gh}$ avoids that image. Its full inverse image in $B$
is therefore contained in $W$. It is the actual affinoid
generic fibre of the principal formal restriction
\[
                  B_0\langle U\rangle/(h(T)U-1)
\]
by Proposition~\ref{fnd:formalprincipalopenflat}.
This restriction is finite projective and \textup{\'etale}
over the principal base chart for $gh$, by ordinary base
change and \cite[Tag 02GO]{StacksAlg}. Its special algebra
is the corresponding nonzero localization of the integral
special algebra of $B_0$.
The ordinary-neighbourhood isomorphism transports its actual
affinoid domain into $X$. Its unique component point is
transported from $w$ to $v$, proving the assertion.
\end{proof}
