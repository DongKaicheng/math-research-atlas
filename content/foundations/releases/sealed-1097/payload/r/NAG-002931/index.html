Atomic calibration on the projective model

I Descent of the polarized mixed tower

Open in the English reader · PDF page 2063

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{proposition}[Atomic calibration on the projective model]
\label{f9mi:atomiccalibration}
For any set $J$ of $r$ remaining lines on $\mathbf P(E)$, the specified
mixed model measure and its mass are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ex68:f9mi-projective:one-atom}
 \mu_J=D_J\delta_{\xi_E},\qquad
 D_J=\prod_{j\in J}m_j
       =\deg_{(L_j)_{j\in J},K}(\mathbf P(E_K)).
\end{equation}
The empty product in dimension zero is one. For a further nonzero row
$\ell_i$ of content $a_i$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ex68:f9mi-projective:logterm}
 \int_{\mathbf P(E_K)^\an}-\log\|s_i\|_i\,d\mu_J
                   =\lambda(m_i a_i+c_i)D_J.
\end{equation}
The logarithm is finite at the atom, and every proper algebraic closed
subset has measure zero. For $r\geq1$ the normalized horizontal
coefficient is $m_i$. For $r=0$ the generic divisor is empty and the
horizontal part is zero. The normalized vertical coefficient is
$m_i a_i+c_i$, and the special-fibre multiplicity is one.
There is no additional factorial.
\end{proposition}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
For projective space, the dimension of homogeneous polynomials of
degree $nq$ is $\binom{nq+r}{r}$, with leading term $n^rq^r/r!$.
Thus $\deg_{\cO(n)}\mathbf P^r=n^r$. Polarizing this polynomial
shows that the mixed degree of $\cO(n_1),\ldots,\cO(n_r)$ is
$\prod_jn_j$: the coefficient of $t_1\cdots t_r$ in
$(\sum_jn_jt_j)^r$ is $r!\prod_jn_j$.
The same normalization has a direct complete-intersection check.
Choose the $r$ coordinate equations $z_j^{n_j}=0$, $1\leq j\leq r$.
They meet at $[1:0:\cdots:0]$, and their local algebra is
\[
                   k[t_1,\ldots,t_r]/(t_1^{n_1},\ldots,t_r^{n_r}).
\]
Its monomials with exponents smaller than $n_j$ form a basis of size
$\prod_jn_j$. These are regular successive coordinate powers, and the
quotient retains all nilpotents. Its residue field is $k$, so no
residue-degree factor is suppressed. The same calculation holds over
$K$.

For the root model lines $\cO(e_jm_j)$, this mixed special-fibre
degree is $\prod_j e_jm_j$. The BPS defining atomic weight divides
by $\prod_je_j$ and multiplies by the special-fibre multiplicity,
which is one. This proves \eqref{eq:ex68:f9mi-projective:one-atom} independently of the
chosen clearing exponents. The constant shifts do not change these
special-fibre lines.

The Gauss seminorm is multiplicative by NAG-000182. Its kernel on
the affine coordinate ring is zero, so its scheme point is generic;
this proves the zero-mass assertion. The Gauss value of the row $\ell_i$
is the maximum absolute value of its coefficients, namely $|\pi|^{a_i}$.
The denominator in \eqref{eq:ex68:f9mi-projective:metric} is one in this chart. Hence
$-\log\|s_i(\xi_E)\|=\lambda(m_i a_i+c_i)$. Integrating a finite
value against the one-atom measure proves \eqref{eq:ex68:f9mi-projective:logterm}.
For $r=0$ the row is a nonzero scalar and the same calculation applies.
\end{proof}
