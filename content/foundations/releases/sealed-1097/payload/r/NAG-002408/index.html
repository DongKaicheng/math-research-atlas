An integral independent of finite affinoid chart data

5 Tropicalization, multiplicities and balancing

Open in the English reader · PDF page 1032

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{theorem}[An integral independent of finite affinoid chart data]
\label{v037:integration:finitechartintegral}
Fix a finite $A$-module $M$. For finite moment-chart data
of bidegree $(d,d)$ on $X$, put $V_I=\bigcap_{i\in I}V_i$,
$A_I=\mathcal O(V_I)$ and $M_I=M\otimes_A A_I$.
Choose any $i\in I$ to represent the restricted form on
$V_I$, and set
\begin{equation}
\label{v037:eq:finitechartintegral}
 \mathcal I_M(\alpha)
   =\sum_{\varnothing\ne I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,s\}}
       (-1)^{|I|+1}
       \mathcal I_{M_I,f_i|_{V_I}}(\alpha_i).
\end{equation}
An empty intersection contributes zero. The expression
is independent of each chosen $i$, of the finite cover,
of the unit tuples and of the representatives within
the equivalence of
Definition~\ref{v037:integration:finitechartdata}.
It is linear in the form and additive in short exact
sequences of finite $A$-modules. It is nonnegative if
all chart representatives have nonnegative tangential
top coefficients. For a single global
moment presentation it is exactly the global integral
given by the \hyperref[fnd:nag002405:finite-cell-functional]{finite-cell functional}.
\end{theorem}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Finite intersections are actual affinoid domains by
Corollary~\ref{fnd:generalizeddomainintersections}; their
algebras and restriction maps are the actual domain
ones. Every nonempty intersection is pure of analytic
dimension $d$ by
Theorem~\ref{v036:analytic:domainpurity}. Thus all the
displayed integrals exist by the \hyperref[fnd:nag002405:finite-cell-functional]{finite-cell functional}. Compatibility of the chart
data restricts to further intersections by
Theorem~\ref{v037:integration:globalchartfaithfulness}.
On $V_I$, the \hyperref[fnd:nag002405:same-affinoid-coordinate-augmentation]{same-affinoid coordinate augmentation} identifies the
integrals of any two chosen representatives with
the integral of their equal joint pullbacks. This
proves independence of the choice of $i$.

First suppose the data come from a single tuple on
$X$. The domain-cover identity of
Theorem~\ref{v037:localization:globalcoveradditivity}
gives exactly \eqref{v037:eq:finitechartintegral}.
The same identity holds after restricting a single
chart presentation to any of its affinoid subdomains.

Now compare compatible data on two finite covers
$(V_i)_{i=1}^s$ and $(W_j)_{j=1}^t$.
The module on every overlap is the restriction of the
same $M$, by the actual domain tensor identifications
of Proposition~\ref{fnd:generalizedfinitetensor} and
Theorem~\ref{fnd:generalizeddomainbasechange}.
For each nonempty $I$, cover $V_I$ by the
$V_I\cap W_j$ and apply the preceding single-chart
identity to its chosen representative. Substitution
in \eqref{v037:eq:finitechartintegral} gives
\[
 \sum_{I\ne\varnothing}\sum_{J\ne\varnothing}
       (-1)^{|I|+|J|}
       \mathcal I_{M|_{V_I\cap W_J}}(\alpha|_{V_I\cap W_J}).
\]
The two representatives on each of these intersections
have equal integrals by their joint-coordinate comparison.
Interchange the two finite sums and apply the domain-cover
identity to each $W_J$. This recovers precisely the
expression for the second cover. Thus the value is
unchanged under compatible replacement or refinement.
For data identified after a common refinement, apply
this comparison to each of the two original covers
and that common refinement. This proves all claimed
independences.

Linearity is termwise. For a short exact sequence of
finite $A$-modules, domain flatness
\hyperref[fnd:nag001832:ordinary-single-domain-flatness]{on a single actual domain} keeps it exact on
every $V_I$. The global module-chain additivity of
the \hyperref[fnd:nag002405:global-finite-module-chain-weights]{finite-module chain and weights} gives additivity
of each integral, and hence of the finite alternating
sum. The assertion about a single global presentation
was proved in the second paragraph.

To verify positivity, order the cover and group the
alternating sum by the largest index $j$ in $I$.
The $j$-th group uses the single representative
$\alpha_j$ and is the integral of the chain
\[
 C_j=\sum_{J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,j-1\}}(-1)^{|J|}
       \mathcal T_{f_j|_{V_j\cap V_J}}
                     (M|_{V_j\cap V_J}),
\]
with the empty intersection in this formula understood
to be $V_j$. Each $C_j$ is nonnegative. Indeed, choose
a common finite subdivision of these actual moment
images and their boundary images by the \hyperref[cand:polyhedralrefinementproduct]{common finite subdivision lemma}. The latter have
dimension at most $d-1$ by
Theorem~\ref{v037:globaltrop:finiteimage}. At a generic
point of a $d$-cell, apply
Theorem~\ref{v037:localization:fibrecover} on $V_j$ to
the family consisting of $V_j$ itself and the domains
$V_j\cap V_i$ for $i<j$. It supplies idempotents $e_i$
in the actual whole-fibre algebra; the member $V_j$
ensures the cover hypothesis. For a transverse Gauss
algebra $E_Q$ and its common lattice divisor $c_Q$,
the weight of $C_j$ is
\[
 \frac{1}{c_Q}\dim_{H_Q}
       \left(\prod_{i<j}(1-e_i)\right)
                \bigl(M|_{V_j}\otimes_{A_{V_j}}E_Q\bigr)
                                      \geq0.
\]
This is the elementary expansion of the product of
the complementary idempotents, with all module
structures retained. It is already an integer because
it is the displayed alternating sum of integer module
weights. Thus every generic coefficient of $C_j$ is
nonnegative. Its integral against $\alpha_j$ is
nonnegative by the \hyperref[v037:integration:polyhedralcalculus]{nonnegative cell integral} on each cell. Summing
these finitely many groups proves positivity.
\end{proof}
