\begin{theorem}[Uniform eventual equality on bounded sets]\label{thm:stationarity}
Let $D_M:E\to\R$ be linear, for real $M\geq0$. Assume that every
$D_M Q_i$ is continuous, and that for every $i$ there is $H_i<\infty$
such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stagezero}
 D_M Q_i=0\qquad\hbox{for all }M\geq H_i.
\end{equation}
Then each $D_M$ is continuous. For every bounded set $B\subset E$ there
exists $H_B<\infty$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boundedzero}
 D_M(b)=0\qquad(M\geq H_B,\ b\in B).
\end{equation}
In particular $D_M\to0$ in $E'_b$. No bounded-set lifting, countable
cofinality, regular inductive limit, or uniform bound on the $H_i$ is
assumed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Continuity of $D_M$ is exactly the final seminorm criterion.
Every $x\in E$ is a finite sum of elements of presentation images, so
\eqref{eq:stagezero} makes $D_M(x)$ eventually zero.
Suppose \eqref{eq:boundedzero} fails. Choose $M_j\geq j$ and $b_j\in B$
with $D_{M_j}(b_j)\ne0$, and put
\[
 c_j=\frac{j}{|D_{M_j}(b_j)|},\qquad
 P(x)=\sum_{j\geq1}c_j|D_{M_j}(x)|.
\]
The sum is finite for every $x$, so $P$ is a finite-valued seminorm.
For each fixed $i$ all terms with $j\geq H_i$ vanish on $Q_i(E_i)$.
Thus $P Q_i$ is a finite sum of continuous seminorms and is continuous.
The final topology makes $P$ continuous on $E$. But
$P(b_j)\geq j$, contradicting boundedness of $B$. This contradiction
proves \eqref{eq:boundedzero}, for the full real parameter family.
\end{proof}
