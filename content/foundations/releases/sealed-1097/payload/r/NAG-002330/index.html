The least marked semistable hull

4 Analytic curves and skeleta

Open in the English reader · PDF page 792

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{theorem}[The least marked semistable hull]
\label{v037:f5:leastsemistablehull}
Suppose $C$ admits a semistable algebraic model over $O$, and fix
an unramified marking datum $D$. For every finite nonempty set
$S\subset C^{\an}$ of points with unramified branch certificates,
there is a least finite vertex set $H_D(S)$ admissible for $(C,D)$
and containing $S$. Equivalently, there is a marked semistable
algebraic model $\mathcal M_D(S)$ containing $S$ such that every
marked semistable algebraic model containing $S$ has a unique
marked morphism to $\mathcal M_D(S)$ inducing the identity on $C$.

For $S\subset S'$ the resulting models have a unique compatible
morphism $\mathcal M_D(S')\to\mathcal M_D(S)$.
Any finite nonempty family of models marked by $D$ has a least
common dominating model: take $S$ to be the union of their vertex
sets. Any group of compatible semilinear isometric automorphisms
of $(C,D)$ preserving $S$ extends uniquely to $\mathcal M_D(S)$.
\end{theorem}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{v037:f5:vertexcertificate} constructs an original-field
semistable model containing $S$. Apply
Theorem~\ref{v037:f5:unramifiedrefinement} to give it the prescribed
smooth marking $D$, while retaining $S$; call the resulting model
$\mathcal X$. Proposition~\ref{v037:f5:leastcontraction} gives
$\mathcal M=\mathcal X_S$.

Let $\mathcal Y$ be any other model marked by $D$ and containing
$S$. Theorem~\ref{v037:f5:commondomination} gives an actual common
marked semistable model $\mathcal T$ of $\mathcal X$ and
$\mathcal Y$. Its geometric graph can be pruned to that of
$\mathcal X$, and then to the terminal graph of $\mathcal M$,
by Proposition~\ref{v036:f5:forestnecessity} and
Lemma~\ref{v037:f5:pruningconfluence}. It can also be pruned to
the graph of $\mathcal Y$. The protected geometric labels are
the same entire inverse image of $S$ throughout, by
Proposition~\ref{v036:f5:vertexarithmetic}. Confluence
therefore makes the terminal graph obtained from $\mathcal Y$
equal to that of $\mathcal M$, with the same retained analytic
labels. In particular $V(\mathcal M)\subset V(\mathcal Y)$.
Theorem~\ref{v037:f5:algebraicdomination} and
Corollary~\ref{v037:f5:intrinsicuniqueness} give the unique marked
map $\mathcal Y\to\mathcal M$.

The vertex set $H_D(S)=V(\mathcal M)$ is admissible by
Theorem~\ref{v037:f5:intrinsiccorrespondence}. Conversely every
admissible finite set containing $S$ is the vertex set of a model
in the preceding comparison. Thus it contains $H_D(S)$.
This proves both the minimum and independence of the initially
chosen model. It also proves that $\mathcal M$ is unique up to
its unique generic-identity marked isomorphism.

If $S\subset S'$, the model for $S'$ contains $S$, giving the
stated unique morphism and all composition compatibilities.
For a finite family of marked models, each vertex has a certificate
by Theorem~\ref{v037:f5:vertexcertificate}. Apply the construction
to their finite nonempty union. Its model dominates every family
member by vertex inclusion. Every common dominating model contains
that union and therefore has a unique map to this one.

Finally a compatible semilinear isometry preserving $S$ transports
the class of marked models containing $S$ to itself. The universal
property identifies its transform of $\mathcal M$ with
$\mathcal M$; equivalently, it preserves $H_D(S)$.
Corollary~\ref{v037:f5:intrinsicactions} extends these isometries
to the model and proves the group law and uniqueness.
Every construction kept the original valuation ring and absolute
value; $\Omega$ was used only for the geometric graph comparison.
\end{proof}
