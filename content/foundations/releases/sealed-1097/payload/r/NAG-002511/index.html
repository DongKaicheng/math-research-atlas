Module base change at a complete Gauss field

C Native scalar extension and finite restriction of constants

Open in the English reader · PDF page 1761

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{lemma}[Module base change at a complete Gauss field]
\label{f9cand:gauss-module}
Let $D$ be a finite canonical $R_{K,r}$-algebra and $P$ a finite
$D$-module, with its canonical norm. Then
$D_L=D\wt_K L=D\otimes_{R_{K,r}}R_{L,r}$ and
$P_L=P\wt_K L=P\otimes_{R_{K,r}}R_{L,r}$, with bounded inverse
comparisons. There is a canonical isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gauss-module}
 P_L\otimes_{R_{L,r}}H_{L,r}
 \simeq
 (P\otimes_{R_{K,r}}H_{K,r})\otimes_{H_{K,r}}H_{L,r}.
\end{equation}
The native vector-space dimension is unchanged. If
$E=D\otimes_{R_{K,r}}H_{K,r}=\prod_j E_j$ and
$Q=P\otimes_{R_{K,r}}H_{K,r}$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gauss-lengths}
 \dim_{H_{K,r}}Q
   =\sum_j\len_{E_j}Q_j\,[E_j/\mathfrak m_j:H_{K,r}].
\end{equation}
The corresponding formula after extension has the same total.
\end{lemma}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Composing finite free quotient presentations makes $P$ a finite
canonical $R_{K,r}$-module; the quotient norm is the given canonical
$D$-module norm up to uniform equivalence. The actual scalar pushouts
and finite tensor comparison [\ref{fnd:generalizedfinitealgebras}, \ref{fnd:generalizedfinitebasechange}, \ref{fnd:generalizedfinitetensor}] give the two
identifications with $R_{L,r}$. Equivalently one may apply the
algebra statement [\ref{v036:gaussbasechange}, \ref{v036:flatgermweight}] to the square-zero finite algebra
$D\oplus P$ and take its $P$ summand.
The coefficient embedding $H_{K,r}\hookrightarrow H_{L,r}$ is isometric
by [\ref{v036:gaussbasechange}, \ref{v036:flatgermweight}]. Associativity of these finite tensors gives
\eqref{eq:gauss-module}. A basis of a finite-dimensional vector space
remains a basis after field extension, so the dimensions agree.
The finite algebra $E$ is the product of its Artinian local factors
\cite[Tag 00JB, Lemma 10.53.6]{StacksAlg}; the simple factors of $Q_j$ are its full residue
field. Taking dimensions proves \eqref{eq:gauss-lengths}.
No separable degree or reduced quotient enters this computation.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The entire Gauss module}
For a positive real polyradius $r$, let
\[
 R_{K,r}=K\{r^{-1}T,rU\}/(T_iU_i-1),\qquad
 H_{K,r}=\widehat{\Frac(R_{K,r})}
\]
for the multiplicative Laurent Gauss norm. Empty tuples give $R=H=K$.
