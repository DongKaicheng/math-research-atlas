Proper morphisms are finite near generic moment levels

5 Tropicalization, multiplicities and balancing

Open in the English reader · PDF page 1068

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{corollary}[Proper morphisms are finite near generic moment levels]
\label{v038:degree:properfinitecollar}
Let $p:X\to Y$ be a proper morphism of good Hausdorff analytic
spaces, with $\dim_K X\le d$. Let $V\subseteq Y$ be an affinoid
domain and let $f$ be a unit tuple on $V$. There is a compact
finite integral-affine polyhedral set $E\subseteq\mathbb R^n$
of dimension at most $d-1$ such that, for every $w\notin E$,
one can choose a closed moment box $P$ about $w$ for which
\[
       V_P=t_f^{-1}(P),\qquad X_P=p^{-1}(V_P),\qquad
                             p_P:X_P\longrightarrow V_P
\]
are actual affinoid domains and a finite morphism. Empty domains
are allowed. The box can be made arbitrarily small.

In particular, if $\operatorname{Fin}(p_V)$ is the ordinary
finite-fibre locus of $p_V:p^{-1}(V)\to V$, then
\[
                    t_f\bigl(V\setminus\operatorname{Fin}(p_V)\bigr)
                                                        \subseteq E.
\]
The same inclusion holds for the image of every positive-dimensional
analytic fibre. When $V$ is pure of dimension $d$, the complement
of $E$ is dense on every $d$-dimensional cell of its actual moment
image.
\end{corollary}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Properness makes $Z=p^{-1}(V)$ compact Hausdorff. It is good by the actual product charts. At a point of $Z$,
choose an ordinary affinoid neighborhood $U$ of its image in $Y$
and ordinary affinoid neighborhoods $X_0$ and $V_0$ of
its projection points in $X$ and $V$, both mapping into $U$.
The affinoid product $X_0\times_U V_0$ identifies with the
intersection of the two inverse images in $X\times_Y V$ by
Theorem~\ref{fnd:generalanalyticproducts}. It contains the
intersection of the inverse images of their ordinary interiors,
an ordinary neighborhood of the chosen point. Its dimension is at most
$d$, since it is an analytic domain of $X$.
Apply Theorem~\ref{v038:degree:compactmomentcollar} to $Z$
and the unit tuple $p^*f$. It gives the set $E$ and a finite
map $X_P\to\mathbb T_P$. The intermediate domain $V_P$
is affinoid by the actual simultaneous Laurent inequalities
of Theorem~\ref{fnd:generalizedrationaluniversal}.
Write their algebras as
\[
             R_P=\mathcal O(\mathbb T_P)\longrightarrow
             A_P=\mathcal O(V_P)\longrightarrow B_P=\mathcal O(X_P).
\]
The algebra $B_P$ is finite over $R_P$, and the same finite
generating family makes it finite over $A_P$.

Here its given norm is the canonical finite-$A_P$-module norm.
Indeed, using Theorem~\ref{fnd:generalizedfinitemodules},
choose $R_P$-module generators $b_1,\ldots,b_m$ and
their controlled lifts for the canonical $R_P$-module norm.
They are also $A_P$-module generators. Boundedness of
$R_P\to A_P$ makes the quotient $A_P$-module norm bounded
above by a constant times the given norm of $B_P$.
Conversely, the bounded analytic map $A_P\to B_P$ and the
finitely many $b_i$ bound the given norm by a constant times
that quotient norm. The two norms are therefore equivalent.
This proves norm-admissible finiteness over $V_P$.

Every point of $V$ of weight $w\notin E$ lies in the ordinary
interior of $V_P$, because the defining moment inequalities
are strict there. Its full fibre is consequently finite.
Lemma~\ref{v038:degree:wholefinitefibre} identifies the resulting
ordinary finite locus. A positive-dimensional analytic fibre
cannot be the finite fibre of a finite morphism: its affinoid
pieces would be finite algebras over the completed target
residue field and hence have analytic dimension zero, by
finite residue extensions and the Abhyankar maximum formula.
Finally $E$ has dimension at most $d-1$, so it cannot contain
a relatively open subset of a $d$-cell. This proves the density
assertion exactly on those cells.
\end{proof}
