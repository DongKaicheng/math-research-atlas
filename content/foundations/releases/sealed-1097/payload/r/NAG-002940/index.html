\begin{lemma}[The interchange defect]
\label{f9mi:interchangedefect}
Let $i\ne j$, let $J$ index the other $r-1$ entries, and put
\[
 R_i=H_{\cH_iZ}(j,J)+\cV_Z(i;j,J),\qquad
 R_j=H_{\cH_jZ}(i,J)+\cV_Z(j;i,J).
\]
Under the preceding explicit operation and lower-dimensional recursion
hypotheses, their difference is exactly
\begin{align}\label{eq:ex68:f9mi:defect}
 R_i-R_j={}&H_{\cH_j\cH_iZ-\cH_i\cH_jZ}(J)\\
 &+\cV_{\cH_iZ}(j;J)-\cV_{\cH_jZ}(i;J)\notag\\
 &+\cV_Z(i;j,J)-\cV_Z(j;i,J).\notag
\end{align}
Consequently equality of the horizontal codimension-two cycles and the
vertical interchange identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ex68:f9mi:verticalinterchange}
 \cV_{\cH_iZ}(j;J)+\cV_Z(i;j,J)
       =\cV_{\cH_jZ}(i;J)+\cV_Z(j;i,J)
\end{equation}
are sufficient for equality of the two first choices. Without that
horizontal-cycle equality, vanishing of the full right side of
\eqref{eq:ex68:f9mi:defect} is the exact necessary and sufficient condition.
If the appropriate identities hold at every node, and the curve/point
base cases are compatible, the recursion is independent of its order.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Apply the lower-dimensional recursion to $H_{\cH_iZ}(j,J)$ and to
$H_{\cH_jZ}(i,J)$. Their horizontal terms are respectively
$H_{\cH_j\cH_iZ}(J)$ and $H_{\cH_i\cH_jZ}(J)$, and their vertical
terms are the first two vertical expressions in \eqref{eq:ex68:f9mi:defect}.
Subtract, use cycle linearity, and retain the two original vertical
terms. This proves the exact formula and both stated criteria.
Induct on dimension for every possible first choice, using the criteria
to compare any two first choices and the induction hypothesis for later
choices. The separately assumed curve and point cases start this
induction. No equality of Chow classes is substituted for a required
cycle or functional identity.
\end{proof}
