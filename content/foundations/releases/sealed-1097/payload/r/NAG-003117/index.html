The missing component-degree projection identity

I Descent of the polarized mixed tower

Open in the English reader · PDF page 2144

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{theorem}[The missing component-degree projection identity]
\label{f9sc:degree}
Let $q:W\to V$ be a proper morphism of proper integral $k$-schemes,
with $\dim W=d$ and $\dim V\leq d$, and let $M_1,\ldots,M_d$
be invertible sheaves on $V$. If $\dim q(W)<d$, then
\[
                  \delta(W;q^*M_1,\ldots,q^*M_d)=0.
\]
If $q$ is dominant and $\dim V=d$, then
\begin{equation}\label{ex68:support:eq:degprojection}
 \delta(W;q^*M_1,\ldots,q^*M_d)
       =[k(W):k(V)]\delta(V;M_1,\ldots,M_d).
\end{equation}
In particular, let $p:\cY\to\cX$ be a supplied proper map of
integral normal proper flat DVR models, inducing the identity on the
generic fibre, and an isomorphism over an open containing every
height-one point of $\cX$. For arbitrary model lines $\cL_i$, each
strict-transform special component retains its mixed degree and each
exceptional special component has mixed degree zero.
The finite model measures with masses
$b_V\delta_V/\prod_i e_i$, where $b_V=\ord_V(\pi)$, are consequently
equal on every continuous test and every Borel test finite on their
finite support. The conclusion concerns these supplied pullback models.
\end{theorem}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Use the global pairs $(M_i,0)$ on $V$. Their Gysin operations are
exactly $c_1(M_i)$, since their zero schemes are all of $V$.
Iterating \eqref{eq:f9sc:projection} gives
\[
 q_*\bigl(c_1(q^*M_1)\cdots c_1(q^*M_d)\cap[W]\bigr)
       =c_1(M_1)\cdots c_1(M_d)\cap q_*[W].
\]
In the contracted case $q_*[W]=0$. In the dominant equal-dimensional
case it is $[k(W):k(V)][V]$, with the full field degree. Proper
pushforward preserves degree over $k$, proving both formulas.

For the models, the supplied open identifies the ordinary DVRs at a
special component and its unique strict transform. The proper map between
these integral special components has degree one. An exceptional
component has image dimension less than $d$, so the two formulas give
respectively the old mixed degree and zero. The identified DVRs have
the same $b_V$ and the same valuation point on the common generic
analytic fibre. The additional exceptional terms have zero weight.
Substitution in the specified finite atomic measure proves equality,
and hence equality for all the stated tests. Neither existence of the
map nor an analytic integral comparison enters this argument.
\end{proof}

Standing context 1 · exact TeX
For a proper $d$-dimensional integral $k$-scheme $W$ and invertible
sheaves $M_1,\ldots,M_d$, use the mixed degree
\[
 \delta(W;M_1,\ldots,M_d)
       =\deg_k\bigl(c_1(M_1)\cdots c_1(M_d)\cap[W]\bigr).
\]
By \st{0BFI} this agrees with the usual numerical mixed intersection,
with no extra factorial. No ampleness or nefness is required in this
mixed formula.
