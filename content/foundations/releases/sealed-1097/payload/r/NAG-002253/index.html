Skeletal valuations of rational functions

5 Tropicalization, multiplicities and balancing

Open in the English reader · PDF page 934

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{proposition}[Skeletal valuations of rational functions]
\label{v035:faithfulrationalpl}
Every point of $S$ is a norm on $K(X)$. For every
$f\in K(X)^\times$, the function $v(f)|_S$ is continuous
and finite piecewise $\Gamma$-integral-affine. Moreover the
set of specializations of points of $S$ is finite and is
the set of generic residual-stratum points occurring in
the completed charts.
\end{proposition}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Consider a chart meeting $S$, and write
$C_K=C_\lambda\otimes_O K$. It is a nonzero domain because
$\mathcal U_{\lambda,K}$ is a nonempty affine open of
the integral scheme $X$. The algebraic generic chart base
\[
 R=K[X_0,\ldots,X_r,T]/(X_0\cdots X_r-a)
\]
is a domain. The map $R\to C_K$ is \etale, hence flat
by \cite[Tag 00U2(3),(6)]{StacksAlg}. A nonzero flat algebra
over a domain is torsion free, so this map is injective.
For $F_0=\operatorname{Frac}(R)$, the algebra
$C_K\otimes_R F_0$ is a nonzero domain and an affine
\etale algebra over $F_0$. By \cite[Tag 02GL]{StacksAlg}
it is a finite separable field extension. Since it
contains $C_K$ and is contained in $\operatorname{Frac}(C_K)$,
it equals $\operatorname{Frac}(C_K)=K(X)$.

For a point $x$ of the chart skeleton, evaluation on
$R$ is the standard Gauss norm. It is nonzero on every
nonzero element of $R$. Thus the map
$C_K\to\mathcal H(x)$ extends to
$C_K\otimes_R F_0=K(X)$. This is a unital map from a
field, hence injective. The absolute value on
$\mathcal H(x)$ gives the asserted norm on $K(X)$.
All points of $S$ belong to one of these charts.

On each chart write $f=g/h$ with nonzero $g,h\in C_K$.
Their images are analytic on its completed generic fibre
and are nonzero at every skeletal point by the preceding
argument. Proposition~\ref{v035:faithfulanalyticpl} applies
to both. Their valuation difference is continuous and
has finitely many integral-affine formulas there. The
finite cover gives the assertion globally, with the
formulas agreeing on common faces by evaluation of the
same rational function.

By Proposition~\ref{cand:actualskeletalfibrestrata}, a
point of a chart skeleton over
$w\in\operatorname{relint}\Delta_I$ specializes to its
label $\eta\in Q_I$. Conversely every such label has
its nonempty relative open simplex. Each $Q_I$ is finite,
there are finitely many $I$ and charts, and the formal
specializations agree on overlaps. This proves the last
assertion.
\end{proof}
Standing context 1 · exact TeX
Assume that $\mathcal X_s$ has a finite affine open cover
$\mathcal U_\lambda=\operatorname{Spec}C_\lambda$, where
each $\mathcal U_\lambda$ admits an \etale $O$-morphism to
\[
 \operatorname{Spec}
 O[X_0,\ldots,X_{r_\lambda},T_1,\ldots,T_{q_\lambda}]/
       (X_0\cdots X_{r_\lambda}-a_\lambda),
 \qquad 0<|a_\lambda|<1.
\]
Here an affine open cover of $\mathcal X_s$ means affine
opens of $\mathcal X$ whose special fibres cover
$\mathcal X_s$. Complete along any $\pi\in O$ with
$0<|\pi|<1$. Lemma~\ref{cand:affineflatformalcompletion}
and Theorem~\ref{cand:projectiveformalcompletion} identify
the completion with an admissible model of $X^{\an}$.
The completed charts are formally \etale and form a finite
formal-open cover: a square-zero lifting problem modulo
$\pi^m$ is the algebraic \etale lifting problem, and its
compatible solutions give the completed lifting property.
Consequently Theorem~\ref{cand:globalintegralaffineskeleton}
gives its compact finite integral-affine skeleton $S$.
