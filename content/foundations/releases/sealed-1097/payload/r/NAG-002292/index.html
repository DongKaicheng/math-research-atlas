Dimension at maximal ideals of a strict affinoid

5 Tropicalization, multiplicities and balancing

Open in the English reader · PDF page 952

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{lemma}[Dimension at maximal ideals of a strict affinoid]
\label{v036:analytic:maximaldimension}
Let $B$ be a nonzero strict $K$-affinoid algebra and let
$\mathfrak m$ be a maximal ideal. Then
\[
 \dim B_{\mathfrak m}
   =\max_{\substack{\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Min}(B)\\
                    \mathfrak p\subseteq\mathfrak m}}
       \dim(B/\mathfrak p).
\]
In particular, if $B$ is pure of dimension $d$, then every maximal
localization has dimension $d$.
\end{lemma}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
First let $R=K\langle T_1,\ldots,T_e\rangle$ and let
$\mathfrak n$ be maximal. Theorem~\ref{fnd:strictpointunitcriterion}
makes $E=R/\mathfrak n$ a finite extension of $K$. Write $a_i$ for
the image of $T_i$; boundedness of the residue point gives
$|a_i|\leq1$. A finite $K$-basis of $E$, together with the norm
comparison of Lemma~\ref{fnd:finiteextensionnorms}, identifies
$E\langle T\rangle$ with the finite free $R$-module $R\otimes_KE$:
expanding coefficients in that basis preserves convergence in both
directions. This ring extension is integral by
\cite[Tag 00GK]{StacksAlg}.

In $E\langle T\rangle$, the coordinate ideals
\[
 (0)\subset(T_1-a_1)\subset\cdots\subset
                  (T_1-a_1,\ldots,T_e-a_e)
\]
form a strict chain of prime ideals. Translation by the $a_i$ is a
bounded automorphism, and each quotient is a Tate algebra in the
remaining variables. The last ideal contracts to $\mathfrak n$.
Incomparability for an integral extension,
\cite[Tag 00GT]{StacksAlg}, makes all contractions in this chain
distinct. Hence $\dim R_{\mathfrak n}\geq e$. The reverse inequality
follows from $\dim R=e$, proved in
Proposition~\ref{fnd:stricttatedimension}.

Now suppose that $B$ is integral, of dimension $e$. Choose a finite
injection $R=K\langle T_1,\ldots,T_e\rangle\hookrightarrow B$ by
Theorem~\ref{fnd:strictfinitenormalization} and
Proposition~\ref{fnd:stricttatedimension}. The contraction
$\mathfrak n=\mathfrak m\cap R$ is maximal: its quotient is a
$K$-subalgebra of the finite extension $B/\mathfrak m$, hence is a
finite-dimensional domain and a field by
\cite[Tag 00GS(1)]{StacksAlg}. The ring $R$ is normal by
Theorem~\ref{fnd:stricttatefactorial}. Going down for an integral
extension over a normal domain, \cite[Tag 00H8]{StacksAlg}, lifts a
chain of length $e$ ending at $\mathfrak n$ to a chain ending at the
specified $\mathfrak m$. Thus $\dim B_{\mathfrak m}=e$.

For general $B$, apply this result to $B/\mathfrak p$ for each
minimal prime contained in $\mathfrak m$. These are finitely many,
by \cite[Tag 00FR]{StacksAlg} and
Theorem~\ref{fnd:strictfinitemodules}. Their localized spectra cover
$\operatorname{Spec}(B_{\mathfrak m})$: any prime in a chain contains
a minimal prime of $B$, by \cite[Tag 00E0(4)]{StacksAlg}. A chain is
therefore a chain in one such quotient. Taking the maximal possible
length gives the displayed equality.
\end{proof}
