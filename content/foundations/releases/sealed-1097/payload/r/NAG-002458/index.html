The finite locus at maximal-dimensional analytic points

5 Tropicalization, multiplicities and balancing

Open in the English reader · PDF page 1065

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{theorem}[The finite locus at maximal-dimensional analytic points]
\label{v038:degree:properfinitelocus}
Let $p:X\to Y$ be a proper morphism of good Hausdorff analytic
spaces, with $\dim_K X\leq d$. If $y\in Y$ satisfies
$d_K(y)=d$, then its entire analytic fibre is finite, and
$p$ is finite over an ordinary neighbourhood of $y$.
If $Y$ is pure of analytic dimension $d$, the ordinary
finite-fibre locus is open and dense in $Y$.
\end{theorem}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
We first verify the zero-dimensional fact needed for the
actual fibre. A nonempty generalized affinoid space of
analytic dimension zero has a finite underlying set.
Indeed choose a strictifying complete extension by
Theorem~\ref{fnd:simultaneousstrictification}.
Theorem~\ref{fnd:analyticdimensionindependence} makes the
strict algebra after this extension zero-dimensional.
Finite normalization, Theorem~\ref{fnd:strictfinitenormalization},
and Proposition~\ref{fnd:stricttatedimension} make it finite
over the new ground field itself. Its actual strict norm is
the canonical finite-module norm by
Proposition~\ref{fnd:strictfinitealgebras}. Its spectrum is
finite by Lemma~\ref{fnd:finiteanalyticbranchseparation}.
The scalar projection onto the original spectrum is
surjective by Theorem~\ref{fnd:scalarextensionspectralsurjective},
so the original spectrum is finite too. This argument concerns
analytic dimension, with Krull dimension used only after
strictification.

Put $H=\mathcal H(y)$. To see that the actual analytic fibre $X_y$ is good, choose
at each $z$ above $y$ ordinary affinoid neighborhoods of $y$
and $z$, the latter mapping into the former. The completed-residue
fibre of the resulting affinoid map is affinoid by
Theorem~\ref{fnd:allpointaffinoidfibres}. It contains the
intersection with the ordinary neighborhood of $z$ and thus is an
ordinary affinoid neighborhood in the whole fibre.
Its underlying topology is that of $p^{-1}(y)$, and the
completed residue field at a point $z$ is the original
$\mathcal H_X(z)$ with its structural isometric inclusion
$H\hookrightarrow\mathcal H_X(z)$. These assertions follow
on each affinoid chart from
Theorem~\ref{fnd:allpointaffinoidfibres}, and agree under the
domain restrictions of Theorem~\ref{fnd:generalanalyticproducts}.
The fibre is compact Hausdorff: it is closed in the compact
inverse image of any affinoid neighbourhood of $y$.

For every point $z$ in this fibre, the valued-field tower
formula, Lemma~\ref{fnd:valuedfielddimensioncalculus}, gives
\[
 d_H(z)=d_K(z)-d_K(y)\leq\dim_K X-d\leq0.
\]
Here the point-dimension bound is
Theorem~\ref{fnd:generalanalyticabhyankar}, and $d_H(z)$ is
nonnegative. It is consequently zero. The maximum formula
of Theorem~\ref{fnd:affinoidabhyankarmaximum} makes every
nonempty affinoid chart of $X_y$ zero-dimensional over $H$.
Compactness supplies finitely many such charts, each with a
finite underlying set by the first paragraph. Thus the
entire fibre is finite. Properness supplies relative
interiority of all its points, so
Lemma~\ref{v038:degree:wholefinitefibre} gives the asserted
ordinary finite neighbourhood, including the empty-fibre case.

If $Y$ is pure of dimension $d$, every nonempty ordinary
open subset contains a nonempty affinoid neighbourhood
$V$ of dimension $d$, by goodness and
Lemma~\ref{v037:integration:puritylocality}.
The Abhyankar maximum formula supplies a point of $V$
with $d_K(y)=d$. Corollary~\ref{fnd:domainresiduefields}
leaves its completed residue field unchanged under domain
restriction, so it is a point of the finite
locus just proved. That locus therefore meets every
nonempty ordinary open subset. Its openness is
Lemma~\ref{v038:degree:wholefinitefibre}, proving density.
\end{proof}
