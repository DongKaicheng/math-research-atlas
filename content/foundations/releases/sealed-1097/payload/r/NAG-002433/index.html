A contractive normalized trace

4 Analytic curves and skeleta

Open in the English reader · PDF page 811

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{lemma}[A contractive normalized trace]
\label{v038:f5:contractivetrace}
Let $L/H$ be a finite extension of degree $n$, with the extending
absolute value. Then
\[
                 |\operatorname{Tr}_{L/H}(x)|\leq |x|
                       \qquad(x\in L).
\]
If $|n|=1$, the map
\[
              P=n^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}_{L/H}:L\longrightarrow H
\]
is an $H$-linear retraction of norm at most one. In particular, a
finite immediate extension of degree $n$ with $|n|=1$ is trivial.
\end{lemma}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Fix $x\in L$, and let
$Q(T)=T^d+a_{d-1}T^{d-1}+\cdots+a_0$ be its minimal polynomial
over $H$. Choose an algebraic closure of $L$ by \cite[Tag 09GT]{StacksAlg}
and let $M/H$ be the finite extension generated by the roots of $Q$.
Give $M$ its extending absolute value using
Lemma~\ref{fnd:finiteextensionnorms}. Every root $x_i$ of $Q$,
counted with its multiplicity, has absolute value $|x|$:
the $H$-isomorphism $H(x)\to H(x_i)$ is isometric by uniqueness
of the extending absolute value. Thus
\[
                    |a_{d-1}|=\left|\sum_{i=1}^d x_i\right|
                                  \leq |x|.
\]
Choose an $H(x)$-basis $e_1,\ldots,e_m$ of $L$.
In the $H$-basis $x^je_i$, $0\leq j<d$, multiplication by $x$
has $m$ companion-matrix blocks, each of trace $-a_{d-1}$.
Consequently
\[
                  \operatorname{Tr}_{L/H}(x)=-m a_{d-1}.
\]
The ultrametric inequality gives $|m|\leq1$, proving the bound.
For $h\in H$, its multiplication matrix is $h$ times the identity,
so $\operatorname{Tr}_{L/H}(h)=nh$. This proves the assertion
about $P$ when $|n|=1$.

Suppose now that $L/H$ is immediate and nontrivial. The retraction
$P$ has a nonzero kernel; take $0\ne x\in\ker P$. Equality of
value groups supplies $c\in H^\times$ with $|c|=|x|$.
Since the residue fields agree, choose $b\in H^\circ$ with the
same residue as $x/c$. Then $a=cb$ satisfies
\[
                         |x-a|<|x|=|a|.
\]
On the other hand, $P(x-a)=-a$, so contractivity gives
$|a|\leq|x-a|$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}
