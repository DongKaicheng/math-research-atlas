Fixed special points and generic finite orbits

Proper direct image on historical ordinary compact tests

Open in the English reader · PDF page 1895

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{lemma}[Fixed special points and generic finite orbits]
\label{cand:f5glue:invariantaffines}
Let \(Y'\) be a separated \(O'\)-scheme, locally of finite
presentation, with a semilinear \(G\)-action.
Suppose that every point of \(Y'_k\) is fixed by \(G\).
Assume also that \(Y'_L\) is of finite type over \(L\) and
has dimension at most one.
Then \(Y'\) has a cover by \(G\)-invariant affine open subschemes.
If \(Y'\) is quasi-compact, the cover can be chosen finite.

Alternatively, properness of \(Y'\to\operatorname{Spec}O'\)
may replace the hypothesis on the generic fibre.
Every finite intersection of the affine opens just obtained
is affine, invariant, and of finite presentation over \(O'\).
\end{lemma}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Choose \(0\ne\pi\in\mathfrak m\).
Rank one gives \(O'[1/\pi]=L\) and
\(\sqrt{\pi O'}=\mathfrak m'\): any positive real valuation
eventually dominates any other after multiplication by a
positive integer. Hence the generic fibre is the open
\(D(\pi)\subset Y'\), and its complement is the underlying
special fibre.

Let \(x\in Y'_k\), and choose an affine open \(V\subset Y'\)
containing \(x\). Every translate \(gV\) contains \(x\), because
the point is fixed. The intersection
\[
                         U_x=\bigcap_{g\in G}gV
 \tag{2}
\]
is invariant and still contains \(x\). It is affine by
\stacks{01KP}(1)(a), since \(Y'\) is separated over the affine
base \(\operatorname{Spec}O'\).

For \(x\in Y'_L\), its orbit is a finite subset of the
separated finite-type \(L\)-scheme \(Y'_L\).
By \stacks{0A25}, this scheme is a locally closed subscheme
of some projective space over \(L\). Therefore \stacks{01ZY}
places the entire finite orbit in an affine open
\(V\subset Y'_L\). This is an affine open of \(Y'\), since
\(Y'_L\) is open. Formula (2) again gives an invariant affine
neighborhood of \(x\). This covers every point of \(Y'\).
Quasi-compactness supplies a finite subcover when required.

For the alternative proper case, let \(U\) be the union of
the invariant affine neighborhoods constructed at special-fibre
points. The closed complement \(Z=Y'\setminus U\), with its
reduced closed subscheme structure, is proper over \(O'\).
Its image is closed and misses the closed point of
\(\operatorname{Spec}O'\). Every nonempty closed subset of a
local spectrum contains its closed point, so \(Z\) is empty.
A finite subcover again exists by properness.

Finally, finite intersections remain invariant and affine
by \stacks{01KP}. Their structural morphisms are locally of
finite presentation, and a morphism between affine schemes
is quasi-compact and quasi-separated. They are therefore
of finite presentation.
\end{proof}
