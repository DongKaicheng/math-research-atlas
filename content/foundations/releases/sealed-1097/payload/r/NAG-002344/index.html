Discrete original-field vertex recognition

4 Analytic curves and skeleta

Open in the English reader · PDF page 807

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{theorem}[Discrete original-field vertex recognition]
\label{v037:f5:discretenumericalvertices}
Suppose $K$ is complete and discretely valued, with its specified
nontrivial absolute value. Let $C/K$ be a smooth projective
geometrically connected curve. Let $v\in C^{\an}$ satisfy
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &|\mathcal H(v)^\times|=|K^\times|,\\
 &\widetilde{\mathcal H(v)}/k
   \text{ is separably generated of transcendence degree one}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Then $v$ is the unique component point of an actual integral
\textup{\'etale} Gauss chart in $C^{\an}$, and has an
unramified branch certificate.
If $C$ admits one semistable algebraic model over the original
$O$, these two displayed conditions are necessary and sufficient
for $v$ to occur as a component vertex of such a model.
\end{theorem}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Put $L=\mathcal H(v)$ and $l=\widetilde L$.
The scheme image of $v$ is the generic point of $C$.
Indeed, a nongeneric point on an integral curve is closed;
its residue field is finite over $K$ by
\cite[Tag 00FV]{StacksAlg}. Its analytic completed residue
field is that same finite complete field by
Proposition~\ref{fnd:analytificationvaluedfibres} and
Lemma~\ref{fnd:finiteextensionnorms}, whose residual field
is algebraic over $k$ by
Lemma~\ref{fnd:valuedfielddimensioncalculus}.
This contradicts the assumed residual transcendence degree.
Thus $F=K(C)$ embeds densely in $L$, by the same
analytification-valued-fibre proposition.

Choose $\bar t\in l$ as a separating transcendence basis,
so that $l/k(\bar t)$ is algebraic separable.
Density supplies $t_0\in F$ of norm one with residue
$\bar t$. Choose a rational smooth coordinate
$s\in F$ from a smooth affine chart at the generic point,
using \cite[Tag 054L]{StacksAlg}.
In its standard smooth Jacobian presentation, solving the
invertible square subsystem expresses every differential
in terms of $ds$; consequently $\Omega_{F/K}$ has basis
$ds$. For a uniformizer $\varpi$ of $K$, choose a sufficiently
large integer $N$ and set
\[
                            t=t_0+\varpi^Ns.
\]
Then $|\varpi^Ns(v)|<1$, so the residue of $t$ is still
$\bar t$. We can require $dt\ne0$: in the one-dimensional
space $\Omega_{F/K}$, at most one scalar among the distinct
$\varpi^N$ makes $dt_0+\varpi^Nds$ vanish.

The rational function $t$ is transcendental over $K$.
The finitely generated field $F/K$ has transcendence degree
one, so it is a finite extension of $K(t)$: its finite
field generators are algebraic over $K(t)$.
The quotient of $\Omega_{F/K}$ obtained by imposing $dt=0$
is $\Omega_{F/K(t)}$, as follows directly from the universal
property of derivations. It is zero.
On an affine open where $t$ is regular, the map to the
affine line is of finite presentation; this follows from
\cite[Tag 00FO]{StacksAlg} and a finite polynomial presentation.
At its generic point the local map is the field extension
$K(t)\to F$, which is flat by \cite[Tag 00HD]{StacksAlg}
because the only ideals of the base field are zero and itself.
The criterion \cite[Tag 02GU]{StacksAlg} therefore makes
this map \textup{\'etale} at the generic point.
This algebraic open analytifies to an ordinary open by
Proposition~\ref{fnd:analytificationzariskiopens}.
Theorem~\ref{fnd:analytificationetalepreservation} gives an
actual analytic \textup{\'etale} map near $v$.

For a polynomial $Q\in K[T]$, division by a coefficient
of maximal size and reduction at $v$ give
\[
                             |Q(t)|=\max_i|Q_i|,
\]
since $\bar t$ is transcendental over $k$.
Thus $v$ maps to the unit Gauss point, and completion
embeds the Gauss field $H$ isometrically into $L$.
The image of $F\otimes_{K(t)}H\to L$ is a finite-dimensional
$H$-subalgebra of the field $L$, containing $F$.
It is a field: multiplication by a nonzero element is
injective on this finite-dimensional space and hence
surjective. It is complete and therefore closed by
Lemma~\ref{fnd:finiteextensionnorms}.
Since $F$ is dense in $L$, this image is all of $L$.
In particular $L/H$ is finite.

The Gauss-field computation in
Proposition~\ref{v037:f5:integralgaussdegree} makes $H$
discretely valued with value group $|K^\times|$ and residue
field $k(\bar t)$.
The assumed value-group equality and
Lemma~\ref{v037:f5:discreteunramifiedbasis} give
\[
                             [L:H]=[l:k(\bar t)].
\]
In particular the right side is finite; by the choice of
$\bar t$, this residual extension is separable.
Proposition~\ref{v037:f5:gaussgermintegralchart} constructs
the actual integral chart in $C^{\an}$.
Theorem~\ref{v037:f5:gausschartcertificate} supplies its
unramified branch certificate.

When one original-field semistable model exists, realization
follows from Theorem~\ref{v037:f5:vertexcertificate}.
Conversely, every component vertex of such a model has
exactly the stated value group and separably generated
residual function field by
Lemma~\ref{v036:f5:vertexarithmetic}.
The field, its absolute value and the uniformizer used in
the proof have not been replaced by an extension or rescaling.
\end{proof}
