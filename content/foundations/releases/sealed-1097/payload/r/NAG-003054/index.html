Common domination and the uniformizer test

Proper direct image on historical ordinary compact tests

Open in the English reader · PDF page 1924

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{proposition}[Common domination and the uniformizer test]
\label{f9md:supportedprojection}
Assume the four interface properties for all cycles and operations below.
Let $p:\cY\to\cX$ be a proper common-generic-fibre domination with
$p_*[\cY]=[\cX]$. Let $D_i$ be model pseudo-divisors with lines
extending $L_i^{\otimes e_i}$ and specified sections $s_i^{\otimes e_i}$,
$0\leq i\leq d$. Suppose only that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:f9md:genericsupport}
                    \bigcap_{i=0}^d |D_i|\cap X=\varnothing.
\end{equation}
No pairwise or intermediate proper intersection is required. With
$E_0=\prod_{i=0}^d e_i$, define the supported model value by
\[
 J_{\cX}(D_0,\ldots,D_d)
     =\frac{\lambda}{E_0}\deg_k(D_0\cdots D_d\cdot[\cX]).
\]
Then it is symmetric and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:f9md:supportedpush}
                  J_{\cY}(p^*D_0,\ldots,p^*D_d)
                            =J_{\cX}(D_0,\ldots,D_d).
\end{equation}
Hence two model presentations have equal values if their divisor data
pull back compatibly to a common domination satisfying the same
properties. This requires the actual pulled-back lines, sections and
supports, not just their generic line-bundle classes.

Replacing $s_i$ by $\pi s_i$ at a fixed metric gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:f9md:piregression}
 \Delta J=+\frac{\lambda}{\prod_{j\ne i}e_j}
              \sum_V b_V\deg_{(\cL_j|_V)_{j\ne i},k}(V).
\end{equation}
This equals $+\lambda$ times the corresponding model-measure mass.
It is $+\lambda$ times the generic degree only when the relevant
specialization-of-degree identity has also been established.
\end{proposition}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
The support after all $d+1$ operations lies in $\bigcap_i|D_i|$.
By \eqref{eq:f9md:genericsupport} this is vertical, so the degree in the
definition is available, even when the total support has positive
dimension. The operations produce a zero-dimensional class there.
Their commutativity proves symmetry.
Iterating the second property gives
\[
 p_*\bigl(p^*D_0\cdots p^*D_d\cdot[\cY]\bigr)
            =D_0\cdots D_d\cdot p_*[\cY]
            =D_0\cdots D_d\cdot[\cX].
\]
The retained vertical degree and identical root exponents prove
\eqref{eq:f9md:supportedpush}. Applying this equality to each side of a
common domination proves the next assertion. This proof does not
manufacture the supported operators or their projection formula.

The model divisor changes by $e_i\divi(\pi)$ when $s_i$ is
multiplied by $\pi$. Additivity, commutativity and the third property
turn the change in the final class into
\[
 e_i\sum_V b_V\left(\prod_{j\ne i}c_1(\cL_j|_V)\right)[V].
\]
Taking its degree and dividing by $E_0/\lambda$ proves
\eqref{eq:f9md:piregression}. The generic support condition is unchanged;
the model support is enlarged by the special fibre when needed.
On a model with only the standard projective component and degrees
$m_j$, this gives $+\lambda\prod_{j\ne i}m_j$, as required by the
point normalization. The blowup computation gives the same mass for
pulled-back data, so it is also a common-model regression test.
\end{proof}
Standing context 1 · exact TeX
Let $K$ be complete and nontrivially discretely valued, with valuation
ring $R$, residue field $k$, uniformizer $\pi$, and the fixed number
$\lambda=-\log|\pi|>0$. Let $X$ be an integral proper $K$-variety of
dimension $d\geq2$. A model in this note is proper, flat, integral and
normal over $R$, of finite type, with generic fibre identified with $X$.
Retain explicitly the ordinary DVR local rings at all height-one points
used below. Its special components have dimension $d$.
Standing context 2 · exact TeX
For a proper model $\cT$ and closed support $S$, suppose there are
groups $C_r(\cT;S)$ of $r$-dimensional cycles modulo equivalences
\emph{retaining that support}. Suppose model pseudo-divisors $D$ act by
operators $D\cdot:C_r(\cT;S)\to C_{r-1}(\cT;S\cap|D|)$.
For the objects used, require the following four properties:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The operators are additive and commute; the first operation on an
integral model is its Cartier divisor cycle when that is defined.
\item Proper pushforward retains the prescribed supports and satisfies
$p_*(p^*D\cdot\alpha)=D\cdot p_*\alpha$.
\item On vertical cycles, $D$ acts by the ordinary first Chern operation
of its model line, even if its chosen section restricts identically to
zero. Moreover $\divi(\pi)\cdot[\cT]=\sum_Vb_V[V]$.
\item Vertical zero-cycles have degree over $k$, with actual local
lengths and full residue-field degrees, and this degree is preserved by
proper pushforward.
\end{enumerate}
These are interface hypotheses. In particular, the second property is
the supported algebraic projection formula, not F6's analytic theorem.
