Finite polyhedral upper bounds without an interior hypothesis

5 Tropicalization, multiplicities and balancing

Open in the English reader · PDF page 982

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{theorem}[Finite polyhedral upper bounds without an interior hypothesis]
\label{v037:globaltrop:upperbound}
Let $X=\mathcal M(A)$ be a nonempty generalized affinoid
space of analytic dimension $d$, and let analytic units
$f_1,\ldots,f_n$ define $t:X\to\mathbb R^n$.
For every $w\in t(X)$ there are a finite rational polyhedral
fan $C$ of dimension at most $d$ and a neighbourhood $V$ of
$w$ such that
\[
                         t(X)\cap V\subset(w+|C|)\cap V.
\]
Consequently $t(X)$ is contained in the support of a finite
integral affine polyhedral complex of dimension at most $d$.
\end{theorem}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Choose a complete isometric extension $L/K$ containing
elements with sizes equal to every radius in a fixed
presentation of $A$, and elements $c_i$ with
$|c_i|=e^{-w_i}$; require also that $L$ be nontrivially valued.
Such a field is explicit: take the completion of a rational
function field with weighted Gauss norms assigning these
finitely many positive radii to its variables, and add a
variable of size strictly between zero and one.
The Gauss construction is
Proposition~\ref{fnd:gaussnorm}; its valued completion is
the one in Lemma~\ref{fnd:gaussvaluedfielddimension}.
The actual scalar presentation
\ref{fnd:generalscalarpresentation}, followed by rescaling
the finitely many presentation variables, makes $X_L$ strict.
Its dimension is $d$ by
Theorem~\ref{fnd:analyticdimensionindependence}.

Put $h_i=f_i/c_i$. Scalar spectral surjectivity
\ref{fnd:scalarextensionspectralsurjective} gives
\[
                         t_h(X_L)=t(X)-w.
\]
It suffices to prove the upper bound at zero for this
actual strict space. Its whole fibre $F=t_h^{-1}(0)$ is
a nonempty strict affinoid domain by
Theorem~\ref{fnd:generalizedrationaluniversal}.
Let $D$ be its algebra. It has an admissible integral model
$D_0$ by Proposition~\ref{fnd:affineformalpresentations}.
The elements $h_i^{\pm1}$ are power-bounded in $D$, since
their values on $F$ are one and
Theorem~\ref{fnd:generalizedpointwisepowerbound} applies.
Adjoin them to $D_0$ by
Theorem~\ref{fnd:powerboundedmodelblowup}. The resulting
algebra is still an actual admissible model of the same
$D$, and contains all these elements. Use this enlarged
model in what follows.

Write $k=\widetilde L$, $\mathfrak m=L^{\circ\circ}$, and
\[
 J=\ker\bigl(k[T_1^{\pm1},\ldots,T_n^{\pm1}]
                         \longrightarrow D_0/\mathfrak mD_0\bigr),
 \qquad R=k[T^{\pm1}]/J.
\]
Thus $R$ injects into the actual special-fibre algebra.
For every minimal prime $\mathfrak p$ of $R$, localization
preserves this injection, by \cite[Tag 00HT]{StacksAlg}.
The algebra
$(D_0/\mathfrak mD_0)\otimes_RR_{\mathfrak p}$ is nonzero,
so it has a prime by \cite[Tag 00E0(1)]{StacksAlg}.
Its contraction is the unique prime of $R_{\mathfrak p}$.
It therefore gives a prime $\mathfrak q$ of the special
fibre lying above $\mathfrak p$; no finiteness of the
injection $R\to D_0/\mathfrak mD_0$ is required here.
Actual specialization surjectivity,
Theorem~\ref{fnd:affineformalspecializationsurjective}, supplies
$x\in F$ specializing to $\mathfrak q$. The induced
injections give
\[
                    \operatorname{Frac}(R/\mathfrak p)
                         \hookrightarrow\widetilde{\mathcal H(x)}.
\]
By \cite[Tag 00P0]{StacksAlg} and
Theorem~\ref{fnd:affinoidabhyankarmaximum},
\[
 \dim(R/\mathfrak p)
   =\operatorname{trdeg}_k\operatorname{Frac}(R/\mathfrak p)
   \leq d_L(x)\leq d.
\]
The residue field here is the same as for $x\in X_L$, by
Corollary~\ref{fnd:domainresiduefields}.

The finite-type $k$-algebra $R$ is Noetherian by
\cite[Tag 00FO]{StacksAlg}, and has finitely many minimal primes, by
\cite[Tag 00FR]{StacksAlg}. Its tropical support is the
union of the supports of the corresponding integral
quotients: every analytic prime kernel contains a minimal
prime by \cite[Tag 00E0(4)]{StacksAlg}.
Theorem~\ref{cand:tropicalpuredimension} bounds the dimension
of each of these algebraic tropicalizations by $d$.
Lemma~\ref{v037:analytic:finitetropicalbasis} gives a finite
rational fan $C$ with support $\operatorname{Trop}(\operatorname{Spec}R)$
and finitely many nonzero $p_j\in J$ cutting out that
support tropically. Its dimension is therefore at most $d$.

Lift every nonzero coefficient of each $p_j$ to an element
of $L^\circ$ of absolute value one, obtaining a Laurent
polynomial $P_j$. Since $p_j\in J$,
$P_j(h)\in\mathfrak mD_0$. Express this membership as a
finite sum $\sum_\ell b_{j\ell}d_{j\ell}$ with
$|b_{j\ell}|<1$ and $d_{j\ell}\in D_0$.
Every $d_{j\ell}$ has absolute value at most one on $F$,
by the actual model presentation. Hence there is a real
$c_j<1$ with $|P_j(h)|<c_j$ throughout $F$.
The compact subset of $X_L$ where $|P_j(h)|\geq c_j$ has
compact moment image missing zero. Avoiding these finitely
many images gives a neighbourhood $V_0$ of zero where
all these strict inequalities hold on the entire inverse
image. This uses the compact spectrum of
Proposition~\ref{fnd:boundedspectrumcompact}.

Shrink $V_0$ further so that, for every monomial of every
$P_j$, its norm at a weight $v\in V_0$ is greater than
$c_j$. At zero all these finitely many norms equal one,
so continuity permits the simultaneous choice. If an
actual moment value $v\in V_0$ lay outside $|C|$, some
$p_j$ would have a unique least-weight term there.
The same term of $P_j(h)$ would have strictly largest
absolute value at any point over $v$, and that value
would exceed $c_j$. The non-Archimedean triangle equality
would give $|P_j(h)|>c_j$, a contradiction.
Thus $t_h(X_L)\cap V_0\subset |C|$. Translating back proves
the local assertion over the original field and norm.

Finally, at every $w\in t(X)$ choose a closed rectangular
box centred at $w$ inside its upper-bound neighbourhood.
Finitely many box interiors cover the compact set $t(X)$.
The union of the intersections of these boxes with the
corresponding translated finite fans contains $t(X)$.
It is a finite union of integral affine polytopes, all
of dimension at most $d$. Cutting by their finitely many
defining hyperplanes gives a finite common complex, as
in Lemma~\ref{cand:polyhedralrefinementproduct}.
This proves the final assertion.
\end{proof}
