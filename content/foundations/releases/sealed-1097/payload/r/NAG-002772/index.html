Statement · exact TeX
\begin{theorem}[Cartier coefficient transport with scoped gluing; F8ST-C-R1]
\label{fnd:nag002772:cartier}
Let \(A\) be generalized affinoid and \(f\in A\) a regular element.
Put \(B=A/(f)\), with its actual quotient analytic structure. For a
component \(Y=\cM(A/\mathfrak p)\) of the Cartier zero space,
\(\mathfrak p\) minimal over \((f)\), its coefficient is
\begin{equation}\label{fnd:nag002772:effective-coefficient}
 c_Y(f)=\length_{A_{\mathfrak p}}(A_{\mathfrak p}/fA_{\mathfrak p})
       =\length_{\cO_{X,x}}(\cO_{X,x}/f\cO_{X,x}),
 \qquad X=\cM(A),
\end{equation}
where \(x\) can be chosen on \(Y\), away from the other zero-space
components, of maximal Abhyankar dimension in \(Y\). Equivalently,
the right side is the generic length of the analytic quotient sheaf.
The lengths are finite positive integers. The assertion applies in
particular to every codimension-one Cartier component. It makes no
claim about an arbitrary closed integral subspace of the zero locus.

The effective coefficients are compatible with actual affinoid-domain
restriction and ordinary gluing. For regular \(a,b\), product additivity
is asserted when \(\Min_A(a)\cup\Min_A(b)\) is an antichain: no two
distinct primes in this union contain one another. Here \(\Min_A(a)\)
means primes of the actual common ring \(A\) minimal over \((a)\),
with the empty set for a unit. A sufficient condition is that the two
actual zero spaces are empty or pure of one common analytic dimension.

For regular meromorphic presentations, the differences
\(\divis(a)-\divis(b)\) are independent and glue among atlases for
which this antichain condition holds for the cross-products on every
actual common affinoid refinement. In particular this holds when all
local numerator and denominator zero spaces are empty or pure of one
fixed analytic dimension. The effective coefficient formula above
needs no such purity assumption. No reduction of \(A\), \(B\), or their
finite fibres is permitted. The previously unrestricted product and
meromorphic clauses are not claimed closed in this induced revision.
\end{theorem}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
The prime \(\mathfrak p/(f)\) is minimal in \(B\), so
\(B_{\mathfrak p/(f)}=A_{\mathfrak p}/(f)\) is Artinian. Its length
as a module over itself is its length as an \(A_{\mathfrak p}\)-module.
Choose \(x\) on the integral component \(Y\) of maximal Abhyankar
dimension. It has kernel \(\mathfrak p/(f)\) on \(B\) and is on no
other component, by the dimension-drop argument in
Proposition~\ref{fnd:nag002769:generic-length}. Shrink to an affinoid ordinary
neighbourhood in the zero space avoiding the other components.
It is pure of dimension \(\dim_KY\) by the \hyperref[fnd:nag002772:mixed-neighbourhood-purity]{mixed-neighbourhood argument} above and domain purity
\tagg{NAG-002413}. The proof of Proposition~\ref{fnd:nag002769:generic-length} makes
its ordinary stalk at \(x\), hence \(\cO_{\cM(B),x}\), Artinian.
The regular comparison \tagg{NAG-001782} for \(B\), followed by
Lemma~\ref{lem:length}, gives its length as
\(\length B_{\mathfrak p/(f)}\).
The finite closed-immersion case of \tagg{NAG-001735} identifies
\[
 \cO_{\cM(B),x}=\cO_{X,x}\otimes_A B
                 =\cO_{X,x}/f\cO_{X,x}.
\]
Length over a local ring is unchanged when computed over a quotient
annihilating the module, proving \eqref{fnd:nag002772:effective-coefficient}. The same argument
on the pointed domain colimit proves the generic-length interpretation.

\phantomsection\label{fnd:nag002772:effective-domain-transport}
For a domain \(\cM(C)\subset\cM(B)\) and
\(\mathfrak q\in\Min C\), its contraction \(\mathfrak r\) is
minimal in \(B\) by flat going down (\href{https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00HS}{Stacks 00HS}). Choose an analytic point of
\(\cM(C)\) with kernel \(\mathfrak q\), using
\tagg{NAG-001860}. The upper arrow of \tagg{NAG-001791} gives a
regular local map \(B_{\mathfrak r}\to C_{\mathfrak q}\).
Both rings are Artinian, so their lengths agree. The same calculation
holds for any finite coherent module. Actual domain/quotient base
change, \tagg{NAG-001702}/\tagg{001846}, identifies this quotient
with the restriction of \(A/(f)\). Flatness preserves the injectivity
of multiplication by \(f\), so the restricted equation is still
regular. This proves compatibility on every actual domain and hence
on affinoid refinements of overlaps.

We first supply the missing component inclusion under the stated
antichain hypothesis. In any Noetherian ring, \(\Min_A(ab)\) is the
set of inclusion-minimal members of \(\Min_A(a)\cup\Min_A(b)\).
Indeed a prime containing \(ab\) contains a factor and therefore a
prime minimal over that factor by \href{https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00E0}{Stacks 00E0(4)}. A prime minimal over \(ab\) must
equal that smaller prime. Conversely, if \(p\) is inclusion-minimal
in the union and \(q\subset p\) contains \(ab\), choose by \href{https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00E0}{Stacks 00E0(4)} a factor-minimal
prime \(r\subset q\). Then \(r\subset q\subset p\) forces \(r=q=p\).
Thus, for an antichain, every factor component occurs in the product:
\[
             \Min_A(ab)=\Min_A(a)\cup\Min_A(b).
\]

The analytic purity criterion for this hypothesis uses only frozen
\tagg{NAG-002374}. If the factor zero spaces are empty or pure of
one common dimension \(d\), all primes in the union define integral
affinoids of dimension \(d\). A strict inclusion \(q\subsetneq p\)
would make the nonzero ideal \(p/q\) in \(A/q\) cut out
\(\cM(A/p)\) of dimension at most \(d-1\), or the empty space when
\(d=0\). This is impossible. Their union is therefore an antichain,
and the product zero space is also empty or pure of dimension \(d\).

For regular \(a,b\), multiplication by \(b\) gives the exact sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow A/(a)\xrightarrow{\,b\,} A/(ab)
       \longrightarrow A/(b)\longrightarrow0.
\]
At each prime in the now identified component union, each factor
quotient is either zero (a unit after localization) or Artinian.
A prime in the union containing a factor is minimal over that factor;
otherwise a smaller factor-minimal prime would violate the antichain
condition. Add localized finite lengths to get the coefficient
identity on every component, including all factor components.

For meromorphic representatives in the stated scope, equality
\(a/b=c/e\) gives \(ae=bc\) on an actual common affinoid restriction:
localization at regular elements is injective. The cross-product
antichain hypotheses hold there by assumption. In the common
pure-zero-space case they hold by the preceding dimension-drop
argument: quotient/domain base change identifies the restricted zero
spaces with actual domains in the original zero spaces, and
\tagg{NAG-002413} preserves their purity. Additivity yields
\(\divis(a)-\divis(b)=\divis(c)-\divis(e)\).
\phantomsection\label{fnd:nag002772:ordinary-quotient-shrinking}
On a good space, local quotient representatives may be taken on
ordinary neighbourhoods. If regularity is initially known only at a
germ, the kernel of multiplication is coherent, so it vanishes after
shrinking. For a unit of the local total quotient ring, a representative
numerator is regular as well: multiplying by an inverse shows that it
divides a regular element. These observations justify regular numerator
and denominator representatives after the necessary restrictions.
Domain compatibility just proved makes their local cycles glue.
\end{proof}
