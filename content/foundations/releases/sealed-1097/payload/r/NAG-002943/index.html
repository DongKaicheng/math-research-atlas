\begin{lemma}[Regular elements avoid minimal primes; F8ZP-N]
\label{lem:avoid}
Let $A$ be a nonzero ring and suppose multiplication by $f\in A$ is
injective. Then $f\notin\mathfrak p$ for every $\mathfrak p\in\Min(A)$.
\end{lemma}
% CLAIM F8ZP-N PROOF
\begin{proof}
Localization preserves the injection. If $f\in\mathfrak p$, its image
in the nonzero ring $A_{\mathfrak p}$ belongs to the unique prime of
that ring and is therefore nilpotent, by the nilradical criterion.
A nilpotent element cannot act injectively on a nonzero ring: iterating
an injective multiplication would make the zero multiplication
injective. This contradiction proves the assertion. No associated-prime
or $S_1$ assertion is used.
\end{proof}
