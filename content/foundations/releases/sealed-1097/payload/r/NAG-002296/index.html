Constant degrees for separated groups of moment fibres

5 Tropicalization, multiplicities and balancing

Open in the English reader · PDF page 956

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{proposition}[Constant degrees for separated groups of moment fibres]
\label{v036:analytic:clusterdegree}
Let $X=\mathcal M(B)$ be pure of analytic dimension $d$, let
$f_1,\ldots,f_n\in B^\times$ define $t:X\to\mathbb R^n$, and let
$Q\in M_{d\times n}(\mathbb Z)$. Suppose
\[
 s_0\notin Q(t(\partial_KX)),\qquad
 Q^{-1}(s_0)\cap t(X)=\{w_1,\ldots,w_m\}\ne\varnothing
\]
is finite. Choose pairwise disjoint neighborhoods $N_i$ of the
$w_i$. There are closed rectangular boxes $W_i\subset N_i$ with
$w_i\in\operatorname{Int}(W_i)$ and a closed rectangular
neighborhood $P$ of $s_0$ such that
\[
 Y=(Q\circ t)^{-1}(P)=\coprod_{i=1}^m Y_i,
 \qquad Y_i=Y\cap t^{-1}(W_i),
 \qquad t(Y_i)\subset\operatorname{Int}(W_i).
\]
This is a finite disjoint affinoid cover and gives a bounded
product decomposition $D=\prod_iD_i$ over $A_P$. Each $D_i$ is
finite over $A_P$, and there are positive integers $\delta_i$ such
that
\[
       \dim_{H_s}(D_i\widehat\otimes_{A_P}H_s)=\delta_i
                        \qquad(s\in P).
\]
At $s=s_0$, the algebra being measured is the actual finite
analytic fibre of $B_{w_i}$ over the projected Gauss point.
Thus each original moment fibre has positive projected Gauss
degree, extending to the constant Gauss degree of its separated
group. Whenever $Q^{-1}(s)\cap t(X)$ is finite, $\delta_i$ is the
sum of the actual projected Gauss degrees of its moment fibres
whose values lie in $W_i$.
\end{proposition}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Take closed boxes $W_i$ as stated, still pairwise disjoint. Put
$q=Q\circ t$; its coordinates are the logarithms of the units
$g_j=\prod_k f_k^{q_{jk}}$. The compact set
\[
       X\setminus t^{-1}\!\left(\bigcup_i
                                  \operatorname{Int}(W_i)\right)
\]
has $q$-image disjoint from $s_0$. Shrink the box supplied by
Theorem~\ref{v036:analytic:finitecollar} to avoid this compact
image. Then every point of $Y$ belongs to exactly one $Y_i$, and
its $t$-image lies in the interior of the corresponding box.
Each $Y_i$ is the actual Laurent domain obtained by imposing the
coordinate bounds defining $W_i$, by
Theorem~\ref{fnd:generalizedrationaluniversal}. The sets are both open and
closed in $Y$.

The norm-strict equalizer of
Theorem~\ref{fnd:generalizedaffinoidcovercech} for this disjoint
cover is the asserted product isomorphism: all pairwise
intersections have zero algebra. Its factors are direct summands
of the finite $A_P$-module $D$, so they are finite; their induced
norms are canonical by Theorem~\ref{fnd:generalizedfinitemodules}.
Each contains $t^{-1}(w_i)$ and is nonempty;
Theorem~\ref{v036:analytic:domainpurity} makes it pure of analytic
dimension $d$. Proposition~\ref{v036:analytic:polyannulusdegree}
gives the constants $\delta_i>0$.

At $s_0$, the imposed equalities $Q\circ t=s_0$ leave only
$w_1,\ldots,w_m$ as possible moment values. Within $W_i$ the
remaining domain is exactly $t^{-1}(w_i)$. The rational universal
property identifies its algebra with $B_{w_i}$, and identifies the
base change of $D_i$ to the exact-radius polytorus with that
algebra. Laurent polynomial density identifies the completed
residue fields of its Gauss point and $\eta_{s_0}$. The finite
tensor comparison of Proposition~\ref{fnd:generalizedfinitetensor}
and Theorem~\ref{fnd:allpointaffinoidfibres} now identify the tensor
in the statement with the actual fibre. If the joint moment image
over another $s\in P$ is finite, apply
Proposition~\ref{v035:analytic:projectedfibrefactors} at $s$ and
retain the factors belonging to $W_i$. Finite products commute
with these tensors, so their dimensions add, proving the last
assertion.
\end{proof}
