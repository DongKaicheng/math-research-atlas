Algebraic quotient and regular elements; F8U-A

Proper direct image on historical ordinary compact tests

Open in the English reader · PDF page 1833

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{lemma}[Algebraic quotient and regular elements; F8U-A]
\label{lem:algebra}
The ideal \(T(A)\) is finite and nilpotent, and
\(T(A)_{\mathfrak p}=0\) at every minimal prime.
The ring \(A^{\mathrm u}\) has the same minimal primes and generic
local rings as \(A\), and has no embedded associated primes; hence it
satisfies \(S_1\).
If \(f\) is a non-zero-divisor in \(A\), it is a non-zero-divisor in
both \(T(A)\) and \(A^{\mathrm u}\), and
\[
 0\longrightarrow T(A)/fT(A)\longrightarrow A/fA
       \longrightarrow A^{\mathrm u}/fA^{\mathrm u}\longrightarrow0
                                                                    \tag{1.2}
\]
is exact.
\end{lemma}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
All ideals are finite. If \(a\in T(A)\), then for each minimal
\(\mathfrak p\) some element outside \(\mathfrak p\) kills \(a\).
Finite prime avoidance applied to \(\operatorname{Ann}(a)\) produces
\(s\in S\) with \(sa=0\). Conversely such an \(s\) is a unit in every
\(A_{\mathfrak p}\), so \(sa=0\) implies \(a\in T(A)\).
Thus \(T(A)\) is exactly the \(S\)-torsion, and
\(A^{\mathrm u}\) embeds in \(S^{-1}A\).
Each \(a\in T(A)\) belongs to every minimal prime of \(A\); hence
\(T(A)\) lies in the nilradical and is nilpotent. Localizing (1.1)
at a minimal prime gives \(T(A)_{\mathfrak p}=0\), including for a
finite generating set, so the generic local rings are unchanged.

Every \(s\in S\) acts injectively on \(A^{\mathrm u}\).
An associated prime \(\mathfrak q\) of \(A^{\mathrm u}\) is therefore
disjoint from \(S\). Finite prime avoidance implies
\(\mathfrak q\subseteq\mathfrak p\) for some minimal prime
\(\mathfrak p\), so \(\mathfrak q=\mathfrak p\).
This proves absence of embedded primes. The equivalence with \(S_1\)
is the Noetherian algebra criterion
\href{https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/031Q}{Stacks 031Q};
no analytic claim is hidden in this step.

A regular \(f\) avoids every minimal prime, so \(f\in S\) and its
action on \(A^{\mathrm u}\) is injective.
It acts injectively on \(T(A)\subseteq A\) as well.
Moreover \(fa\in T(A)\) implies \(a\in T(A)\).
Thus \(T(A)\cap fA=fT(A)\), which proves (1.2).
\end{proof}
