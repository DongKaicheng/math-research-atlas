Meromorphic coherent-cycle comparison; F8CA-I

I Descent of the polarized mixed tower

Open in the English reader · PDF page 2091

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{theorem}[Meromorphic coherent-cycle comparison; F8CA-I]
\label{thm:global}
Let \(X\) be good, Hausdorff, and pure of analytic dimension \(n\geq1\).
Let \(f\) be regular meromorphic and admit ordinary local presentations
\(a/b\), with \(a,b\) regular and their actual zero spaces empty or
pure of dimension \(n-1\). Restrict comparisons to such presentations.
Then the differences of the effective Cartier cycles are independent
of presentation, glue on actual common domains, and have the
coherent-cycle integration pairing asserted in the pure-dimensional
clause of \tagg{F8ST-I}, on ordinary \((n-1,n-1)\)-tests with compact
support in \(\Int(X/K)\).
\end{theorem}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Two representatives can be restricted to an actual common ordinary
affinoid neighbourhood, say \(\cM(A)\), where their four equations
\(a,b,c,e\) are defined and regular and \(a/b=c/e\). The zero spaces
of these restricted equations are actual affinoid domains in the
original zero spaces, by quotient/domain base change
\tagg{NAG-001702}, \tagg{001846}. Frozen domain purity
\tagg{NAG-002413} makes each one empty or pure of dimension \(n-1\).
Thus Lemma~\ref{lem:pure} applies to \((a,e)\) and \((b,c)\), and
also to any further products used for a common denominator.

Localization at regular elements is injective, so equality of the
fractions gives \(ae=bc\) in this actual common ring. Theorem~\ref{fnd:nag002790:antichain-additivity}
now gives
\[
 \divis(a)+\divis(e)=\divis(ae)=\divis(bc)
                    =\divis(b)+\divis(c).
\]
Subtracting proves independence. The already verified effective
actual-domain comparison makes these equalities agree on refinements
and gives ordinary gluing. For triple overlaps use a common further
affinoid refinement; the same equality is transitive there.

The effective chain identity of \tagg{F8ST-I} retains the actual
whole Gauss algebra, all generic lengths, full residue degrees, and
one lattice-index division. Subtract its two effective formulas.
The independence just proved now justifies the finite localization
and partition sum for a compact test, using \tagg{NAG-002425} and
\tagg{002428}. Its restriction to each zero space is compact and
inside that space's analytic interior: a closed immersion is finite
and relatively interior, and \tagg{NAG-001704} composes interiors.
No boundary term, logarithmic limit, or test-space convergence is
introduced.
\end{proof}
