Exact realization by a split nodal model

4 Analytic curves and skeleta

Open in the English reader · PDF page 759

Statement · exact TeX
\begin{theorem}[Exact realization by a split nodal model]
\label{v035:f5:exactrealization}
Let $K$ be algebraically closed, complete and nontrivially valued,
with its original absolute value. Let $C/K$ be a smooth connected
projective curve and let $V$ be a semistable vertex set of $C^{\an}$.
There is a split nodal algebraic model $\mathcal C_V/O$ whose
component vertices are exactly the original points of $V$.
More precisely, from every split nodal algebraic model
$\mathcal C_0$ with vertex set containing $V$, there is a proper
morphism $\mathcal C_0\to\mathcal C_V$ inducing the identity on $C$.
\end{theorem}
Full selected proof · exact TeX
\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{cand:algebraicallyclosedmarkedmodels}, with empty
marking, supplies a split nodal model, and
Theorem~\ref{cand:finitetypeiirealization} supplies one whose
vertex set $W$ contains $V$. Start with any such $\mathcal C_0$.
Apply Lemma~\ref{v035:f5:elliptictails}, obtaining
$\mathcal C_0\to\mathcal Y_0\to\mathcal Y$.
The generic normalization of the stable curve $\mathcal Y_K$ is
the disjoint union of $C$ and the labelled auxiliary elliptic curves.
The special nodes of $\mathcal Y$ have the required split pointed
charts: apply Theorem~\ref{cand:simultaneousnodalsplitting}; its
finite extension of the algebraically closed field $K$ is $K$.
Proposition~\ref{cand:nodalmodelgenericnormalization} therefore
gives a finite relative normalization
\[
             \mathcal N=\mathcal C_V\amalg\coprod_j\mathcal E_j
                         \longrightarrow\mathcal Y.
\]
The $C$-labelled piece $\mathcal C_V$ is projective, flat and
finitely presented with split reduced nodal special fibre.

The source $\mathcal C_0$ is integral and normal. Flatness embeds
the ring of each nonempty affine open into its generic ring, the
ring of a nonempty open of the integral curve $C$; this proves
integrality. Smooth points are normal over the normal valuation
ring by \cite[Tag 033C]{StacksAlg}. At a special node, the smoothing
parameter is nonzero, so Lemma~\ref{cand:valuationnodenormalization}
gives normality of its standard ring $O[u,v]/(uv-a)$.
Normality ascends along the pointed \textup{\'etale} chart and
descends to the model local ring by
\cite[Tags 033C, 033G]{StacksAlg}. These loci cover $\mathcal C_0$.

The generic restriction of $\mathcal C_0\to\mathcal Y$ has its
specified lift to the $C$-labelled generic normalization, since the
generic stabilization is the identity. Lemma~\ref{v035:f5:normalizationlift}
gives a proper morphism $q:\mathcal C_0\to\mathcal C_V$ inducing
the identity on $C$.

By Lemma~\ref{v035:f5:elliptictails}, the original surviving special
components are precisely those indexed by $V$. At their generic
points $\mathcal Y$ is relatively smooth, so relative normalization
is the identity there. The local normalization formula of
Lemma~\ref{cand:valuationnodenormalization} introduces no extra
vertical components: at $a\ne0$ it is the identity, and at $a=0$
it separates the two branches. Each auxiliary elliptic model is
normal by smoothness over the normal base and is integral by flatness
and its integral generic fibre. The same normalization-lift lemma
puts its surviving special
component in its own generic-labelled piece $\mathcal E_j$.
Thus these auxiliary components belong to none of the other pieces,
and $\mathcal C_{V,s}$ has exactly the surviving original components.

For $v\in V$, let $\eta_v$ be its original component generic point.
The point $q_s(\eta_v)$ is the generic point of its surviving
component in $\mathcal C_{V,s}$. Functoriality of specialization
for the completed morphism gives
\[
       \operatorname{sp}_{\mathcal C_V}(v)=q_s(\eta_v).
\]
The singleton-fibre theorem
\ref{cand:reducedmodelcomponentpoints} identifies the new component
vertex with the original point $v$. Thus the vertex set is exactly
$V$ as a subset of $C^{\an}$.
\end{proof}
Standing context 1 · exact TeX
For the rest of this section and the next, assume that $K$ is
algebraically closed. Let $C/K$ be a smooth connected projective curve
and put $X=C^{\an}$. A split nodal algebraic model means a projective,
flat, finitely presented $O$-model of $C$ with reduced nodal special
fibre and the split pointed algebraic charts of
Theorem~\ref{cand:pointednodalvaluationchart}. The smoothing parameters
at its nodes are nonzero. Theorems~\ref{cand:projectiveformalcompletion}
and~\ref{cand:splitmodelskeleton} identify its completed generic fibre
with $X$ and supply its embedded finite graph and retraction.
The space $X$ is compact Hausdorff by
Proposition~\ref{fnd:analytificationprojectivecompact}.
