\begin{proposition}[Two precise failures of weaker analytic premises]\label{f9tf:obstructions}
The following elementary examples are topology and convergence obstructions, not asserted native analytic spaces.

First, let $C=\{0\}\cup\{1/k:k\geq1\}$ and let $E_N$ be the functions on $C$ constant on $\{0\}\cup\{1/k:k>N\}$. Put $E=\bigcup_NE_N$ with its final locally convex topology from the finite-dimensional supremum-norm spaces $E_N$. Then
\[
 F(f)=\sum_{k\geq1}(f(1/k)-f(0))
\]
is well-defined and has order-zero bound $|F(f)|\leq2N\|f\|_C$ on $E_N$, but it has no finite signed Radon representation on $C$. Indeed there are $f_N\in E_N$ with $0\leq f_N\leq1$ and $F(f_N)=N$.

Second, on $[0,1]$ the signed measures
$\sigma_m=m(\delta_{1/m^2}-\delta_0)$ satisfy
\[
 |\sigma_m(h)|\leq\|h'\|_\infty/m
 \quad(h\in C^1([0,1])),\qquad
 |\sigma_m|([0,1])=2m,
\]
while $\sigma_m(\sqrt{x})=1$. Thus convergence to zero against all smooth tests does not imply convergence against a fixed continuous multiplier without a suitable variation bound.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every $f\in E_N$ makes only its first $N$ summands nonzero, proving the first bound and continuity by finality. Let $f_N$ be one on the first $N$ nonzero points and zero on the remaining points and on zero. This is a continuous member of $E_N$ and gives $F(f_N)=N$. A finite signed representing measure $\nu$ would instead give $|F(f_N)|\leq|\nu|(C)$ for every $N$, a contradiction. The space $E$ is uniformly dense in $C(C)$, by uniform continuity at zero, so lack of smooth-density analogues is not the issue.

For the second assertion, the fundamental theorem gives
$|h(1/m^2)-h(0)|\leq\|h'\|_\infty/m^2$.
The two atoms are distinct with opposite masses of size $m$, so their total variation is $2m$. Finally $m\sqrt{1/m^2}=1$. All claimed inequalities and failures follow directly.
\end{proof}
