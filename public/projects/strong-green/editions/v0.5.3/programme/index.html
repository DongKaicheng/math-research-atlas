Research programme

From Green forms to height pairings

The Chow obstruction, prescribed curved Green pairs and the analytic foundations of the height construction have internal proofs in their stated scope. Vanishing of the obstruction on all homologically trivial classes remains the general local problem on this route. The principal-push theorem (PH16; Theorem 7.5) gives the full Beilinson–Bloch height pairing from Strong G at every finite completion in both complementary degrees. General admissible liftings, positivity and finiteness remain separate research questions.

The main objective

For a smooth, projective and geometrically integral variety X/FX/FX/F of dimension nnn over a number field, construct a canonical pairing

hX:CH⁡r(X)Q,hom×CH⁡n+1−r(X)Q,hom⟶R. h_X:\operatorname{CH}^r(X)_{\mathbb Q,\mathrm{hom}}\times\operatorname{CH}^{n+1-r}(X)_{\mathbb Q,\mathrm{hom}}\longrightarrow\mathbb R. hX​:CHr(X)Q,hom​×CHn+1−r(X)Q,hom​⟶R.

Theorem 7.5 (PH16) establishes the finite sum of local terms, independence of Green choices and descent to both Chow groups from Strong G at every finite completion in both complementary codimensions, within the stated analytic foundations. This identifies the significance of the local conjecture for the global height problem.

The Chow obstruction and prescribed Green-pair theorem now establishes Hypothesis 2.2 over the original valued field, for smooth integral projective varieties and every prescribed rational cycle. On the obstruction route, the remaining general local task is vanishing of this class on homologically trivial cycles.

The logical implications

There are two routes to the global pairing: enlarge the obstruction-zero domain, or apply the proved principal-push theorem directly to strong Green forms. Each route keeps its stated analytic hypotheses and review status.

The construction and further research

Step 1 is the established curved Green construction. Step 2 is the remaining general source-vanishing problem. Step 3 is the proved conditional passage to a full height pairing. Step 4 concerns comparison with classical constructions. Steps 5–6 ask for additional arithmetic properties.

1. Chow obstructions and curved Green pairs

Established: programme Theorem 2.2, with its complete proof in construction Theorem 1.1, applies to smooth integral projective varieties over the original non-Archimedean field, including varieties that are not geometrically integral.

Proved construction
Strict actual model Chern characters and the restricted scalar–curvature finite trace give the supported-complex identity. Global primitives for generic Chow-zero characters make the obstruction independent of model and presentation and compatible with genuine pullbacks. Summing the component constructions retains the prescribed cycle coefficients and exact complement, against all compact actual tests.
Consequence
The pair exists for every rational cycle. Zero obstruction then supplies a global actual primitive; subtracting it gives Strong G on the same support complement.
Prerequisites
The actual calculus and the analytic and algebraic arguments proved in the construction paper. Its restricted trace acts on the specified scalar–curvature module. Full finite trace, projective-bundle integration and the supported height identities have separate proofs in the height foundations.

2. Vanishing of the Bott–Chern class

Open in general: show that homologically trivial cycles have zero curvature class in the actual Bott–Chern quotient.

oX,Kr(CH⁡r(X)Q,hom)=0\mathfrak o^r_{X,K}\bigl(\operatorname{CH}^r(X)_{\mathbb Q,\mathrm{hom}}\bigr)=0oX,Kr​(CHr(X)Q,hom​)=0.

Proposed argument
First apply the algebraic-equivalence and Chow-correspondence arguments on their stated domains. For the remaining classes, seek a comparison theorem showing that vanishing of the cohomological cycle class forces every actual obstruction period to vanish.
Consequence
Together with the established construction in Step 1, this gives Strong G. The tropical approach is an alternative method for constructing the required global actual primitives, with its own descent and integration comparisons.
Prerequisites
The established Chow obstruction and curvature correction in Step 1, the proved rational-fibre rigidity, numerical descent and Chow-kernel covariance, and the exact geometric or model conditions in each application.

3. The proved height construction from Strong G

Apply Theorem 7.5 (PH16): Strong G gives a local sum independent of Green choices that descends through rational equivalence in both variables, using the proved height foundations.

hX(Z,W)=∑v[Fv:Qv][F:Q] λv(Zv,Wv)h_X(Z,W)=\sum_v \dfrac{[F_v:\mathbb Q_v]}{[F:\mathbb Q]}\,\lambda_v(Z_v,W_v)hX​(Z,W)=∑v​[F:Q][Fv​:Qv​]​λv​(Zv​,Wv​).

Proved construction
The principal-push proof computes the principal-cycle norm from −log|u| on a normal carrier. Reciprocity transfers the calculation to arbitrary supplied strong Green forms. Good-place vanishing makes the sum finite, and the product formula gives Chow descent. The alternative obstruction-kernel route reaches full domain by vanishing on localized global classes in both degrees.
Consequence
A canonical real symmetric pairing on both full geometrically homologically trivial rational Chow groups, with the prescribed normalization and Bloch-biextension interpretation. Positivity and finiteness remain separate questions.
Prerequisites
Strong G at every finite completion in both complementary codimensions, together with the analytic propositions proved in §2. The direct theorem does not require vanishing of a chosen obstruction map. The separate fixed finite-test description of the all-local-kernel domain still requires its identification hypothesis.

4. Correspondence adjunction and classical heights

Apply the proved correspondence identity on the all-local-kernel domain and the comparison with classical heights on supplied admissible arithmetic models.

hX(Γ∗z,w)=hY(z,tΓ∗w)h_X(\Gamma_*z,w)=h_Y(z,{}^t\Gamma_*w)hX​(Γ∗​z,w)=hY​(z,tΓ∗​w).

Proposed argument
Apply the proper-intersection projection formula to correspondences. Compare the finite-place terms on supplied admissible arithmetic models, and match the archimedean determinant and Poincaré normalizations. At bad reduction, construct the required vertical corrections.
Consequence
Identification with the classical Beilinson–Bloch pairing on the common domain, together with the normalization needed for subsequent height identities.
Prerequisites
The constructed kernel-domain pairing, its proved proper-intersection and correspondence identities, and supplied admissible model data. Existence of such lifts for all classes, and the general arithmetic-model projection formula retained in the Zhang route, remain separate requirements.

5. Arithmetic Lefschetz and positivity

For pointed products of curves, prove the Chow vanishing and central positivity assertions used by the arithmetic reduction theorem.

Π∗CH⁡c(Y)Q,hom=0(k≠2c−1),Y=∏i=1kCi\Pi_*\operatorname{CH}^c(Y)_{\mathbb Q,\mathrm{hom}}=0\quad(k\ne2c-1),\qquad Y=\prod_{i=1}^{k}C_iΠ∗​CHc(Y)Q,hom​=0(k=2c−1),Y=∏i=1k​Ci​, where Π=⨂i(ΔCi−oi×Ci−Ci×oi)\Pi=\bigotimes_i(\Delta_{C_i}-o_i\times C_i-C_i\times o_i)Π=⨂i​(ΔCi​​−oi​×Ci​−Ci​×oi​) is the projector onto the product of the h1(Ci)h^1(C_i)h1(Ci​) factors.

Proposed argument
Work separately on off-central vanishing, injectivity of the central algebraic Abel–Jacobi map, and real positive definiteness of the residual height. Keep the fixed product polarization and the height axioms H1–H4 throughout.
Consequence
Under H1–H4, these assertions yield arithmetic hard Lefschetz, primitive positivity and complementary nondegeneracy by the central-block identities.
Prerequisites
The height system H1–H4 on all required subproducts, source curves of correspondences and finite extensions; these arithmetic assertions are additional to height existence.

6. Integral Northcott finiteness

Prove bounded-height finiteness for the projected images of integral cycles, first over a fixed field and then in bounded degree.

#{z∈Λ≤d:q(z)≤B}<∞\#\{z\in\Lambda_{\le d}:q(z)\le B\}<\infty#{z∈Λ≤d​:q(z)≤B}<∞. Here qqq is the signed residual quadratic height and Λ≤d\Lambda_{\le d}Λ≤d​ consists of projected integral classes defined over extensions of degree at most ddd of the fixed base field.

Proposed argument
Over each fixed field, combine real positivity with a common finite-rank lattice, or use real positivity and a proved height gap with a finite Kummer quotient for some integer m ≥ 2. Then bound ramification of the minimal fields of definition of bounded-height classes.
Consequence
A common finite-rank lattice and real positivity give fixed-field Northcott; the additional ramification control gives bounded-degree Northcott. Positive definiteness alone does not give either finiteness assertion.
Prerequisites
The residual quadratic height and integral image from the arithmetic programme. Integral control and field control are separate requirements.
