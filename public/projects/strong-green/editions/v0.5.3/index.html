The goal of this project

Beilinson–Bloch heights
and the Strong Green conjecture

We seek an unconditional construction of the canonical real Beilinson–Bloch height pairing in arbitrary dimension. Our approach is to construct zero-curvature Green forms on non-Archimedean analytic spaces and assemble their local integrals into a global height.

History and significance

From heights on curves to higher algebraic cycles

For degree-zero divisors on a curve, height pairings are governed by the Néron–Tate theory of its Jacobian. Bloch and Beilinson developed higher-codimension height pairings in the 1980s; Gillet–Soulé’s arithmetic intersection theory provides a construction through arithmetic models. Historical background and the abelian-variety case.

A homologically trivial cycle can still carry arithmetic information that its cohomology class does not detect. Heights measure this finer information and enter conjectural formulas for the leading terms of motivic L-functions. The problem is to construct the pairing for general varieties, with local contributions that combine into a number depending only on the two Chow classes.

Open in general

The strong zero-curvature Green conjecture

Strong Green asks for an actual non-Archimedean differential form whose second derivative cancels the integration current of a prescribed homologically trivial cycle. Here the forms belong to Mihatsch’s sheaf of actual δ-forms.

The local-to-global argument

Why Strong Green would give Beilinson–Bloch heights

Theorem 7.5 proves this passage using the analytic and global foundations proved in §2. Strong Green is required at every finite completion, for every prescribed geometrically homologically trivial cycle in both complementary codimensions.

Which analytic and global foundations enter the argument?

Restriction, principal-cycle operations and reciprocity

Actual-test currents, the supported-pair point residues, normal-carrier germ extension and projective integration prove the principal norm law. Retain the normal carriers, refined coefficients and both ordered degrees.

Moving, archimedean identities and good-place vanishing

A good higher-Chow one-chain over F gives one refined norm product at every place. Smooth good models give finite support for each disjoint homologically trivial pair, and complex Green theory supplies the matching infinite-place identities.

Other approaches to the height problem

Other approaches to Beilinson–Bloch heights

Vanishing on global cycle classes →

Use the curved Green construction and prove that its local obstruction vanishes on classes coming from the number field, at every finite place and in both degrees. This can suffice for the height construction without solving Strong Green for every local cycle.

The obstruction route and its precise hypotheses

Zhang: arithmetic models and admissible liftings →

Correct cycle closures by vertical cycles, then apply arithmetic intersection. Zhang’s approach relates the general lifting problem to standard conjectures on degenerate fibres.

Current progress

Curved Green pairs and the height foundations

Theorem 2.2 constructs a curved actual Green pair for every prescribed rational cycle and identifies its Chow-invariant curvature obstruction. If that obstruction vanishes, a global primitive removes the curvature and gives a strong Green form. General vanishing on homologically trivial classes remains open.

The current edition also gives explicit proofs for the finite trace, projective integration, rigidity and height identities used by the construction. The 22-item source review records each former hypothesis, its proof and the conditions that remain.
