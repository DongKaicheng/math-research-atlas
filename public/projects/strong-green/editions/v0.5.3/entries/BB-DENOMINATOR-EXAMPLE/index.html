Why p-adic finiteness alone is not Northcott

An abstract denominator example isolates a missing global condition.

Arithmetic Lefschetz and Integral Heights

Statement

The group Λ=∑p primeZ p−1⊂Q\Lambda=\sum_{p\ \mathrm{prime}}\mathbf Z\,p^{-1}\subset\mathbf QΛ=∑p prime​Zp−1⊂Q, with q(x)=x2q(x)=x^2q(x)=x2, has Λ⊗Zp=p−1Zp\Lambda\otimes\mathbf Z_p=p^{-1}\mathbf Z_pΛ⊗Zp​=p−1Zp​ at every prime, yet fails Northcott. This is an example of abstract groups, not a counterexample to a geometric conjecture.

Argument

All denominators are squarefree. After tensoring with Zp\mathbf Z_pZp​, all denominators away from ppp become units, and the remaining factor gives p−1Zpp^{-1}\mathbf Z_pp−1Zp​. Nevertheless the distinct elements 1/p1/p1/p satisfy q(1/p)=1/p2→0q(1/p)=1/p^2\to0q(1/p)=1/p2→0. Thus finite generation at each coefficient prime gives no single global denominator bound.
