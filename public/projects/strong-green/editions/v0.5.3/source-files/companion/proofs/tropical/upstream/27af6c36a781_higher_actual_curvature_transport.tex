\section{Finite actual curvature transport in every relation degree}
\label{sec:r12-higher-actual-transport}

\paragraph{Status and scope.}
The constructor of this source is \texttt{/root}.  The formulas below
are submitted as \textbf{PROVED THIS ROUND}, subject to exact-source
independent review.  Their degrees one and two recover the frozen CPT
and QHT formulas, respectively; those cases are not new results.
The new statement is one finite nonclosed homotopy, with its entire
defect retained, in every higher relation degree.  This is an actual
cochain tool, not a theorem that an arbitrary matrix overlap or a
higher raw cube has the necessary factorization.  No new cycle-existence
class or full-kernel theorem is asserted here.

\subsection{The actual complex and the full nonclosed formula}

On an ordinary analytic open $W$ over the original field, use the
array convention
\[
 L_k^s(W)=\bigoplus_{i=0}^k B^{s-i,s-k+i}(W),\qquad
 (\partial_k u)_i=d''u_i+d'u_{i+1}\quad(0\le i<k).
 \tag{HCT1}
\]
Negative bidegrees mean zero.  Put $L_0^s=B^{s,s}$ and
$D=d'd''$ on this term.  Full anticommutation gives
$\partial_{k-1}\partial_k=0$ for $k\ge2$ and $D\partial_1=0$.
The array orders in the frozen notation are
\[
 L_1:(\beta,\alpha),\qquad L_2:(B,M,A),\qquad
 L_3:(V,Q,P,U).
 \tag{HCT2}
\]
Thus (HCT1) is exactly the two-colour complex already used for
actual pairs, triples, quadruples and finite higher descent.

Let $h\in B^{t,t}(W)$ be actual, with $t\ge0$.  For $k\ge1$
and an arbitrary, not necessarily closed $u\in L_k^s(W)$, define
$J_k(h,u)=v\in L_{k+1}^{s+t+1}(W)$ by
\[
 \begin{split}
 v_i&=-d'h\wedge u_i &&(0\le i\le k-1),\\
 v_k&=-h\wedge d''u_{k-1},\\
 v_{k+1}&=d''h\wedge u_k.
 \end{split}
 \tag{HCT3}
\]
For $g\in L_0^s(W)$ define instead
\[
                 J_0(h,g)=(h\wedge d'g,\ d''h\wedge g).
 \tag{HCT4}
\]
All indices and bidegrees in these formulas are literal; no inverse
operator or choice of a primitive is involved.

\begin{theorem}[All-degree identity with its nonclosed defect]
For every $k\ge1$ and every actual $u\in L_k^s(W)$,
\[
 \partial_{k+1}J_k(h,u)
       =Dh\wedge u-J_{k-1}(h,\partial_k u).
 \tag{HCT5}
\]
At degree zero the precise endpoint identity is
\[
                  \partial_1J_0(h,g)=Dh\wedge g-h\wedge Dg.
 \tag{HCT6}
\]
In particular, if $\partial_k u=0$ has been independently verified,
then $Dh\wedge u$ is the displayed actual boundary
$\partial_{k+1}J_k(h,u)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $p=\partial_k u$.  Since $h$ has even total degree, its first
derivatives have odd total degree.  For $0\le i\le k-2$, full
Leibniz expansion gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 d''v_i+d'v_{i+1}
 &=Dh\wedge u_i+d'h\wedge d''u_i+d'h\wedge d'u_{i+1}\\
 &=Dh\wedge u_i+d'h\wedge p_i.
 \end{split}
 \tag{HCT7}
\]
The two final components, without using closedness of $u$, are
\[
 \begin{split}
 d''v_{k-1}+d'v_k&=Dh\wedge u_{k-1}-h\wedge Du_{k-1},\\
 d''v_k+d'v_{k+1}&=Dh\wedge u_k-d''h\wedge p_{k-1}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{HCT8}
\]
For $k\ge2$, the penultimate component of $J_{k-1}(h,p)$ is
$-h\wedge d''p_{k-2}=h\wedge Du_{k-1}$.  Its other components
are exactly the negatives of the extra terms in (HCT7)--(HCT8).
For $k=1$, (HCT4) gives the same result because
$d'p_0=Du_0$.  Finally,
\[
 d''(h\wedge d'g)+d'(d''h\wedge g)
             =Dh\wedge g-h\wedge Dg,
 \tag{HCT9}
\]
which proves (HCT6).  No property of the coefficient forms beyond
actualness, the full Leibniz rules and anticommutation has been used.
\end{proof}

If $k=1$ and $u=(\beta,\alpha)$ is closed, then the middle
component in (HCT3) is $h\wedge d'\alpha$; after the reordering
(HCT2) this is exactly CPT2.  For $k=2$, reordering (HCT3) is
exactly QH2, including its nonclosed correction.  These identifications
also fix all signs; the higher statement does not replace either
frozen formula by a different representative.

\subsection{An extending higher coefficient and a singular curvature}

Let $W=N\setminus C$ be ordinary opens, let $R\in B^{1,1}(N)$
be closed in both colours, and let
\[
 e\in L_k^a(N),\qquad \partial_k e=0,\qquad k\ge1.
 \tag{HCT10}
\]
Suppose actual scalars on $W$ give $C_j=R-D\Phi_j$ for
$1\le j\le b$.  For a multi-index $\nu$ of total degree $d\ge1$
put
\[
 h_\nu=-\sum_{j:\nu_j>0}\Phi_j
 \left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{\nu_j-1}C_j^\ell R^{\nu_j-1-\ell}\right)
 \left(\prod_{i<j}C_i^{\nu_i}\right)R^{\sum_{i>j}\nu_i}.
 \tag{HCT11}
\]
For a homogeneous $P=\sum_\nu t_\nu X^\nu$ of degree $d$,
write $h_P=\sum_\nu t_\nu h_\nu$.  This is the literal CPT7
potential, with all powers and mixed terms retained.  Its finite
telescoping identity is
\[
                 Dh_P=P(C_1,\ldots,C_b)-P(1,\ldots,1)R^d.
 \tag{HCT12}
\]
Consequently
\[
 P(C_1,\ldots,C_b)e|_W
   =\partial_{k+1}J_k(h_P,e|_W)+P(1,\ldots,1)R^d e|_W.
 \tag{HCT13}
\]
The last cochain is actual on all of $N$.  For $d=0$ use a zero
witness and the evident equality.

For example, a separately established entire-overlap equality
\[
 u_1-u_0=\partial_{k+1}v+
                 \sum_j P_j(C_{j,1},\ldots,C_{j,b_j})e_j|_W,
                 \qquad \partial_k e_j=0\text{ on }N,
 \tag{HCT14}
\]
has the explicit boundary-plus-extending-cochain presentation obtained
by adding $J_k(h_{P_j},e_j|_W)$ to $v$ and replacing each polynomial
factor by $P_j(1,\ldots,1)R^{\deg P_j}$.  The equality (HCT14),
and the closedness of every coefficient $e_j$ by itself, are genuine
input hypotheses.  Neither follows from closedness of the entire
sum, from a relation in a formal coefficient algebra, or by cancelling
a possibly zero-divisor curvature factor.  No assertion is made that
multiplication by a singular $C_j$ acts on a quotient modulo extending
cochains.

\subsection{Explicit higher unit cycles}

Let $a_j=v(\lambda_j)$ for original-$K$ analytic units on $N$,
so $Da_j=0$ in actual $B$.  Define
\[
 E_2(a_1,a_2)=(a_2d'a_1,\ a_1d''a_2)\in L_1^1(N),
 \qquad
 E_k(a_1,\ldots,a_k)=J_{k-2}(a_k,E_{k-1})\quad(k\ge3).
 \tag{HCT15}
\]
These are finite, multilinear actual expressions, with no formal
unit quotient being substituted for a form.  They satisfy
\[
 E_k\in L_{k-1}^{k-1}(N),\qquad \partial_{k-1}E_k=0.
 \tag{HCT16}
\]
Indeed the two terms in $\partial_1E_2$ cancel, including the terms
that vanish because $Da_1=Da_2=0$, and induction follows from
(HCT5) with $Da_k=0$.

The final component and its full double-prime curl are
\[
 \begin{split}
 (E_k)_{k-1}
   &=\epsilon_k a_1\,d''a_2\wedge\cdots\wedge d''a_k,\\
 d''(E_k)_{k-1}
   &=\epsilon_k d''a_1\wedge\cdots\wedge d''a_k,
 \qquad \epsilon_k=(-1)^{(k-1)(k-2)/2}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{HCT17}
\]
The induction sign comes from moving $d''a_k$ past $k-2$
double-prime factors.  For $k=3$, (HCT15), reordered as $(A,B,M)$,
is precisely the already frozen CU6 triple
\[
 (-a_1d''a_2\wedge d''a_3,\quad
   a_2d'a_1\wedge d'a_3,\quad a_3d'a_1\wedge d''a_2).
 \tag{HCT18}
\]
Its recognition at degree three is not a new independent residue
theorem.  For $k\ge4$, no identification of (HCT15) with any
specified higher raw Gaussian or commuting-diagonal cell is claimed.
Such an identification would require another entire-expression proof.

\subsection{Why a strict higher witness cannot be required}

\begin{proposition}[A specified extending higher cycle need not be strict]
For every $r\ge k\ge2$ and every $q\ge0$, there is an ordinary
original-$K$ analytic model of dimension $n=r+q$ and an actual
cochain $e\in L_{k-1}^{r-1}$, defined everywhere on that model,
such that $\partial_{k-1}e=0$ but no actual $t\in L_k^{r-1}$
satisfies $e=\partial_k t$ there.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Use
\[
 N=(\mathbb G_m^k)^{\rm an}\times(\mathbf P^{r-k}_K)^{\rm an}
                       \times(\mathbf P^q_K)^{\rm an}.
 \tag{HCT19}
\]
Write $a_i=v(T_i)$ for the original torus coordinates.  Let $R$ be
the standard projective curvature on the middle factor and let
$\Omega$ be the $q$th power of the standard projective curvature
on the last factor.  A zero-dimensional projective factor has
integral one, and the corresponding zeroth power is $1$.  Set
\[
                        e=R^{r-k}E_k(a_1,\ldots,a_k).
 \tag{HCT20}
\]
Its bidegrees and closedness follow from (HCT16).  Choose an original
$\pi\in K$ with $\gamma=v(\pi)>0$, and a nonzero nonnegative
$\chi\in C_c^\infty((0,\gamma)^k)$.  Put
\[
 \eta=\chi(a_1,\ldots,a_k)\,
            d'a_1\wedge\cdots\wedge d'a_k\wedge\Omega.
 \tag{HCT21}
\]
This is an actual compactly supported form of bidegree $(k+q,q)$.
Compactness holds because a compact valuation box has compact
polyannulus inverse image and both projective factors are proper.
The prime degree on the torus is already maximal, hence $D\eta=0$.
No rational-valued intercept or density of rational points is needed;
all points of the Berkovich torus participate in the actual form.

If $e=\partial_k t$, its final component gives
\[
                         d''e_{k-1}=-Dt_k.
 \tag{HCT22}
\]
For $r=k$ the degree of $t_k$ has negative first entry, so it is
zero; for all other ranks, two actual Stokes identities after wedging
with (HCT21) give the same conclusion
\[
                      \int_N d''e_{k-1}\wedge\eta=0.
 \tag{HCT23}
\]
On the other hand, original-field torus integration and the standard
projective calibrations give
\[
 \int_N d''e_{k-1}\wedge\eta
   =\epsilon_k\int_N R^{r-k}\wedge d''a_1\wedge\cdots\wedge d''a_k
                                      \wedge\eta\ne0.
 \tag{HCT24}
\]
The orientation fixes the sign, which is irrelevant to nonvanishing;
the projective masses are one and $\int\chi>0$.  This contradicts
(HCT23).  Only products and actual integration are used, not an
unproved preservation of actual forms by arbitrary proper pushforward.
\end{proof}

This proposition is a \textbf{ROUTE FAILURE} only for insisting on
a strict witness for the specified cochain (HCT20).  The very same
cochain is already an extending error.  Thus it supplies no
counterexample to relative higher gluing, original-support Green
existence, or entire actual OPEN-KERNEL.  It is not a claim that
every higher raw path has this obstruction.  The mixed-degree test
is used to detect a single false actual equality, not to replace the
all-actual-test requirement in the original conjecture.

\subsection{Actual presentations, topology and the original-field interface}

Every object in (HCT1)--(HCT24) is an actual Mihatsch form or a finite
tuple of such forms on an ordinary open.  The equivalence used for
closed cochains is difference by a displayed actual $\partial$
boundary; a relative equality additionally specifies an actual
cochain on the unpunctured ordinary open.  No completion, dual
element or unspecified equivalence class is counted as a witness.

The local presentations use only finitely many actual coefficient
presentations, full derivatives and wedge products.  All identities
commute with restriction, common refinement and analytic substitution
whenever the input actual forms admit the stated pullback.  Unlike a
native radial contraction, (HCT3) does not operate on a chosen ambient
coordinate that might disappear in the analytic quotient: it is built
intrinsically from the input actual $h,u$ themselves.  Consequently
an actual zero relation stays zero in every argument where the formula
is linear.  Nonproduct unit tuples such as $(T_1,T_2,T_1+T_2)$ on
their domain of invertibility cause no extra assumption.  A vanishing
pullback of their top wedge merely makes that particular detector
vanish; it does not alter the actual identities.

Give each $L_k^s$ the product of its own inherited actual coefficient-final
topologies, and its closed-cochain subspace the inherited topology.
For fixed finite common presentations, all mixed coefficient
seminorms of (HCT3) are controlled by finite Leibniz estimates.
Nothing here identifies a full final topology with an inherited
kernel topology, proves a general closed-range theorem, or produces
a globally continuous linear retraction.

The use in Green theory is only as follows: first prove an actual
entire-overlap factorization such as (HCT14), then insert the explicit
witnesses into the precisely specified finite higher descent data,
and finally apply frozen RSP on the original exact $U$.  This source
does not supply the missing factorization or the remaining higher
Cech data.  It introduces no extension of $K$, no change of absolute
value, no division by a field degree, and no extra growth condition.
