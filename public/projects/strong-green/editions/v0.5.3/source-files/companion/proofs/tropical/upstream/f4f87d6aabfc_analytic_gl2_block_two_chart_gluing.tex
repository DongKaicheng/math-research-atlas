\section{Automatic two-chart coherence for an analytic invertible two-row block}

\subsection{The original-field two-chart atlas}

Let $N$ be an ordinary analytic open in the original smooth
$K$-analytic space, with the ordinary smooth partition interface
of frozen RAG.  It need not be compact.  Let $f$ be an $r$-tuple
of sections of the same actual metrized line bundle, $r\ge2$,
and put $W=N\setminus|f=0|$, $s=r-1$, $R=c_1(\overline L)$.
After a fixed relabeling of two selected rows, supply the analytic
matrix
\[
 M=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\\gamma&\delta\end{pmatrix}
                      \oplus I_{r-2},\qquad
 \Delta=\alpha\delta-\beta\gamma\in\mathcal O(N)^\times.
 \tag{GB1}
\]
Only this block is changed; the other rows remain exactly unchanged.
No coefficient bound, common lattice, or Zariski presentation is
assumed.  Write $D_i(\lambda)$ for a unit scaling of row $i$,
$E_{ij}(t)$ for an elementary shear, and
\[
                J=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}
                      \oplus I_{r-2}.
 \tag{GB2}
\]
The fixed norm isometry $J$ has primitive pair zero.

The two ordinary opens
\[
 N_\alpha=\{\alpha\ne0\},\qquad N_\gamma=\{\gamma\ne0\}
 \tag{GB3}
\]
cover $N$: an analytic point with both entries zero would have
$\Delta=0$ in its completed residue field.  Reciprocal pivot
functions are analytic units on these ordinary opens.  We are not
replacing rational domains by ordinary opens, nor requiring the
entries to be units before restricting to (GB3).

On the first chart set $u=\beta/\alpha$, $t=\gamma/\alpha$ and
$h=\Delta/\alpha$.  The first native Gaussian word is
\[
               w_\alpha=E_{21}(t)D_1(\alpha)D_2(h)E_{12}(u).
 \tag{GB4}
\]
On the second chart the word is
\[
        w_\gamma=J E_{21}(-\alpha/\gamma)
            D_1(\gamma)D_2(\Delta/\gamma)E_{12}(\delta/\gamma).
 \tag{GB5}
\]
All words are matrix products, with the rightmost factor executed
first.  Direct multiplication proves $w_\alpha=w_\gamma=M$ on
their respective domains.  Let $p_\alpha,p_\gamma$ be their full
native path pairs, with the pair for $J$ equal to zero.  Frozen
SHT3--SHT5 give
\[
 G_{Mf}-G_f=\partial_1p_\nu+v(\Delta)R^s,
                 \qquad \nu=\alpha,\gamma.
 \tag{GB6}
\]
The two diagonal weights sum to $v(\Delta)$ in either word; all
shear weights sum to zero.  The arbitrary shear coefficients in
these words may vanish, and their native zero extensions are part
of (GB6).

\subsection{Exact conventions for finite native word substitutions}

For a finite word $w$ let $p(w;g)$ denote its chronological native
pair sum starting at $g$.  If two subwords have the same matrix,
the pairs contributed by an identical suffix agree literally,
because they start at the same tuple.  An identical prefix puts
both subwords at the same actual intermediate tuple.  Thus a
displayed relation between the two subwords may be evaluated there
and inserted into a longer word by adding exactly its actual
witness.  This is a finite substitution rule, not an assertion
that two arbitrary words are related by the available rules.

Two literal rules need no cochain.  A unit edge followed by its
inverse cancels by SHT antisymmetry.  A shear $E_{ij}(z)$ followed
by $E_{ij}(-z)$ also cancels: their coefficients have equal
valuations, and their native middle norm vertices agree by
$E_{ij}(1)$ and the norm-one sign scaling.  The equality persists
through $z=0$ by the native SHT zero extension.

The other literal rule is signed-permutation equivariance, for
the fixed matrices whose nonzero entries are $\pm1$.  This is
checked for the whole presentation, not inferred from equal
endpoints.  A diagonal edge conjugates to the permuted row with
the same unit weight; both of its endpoint norms are unchanged.
For a shear, conjugation permutes its source and target and
possibly replaces $z$ by $-z$.  Its unit weight is unchanged,
and its middle normalizer is the signed permutation of the old
one, up to an additional norm-one diagonal sign.  Hence the
entire two-edge SHT formula has the same three norm potentials
and weights.  Zero-coefficient gluing is preserved because
$|z|=|-z|$.  This proves the exact pair equivariance used when
moving $J$ through diagonal factors below.  No equivariance under
a general norm-isometric matrix is claimed.

Use the following specified actual witness notation:
\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
 Q^{\rm tar}_{ij;z,\lambda}(g)&\text{the exact MX2 target witness},\\
 Q^{\rm src}_{ij;z,\lambda}(g)&\text{the exact NSC3 source witness},\\
 Q^{\rm sl}_{i;\lambda,\mu}(g)&\text{the SL9 loop for }
       (\lambda,\mu,(\lambda\mu)^{-1}),\\
 Q^{\rm cs}_{i,\lambda;j,\mu}(g)&\text{the CS3 square: temporal }
           (i,\lambda),(j,\mu)\text{ minus the reverse order},\\
 Q^{\rm add}_{ij;z_1,z_2}(g)&\text{the exact SAR8 witness},\\
 Q^{\rm W}_{ij;z}(g)&\text{the exact WNR18 witness}.
 \end{array}
 \tag{GB7}
\]
The first three, fifth and sixth are strict.  The fourth has the
displayed extending pair
\[
 e_{x,y}R^{r-2},\qquad
 e_{x,y}=(x\,d''y,y\,d'x),\quad x=v(\lambda),\ y=v(\mu).
 \tag{GB8}
\]
Every symbol in (GB7) denotes its fixed finite actual formula in
the stated exact source, with any positive cap constant fixed
once.  No linear primitive selector or quotient lifting is used.

\subsection{A strict pivot-exchange subword}

For an analytic unit $t$, define $Z=E_{12}(t^{-1})$.  The identity
needed for comparing the two Gaussian words is
\[
 E_{21}(t)=J E_{21}(-t^{-1})D_2(t^{-1})D_1(t)E_{12}(t^{-1}).
 \tag{GB9}
\]
It has the following explicit strict native witness:
\[
 \begin{split}
 Q^{\rm piv}_t(g)={}&
 Q^{\rm W}_{21;t}\bigl(E_{21}(-t)Zg\bigr)
 +Q^{\rm src}_{21;-t,t}(Zg)\\
 &+Q^{\rm tar}_{21;-1,t^{-1}}\bigl(D_1(t)Zg\bigr).
 \end{split}
 \tag{GB10}
\]
In particular, the native pair of the left word minus that of
the right word in (GB9) equals $\partial_2Q^{\rm piv}_t(g)$.

\begin{proof}
WNR with the two row labels interchanged gives
\[
 E_{21}(t)E_{12}(-t^{-1})E_{21}(t)
                     =D_1(t^{-1})D_2(t)J.
 \tag{GB11}
\]
Insert this relation after the prefix
$E_{21}(-t)E_{12}(t^{-1})$.  On the left the two adjacent
shear-inverse pairs cancel literally, leaving $E_{21}(t)$.
The strict discrepancy is the first term of (GB10).
Signed-permutation equivariance moves $J$ to the left of the
two diagonal factors, giving
\[
 J D_2(t^{-1})D_1(t)E_{21}(-t)E_{12}(t^{-1}).
 \tag{GB12}
\]
Apply NSC to
$D_1(t)E_{21}(-t)=E_{21}(-1)D_1(t)$, then MX to
$D_2(t^{-1})E_{21}(-1)=E_{21}(-t^{-1})D_2(t^{-1})$.
Their starting tuples are precisely the second and third
arguments in (GB10), and both orientations are positive.
The result is (GB9), proving the claimed full pair identity.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The entire Gaussian overlap witness}

On the whole ordinary overlap $N_{\alpha\gamma}=N_\alpha\cap
N_\gamma$, all of $\alpha,\gamma,\Delta,t,h$ are units.  Put
\[
 t=\gamma/\alpha,\quad h=\Delta/\alpha,\quad
 u=\beta/\alpha,\quad v=h/(\alpha t)=\Delta/(\alpha\gamma).
 \tag{GB13}
\]
Then
\[
 \gamma=\alpha t,\qquad \Delta/\gamma=h/t,
                   \qquad \delta/\gamma=u+v.
 \tag{GB14}
\]
The last coefficient can be zero; no unit assumption on $u$ or
$u+v$ is made.

Define actual intermediate tuples
\[
 \begin{split}
 g_0&=D_1(\alpha)D_2(h)E_{12}(u)f,\\
 g_1&=D_2(h)E_{12}(u)f,\qquad g_2=E_{12}(u)f,\\
 g_3&=D_2(h)E_{12}(v)E_{12}(u)f,\qquad
 g_4=E_{12}(v)E_{12}(u)f.
 \end{split}
 \tag{GB15}
\]
All invertible matrices here are defined on the whole overlap,
and every tuple has the same common zero set as $f$.  Set
\[
 \begin{split}
 Q_{\alpha\gamma}={}&Q^{\rm piv}_t(g_0)
 -Q^{\rm tar}_{12;(\alpha t)^{-1},\alpha}(g_1)
 -Q^{\rm src}_{12;v,h}(g_2)\\
 &+Q^{\rm sl}_{1;\alpha,t}(g_3)
 +Q^{\rm cs}_{1,\gamma;2,t^{-1}}(g_3)
 +Q^{\rm sl}_{2;h,t^{-1}}(g_4)
 +Q^{\rm add}_{12;u,v}(f).
 \end{split}
 \tag{GB16}
\]
This is a finite explicit sum of actual triples on
$W\cap N_{\alpha\gamma}$.  Its extending pair on the full,
unpunctured overlap is
\[
 \epsilon_{\alpha\gamma}=e_{v(\gamma),-v(t)}R^{r-2}
                              \in L_1^{r-1}(N_{\alpha\gamma}).
 \tag{GB17}
\]

\begin{lemma}[Exact two-chart native relation]
On the entire punctured overlap,
\[
          p_\alpha-p_\gamma
                =\partial_2Q_{\alpha\gamma}
                           +\epsilon_{\alpha\gamma}|_W.
 \tag{GB18}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Replace the leftmost factor $E_{21}(t)$ in (GB4) by (GB9),
using the first term of (GB16).  The remaining explicit word is
\[
 J E_{21}(-t^{-1})D_2(t^{-1})D_1(t)
                   E_{12}(t^{-1})D_1(\alpha)D_2(h)E_{12}(u).
 \tag{GB19}
\]
Move $E_{12}(t^{-1})$ through $D_1(\alpha)$ to the right by
the target relation, obtaining coefficient $(\alpha t)^{-1}$.
This uses the negative of MX2 at $g_1$, as written in (GB16).
Move that shear through $D_2(h)$ to the right by the source
relation, obtaining coefficient $v$; this uses the negative
of NSC3 at $g_2$.  We have now reached
\[
 J E_{21}(-t^{-1})D_2(t^{-1})D_1(t)D_1(\alpha)D_2(h)
                                       E_{12}(v)E_{12}(u).
 \tag{GB20}
\]
Merge the two consecutive row-one scalings by the SL loop with
temporal factors $\alpha,t$, evaluated at $g_3$.  Next swap
$D_2(t^{-1})$ and $D_1(\gamma)$ using the complete CS square
at the same $g_3$.  Its temporal order is first row one with
factor $\gamma$, then row two with factor $t^{-1}$.  It therefore
contributes exactly the positive error (GB17), not its reverse.
No other step has an extending error.

Merge the row-two scalings with temporal factors $h,t^{-1}$
at $g_4$, and merge $E_{12}(u)$ followed by $E_{12}(v)$ by
SAR8 at $f$.  The resulting word is
\[
 J E_{21}(-t^{-1})D_1(\gamma)D_2(h/t)E_{12}(u+v),
 \tag{GB21}
\]
which is exactly (GB5) by (GB14).  The actual witnesses of
these seven substitutions are precisely (GB16).  Common
prefixes and suffixes cancel in the entire native expressions.
This proves (GB18), including all metric transport, coefficient
zeros and mixed jets already present in the input witnesses.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Actual gluing on the whole original neighborhood}

Choose an ordinary smooth actual partition
$\theta_\alpha+\theta_\gamma=1$ subordinate to (GB3), and put
$\theta=\theta_\alpha$.  Write
$Q_{\alpha\gamma}=(A_2,B_2,M_2)$ and
$p_\nu=(p'_\nu,p''_\nu)$.  With the closed-support zero-extension
conventions of RAG, define
\[
 \begin{split}
 p'={}&\theta_\gamma p'_\gamma+\theta_\alpha p'_\alpha
                         +d'\theta\wedge A_2+d''\theta\wedge M_2,\\
 p''={}&\theta_\gamma p''_\gamma+\theta_\alpha p''_\alpha
                         +d''\theta\wedge B_2+d'\theta\wedge M_2,\\
 E={}&v(\Delta)R^s
              -d'\theta\wedge\epsilon'_{\alpha\gamma}
              -d''\theta\wedge\epsilon''_{\alpha\gamma}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{GB22}
\]
The pair $p$ is actual on $W$; the form $E$ is actual on all
of $N$.  The overlap error was already supplied across $|f=0|$.
The support of each differentiated partition factor is closed
in $N$ and contained in the whole ordinary overlap, so its
product with an unbounded analytic-unit expression extends
by zero.  No additional growth condition is used.

\begin{theorem}[An analytic two-row block has a whole-neighborhood relative primitive]
For any original-$K$ data (GB1), in every rank $r\ge2$, the
explicit actual forms (GB22) satisfy
\[
                      G_{Mf}-G_f=\partial_1p+E|_W.
 \tag{GB23}
\]
Only one finite ordinary two-chart cover is used.  Its overlap
compatibility is constructed by (GB10) and (GB16), not assumed.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Equations (GB6) and (GB18) are exactly the hypotheses of the
two-open RAG theorem with $p_0=p_\gamma$, $p_1=p_\alpha$.
Its complete mixed partition computation is (GB22), proving
(GB23).  If either pivot chart is empty, the other chart covers
$N$ and (GB6) already gives the conclusion; the overlap sums
are empty.  Thus no exceptional global pivot is required.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Original-support Green forms and finite supplied block products}

Let the original global tuples $s_0,s_1$ be regular sections of
the same $r$-fold sum of the same metrized line bundle on $X/K$.
Retain
\[
 Z=[Y_1]-[Y_0],\quad U=X^{\rm an}\setminus|Z|,\quad
 V=X^{\rm an}\setminus(|Y_0|\cup|Y_1|),\quad
 C=(|Y_0|\cup|Y_1|)\cap U.
 \tag{GB24}
\]
Suppose an ordinary neighborhood $N\subset U$ of $C$ carries
one analytic block (GB1) with the actual section identity
$s_1=Ms_0$ on all of $N$.  Then (GB23) supplies the RSP data
on $N\cap V$ and $N$ without an additional path-overlap hypothesis.
Choose the frozen ordinary cutoff $\chi$ on $U$ supported in
$N$ and equal to one near $C$.  Set
\[
        g_Z=G_1-G_0-d'(\chi p')-d''(\chi p'')\quad\hbox{on }V
 \tag{GB25}
\]
with the RSP closed-support extensions.  Near $C$ this agrees
with the actual form $E$, so it glues to an actual
$g_Z\in B^{r-1,r-1}(U)$.

For every actual compact
$\eta\in B_c^{n-r,n-r}(X^{\rm an})$, the frozen support lemma
gives $\operatorname{supp}D\eta\Subset V$.  The two correction
products in (GB25) therefore have compact support in $V$;
actual Stokes annihilates them.  The individual frozen CI
identities give
\[
                    \int_Ug_Z\wedge D\eta=-\int_Z\eta.
 \tag{GB26}
\]
The original cycle, field, absolute value and exact support
complement are unchanged.  The original CI0 condition is not
modified: this is a separate extension construction when its
geometric certificate is provided.

The same conclusion holds if $s_1=M_m\cdots M_1s_0$ on $N$
is a supplied finite product of analytic invertible two-row
blocks, with possibly different row pairs at different steps.
Apply (GB23) to each actual intermediate tuple.  Every step
has the same common zero set, and summing its actual $p$ and
extending $E$ gives the required whole-neighborhood pair and
residue.  This finite product statement does not infer that
an arbitrary analytic $\mathrm{GL}_r$ matrix has such a global
factorization.

\paragraph{Exact scope and topology.}
The neighborhood need not be closed in $X$ or separated there
from the remaining support.  It is required to lie in $U$ and
to carry the displayed analytic block identity.  Thus the
certificate is analytic and directly checkable on the original
space; an ideal-equality claim without this matrix is not its
substitute.  No ordinary-open descent from a rational cover,
field extension, analytic root, coefficient bound or universal
unit majorant is used.

All expressions have finite local actual presentations.  The
whole-presentation zero relations, including non-product units
and moving-puncture refinements, are retained by each exact
input and by the explicit word substitutions.  This constructs
actual forms, not a class in an Aeppli quotient or a completion.
It does not prove a global continuous right inverse, a full
lower-middle inherited-topology theorem, arbitrary higher
matrix-word coherence, or the general homologically trivial
cycle case of Strong G.  It also supplies none of the additional
Beilinson--Bloch height axioms automatically.
