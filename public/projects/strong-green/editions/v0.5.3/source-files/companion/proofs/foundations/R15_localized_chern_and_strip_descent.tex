\section{Localized normal Chern classes and the first two Bott--Chern strips}
\label{r15m:section}

Let $X/K$ be smooth integral projective, let
$j:C\hookrightarrow X$ be a nonempty smooth integral closed
center of codimension $c\geq2$, and write
\[
 q:Y=\operatorname{Bl}_C X\longrightarrow X,
 \quad i:E=\mathbf P_C(N_{C/X})\hookrightarrow Y,
 \quad \pi:E\longrightarrow C.
\tag{SD15.1}\label{r15m:setup}
\]
Projectivization parametrizes lines.  Thus
$\mathcal O_Y(-E)|_E=\mathcal O_E(1)$.
All spaces, ordinary sheaves, tests, and degrees are over
the original $K$.  A disconnected smooth center will be
handled component by component after the integral case.

\begin{lemma}[A smooth divisor curvature with prescribed local support]
\label{r15m:localized-metric}
Let $V/K$ be smooth integral projective, let $D\subset V$
be an effective Cartier divisor, and let $W$ be any ordinary
open neighborhood of $D^{\mathrm{an}}$.  The line bundle
$\mathcal O_V(-D)$ admits a smooth metric whose curvature
$h$ is separately closed and satisfies
\[
                         \operatorname{Supp}(h)\subset W.
\tag{SD15.2}\label{r15m:localized-support}
\]
It may be chosen to agree near $D$ with any prescribed
initial smooth metric.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose that initial smooth metric and let $h_0$ be its
curvature.  On $V\setminus D$, the inverse of the canonical
section of $\mathcal O_V(D)$ is a rational frame $t$ of
$\mathcal O_V(-D)$.  It is not asserted to be a global
regular section.  The metric weight
$g_0=-\log\|t\|_0$ is smooth on $V^{\mathrm{an}}\setminus
D^{\mathrm{an}}$ and satisfies $Dg_0=h_0$ there.

Choose an ordinary smooth scalar $\lambda$ equal to one on
an ordinary neighborhood of $D^{\mathrm{an}}$, with
$\operatorname{Supp}(\lambda)\subset W$.
Compactness of the divisor and ordinary smooth partitions
of unity give this cutoff with the indicated closed support.
The scalar
\[
             u=-(1-\lambda)g_0
                  \quad\hbox{on }V^{\mathrm{an}}\setminus D^{\mathrm{an}}
\]
extends by zero to a global smooth scalar: it is identically
zero on a whole neighborhood of $D$ before extension.
Modify the additive weights of the initial metric by $u$.
Its curvature is $h=h_0+Du$ and it agrees with $h_0$ near
$D$.  Wherever $\lambda$ is identically zero, the new
weight in the frame $t$ is $g_0+u=0$, so $h=0$ on that
ordinary open.  Hence
$\operatorname{Supp}(h)\subset\operatorname{Supp}(\lambda)
\subset W$.  Separate closedness follows from the smooth
curvature construction.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Extending positive normal Chern powers times Aeppli classes]
\label{r15m:localized-extension}
In \eqref{r15m:setup}, let
$\alpha\in B^{u,v}(C^{\mathrm{an}})$ satisfy $D\alpha=0$,
where $u,v\geq0$, and let $\ell\geq1$.
There is a global actual $D$-closed form
$\Theta\in B^{u+\ell,v+\ell}(Y^{\mathrm{an}})$ whose
exceptional restriction has Aeppli class
\[
                  [i^*\Theta]=[\xi]^\ell\wedge\pi^*[\alpha],
\tag{SD15.3}\label{r15m:extension-class}
\]
where $\xi$ is any smooth first Chern representative of
$\mathcal O_E(1)$.  More precisely, a smooth representative
$h_E=i^*h$ of this class can be chosen so that
$i^*\Theta=h_E^\ell\wedge\pi^*\alpha$ literally.
No closed extension of $\alpha$ to all of $X$ is assumed.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The frozen degreewise extension theorem for an algebraic
closed immersion gives an actual
$\widetilde\alpha\in B^{u,v}(X)$ with
$j^*\widetilde\alpha=\alpha$.
Its derivative has zero restriction to $C$:
$j^*D\widetilde\alpha=D\alpha=0$.
The frozen ordinary immersion-germ isomorphism therefore
makes $D\widetilde\alpha$ zero on an ordinary neighborhood
$U$ of $C^{\mathrm{an}}$.
This is a vanishing germ for the derivative of a degreewise
extension, not a global closed ambient lifting theorem.

Apply Lemma~\ref{r15m:localized-metric} on $Y$ with
$D=E$ and $W=q^{-1}(U)$.  Let $h$ be the resulting smooth
curvature of $\mathcal O_Y(-E)$ and define
\[
                   \Theta=h^\ell\wedge q^*\widetilde\alpha.
\tag{SD15.4}\label{r15m:extension-form}
\]
It is a global actual form.  Since $h$ is separately closed,
\[
          D\Theta=h^\ell\wedge q^*(D\widetilde\alpha)=0:
\]
on $q^{-1}(U)$ the second factor is zero, and off that open
the first factor has zero germ by its support condition.
Restriction gives the stated literal identity with $h_E=i^*h$.

For the class comparison, $h_E$ and any other smooth
curvature $\xi$ on the same line differ by $Df$ for a
global smooth scalar $f$.  Write
$h_E^\ell-\xi^\ell=Df\wedge A$, where
$A=\sum_{k=0}^{\ell-1}h_E^k\xi^{\ell-1-k}$ is smooth
and separately closed.  The form
$\gamma=A\wedge\pi^*\alpha$ is $D$-closed.
The explicit identity
\[
             Df\wedge\gamma
                =d'(d''f\wedge\gamma)+d''(f\,d'\gamma)
\tag{SD15.5}\label{r15m:metric-Aeppli}
\]
shows that the difference is an Aeppli boundary.
It need not be a $D$-boundary, because $d'\gamma$ and
$d''\gamma$ need not vanish.  This proves
\eqref{r15m:extension-class}.  If a model metric is used
instead, its ratio is PWS and the same actual identity
applies.  The whole construction is native over $K$.
\end{proof}

We next state the exact mixed-bidegree sequence needed for
the strip application.  The frozen Round 13 source
\texttt{analytic\_descent/retracted\_modification\_descent.tex},
Theorem \texttt{r13d:connecting}, states its displayed
sequence only in diagonal degree.  The underlying relative
double-complex isomorphism and degreewise extension are in
all bidegrees.  The following proof checks the extension
directly on representatives; it is not a claim that the
mixed formula was already written in the frozen source.

\begin{proposition}[The mixed Aeppli--Bott--Chern connecting sequence]
\label{r15m:mixed-connecting}
For any $a,b\geq0$, the sequence
\[
 \begin{split}
 H_{\mathrm A}^{a,b}(Y)\oplus H_{\mathrm A}^{a,b}(C)
 &\xrightarrow{i^*-\pi^*}H_{\mathrm A}^{a,b}(E)
  \xrightarrow{\partial_D}H_{\mathrm{BC}}^{a+1,b+1}(X)\\
 &\xrightarrow{(q^*,j^*)}
 H_{\mathrm{BC}}^{a+1,b+1}(Y)
                       \oplus H_{\mathrm{BC}}^{a+1,b+1}(C)
 \end{split}
\tag{SD15.6}\label{r15m:mixed-sequence}
\]
is exact at the middle two terms.  All groups are actual.
For a $D$-closed representative $\theta$ on $E$,
\[
                   \partial_D[\theta]
                          =[T(D\widetilde\theta)],
 \qquad i^*\widetilde\theta=\theta,
\tag{SD15.7}\label{r15m:mixed-connecting-formula}
\]
where $T$ is the frozen inverse of relative pullback
$q^*: \ker j^*\xrightarrow{\sim}\ker i^*$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The frozen relative isomorphism exists degreewise and
commutes with $d'$ and $d''$.  A degreewise actual
extension $\widetilde\theta$ exists by the same frozen
closed-immersion extension theorem.
Because $i^*D\widetilde\theta=D\theta=0$,
its derivative lies in $\ker i^*$.
Its descent under $T$ is separately closed and vanishes
on $C$, so defines the Bott--Chern class in
\eqref{r15m:mixed-connecting-formula}.

If the extension is changed, its difference is relative,
equal to $q^*\zeta$ for an actual form on $X$.
The descended curvature changes by $D\zeta$.
If $\theta$ changes by $d'\mu+d''\tau$, extend the two
forms degreewise and change the extension by the same
boundary; its $D$ is zero.  Negative degrees are zero,
so the argument also applies on an outer edge.
This proves that $\partial_D$ is well defined.

A $D$-closed form on $Y$ maps to zero, using itself as
the extension.  For a $D$-closed form $\gamma$ on $C$,
extend it to an actual $\eta$ on $X$ and extend
$\pi^*\gamma$ by $q^*\eta$.  Its descended derivative
is $D\eta$, so its Bott--Chern class is zero.
Conversely, if $\partial_D[\theta]=0$, write
$T(D\widetilde\theta)=D\eta$ on $X$.
Then $\chi=\widetilde\theta-q^*\eta$ is $D$-closed
on $Y$, and $\gamma=j^*\eta$ is $D$-closed on $C$
because the descended curvature restricts to zero.
The equality $\theta=i^*\chi+\pi^*\gamma$ proves
exactness at $H_{\mathrm A}^{a,b}(E)$.

The class of \eqref{r15m:mixed-connecting-formula} has
zero pullback class on $Y$ and zero restriction on $C$.
Conversely, let a separately closed actual $\Omega$
represent a class in the kernel of $(q^*,j^*)$, and choose
actual primitives
$q^*\Omega=D\beta$ and $j^*\Omega=D\zeta$.
Extend $\zeta$ to an actual $\eta$ on $X$ and put
$\Omega_0=\Omega-D\eta$, $\beta_0=\beta-q^*\eta$.
Then $j^*\Omega_0=0$ and $D\beta_0=q^*\Omega_0$.
The form $\theta=i^*\beta_0$ is $D$-closed, and using
$\beta_0$ as extension in
\eqref{r15m:mixed-connecting-formula} sends its class
to $[\Omega_0]=[\Omega]$.
This proves exactness at the Bott--Chern term by explicit
representatives in bidegrees $(a,b)$ and $(a+1,b+1)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The normalized exceptional quotient in the first strip]
\label{r15m:normalized-quotient}
If $\min(a,b)\leq1$, there is a canonical isomorphism
\[
 \frac{H_{\mathrm A}^{a,b}(E)}
      {i^*H_{\mathrm A}^{a,b}(Y)+\pi^*H_{\mathrm A}^{a,b}(C)}
 \ \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\
 \ker\bigl(q^*:H_{\mathrm{BC}}^{a+1,b+1}(X)
                 \longrightarrow H_{\mathrm{BC}}^{a+1,b+1}(Y)\bigr).
\tag{SD15.8}\label{r15m:quotient}
\]
For a supplied primitive $D\beta=q^*\Omega$, choose
$D\zeta=j^*\Omega$ on $C$.  Its corresponding
exceptional class is
$[i^*\beta-\pi^*\zeta]$ in the displayed quotient.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The projective bundle $\pi:E\to C$ is a projective
surjective map between normal integral projective varieties
with constant fiber dimension.  The frozen theorem
\texttt{r14j:equidimensional} makes its pullback on
$H_{\mathrm{BC}}^{a+1,b+1}$ injective because the smaller
bidegree is at most two.  The commutative square gives
$\pi^*j^*[\Omega]=i^*q^*[\Omega]$.
Thus $q^*[\Omega]=0$ implies $j^*[\Omega]=0$, and
$\ker q^*=\ker(q^*,j^*)$.
Proposition~\ref{r15m:mixed-connecting} now proves
\eqref{r15m:quotient} and the representative formula.

If an explicit center primitive is desired, choose the
frozen finite normal multisection $h:T\to E$ for which
$f=\pi h:T\to C$ is finite dominant of native degree
$e$, and take
\[
                   \zeta=e^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}_f(h^*i^*\beta).
\]
The primitive bidegree is $(a,b)$ with minimum at most
one, so the actual finite-strip trace and its all-actual-test
derivative identity apply.  The degree $e$ includes any
constant-field factor.  No positive-dimensional actual
trace is used for this normalization.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Smooth-center blowup injectivity in both first Bott--Chern strips]
\label{r15m:strip-injectivity}
Assume the independently proved projective-bundle Aeppli
edge and first-strip formulas of
Theorems~\ref{r15s:edge} and \ref{r15s:first-strip}.
Let $X/K$ be smooth integral projective and let
$C\hookrightarrow X$ be any smooth closed nowhere dense
center.  Then for $q:Y=\operatorname{Bl}_C X\to X$,
\[
                  q^*:H_{\mathrm{BC,act}}^{u,v}(X)
                          \longrightarrow H_{\mathrm{BC,act}}^{u,v}(Y)
                  \quad\hbox{is injective whenever }\min(u,v)\leq2.
\tag{SD15.9}\label{r15m:strip-injection}
\]
Equivalently, for $u,v\geq1$ in this range, every supplied
actual identity $D\beta=q^*\Omega$, with $\Omega$
separately closed, implies a global ordinary actual
$\Psi\in B^{u-1,v-1}(X)$ with $D\Psi=\Omega$.
Finite compositions of such blowups have the same property.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
An empty center or a Cartier center gives an isomorphism.
First suppose the center is integral and of codimension
at least two.  If $u=0$ or $v=0$, there are no $D$-boundaries
in that bidegree; faithful actual pullback on the dense
isomorphism locus, together with the frozen dense-faithfulness
lemma, already gives the assertion.

For $u,v\geq1$, put $(a,b)=(u-1,v-1)$.
Then $\min(a,b)\leq1$ and the kernel is the quotient
in \eqref{r15m:quotient}.
If one of $a,b$ is zero, the edge formula says that its
numerator is entirely the image from $C$, so the quotient
is zero.  If $b=1$ and $a\geq1$, the first-strip formula
says that the numerator is
\[
             \pi^*H_{\mathrm A}^{a,1}(C)
                   +[\xi]\wedge\pi^*H_{\mathrm A}^{a-1,0}(C).
\]
The first summand lies in the denominator already.
Proposition~\ref{r15m:localized-extension}, applied with
$\ell=1$ and $\alpha$ of degree $(a-1,0)$, puts the
second summand in $i^*H_{\mathrm A}^{a,1}(Y)$.
The quotient is again zero.  The case $a=1$ follows by
the conjugate first-strip formula and the same localized
extension.  These cases exhaust the required bidegrees.

A smooth center is a disjoint union of its smooth integral
connected components.  Blowing up those components in
sequence gives the same blowup.  The integral-center
conclusion applies to each component, including different
codimensions.  Composing the resulting injections proves
the general case and the final assertion.

The primitive formulation is the definition of vanishing
of the actual Bott--Chern class.  It can also be recovered
from the explicit representative proof above: normalize the
center primitive, express the exceptional class in the two
denominator images, extend its first-boundary coefficients
degreewise to $Y$, and apply the frozen relative inverse
$T$ to the resulting form with zero exceptional restriction.
All steps are in ordinary actual sheaves and give an
identity of actual forms.  Its pairings therefore hold
against every compact actual test by actual Stokes, not
only against the smooth tests used to construct the special
projective-bundle PWS operators.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Dominant injectivity in both first Bott--Chern strips]
\label{r15m:dominant-strips}
Under the inputs of Theorem~\ref{r15m:strip-injectivity},
let $X/K$ be smooth integral projective and let
$f:V\to X$ be a dominant morphism from a normal integral
projective $K$-variety.  There is no restriction on
$\dim X$ or on the relative dimension of $f$.  Then
\[
                  f^*:H_{\mathrm{BC,act}}^{u,v}(X)
                       \longrightarrow H_{\mathrm{BC,act}}^{u,v}(V)
                  \quad\hbox{is injective for }\min(u,v)\leq2.
\tag{SD15.10}\label{r15m:dominant-strip-injection}
\]
For $u,v\geq1$ this gives a global ordinary actual primitive
on $X$ from any supplied actual primitive of $f^*\Omega$
on $V$.  No proper actual trace along $f$ is asserted.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Use the frozen finite-multisection diagram of Round 14,
\path{foundations_audit/dominant_degree_one.tex},
Lemma \texttt{r14e:flattened-diagram}:
\[
 \begin{array}{ccc}
 T&\xrightarrow{a}&V\\
 {\scriptstyle g}\downarrow&&\downarrow{\scriptstyle f}\\
 X'&\xrightarrow{b}&X.
 \end{array}
\tag{SD15.11}\label{r15m:dominant-diagram}
\]
Here $T$ is normal integral projective, $X'$ is smooth
integral projective, $g$ is finite dominant, and $b$ is
a composition of blowups in smooth nowhere dense centers.
The construction uses flattening of $f$ over a blowup of
the smooth target, characteristic-zero principalization
of that blowup ideal, and a finite normal multisection
after the full flat base change.  It places no dimension
bound on $V$ and does not assume that $f$ is finite over
any specified complement.

Suppose first $u,v\geq1$ and let
$f^*\Omega=D\beta$ with
$\beta\in B^{u-1,v-1}(V)$.  Actual pullback gives
$g^*b^*\Omega=D(a^*\beta)$.
The smaller primitive bidegree is at most one, so the
frozen actual finite-strip trace is defined on $a^*\beta$.
Set
\[
       \beta'=(\deg_K g)^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}_g(a^*\beta)
                              \in B^{u-1,v-1}(X').
\tag{SD15.12}\label{r15m:dominant-primitive}
\]
Its all-actual-test derivative identity and the native
finite degree formula give $D\beta'=b^*\Omega$ as
actual forms.  Only the primitive is traced; no trace
on an arbitrary curvature of bidegree $(u,v)$ is used.
Apply Theorem~\ref{r15m:strip-injectivity} successively
to the smooth-center factors of $b$.  This makes
$[\Omega]=0$ on $X$ and gives its global actual primitive.

If $u=0$ or $v=0$, vanishing of $f^*[\Omega]$ is
literal vanishing of $f^*\Omega$.  The diagram gives
$g^*b^*\Omega=0$.  Faithfulness of finite actual pullback
gives $b^*\Omega=0$, and the zero-degree case of
Theorem~\ref{r15m:strip-injectivity} gives $\Omega=0$.
Every degree in \eqref{r15m:dominant-primitive} is the
full native function-field degree over $K$, including
constant-field factors.  The use of a possibly singular
normal $T$ is within the frozen finite trace and genuine
actual pullback interfaces.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Limits and future use.}
Proposition~\ref{r15m:localized-extension} applies to any
$D$-closed actual class on the center and any positive
normal Chern power, beyond the first strip.  It supplies
extensions of such explicit exceptional classes; it does
not assert that they span higher exceptional Aeppli groups.
The completed Bott--Chern descent theorem is confined to
$\min(u,v)\leq2$.  Higher primitive bidegrees require
additional input.  Neither an arbitrary proper actual
pushforward nor a converse to the sufficient curvature
criterion for strong Green forms is used or asserted.
