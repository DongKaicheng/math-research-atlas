\section{Actual deletion models and the residual core for all surface products}
\label{r20dp:section}

\paragraph{Status.}
This source reconstructs, with explicit actual maps, the one-prime
coordinate-deletion route proposed in R9. It does not take the old
DG/GL claims as frozen inputs, and it does not use the stronger
all-prime decorated-transition assertion DT/UP. The statements below
are author-proved candidates pending independent review of this
source. The binary geometric and transport lemmas are those proved
and reviewed in PX20; their fan proofs have no dimension restriction.
Only the surface-motive and coefficient arguments below require
factor dimensions at most two.

\subsection{The actual ordered product and its complete boundary}

Let $R$ be a complete DVR with finite residue field $k$, let $m\geq1$,
and let
$\mathcal X_1,\ldots,\mathcal X_m$ be regular projective reduced
strictly semistable models of smooth projective geometrically
integral curves or surfaces. Work after one finite unramified
extension splitting their finitely many connected strata and the
geometric Neron--Severi actions of all surface components. Further
finite unramified extensions may be made to define a finite list
of rational Chow correspondences; they will be descended at the
end. Fix total orders of the original components.

At a local product stratum with $r_a$ branches in factor $a$, put
\[
 \begin{split}
 N&=\{(v^{(1)},\ldots,v^{(m)})\in\textstyle\bigoplus_a
          \mathbf Z^{r_a}:\sum v^{(a)}=\sum v^{(b)}\},\\
 n_{\mathbf i}&=(e_{i_1},\ldots,e_{i_m}).
 \end{split}
 \tag{DP20.1}
\]
The staircase fan consists of cones on chains in the product order.
A maximal chain changes one coordinate at a time. Its first ray
and successive differences form an integral basis of $N$; every
vertical ray has height one. These fans agree on faces.

One may construct the global model inductively by the projective
binary staircase construction of PX20. Order the components of
the previous product lexicographically. On any local simplex
they already form a chain, and the induced order is its path
order. Interleaving that chain with the next factor's chain gives
exactly the full product staircase, independently of this binary
parenthesization. The resulting projective model $\mathcal D$
is regular strictly semistable, with components $D_{\mathbf i}$
and no additional vertical component labels.

\begin{lemma}[Full-boundary component towers]
\label{r20dp:towers}
Every component $D_{\mathbf i}$ is obtained from
$B_{\mathbf i}=\prod_a X_{a,i_a}$ by a finite tower of blowups
of smooth closed strata of the current reduced SNC boundary.
Its entire final reduced boundary is exactly
\[
       \bigcup_{\mathbf h\ne\mathbf i}
                      (D_{\mathbf i}\cap D_{\mathbf h}),
 \tag{DP20.2}
\]
including every connected closed higher stratum.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Induct on $m$. Pull the previous component tower back by the
new smooth component; this is again a tower of boundary-stratum
blowups. Apply the dimension-free binary quotient-star calculation
PX20.4--PX20.5 to this smooth pair and the new component. Its
centers are intersections of one boundary divisor from each
side. They remain smooth because the relevant two-face is not
subdivided before its own step, and stratum blowup preserves
the SNC boundary. This proves the tower assertion.

In the quotient star of the component ray $n_{\mathbf i}$,
each final boundary divisor corresponds to a two-dimensional
cone $\langle n_{\mathbf i},n_{\mathbf h}\rangle$.
This is exactly the intersection with the actual component
$D_{\mathbf h}$. Conversely every actual neighboring component
gives that quotient-star ray. The component tower just constructed
has that final fan; all its original and exceptional rays persist.
Thus it has no extra unlabelled boundary divisor and misses none.
Higher cones give the higher intersections in the same way.
The identifications are over the original component product and
are the identity on the common dense open, so they glue over all
charts. Disconnected intersections are always kept componentwise.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Deletion morphisms, not unmodified remaining products}

For a subset $I$ of factor indices, let $W_{-I,\mathbf i_{-I}}$
be the actual staircase component of the remaining factors.
An empty remaining product means $\operatorname{Spec}k$.
Deleting coordinates in (DP20.1) sends every staircase cone into
a staircase cone: remove repeated vertices of the projected
chain. The corresponding toroidal morphisms glue and give
\[
 f_I:D_{\mathbf i}\longrightarrow
 B_{I,\mathbf i}:=
       \left(\prod_{j\in I}X_{j,i_j}\right)
                     \times W_{-I,\mathbf i_{-I}}.
 \tag{DP20.3}
\]
These maps are proper birational morphisms between smooth
projective varieties and commute with further deletion.
Writing $U_j=X_{j,i_j}\setminus\partial X_{j,i_j}$, they are
isomorphisms over
$\prod_{j\in I}U_j\times W_{-I}$. This follows directly from
the local fan: each such factor has just one vertical ray,
so its fan is $\Delta^0$ and it remains a smooth coefficient.
Properness follows also from properness of the source and
separatedness of the target over $k$.

A double stratum whose two labels agree in all indices $I$ maps
properly birationally to the corresponding connected double
stratum of $B_I$, and is an isomorphism off the indicated factor
boundaries. Here no double component is hidden over a fixed-factor
boundary: the monomial of another branch has valuation zero on
both defining rays and hence is invertible at the generic orbit
of their two-cone. The projection of this two-cone is still a
two-cone, because the remaining labels are different. Over the
smooth coefficient locus the map is the literal product, of
generic degree one. A double changing a fixed index maps into
that factor's boundary. This is the exact multi-factor version
of the no-hidden-double lemma PX20.

\subsection{The commuting transcendental projector lattice}

Let $J\subset\{1,\ldots,m\}$ be the indices of the surface
factors. On the surface component $X_{j,i_j}$ choose the
self-adjoint rational algebraic projector $p_j$ onto
$H^2_{\rm tr}$, zero in other degrees, as in PX20/KMP.
For $I\subset J$ put
\[
 p_I=\boxtimes_{j\in I}p_j,\qquad
 P_I=f_I^*(p_I\boxtimes1)f_{I*},\qquad P_{\varnothing}=1.
 \tag{DP20.4}
\]
These are self-adjoint algebraic cohomological projectors, since
$f_{I*}f_I^*=1$. All statements about their products below are
cohomological identities of actual rational correspondences.
No unproved Chow-idempotent commutation is asserted.

\begin{lemma}[Projector lattice, with the actual deletion model]
\label{r20dp:projectors}
For all $I\subset J$,
\[
                 P_I=\prod_{j\in I}P_{\{j\}}.
 \tag{DP20.5}
\]
The individual projectors commute, including on every exceptional
cohomology summand. On the direct sum of components one keeps
the original labels $i_j$ distinct.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For distinct $j,k$, factor the common deletion map through
\[
 D\xrightarrow{f_j}X_j\times W_{-j}
       \xrightarrow{a}X_j\times X_k\times W_{-\{j,k\}},
 \tag{DP20.6}
\]
and through the analogous $f_k,b$. The map $a$ modifies only
the other coordinates and is an isomorphism away from
$\partial X_k$. In this paragraph $p_j$ denotes its exterior
product with the appropriate identity. The difference
\[
 P_j-f_{\{j,k\}}^*p_jf_{\{j,k\}*}
       =f_j^*p_j(1-a^*a_*)f_{j*}
 \tag{DP20.7}
\]
has image supported over $\partial X_k$: localization puts
$\ker a_*$ there, and the independent factor operator $p_j$
preserves this support. The projector $P_k$ kills every such
supported class, by (PX20.9) and Kunneth with supports.
On the pullback from the common base, $P_k$ acts as $p_k$ on
its factor, because $p_k$ commutes with the other deletion map
$b$ and $f_{k*}f_k^*=1$. Therefore
$P_kP_j=P_{\{j,k\}}$. Transposing gives the opposite order.

For the induction step replace the fixed factor $j$ by the set
$I$: use the common base with factors $I\cup\{k\}$ deleted
from the remaining model. The difference from its pullback is
supported over $\partial X_k$ and is killed by $P_k$.
On the common base the projectors act on distinct factors.
This proves (DP20.5). It retains every exceptional copy in
$W_{-I}$; replacing that variety by the unmodified product would
be invalid, as explicitly demonstrated by HE20.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The actual incidence on these projectors}

Let $A=\sigma\rho$ be the native actual restriction--Gysin
correspondence on the disjoint union of $D_{\mathbf i}$.
The full supported-cohomology transport lemma PX20.7--PX20.8
applies to every same-label double of (DP20.3).
For a double changing label $i_j$, restriction after $P_j$ is
zero because the structural map lands in $\partial X_{j,i_j}$.
Taking adjoints gives the corresponding Gysin statement.
Thus
\[
 AP_j=P_jA,\qquad
 A|_{\operatorname{im}P_I}
       =1_{\bigotimes_{j\in I}t_2(X_{j,i_j})}\boxtimes A_{-I}.
 \tag{DP20.8}
\]
For the nonzero off-diagonal terms, pushing back the actual
same-label double to its deletion-model double uses
$f'_*f'^*=1$ and gives precisely its restriction--Gysin.
For a diagonal term use the same calculation at one endpoint;
the normal self-intersection is the negative sum of all incident
double-divisor classes, since the reduced total special fiber is
principal. This includes all true higher intersections. An edge
changing a fixed index is zero on that sector, as just proved.

Under the identification of $\operatorname{im}P_I$ with its
deletion-model realization, $P_k$ for $k\notin I$ becomes
$1_{p_I}\boxtimes P_k^{(-I)}$. One can see the identity directly
by factoring $f_{I\cup\{k\}}$ through $f_I$ and using
(DP20.5); it is not an inference from a dimension count.
Consequently the orthogonal projectors
\[
 R_I=P_I\prod_{k\in J\setminus I}(1-P_k),\qquad I\subset J,
 \tag{DP20.9}
\]
sum to the identity, commute with $A$, and satisfy
\[
 A|_{R_I}=1_{\bigotimes_{j\in I}t_2(X_{j,i_j})}
                 \boxtimes(A_{-I}|_{Q_{-I}}),
 \qquad Q_{-I}=\prod_{k\in J\setminus I}(1-P_k^{(-I)}).
 \tag{DP20.10}
\]

\subsection{A generator lemma that captures the exceptional copies}

We give the needed geometry in full rather than assume that the
complement of a higher-dimensional birational map is abelian.
For a smooth SNC pair $(P,\bigcup D_i)$, blowing up the closed
stratum $D_K$ gives, on every other closed stratum $D_L$,
\[
 \operatorname{Bl}_{D_{K\cup L}}D_L
 \quad(K\not\subset L),
 \qquad
 \mathbf P_{D_{K\cup L}}
      \left(\bigoplus_{i\in K\setminus L}O(D_i)|_{D_{K\cup L}}\right)
 \tag{DP20.11}
\]
for the nonexceptional and exceptional strata, respectively.
The first is omitted when $K\subset L$; the second is omitted
when $K\setminus L$ is empty. These are the direct coordinate
blowup formulas for an SNC ideal; Cartier blowups are identities.
Apply the blowup and projective-bundle Chow formulas to them
after every smooth projective test product. Induction yields
actual rational motive isomorphisms
\[
 u:M=\bigoplus_\alpha h(S_\alpha)\otimes\mathbb L^{t_\alpha}
            \longrightarrow h(D),
 \qquad v:h(D)\longrightarrow M,\qquad uv=1,
 \tag{DP20.12}
\]
where every $S_\alpha$ is an original closed product stratum and
$\mathbb L$ denotes the Lefschetz motive. Thus this summand
contributes $H^{q-2t_\alpha}(S_\alpha)(-t_\alpha)$ in degree
$q$; the integers $t_\alpha\geq0$ are the actual blowup and
projective-bundle exponents.
Each injection $u_\alpha$ is composed of structural pullback,
stratum Gysin, and powers of boundary or tautological line classes.
All these correspondences are relative to the original component
product. In particular, if the $j$th factor of $S_\alpha$ is
the full surface $X_{j,i_j}$, write
$S_\alpha=X_{j,i_j}\times M_\alpha$. Then $u_\alpha$ is a
correspondence relative to that surface coordinate.
More precisely, choose its class in the Chow group of the
relative fiber product using the actual structural graph classes,
boundary Chern operations, and refined-intersection compositions
that define the displayed decomposition injection. Proper
pushforward from that fiber product gives the ordinary
correspondence. Refined intersections retain their specified
support, and these constructions commute with restriction to
an open subset of the original base. This supplies relative
representatives, rather than merely an equality of the induced
cohomology actions on an open set.

\begin{lemma}[Exact deletion comparison for an actual generator]
\label{r20dp:generator}
For each such $u_\alpha$ there is an actual rational correspondence
$u_{\alpha,-j}$ from $M_\alpha$ to the exact deletion component
$W_{-j}$, with the same cohomological shift, such that
\[
 u_\alpha(p_j\boxtimes1)=
       f_j^*(p_j\boxtimes u_{\alpha,-j})
 \tag{DP20.13}
\]
on full geometric cohomology. In particular its image is contained
in $\operatorname{im}P_j$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Restrict the whole component tower to $U_j$. At its first stage
the smooth pair is $U_j$ times the remaining original pair.
Each later center is an intersection of boundary divisors.
By induction its restriction is either empty, if its original
image lies in $\partial X_j$, or $U_j$ times a closed boundary
stratum of the current remaining pair. Blowing up commutes with
this product. Normal bundles, tautological classes and every
structural injection in the blowup formula also restrict to
the corresponding exterior product. Thus the restricted tower
is $U_j\times R_j$ for one global smooth projective remaining
stratum-blowup tower $R_j\to B_{-j}^{\rm orig}$, and the
injection is $1_{U_j}\boxtimes u_{\alpha,R_j}$; a generator
that disappears has $u_{\alpha,R_j}=0$.

Deletion (DP20.3) identifies that same open piece with
$U_j\times W_{-j}$ over $U_j\times B_{-j}^{\rm orig}$.
This identifies $R_j$ with $W_{-j}$ over the original remaining
product, not just their abstract motives. Indeed, take a closed
point of $U_j$ and enlarge its finite residue extension to a
finite Galois extension. Restricting the product isomorphism
gives an isomorphism $R_j\simeq W_{-j}$ there. An isomorphism
over their common base is unique because it agrees with the
identity on their common dense birational open. Its conjugates
therefore agree, and it descends. The original isomorphism over
$U_j$ is the product of this descended map, by the same
uniqueness. Transfer $u_{\alpha,R_j}$ to $W_{-j}$ to define
$u_{\alpha,-j}$.

The actual correspondences $u_\alpha$ and
$f_j^*(1_{X_j}\boxtimes u_{\alpha,-j})$ are relative to $X_j$
and agree over $U_j$ in its relative Chow group: their graph,
Chern and refined-intersection constructions agree term by term
on the product tower just identified. This equality is not
inferred merely from equality of their cohomological actions.
Their difference
is represented by cycles over $\partial X_j$, by Chow
localization in that relative fiber product. For an integral
support $Z$, choose a smooth projective generically finite
alteration $q:\widetilde Z\to Z$ of total degree $d$.
The associated cohomological correspondence is
$d^{-1}b_*a^*$, with $a,b$ its source and target maps.
The surface coordinate of $a$ factors through a boundary curve
or a point, so its pullback of $p_jH^*(X_j)$ is zero by
(PX20.9). Hence this supported difference annihilates
$p_j\boxtimes1$. This is (DP20.13).

The alterations in the last paragraph are only a proof of a
cohomological supported-cycle identity, with full generic
degrees. They do not replace the fixed semistable witness and
are not claimed finite on any specified cycle complement.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The entire residual core is abelian type]
\label{r20dp:core}
Let $Q=\prod_{j\in J}(1-P_j)$ on a component $D$.
It is a homological direct summand of a finite sum of Tate
shifts of motives of products of abelian varieties. The same
assertion holds on every actual deletion model.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Split each original surface motive in (DP20.12) into its
$t_2$ and complementary Artin--Tate/Picard/Albanese pieces,
using the KMP refined decomposition. The finite Artin actions
have been split in the setup. Curves and points are of abelian
type with Tate shifts. Therefore write the actual finite source
as $M=M_{\rm ab}\oplus M_{\rm tr}$, where $M_{\rm ab}$ is of
abelian type and every original-product summand of $M_{\rm tr}$
has at least one full surface coefficient $t_2(X_j)$.
Choose one such index for that summand. Lemma
\ref{r20dp:generator} puts its image under $u$ in $P_j$,
so $Qu|_{M_{\rm tr}}=0$ on full cohomology. Hence
\[
 Q=Q u_{\rm ab}\operatorname{pr}_{\rm ab}v,
 \qquad
 u_Q=Q u_{\rm ab},\quad v_Q=\operatorname{pr}_{\rm ab}vQ,
 \qquad u_Qv_Q=Q.
 \tag{DP20.14}
\]
All displayed maps are actual rational correspondences. They
give the asserted direct summand in homological motives;
they do not require a Chow-level lift of a new idempotent.
Every deletion model has the same proved tower and boundary
properties, so the same argument applies to it.
\end{proof}

\subsection{One common coefficient prime and the actual inner inverse}

Choose one abelian variety $B$ containing, through finite products
and Tate shifts, all the finitely many abelian carriers in
(DP20.14) for all deletion models and component labels.
Deligne--Clozel Theorem 1 and Remark (b) give one invertible
prime $\ell$ at which numerical and geometric homological
equivalence coincide on every power of $B$. Native numerical
zero implies geometric numerical zero by finite-constant norms
and the full-degree projection formula. Therefore the finite
correspondence groups of these abelian carriers identify with
their numerical versions at this prime.

For clarity, the splitting step can be performed entirely on
those carriers. Put $e=v_Qu_Q$ on $M_{\rm ab}$. It is
idempotent in homological motives by (DP20.14), hence also in
numerical motives. For the degree-one map on the core put
$a=v_Q A u_Q$, with the appropriate target Tate shift.
In the numerical category, Jannsen semisimplicity gives an
inner inverse $g$ in the opposite rectangular corner:
$aga=a$. Choose a rational Chow representative of $g$.
The selected-prime comparison gives the same equality on
cohomology, and
\[
                 G_Q=u_Qg v_Q
 \tag{DP20.15}
\]
is an actual rational correspondence with $AG_QA=A$ on
the $Q$ sector. This argument does not presume that $e$
was an idempotent modulo rational equivalence.

For every sector (DP20.10), tensor the corresponding inverse
on $Q_{-I}$ with the identity of its fixed tensor of surface
transcendental coefficients. Transport by $f_I^*,f_{I*}$ and
$R_I$, and sum over the finitely many $I$ and fixed component
labels. This gives a rational algebraic correspondence $G$,
of degree minus one, such that
\[
                         A^HG^HA^H=A^H
             \quad\hbox{on all geometric cohomology.}
 \tag{DP20.16}
\]
The trivial empty-factor core has $A=0$ and needs no inverse.
The argument retains cohomological degree and Tate labels;
one may construct each degree corner and compose with the
algebraic projectors before summing to the required ordinary
correspondence codimension. Only the abelian coefficient
operator is inverted. No equivalence theorem on an arbitrary
tensor product of $t_2$ motives is asserted.
The degree projectors here are available explicitly: transport
the tensor products of the curve/surface Kunneth projectors
through (DP20.12), retaining the displayed Lefschetz exponents.
No general Chow--Kunneth conjecture on higher-dimensional
components is invoked.

If choices were defined over a further finite residue extension
of the fixed model, normalized Galois averaging descends $G$:
(DP20.16) is linear in $G$ and $A$ descends. This gives one
suitable auxiliary prime for the whole model. It is not an
all-prime assertion and does not use intersections of arbitrary
positive-density prime sets.

\begin{theorem}[Model splitting for every finite product of curves and surfaces]
\label{r20dp:model}
Under the precise model hypotheses of this section, the actual
ordered staircase incidence has a rational algebraic inner
inverse on full cohomology at one invertible prime, as in
(DP20.16). Hence the AK19 lifting defects and the CR19 supported
defects vanish in every degree. The same result holds after
product with an arbitrary smooth projective $R$-scheme.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The inverse has just been constructed. Actual Chow
correspondences preserve the actual algebraic class spans
and numerical equivalence. Thus $x-G^HA^Hx$ is a lift in
$\ker A^H$ of the same numerical class whenever
$p(x)\in\ker A^N$. This is the exact AK19 correction and
gives $\Delta_A=0$. On the supported defect, if an actual
class $\alpha=A^H\beta$, then $G^H\alpha$ is actual and
$A^HG^H\alpha=\alpha$, giving its required numerical
preimage. With a smooth projective coefficient $V$, the
incidence and inverse are $\Delta_{V_0}\boxtimes A$ and
$\Delta_{V_0}\boxtimes G$. Their identity holds on all
Kunneth summands, so mixed Chow classes are included.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Full local strong G on the product domain]
\label{r20dp:green}
Assume the frozen AK19, OW19, VT17, FD17 and R16 descent
interfaces. Fix $K/\mathbf Q_p$ finite and its absolute value.
Let $m\geq1$ and let $X_1,\ldots,X_m/K$ be arbitrary smooth projective
geometrically integral curves or surfaces. Let $V/K$ be
smooth projective geometrically integral with a smooth
projective model after a finite valued extension, allowing
$V=\operatorname{Spec}K$. On
\[
                      X=V\times\prod_{a=1}^mX_a
 \tag{DP20.17}
\]
every specified geometrically homologically trivial rational
cycle, in every codimension $1\le r\le\dim X$, has an
ordinary-actual strong Green form on its exact support
complement, with the identity against every actual compact
test form. This holds afresh after every finite valued
extension and for all new cycles over that extension.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $\dim X\leq2$, the frozen divisor and zero-cycle endpoints
give every codimension in the stated range. Suppose henceforth
that $\dim X\geq3$, so the model criterion AK19 applies.
Take a common finite valued extension and separate projective
strictly semistable alterations of the low-dimensional factors,
repairing later ramified base changes by Hartl 2.2 before
forming the ordered product. The good model of $V$ persists
under further extension. Theorem \ref{r20dp:model} applies
to the resulting actual fixed witness.

Every cohomological degree of each curve or surface satisfies
weight--monodromy; the good factor is pure and unramified.
The tensor product rule and Kunneth give OW19's odd-weight
hypothesis in every needed degree, so the actual inertia
cycle image is zero on this witness. AK19 and the model
splitting annihilate the pulled-back actual Green obstruction.
The R16 dominant detection and finite trace descend it by
the full field and generic degrees, using the original
absolute value. VT17 produces the asserted form with exact
support and all actual tests. No alteration is assumed finite
on the specified complement, and no arbitrary proper
pushforward of analytic actual forms is used.

For any further finite valued extension $K'/K$, the factors
remain smooth projective and geometrically integral, and $V_{K'}$
still has potential smooth projective good reduction. Run this
same construction with $K'$ as the original valued field and
with an arbitrary newly specified homological cycle over $K'$.
All steps and the full-degree descent use its given absolute
value. No assertion that this new cycle descends to $K$ is
needed or made.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Height consequence and honest scope.}
For a number field and a finite product of arbitrary smooth
projective curves and surfaces, the local theorem applies at
every finite place, so frozen PH16 gives the canonical real
Beilinson--Bloch height on the full geometrically homologically
trivial complementary Chow groups. One may additionally
include a fixed smooth projective factor that has potential
smooth projective good reduction at every finite place.
The theorem is applied again for new cycles after each finite
field extension; they are not assumed pulled back from the
original field. The general strong Green conjecture and
arbitrary-variety complete height remain open.

\paragraph{Literature and historical boundary.}
Primary uses are exactly Hartl's model charts and ramified repair,
Kahn--Murre--Pedrini's rational refined surface projectors,
Deligne--Clozel's one-prime all-powers comparison, Jannsen's
numerical semisimplicity, and the standard blowup/projective-bundle,
localization and Poincare identities. Their precise scopes and
links are recorded with PX20. The constructions (DP20.3),
(DP20.5), (DP20.13), and (DP20.14) are proved here and are not
claimed as conclusions of those references. The R9 archive
already contained this route as an internal candidate; its
status is not retrospectively altered by this reconstruction,
and no external-priority assertion is made. The all-prime
factorwise decorated-transition route is a separate stronger
candidate and is unnecessary for (DP20.16)--(DP20.17).
