\section{Finite specialization of geometric Chow certificates and surface moduli}
\label{r20gc:section}

\paragraph{Scope and historical bridge.}
The R9 source
\texttt{root\_geometric\_chow\_splitting\_applications.tex}
already recorded the finite-certificate method and the B\"ulles
application as consequences of its product candidate.  We verify
the method with the present DP20 and frozen CV16/VT17 interfaces.
The statement below permits the auxiliary curves and surfaces to
vary during specialization; they need not be defined over the
original arithmetic field.  This is a consequence of an actual
finite rational-equivalence certificate, not a general motivic
generation assertion or a priority claim.

\subsection{Specializing the correspondences and their relation together}

\begin{lemma}[Finite extension carrying a replacement Chow certificate]
\label{r20gc:finite-certificate}
Let $k$ be a characteristic-zero field, let $X/k$ be smooth,
projective and geometrically integral, and let $\Omega/k$ be a
field extension.  Suppose there are finitely many smooth projective
geometrically integral curves and surfaces $S_\alpha/\Omega$,
finite products $V_i$ of these varieties, and rational Chow
correspondences of opposite degree shifts satisfying
\[
 u_i:X_\Omega\dashrightarrow V_i,\qquad
 v_i:V_i\dashrightarrow X_\Omega,\qquad
 \sum_i v_i\circ u_i=[\Delta_{X_\Omega}]
       \quad\text{in }\operatorname{CH}^{\dim X}
                    (X_\Omega\times X_\Omega)_{\mathbf Q}.
 \tag{GC20.1}\label{r20gc:geometric-certificate}
\]
A product with no factors is permitted and means a point.
Then there is a finite extension $E/k$, smooth projective
geometrically integral curves and surfaces $S_{\alpha,E}'/E$,
the corresponding products $V_{i,E}'$, and rational Chow
correspondences of the same degree shifts with
\[
 \sum_i v_{i,E}'\circ u_{i,E}'=[\Delta_{X_E}].
 \tag{GC20.2}\label{r20gc:finite-identity}
\]
The new auxiliary varieties and cycles need not be base changes
of varieties and cycles over $k$, or of the original ones over
$\Omega$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: retain finite cycle and relation data.}
Choose finite rational cycle representatives for every $u_i,v_i$.
Clear their denominators.  The equality
\eqref{r20gc:geometric-certificate} means that the difference
of the composite cycles and the diagonal is a finite sum of
principal-divisor pushforwards on integral subvarieties.
The equations of the auxiliary varieties, their projective
embeddings, all cycle representatives, all rational functions
certifying the relation, and the finite data for the indicated
products descend to one field $L\subset\Omega$ finitely
generated over $k$.  Enlarge $L$ when necessary to retain
the rational-equivalence certificate itself.  Thus the identity
holds in rational Chow over $L$, not only after returning to
$\Omega$.

\emph{Step 2: spread the smooth projective geometry.}
Choose an integral finite-type $k$-scheme $B$ with function
field $L$.  Since $k$ has characteristic zero, shrink $B$ to
a nonempty smooth open.  Spread each $S_\alpha$ and its
projective embedding to a projective family
$\mathcal S_\alpha\to B$.  After a further shrinking, each
family is smooth with geometrically integral fibers of its
original dimension one or two.  Smoothness and geometric
integrality are open for these projective finite-presentation
families.  Form the indicated relative products $\mathcal V_i$.
The family of $X$ remains the constant family $X\times_k B$.

\emph{Step 3: retain the equality over an open base.}
Take closures of the finitely many cycle representatives in
the appropriate relative products.  Shrink so that their
dimensions and codimensions are constant and the cycle families
are flat over $B$.  Relative correspondence composition is
defined by the smooth projections, intersection in the smooth
total product, and proper projection to the two outer factors.
On the generic fiber it gives the composition in
\eqref{r20gc:geometric-certificate}.  The equality holds over
some nonempty open of $B$: explicitly spread the finitely many
principal-divisor data and remove the closed images of any
remaining vertical cycle components.  Equivalently, Chow
groups over $L$ are the direct limit over these open bases;
this follows directly by spreading cycles and the finite
rational-equivalence relations.  Replace $B$ by that open.
We have an actual identity of relative correspondence classes
on $X\times_k X\times_k B$.

\emph{Step 4: specialize at a finite closed point.}
Choose a closed point $b\in B$.  Its residue field $E=\kappa(b)$
is finite over $k$.  Because $B$ is smooth, $b\hookrightarrow B$
is a regular immersion.  Every ambient relative product is
smooth over $B$, so the base-change squares are Tor-independent
and the fiber inclusions are regular immersions.  Their refined
Gysin pullbacks commute with the proper projections, smooth
pullbacks and diagonal Gysin maps defining composition.
Consequently, for the specialized correspondences,
\[
 i_b^!(v_i\circ u_i)=v_{i,b}\circ u_{i,b},\qquad
 i_b^![\Delta_{X\times B/B}]=[\Delta_{X_E}].
 \tag{GC20.3}\label{r20gc:composition-specializes}
\]
The compatibility is the bivariant refined-intersection
formalism; no cancellation of an ambient Gysin push is used.
Apply it to the equality from Step 3.  This yields
\eqref{r20gc:finite-identity}, with
$S_{\alpha,E}'=\mathcal S_{\alpha,b}$ and the corresponding
products.  The fibers have the required smoothness,
projectivity and geometric integrality by Step 2.
\end{proof}

The refined-Gysin compatibilities used here are, for example,
Stacks Project, Section 42.59, particularly Lemmas 42.59.1,
42.59.6 and 42.59.7, together with the proper-push compatibility
in Definition 42.33.1:
\url{https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0FEZ} and
\url{https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0B75}.
This proof specializes both cycles and their relation.  It is
not a claim that each original geometric cycle descends to a
finite extension.

\subsection{The precise local and global transfer}

\begin{theorem}[Geometrically supplied surface-product Chow envelope]
\label{r20gc:local}
Fix a finite valued $K/\mathbf Q_p$ and its absolute value.
Let $X/K$ be smooth projective geometrically integral and assume
that \eqref{r20gc:geometric-certificate} is supplied over some
field extension $\Omega/K$.  Let $T/K$ be smooth projective
geometrically integral with potentially good projective
reduction, permitting a point.  Then every specified geometrically
homologically trivial rational cycle on $T\times X$ has strong
G in every codimension $1\leq r\leq\dim(T\times X)$.
The conclusion holds after every finite valued extension of
$K$ and for all new homological cycles over it.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply Lemma~\ref{r20gc:finite-certificate} to obtain the actual
diagonal identity over one finite $E/K$.  Tensor it with
$\Delta_{T_E}$.  Each new intermediate variety is
$T_E\times V_{i,E}'$, on which DP20 gives zero obstruction
for the full geometric homological Chow group in every
degree; for an empty product use the frozen potentially-good
case.  By the frozen CV16 correspondence stability, the
actual diagonal identity sends every geometric homological
class on $T_E\times X_E$ into its obstruction kernel.
VT17 gives strong G for each specified cycle there.
The all-degree finite actual trace descends to $K$ with
the complete degree $[E:K]$ and the original absolute value.

For a finite $L/K$ and an arbitrary new homological cycle
over $L$, form a finite compositum $EL$ in an algebraic
closure.  Base change the actual certificate to $EL$,
apply DP20 to the new correspondence images of that cycle,
and repeat the preceding kernel argument.  The finite
actual trace descends with the complete degree $[EL:L]$.
Neither the new cycle nor the intermediate new cycles are
assumed to descend to the original field.  The Green form
comes from VT17 for the specified support, and its test
space is the full ordinary-actual compact test space.
No arbitrary proper actual-form pushforward is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Complex Chow certificates for arithmetic varieties]
\label{r20gc:global}
Let $F$ be a number field and $X/F$ smooth projective
geometrically integral.  Fix one embedding $F\hookrightarrow
\mathbf C$.  Suppose $X_{\mathbf C}$ has an actual rational
Chow retraction as in \eqref{r20gc:geometric-certificate}
through products of complex curves and surfaces.
Then $X$ has full local G at every finite place, including
every finite local extension and all new homological cycles,
and has the full PH16 height domain in all complementary
degrees over $F$ and every finite number-field extension.
The same holds for $T\times X$ if $T/F$ has potentially
good projective reduction at every finite place.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{r20gc:finite-certificate}, with $k=F$ and
$\Omega=\mathbf C$, gives a finite $E/F$ and an actual
certificate through products of curves and surfaces over
$E$.  At any finite place $v$ of $F$, choose $w\mid v$
and use this certificate over $E_w$.  DP20 and CV16 kill
the full obstruction there; finite actual trace descends
by $[E_w:F_v]$.  At any finite extension of $F_v$, pass
to a finite compositum with $E_w$ and reapply the same
certificate to the new cycle.  This proves all the claimed
local quantifiers.  PH16 and GA17 now give the complete
complementary homological Chow groups as the global height
domain.  The argument over any finite extension of $F$
uses the same finite-compositum construction.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The exact B\"ulles application}

\begin{corollary}[Specified smooth projective surface moduli spaces]
\label{r20gc:moduli}
Let $M/F$ be smooth projective geometrically integral over a
number field.  Suppose that for one complex embedding it is
isomorphic to a moduli space in B\"ulles's Theorem 0.1:
a smooth projective moduli space of Gieseker-stable twisted
sheaves, or of stable objects for a generic stability condition,
on a complex projective K3 surface or abelian surface.
The complex surface and stability data need not have been
given over $F$.  Then $M$ has the full conclusions of
Theorem~\ref{r20gc:global}.  Finite products of such $M$,
surface point Hilbert schemes and curve/surface products
have the same conclusion; an everywhere potentially-good
projective coefficient may also be included.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The primary theorem supplies actual rational Chow
correspondences exhibiting
\[
 h(M_{\mathbf C})\mid
       \bigoplus_i h(S_{\mathbf C}^{k_i})(n_i).
 \tag{GC20.4}\label{r20gc:bulles}
\]
This means the inclusion and retraction compose to the
diagonal in rational Chow, with the indicated degree
shifts.  Finite-dimensionality of the K3 motive is not
a hypothesis; it is a separate corollary in that paper.
Apply Theorem~\ref{r20gc:global}.  For products, tensor the
actual diagonal certificates.  Their intermediate varieties
remain finite products of curves and surfaces.  For point
Hilbert schemes the native certificate is HM20's checked
de Cataldo--Migliorini identity.  This proves every assertion.
\end{proof}

The primary source read is T.-H. B\"ulles,
\emph{Motives of moduli spaces on K3 surfaces and of special
cubic fourfolds}, Theorem 0.1 and its proof, especially the
actual diagonal equality in Section 2.1:
\url{https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.08284}.
The theorem is used with its complex hypotheses, followed
by the separately proved specialization; it is not silently
quoted as a theorem over an arbitrary $p$-adic field.

\paragraph{Limits.}
The geometric input must be an actual Chow retraction.
A numerical, cohomological, motivated, or Grothendieck-ring
identity is insufficient.  This does not include all
hyperkahler deformation types, all moduli spaces, or all
varieties.  No all-prime absolute local-class vanishing,
all-prime local-link comparison, positivity or nondegeneracy
of the height is concluded.  The general conjecture G and
arbitrary-variety full unconditional heights remain open.
