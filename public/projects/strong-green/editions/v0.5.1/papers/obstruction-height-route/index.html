Local Curvature Obstructions and Beilinson–Bloch Height Pairings

Curvature correction, Chow correspondences and the global pairing

18 November 2026

Abstract

An obstruction approach to Beilinson–Bloch heights separates the construction of a local Green pair from the removal of its curvature. We formulate the obstruction in the actual Bott–Chern space, prove the curvature-correction argument on the prescribed support complement, and explain the vanishing mechanisms supplied by algebraic equivalence and Chow correspondences. Over a number field, the local constructions first give a pairing on the intersection of the obstruction kernels. We prove a local-to-global criterion and identify the exact additional assertion needed to obtain the full complementary homologically trivial Chow groups: vanishing on localizations of global classes in both degrees. Finite period descriptions require a separate comparison with this original obstruction. The remaining construction, vanishing and comparison problems are stated explicitly.

3. Geometric methods for proving vanishing

The obstruction is a linear map on a Chow group. Algebraic equivalence gives an additive parameter space for its values. A Chow decomposition expresses a class through source varieties on which vanishing is known. A numerical model correction provides a third way of producing the needed primitive. These mechanisms can be used independently or combined.

5.2. Construction, vanishing and arithmetic comparison

Problem 5.3 (The prescribed Green pair and its trace). Prove Hypothesis 2.2 for every smooth projective X/KX/KX/K and every codimension. For the supported-complex method, construct GZG_ZGZ​ on all of UZU_ZUZ​, retain the localized cycle coefficients in (2.1), and prove that changing the model or resolution changes ΩZ\Omega_ZΩZ​ by DDD of a global actual form. Construct the finite trace on the forms used by this proof with presentation independence, both differential compatibilities and all-test adjunction.

The decisive issue is the passage from expressions on covers to an actual form downstairs. A proper generically finite map can be used for algebraic intersection calculations, but the asserted form-level trace must be defined on the finite locus on which it is used. Likewise, an alteration need not become finite over the prescribed complement. A construction relying on that property must provide it for its particular map.

Problem 5.4 (The remaining homologically trivial classes). Assume the local construction. Prove that the induced obstruction on (3.1) is zero, or, for the global goal, prove only (1.3). In a period approach, first establish (5.1) for the original curvature class and then compute its periods on the same Chow source.

A comparison with ℓ\ellℓ-adic, de Rham or Abel–Jacobi data must identify the obstruction-valued map, not just two abstract cohomology spaces. Homological triviality kills the cycle class; it does not by itself kill every secondary invariant. The missing inference is the vanishing of the particular secondary periods that detect o\mathfrak oo.

Problem 5.5 (Local residues and normalized global comparison). Establish Hypothesis 4.1 from the proposed actual-form construction in every relevant codimension. In the principal transformation formula, retain the refined point coefficients and prove that the local scalar is the absolute value of one algebraic element of F×F^\timesF× at every place. Then prove correspondence adjunction and comparison with classical Beilinson–Bloch heights on the common domains, with the absolute normalization (4.1).

These are three different mathematical obligations. The first supplies the local obstruction and a Green pair. The second enlarges the subgroup on which curvature can be removed. The third makes the local numbers descend and identifies the resulting height. Once the local height identities are established, vanishing on the global source in the two complementary degrees is exactly what completes this obstruction route to the full pairing.
