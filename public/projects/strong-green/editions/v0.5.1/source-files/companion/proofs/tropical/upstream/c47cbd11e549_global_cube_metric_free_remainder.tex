\section{An explicit metric-independent remainder for the full raw cube}
\label{sec:r11-global-cube-remainder}

\paragraph{Status and scope.}
This is an R11 construction by \texttt{/root/r11\_cube}, submitted
for independent exact-source review.  It constructs a global actual
quadruple and a completely specified metric-independent actual triple
whose sum is the raw six-face CS cube on the entire original punctured
open.  The latter triple has \emph{not} been proved to extend across
the common zero set.  The final specified closed remainder below is
still OPEN.  No vanishing of a mixed parameter residual, no exactness
of an actual complex, and no new original-cycle Green existence theorem
is asserted.

\subsection{The parameter identity with a retained nonzero defect}

Use exactly the finite algebraic two-colour extension CT1--CT3,
\[
 \mathscr B(W)\otimes\mathbb R[t]\otimes
                       \Lambda(\epsilon',\epsilon''),
 \qquad \widetilde d't=\epsilon',\quad
 \widetilde d''t=\epsilon'',
 \tag{GR1}
\]
with the exterior variables written on the left.  It is an algebraic
device with actual coefficients, not an analytic real-interval
pullback, a completion, or a current space.  All integrations below
are integrations of finitely many real polynomial coefficients.

Let $\widetilde q=(\widetilde A,\widetilde B,\widetilde M)$ be a
finite polynomial triple of bidegrees $L_2^s$, and write
\[
 \widetilde A=A_0+\epsilon'A_1+\epsilon''A_2
                                      +\epsilon'\epsilon''A_{12},
 \tag{GR2}
\]
and similarly for $B,M$.  Write its \emph{complete} boundary as
\[
 \widetilde\partial_2\widetilde q=\widetilde F
                  =(\widetilde F^+,\widetilde F^-),\qquad
 \widetilde F^\pm=F_0^\pm+\epsilon'F_1^\pm
             +\epsilon''F_2^\pm+\epsilon'\epsilon''F_{12}^\pm.
 \tag{GR3}
\]
No coefficient of $\widetilde F$ is assumed zero or closed.
Assume only $A_2(0)=M_1(0)=0$.  Define the actual quadruple
$\mathcal T(\widetilde q)$ by the exact CT7 formula
\[
 \begin{split}
 J_q&=\int_0^1(1-t)M_{12}(t)\,dt,\\
 \mathcal T(\widetilde q)
  &=\left(\int_0^1A_1\,dt,\ \int_0^1B_2\,dt,\
          \int_0^1M_1\,dt-d''J_q,\ \int_0^1M_2\,dt\right).
 \end{split}
 \tag{GR4}
\]
The full retained-defect triple is
\[
 \mathcal E(\widetilde F)=
 \left(\int_0^1F_1^+\,dt,\ \int_0^1F_2^-\,dt,\
       \int_0^1F_2^+\,dt+d'\int_0^1(1-t)F_{12}^+\,dt\right).
 \tag{GR5}
\]

\begin{lemma}[Full finite parameter identity]
With these data one has the actual identity
\[
 \operatorname{ev}_1\widetilde q-\operatorname{ev}_0\widetilde q
            =\partial_3\mathcal T(\widetilde q)
                              +\mathcal E(\widetilde F).
 \tag{GR6}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The six nonconstant-exterior equations, with every defect retained,
are
\[
 \begin{split}
 \dot A_0&=d'A_1+d''M_1+F_1^+,\\
 \dot M_0&=d'A_2+d''M_2+F_2^+,\\
 \partial_t(A_2-M_1)&=-d'A_{12}-d''M_{12}+F_{12}^+,\\
 \dot M_0&=d''B_1+d'M_1+F_1^-,\\
 \dot B_0&=d''B_2+d'M_2+F_2^-,\\
 \partial_t(M_2-B_1)&=-d''B_{12}-d'M_{12}+F_{12}^-.
 \end{split}
 \tag{GR7}
\]
These follow by the same full graded expansion as CT9--CT10.
The first and fifth equations give the first two entries of
(GR6).  For the third entry,
\[
 \dot M_0-d'M_1-d''M_2=d'(A_2-M_1)+F_2^+.
 \tag{GR8}
\]
Its first term, using the third equation of (GR7) and the initial
condition, integrates to
\[
 \int_0^1d'(A_2-M_1)\,dt
   =-DJ_q+d'\int_0^1(1-t)F_{12}^+\,dt.
 \tag{GR9}
\]
Equations (GR8)--(GR9) give precisely the third components of
(GR4)--(GR5), with their signs.  The last two missing exterior
coefficients of $F$ have not been declared zero: (GR7) displays
them, while the asymmetric extraction (GR5) needs only the
indicated four coefficients.  Its bidegrees are exactly those
of $L_2^s$.  All operations are finite actual operations.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The unrestricted CS expression and its exact boundary}

Retain the original data of QH10: $f$ has rank $r\ge3$, three
distinct unit changes have weights $a,b,c$, $W=N\setminus|f=0|$,
$s=r-1$ and $d=s-1$.  Write $\Phi_v$ for the eight actual norm
potentials, indexed by subsets $v\subset\{a,b,c\}$, and use
the original metric curvature $R$.  For a face $\nu$ of the
cube, denote its two ordered weights by $(a_\nu,b_\nu)$ and
its four oriented edges by $e$.  Let $P_\nu$ be the full SHT
square pair and $e_\nu=(a_\nu d''b_\nu,b_\nu d'a_\nu)$.

For \emph{any} actual scalar $\Psi$ on the whole $W$, define
$q_\nu[\Psi]$ to be the finite expression CS36 with this scalar:
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 R_\Psi=R-D\Psi,\qquad p_v=\Phi_v-\Psi,\qquad
 h_\Psi=-\Psi\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}R_\Psi^jR^{d-1-j},\\
 q_\nu[\Psi]=Q_{{\rm edge},\nu}[\Psi]-Q_{{\rm met},\nu}[\Psi]
 -(0,0,a_\nu b_\nu R_\Psi^d/2)
                                     +\mathcal H_{h_\Psi}(e_\nu).
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{GR10}
\]
Here $Q_{\rm edge}$ means \emph{every} term of CS30 with its
CS6--CS9 formulas, and $Q_{\rm met}$ is exactly MT15 for the
unshifted face and the common change $-\Psi$.  Thus (GR10) is
well-defined even when $\Psi$ is not a legal CS cap; no witness
property for an arbitrary $\Psi$ is assumed.

Let $S_{e,s}[\Psi]$ denote the full residual CS7, with the same
normalization.  The unconditional finite boundary identity is
\[
 \begin{split}
 \partial_2q_\nu[\Psi]
    &=P_\nu-e_\nu R^d+F_\nu[\Psi],\\
 F_\nu[\Psi]
    &=\frac12\left(\sum_{e\in\nu}S_{e,s}[\Psi]
                     +\mathfrak a(a_\nu,b_\nu;R_\Psi^d)\right).
 \end{split}
 \tag{GR11}
\]
Indeed CS10 gives
$\partial_2Q_{\rm edge}=P_\nu^*+\sum_eS_{e,s}/2$;
MT16 removes $P_\nu^*-P_\nu$; the scalar correction contributes
$-e_\nu R_\Psi^d+\mathfrak a(a_\nu,b_\nu;R_\Psi^d)/2$;
and the independently closed-pair homotopy contributes
$e_\nu(R_\Psi^d-R^d)$.  Their sum is (GR11).
Neither CS29 nor an independent closedness of any residual
coefficient is used in this derivation.

The same identity holds in (GR1) for a polynomial common
normalization.  All algebraic terms are the full differential
polynomials.  The one support step in CS10 is unchanged:
on a $D|c_e|$ carrier the original norm difference is zero with
its tangential first jet, and subtracting the same formal scalar
from both endpoints leaves that difference and its formal first
jets unchanged.  No parameter coefficient is removed by a
dimension assertion.

\subsection{The simultaneous six-face interpolation}

List the six faces with the exact QH10 signs:
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c|c}
 \nu&\text{face and base tuple}&\sigma_\nu\\\hline
 1&(a,b;f)&+1\\
 2&(a,c;Bf)&+1\\
 3&(b,c;f)&+1\\
 4&(b,c;Af)&-1\\
 5&(a,c;f)&-1\\
 6&(a,b;Cf)&-1.
 \end{array}
 \tag{GR12}
\]
Let $\Psi_\nu$ be that face's original actual CS24 cap with the
prescribed positive constant.  Put $\Phi_\emptyset=\Phi_0$,
$\rho(t)=3t^2-2t^3$, and define on the entire original $W$
\[
 \widetilde\Psi_\nu
     =\Psi_\nu+\rho(t)(\Phi_0-\Psi_\nu),\qquad
 \widetilde Q=\sum_{\nu=1}^6\sigma_\nu q_\nu[\widetilde\Psi_\nu],
 \qquad
 \widetilde F=\sum_{\nu=1}^6\sigma_\nu F_\nu[\widetilde\Psi_\nu].
 \tag{GR13}
\]
All operations in (GR10) are now taken with $\widetilde d',
\widetilde d''$.  No claim is made that the interpolated normalizers
are legal caps or that their individual residuals vanish.

Endpoint and face telescoping give the literal identities
\[
 \operatorname{ev}_0\widetilde Q=\mathcal Q_{a,b,c}(f),\qquad
 \operatorname{ev}_1\widetilde Q=0,\qquad
                 \widetilde\partial_2\widetilde Q=\widetilde F.
 \tag{GR14}
\]
For the middle equality, every face has the same normalizer $\Phi_0$.
Each oriented cube edge occurs twice with opposite signs, so the
entire CS30 edge triples cancel, including their scalar and
tetrahedron terms.  The MT15 sums are linear in the full edge
sums $f_k,\Gamma_k$ when their common scalar and reference are fixed,
so they cancel as well.  Finally the three pairs of parallel faces
have equal ordered weights, the same $R_{\Phi_0}$ and the same
$h_{\Phi_0}$; their scalar and independently closed-pair correction
terms therefore cancel.  This is equality of displayed expressions,
not a claim that $\Phi_0$ is a legal cap on every face.

For the last equality of (GR14), $\sum\sigma_\nu P_\nu=0$ by
the same oriented-edge cancellation, and
$\sum\sigma_\nu e_\nu=0$ by parallel-face cancellation.  Apply
the complete (GR11) in the finite parameter algebra.

Every single-exterior coefficient of $\widetilde Q$ has a factor
$\rho'(t)$, exactly as in CT19.  In particular the initial condition
of (GR6) holds.  The double-exterior coefficients, which contain
$\rho''$ and $(\rho')^2$, are retained.

\begin{theorem}[Explicit global cube reduction]
The following are completely specified actual forms on all of $W$:
\[
 T_{\rm glob}=-\mathcal T(\widetilde Q)\in L_3^s(W),\qquad
 E_{\rm glob}=-\mathcal E(\widetilde F)\in L_2^s(W).
 \tag{GR15}
\]
They satisfy the literal identity
\[
             \mathcal Q_{a,b,c}(f)=\partial_3T_{\rm glob}+E_{\rm glob},
                         \qquad \partial_2E_{\rm glob}=0.
 \tag{GR16}
\]
The triple $E_{\rm glob}$ is independent of the original common
piecewise smooth metric on $L$.  This is not an extension assertion.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply (GR6) to (GR14).  Its endpoint difference is
$-\mathcal Q_{a,b,c}(f)$, giving both minus signs in (GR15).
The raw cube is $\partial_2$-closed by frozen QH11, so
$\partial_2\partial_3=0$ gives the last equality of (GR16).
No actual cochain has been chosen by an abstract inverse.

To prove the metric assertion, write
\[
 \theta_v=\Phi_v-\Phi_0,\qquad
 \kappa_\nu=\Psi_\nu-\Phi_0,\qquad C_0=R-D\Phi_0.
 \tag{GR17}
\]
Under (GR13), the normalized vertex potentials and reference
curvature in every residual become exactly
\[
 \widetilde p_{v,\nu}=\theta_v-(1-\rho)\kappa_\nu,\qquad
 \widetilde R_\nu
                 =C_0-\widetilde D((1-\rho)\kappa_\nu).
 \tag{GR18}
\]
These, the original unit weights and their absolute-value walls,
are all the arguments of $\widetilde F$ in (GR11).
Changing the common metric adds one common actual scalar to all
$\Phi_v$ and $\Psi_\nu$, and its $D$ to $R$.  Thus every quantity
in (GR17)--(GR18) is literally unchanged.  The full single and
double parameter coefficients of $\widetilde F$, and hence (GR5),
are unchanged.  This proves metric independence with every mixed
coefficient retained.
\end{proof}

In particular, for two actual common metrics, the difference of
their raw cubes is the explicitly displayed third boundary
\[
 \mathcal Q^{(1)}-\mathcal Q^{(0)}
                   =\partial_3(T_{\rm glob}^{(1)}-T_{\rm glob}^{(0)}).
 \tag{GR19}
\]
This is a whole-cube metric transport formula.  It does not apply
CPT or QHT to nonclosed lower-degree blocks.

\subsection{The exact remaining actual problem}

Let $\ell=r-3$ and take the independently closed unit triple
$E_{abc}=\mathcal H_c(e_{ab})$ from CU6, which is actual on all of
$N$.  Define the fully specified actual metric-independent triple
\[
                  \mathcal R_f=E_{\rm glob}-C_0^\ell E_{abc}
                                           \in L_2^s(W).
 \tag{GR20}
\]
It is $\partial_2$-closed, but no actual third primitive for it
has been constructed.  For $\ell\ge1$ put
\[
 h_0=-\Phi_0\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}C_0^jR^{\ell-1-j},
                \qquad Dh_0=C_0^\ell-R^\ell.
 \tag{GR21}
\]
Since the coefficient triple $E_{abc}$ is independently closed
on $N$, the exact QHT identity applies without cancellation:
\[
 (C_0^\ell-R^\ell)E_{abc}
                         =\partial_3K_{h_0}(E_{abc}).
 \tag{GR22}
\]
For $\ell=0$ set this correction to zero.  Consequently the
entire raw cube has the exact decomposition
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \mathcal Q_{a,b,c}(f)
  =\partial_3\bigl(T_{\rm glob}+K_{h_0}(E_{abc})\bigr)
                         +R^\ell E_{abc}+\mathcal R_f.
 \quad}
 \tag{GR23}
\]
Every form in this equation has been constructed.  The first
quadruple is actual on $W$; the middle triple genuinely extends
on $N$; the last term is the explicit unresolved actual remainder.

\paragraph{OPEN, not an exactness conclusion.}
Constructing an actual $V_f\in L_3^s(W)$ with
$\mathcal R_f=\partial_3V_f$ would give the desired relative
cube certificate by adding $V_f$ to the quadruple in (GR23).
This is one precise sufficient target for the displayed choice
of extending triple.  It is not asserted to be necessary for
every possible extending error, and no general relative cube
counterexample would follow from failure of this particular
subtraction.  Closedness of $\mathcal R_f$ is not used as a
primitive existence theorem.

The scalars in (GR17) have useful bounds but those do not solve
extension.  If $L_0=|a|+|b|+|c|$, then
$|\theta_v|\le L_0$ and $|\kappa_\nu|\le2L_0+\varepsilon$
for a common cap constant.  These follow from the unit norm
bound and the two branches of each original CS cap.  They do
not imply that any scalar, derivative, or remainder in (GR20)
extends across the common zero set.  For example a capped angular
norm ratio can remain bounded and have different limits on two
original-$K$ normal sections.  No growth condition is imposed;
boundedness is recorded only as a property of the specified
input scalars, not as a replacement for an actual extension proof.
