Northcott forces real positivity

Finiteness excludes real null directions, even without a finite-rank assumption.

v0.5.1 · Arithmetic finiteness

Arithmetic Lefschetz and Integral Heights

Statement

Let Λ\LambdaΛ be an additive subgroup spanning a rational vector space VVV, and let qqq be a real-valued quadratic form. If every upper height ball on Λ\LambdaΛ is finite, the scalar extension of qqq to V⊗QRV\otimes_{\mathbf Q}\mathbf RV⊗Q​R is positive definite.

Motivation

Northcott is stronger than positivity alone. It need not force finite rational rank: weighted orthogonal sums can have finite balls and infinite rank.

Argument

Restrict to any finite-dimensional rational subspace and choose a basis in Λ\LambdaΛ. A negative real direction for its Gram matrix QQQ gives a negative rational direction by approximation. Clearing denominators yields a nonzero integral vector whose multiples contradict Northcott. Hence QQQ is positive semidefinite.

But the vectors ztz_tzt​ are unbounded and therefore take infinitely many values. This contradiction proves positivity on every finite-dimensional real extension, which proves the assertion.
