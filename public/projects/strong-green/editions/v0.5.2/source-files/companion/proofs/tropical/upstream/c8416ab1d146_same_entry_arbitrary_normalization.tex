\subsection{The exact one-edge identity}

Work over the original fixed $K$ and absolute value.  Let $f$ be an
$r$-tuple of sections of the same actual metrized line bundle on an
ordinary open $N$, where $r\ge2$, and put $W=N\setminus|f=0|$,
$R=c_1(\overline L)$ and $D=d'd''$.  One unit edge changes only
entry $i$ by an analytic unit of valuation $c$.  Denote its original
norm potentials by $\Phi_p,\Phi_q$.  On the ordinary open where the
unchanged $(r-1)$-tuple is nonzero, let $\psi$ be its actual norm
potential and put $C_\psi=R-D\psi$.

Supply \emph{any} actual scalar $\Psi$ on $W$.  Set
\[
 R_*=R-D\Psi,\qquad p=\Phi_p-\Psi,\qquad
 q=\Phi_q-\Psi,\qquad w=\psi-\Psi,
 \qquad g=c+p-q=c+\Phi_p-\Phi_q.
 \tag{SAN1}
\]
The original projective curvatures $C_p=R_*-Dp$ and
$C_q=R_*-Dq$ do not depend on $\Psi$.  Retain exactly the complete
CS residual, including its absolute-value walls:
\[
\begin{split}
 S_{e,k}={}&\mathfrak a(p,q;h_{k-1}(R_*,C_p,C_q))\\
 &+\mathfrak a(c_+,q;h_{k-1}(R_*,C_q))
   +\mathfrak a(c_-,p;h_{k-1}(R_*,C_p)),
 \tag{SAN2}
\end{split}
\]
where $\mathfrak a(a,b;Q)=((a\,d''b-b\,d''a)Q,
-(a\,d'b-b\,d'a)Q)$ and $h_j$ is the complete homogeneous
polynomial, with $h_0=1$ and $h_j=0$ for $j<0$.

\begin{lemma}[Entire one-entry residual]
For every $k\ge1$, on the unchanged-tuple nonzero open one has
the actual identity
\[
 \boxed{\qquad
 S_{e,k}=\mathfrak a(g,w;h_{k-1}(R_*,C_\psi)).
 \qquad}
 \tag{SAN3}
\]
It holds with the full derivatives of $\Psi$, even when $D\Psi\ne0$.
It also holds coefficientwise after the finite formal two-colour
parameter extension CT1--CT3, provided all original edge data are
constant in that formal parameter.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
First take $c\ge0$.  The original CS15 carrier identity eliminates
the $C_p$-containing monomials and leaves
\[
 S_{e,k}=\mathfrak a(p+c,q;h_{k-1}(R_*,C_q)).
 \tag{SAN4}
\]
This carrier identity remains valid after any common normalization:
the original old-corner relation $\Phi_p=\Phi_q$ is unchanged by
subtracting $\Psi$, including its tangential first jets.

On a unit presentation for the changed entry, write its valuation
relative to the unchanged norm as $x$.  Then
$p=w+\min(x,0)$ and $q=w+\min(x+c,0)$.  The finite full-wall
minimum identity gives, in each colour,
\[
 \mathfrak a(p+c,q;1)=\mathfrak a(g,w;1),\qquad
 g=(x+c)_+-x_+=c+\min(x,0)-\min(x+c,0).
 \tag{SAN5}
\]
Indeed the additional term
$\mathfrak a(\min(x,0)+c,\min(x+c,0);1)$ vanishes: for
$x\le0$ it is the homogeneous minimum identity, and for $x\ge0$
the second argument is zero.  These statements agree with their
restricted first jets on all the indicated walls.

On every new projective-curvature carrier where the changed entry
is active, $x+c=0$ after comparison with an active unchanged entry.
Thus $g=0$ and its tangential first jet is zero there.  On the part
where the changed entry is strictly inactive, the new norm is
literally the unchanged norm on an ordinary neighborhood, so
$C_q=C_\psi$.  Consequently, after wedging with the displayed
first-form coefficient, every power $C_q^j$ in (SAN4) can be
replaced by $C_\psi^j$.  On the open where the changed entry is
strictly winning, $x+c<0$ and (SAN5) gives $g=0$ identically;
thus the right-hand passive-curvature terms vanish there as well.
This statement retains all intersections
and boundary carriers: any component meeting a changed-active
corner has the zero restricted coefficient just proved.  It does
not cancel a curvature factor or assert a mixed-power nilpotence.
Together with (SAN5) it proves (SAN3) for $c\ge0$.

Reverse the whole edge for $c\le0$; both $S_{e,k}$ and $g$ change
sign, while $w,C_\psi,R_*$ do not.  The wall $c=0$ retains the
full CS restricted-jet identity.  At a zero of the changed entry,
all relevant old and new norms equal the unchanged norm on an
ordinary neighborhood.  There $p=q=w$, $g=c$, and (SAN2) gives
(SAN3) directly; no value of $v(0)$ is assigned.

For the formal assertion, $g$ and all original corner differences
are independent of the formal parameter.  On a killed carrier,
both $g$ and its full actual tangential first jet are zero, so its
product with every single or double formal coefficient of $w$ or
$R_*$ is zero.  The common-shift relations in (SAN4)--(SAN5) are
finite graded differential identities.  Hence the same proof holds
for every coefficient of CT3, not merely at numerical parameter
values.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The whole loop and the finite cap-change witness}

Consider a finite loop changing only entry $i$, with unit weights
$c_e$, initial and final norm potentials equal, and $\sum_e c_e=0$.
For example its analytic unit product may be one, as in frozen SL.
Every edge has the same unchanged tuple and the same $\Psi$.
The scalars (SAN1) telescope exactly:
\[
                     \sum_e g_e=0.
 \tag{SAN6}
\]
Thus (SAN3) proves, for every $k\ge1$,
\[
                     \sum_e S_{e,k}=0.
 \tag{SAN7}
\]
Where the unchanged tuple is zero, the changed entry is nonzero.
The finitely many unit factors are locally bounded with locally
bounded inverses.  On one ordinary neighborhood all intermediate
norms are therefore determined by this single changed entry.
There $C_p=C_q=0$ and $q=p+c$ for every edge; the full CS formula
gives $S_{e,k}=R_*^{k-1}S_{e,1}=0$.  This proves (SAN7) on all
of $W$ without density or extension of a singular unchanged norm.

Let $s=r-1$, and let $\mathcal K_s(e;R_*)$ be exactly CS6--CS10.
Use the complete MT15 expression $Q_{\rm met}[-\Psi]$ for the
whole zero-weight loop, not for an individual edge.  Define
\[
 q[\Psi]=\sum_e\mathcal K_s(e;R_*)-Q_{\rm met}[-\Psi].
 \tag{SAN8}
\]
All vertex potentials in the edge sum are normalized by the same
$\Psi$.  Equations (SAN7), CS10 and MT16 prove
\[
              P=\partial_2q[\Psi],\qquad
              q[0]=\sum_e\mathcal K_s(e;R).
 \tag{SAN9}
\]
In particular the latter is a specified normalization-free actual
witness, not a selection from a closed-range theorem.

For any two supplied actual scalars $\Psi_0,\Psi_1$ on $W$, use
\[
 \rho(t)=3t^2-2t^3,\qquad
 \widetilde\Psi=\Psi_0+\rho(t)(\Psi_1-\Psi_0)
 \tag{SAN10}
\]
in every term of (SAN8), with the full formal differentials CT2.
The formal version of (SAN7), together with the full CS and MT
identities, gives $\widetilde\partial_2\widetilde q=P$ with no
parameter coefficients on the right.  The flat endpoint derivative
$\rho'(0)=0$ gives CT6.  Therefore the exact finite coefficient
recipe CT7, including its $-d''J$ correction, supplies an actual
quadruple $H_{01}$ satisfying
\[
               q[\Psi_1]-q[\Psi_0]=\partial_3H_{01}.
 \tag{SAN11}
\]
This applies to the old SL cap and to a later common two-target cap;
it does not infer arbitrary cross-passive-block cap independence.
The reason is the independently proved all-normalization loop
identity (SAN7), specific to one changed entry.

\begin{corollary}[Prescribed same-target norm-isometric insertions]
The same statements hold when successive unit edges are joined by
supplied norm-isometric steps which leave every entry other than
the same fixed target $i$ literally unchanged.  Require that those
steps carry the prescribed \emph{zero} primitive pair, that the
full norm loop closes, and that its total unit weight is zero.
The unchanged tuple must be the very same tuple at every step;
no unproved identification of merely norm-equivalent presentations
is allowed.  Then (SAN7)--(SAN11) hold for the whole unit-edge sum.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The zero primitive for each inserted step is part of the stated
native data, not deduced for arbitrary chosen primitives from equal
endpoints.  All unit edges have the identical unchanged norm
$\psi$.  Their potential endpoints telescope because the inserted
steps have exactly equal norms.  Thus (SAN6) still holds, and the
proof of (SAN7)--(SAN11) is unchanged.

For the precise application to frozen SAR3, the inserted steps are
the native SHT constant shears $E_{ij}(1)$ (or their inverse
representatives), with their prescribed pair zero.  They change
only target $i$ and preserve the full unchanged tuple literally.
All six unit edges have this same target, their weights sum to
zero, and their norm loop closes.  Hence SAR7 also has arbitrary
common-normalization transport (SAN11).  The strict existence of
same-root additivity remains the previously marked recovery
revalidation; neither missing CG/RA source bytes nor their other
historical conclusions are recovered by this corollary.
\end{proof}
