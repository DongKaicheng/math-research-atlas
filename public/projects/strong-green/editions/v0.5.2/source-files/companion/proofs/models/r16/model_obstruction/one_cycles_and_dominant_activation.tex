\begin{proposition}[All-degree dominant obstruction detection]
\label{r16G:dominant-witness}
Assume \texttt{r16pb:dominant}.  Let $X/K$ be smooth integral
projective, $1\leq r\leq\dim X$, and let $f:V\to X$ be a
projective dominant morphism with $V/K$ smooth integral.
For $z\in\operatorname{CH}^r(X)_{\mathbf Q}$, suppose
\[
                         \mathfrak o_{V,K}^r(f^*z)=0.
\tag{GC16.1}\label{r16G:upstairs}
\]
Then $\mathfrak o_{X,K}^r(z)=0$.  Every specified rational
cycle $Z$ representing $z$ has an ordinary actual form
\[
 \gamma_Z\in B^{r-1,r-1}
          (X^{\mathrm{an}}\setminus |Z|^{\mathrm{an}})
\]
such that, writing $n=\dim X$,
\[
 \int_{X^{\mathrm{an}}\setminus |Z|^{\mathrm{an}}}
                     \gamma_Z\wedge D\eta
       =-\int_{Z^{\mathrm{an}}}\eta,
       \qquad \eta\in B_c^{n-r,n-r}(X^{\mathrm{an}}).
\tag{GC16.2}\label{r16G:exact-G}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The obstruction is functorial for morphisms between smooth
varieties: the exact frozen source is Round 11's curvature
criterion, retained in
\path{round12/inherited/r11_curvature_full.tex}, together
with the graded-ring statement \texttt{r12p:ring} in
\path{round12/foundations_audit/compact_parameter_algebraic_triviality.tex}.
Thus \eqref{r16G:upstairs} is the vanishing of
$f^*\mathfrak o_{X,K}^r(z)$.  The assumed all-degree dominant
injectivity makes the original class zero.

To retain the specified support, take the frozen curved
Green pair $(\Gamma_Z,\Omega_Z)$ for this actual cycle $Z$.
Zero obstruction gives a global ordinary actual
$\Psi\in B^{r-1,r-1}(X^{\mathrm{an}})$ with
$D\Psi=\Omega_Z$.  Set
\[
              \gamma_Z=\Gamma_Z-
                        \Psi|_{X^{\mathrm{an}}\setminus|Z|^{\mathrm{an}}}.
\]
The frozen Green-pair identity and actual Stokes give
\eqref{r16G:exact-G} on every compact actual test.
The correction is global and adds no deleted locus.
No representative of $f^*z$ is pushed forward analytically.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A numerical model on an arbitrary dominant witness]
\label{r16G:numerical-witness}
Assume \texttt{r16pb:dominant}.  In
Proposition~\ref{r16G:dominant-witness}, it suffices that
$V$ be smooth integral projective over some finite extension
$F/K$, mapped dominantly to $X$ over $K$, and possess a
flat integral projective model $\mathcal V/F^\circ$ with
a rational virtual vector bundle $\xi$ satisfying
\[
 \operatorname{ch}_r(\xi|_V)=f^*z,\qquad
 \deg_{k_F}\bigl(\operatorname{ch}_r(\xi|_W)\cap[W]\bigr)=0
\tag{GC16.3}\label{r16G:model-witness}
\]
for every integral vertical $r$-dimensional $W$.
The map $f$ need not be generically finite or finite on a
specified complement.  No regular model of $X$ is assumed.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Regard $\mathcal V$ as a model over $K^\circ$.  It is still
integral, flat, and projective.  Every integral subvariety
of its thickened special fiber lies on the same reduction;
the degree in \eqref{r16G:model-witness} is multiplied by
the positive residue-field degree.  The frozen model-Chern
detector therefore applies in the original $K$-actual
theory and gives $\operatorname{ch}_r(\bar\xi)=0$.
The integral-scope generic-Chow-zero primitive theorem
then gives $\mathfrak o_{V,K}^r(f^*z)=0$.
These are precisely the native-field steps in frozen
Theorem \texttt{r12a:finite-numerical-cover}, although
its later finite descent step is not used here.

Now apply Proposition~\ref{r16G:dominant-witness}.
The finite maps inside \texttt{r16pb:dominant}'s flattened
diagram use their complete native function-field degrees.
Any constant-field factor is retained.  This obtains the
identity on the original actual test class, without an
assumed equality of test categories over $F$ and $K$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Full strong G for geometrically homologically trivial one-cycles]
\label{r16G:one-cycles}
Assume \texttt{r16pb:dominant}, and retain the frozen
actual model-Chern and exact-support foundations.  Let
$X/K$ be smooth projective geometrically integral of
dimension $n\geq2$.  Let $Z$ be any specified rational
one-cycle satisfying, for one prime $\ell\ne p$,
\[
 \operatorname{cl}_\ell(Z)=0
     \quad\text{in }H^{2n-2}(X_{\bar K},\mathbf Q_\ell(n-1)).
\tag{GC16.4}\label{r16G:curve-homology}
\]
Then
\[
                       \mathfrak o_{X,K}^{n-1}([Z])=0.
\tag{GC16.5}\label{r16G:curve-obstruction}
\]
There is an ordinary actual form
\[
 \gamma_Z\in B^{n-2,n-2}
      (X^{\mathrm{an}}\setminus |Z|^{\mathrm{an}})
\]
with
\[
 \int_{X^{\mathrm{an}}\setminus |Z|^{\mathrm{an}}}
                \gamma_Z\wedge D\eta
             =-\int_{Z^{\mathrm{an}}}\eta
       \qquad(\eta\in B_c^{1,1}(X^{\mathrm{an}})).
\tag{GC16.6}\label{r16G:curve-G}
\]
No supplied regular model of $X$ and no finite cover with
a regular model are required.

The same assertion holds for smooth integral projective
$X/K$ if geometric cycle-class vanishing is required on
every geometric component.  All conclusions then remain
in the original $K$-actual theory.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $n=2$ this is the frozen native divisor theorem.  Suppose
$n\geq3$.  In the possibly nongeometrically integral case,
regard $X$ as geometrically integral over its finite field
of constants $K_0=H^0(X,\mathcal O_X)$; smoothness and
properness give this usual constant-field reduction.
In the original geometric case put $K_0=K$.
Take an integral projective flat model of $X$ over
$K_0^\circ$, with no regularity assumption.  The same
de Jong semistable-alteration input already audited in
Round 12 supplies a finite valued extension $F/K_0$, hence
also a finite extension $F/K$, a smooth
projective geometrically integral $F$-variety $V$ of
dimension exactly $n$, a projective dominant generically
finite $K$-morphism $f:V\to X$, and a regular projective
strictly semistable model $\mathcal V/F^\circ$.
The properness check is unchanged: the altered source is
proper over the finite trait, so its open immersion into
the integral projective semistable compactification is
proper and hence surjective.  Thus the entire required
model is available.

Since the generic varieties are smooth, their operational
Chow pullback is defined.  Cycle-map functoriality shows
that $f^*[Z]$ is geometrically $\ell$-homologically trivial.
Its codimension remains $n-1$ and $\dim V=n$, so it is
exactly a one-cycle class on $V$.  This dimension equality
is why the regular-model curve theorem applies; an
arbitrary positive-dimensional dominant witness would
not retain the one-cycle condition.

Frozen Theorem \texttt{r12n:main}, in
\path{round12/numerical_lift/curve_lifts_and_actual_green.tex},
constructs a rational virtual bundle $\xi$ on
$\mathcal V$ with generic Chern character $f^*[Z]$ in
degree $n-1$ and zero supported degree on every vertical
$(n-1)$-dimensional subvariety.  Its proof uses geometric
homological vanishing, the weighted divisor kernel,
compatible divisor cohomology lifts, the multiplicity
trace, and the pure operational $K_0$ representative.
It does not assume a higher-codimension numerical-to-
homological comparison.

Apply Corollary~\ref{r16G:numerical-witness} with $r=n-1$.
It retains the original-field numerical detector and then
uses all-degree dominant Bott--Chern descent.  This proves
\eqref{r16G:curve-obstruction}.  The original prescribed
Green pair construction in
Proposition~\ref{r16G:dominant-witness} proves
\eqref{r16G:curve-G} on all original actual tests and on
the exact prescribed complement.  There is no proper
analytic pushforward of an upstairs Green form and no
assertion that the alteration becomes finite away from
$Z$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The gain over the frozen theorem.}
Round 12 proved the same one-cycle conclusion on a
supplied regular model, or after a genuinely finite
numerical cover.  Round 15 removed that model hypothesis
in dimension three using its degree-two dominant
injectivity.  The activated Theorem~\ref{r16G:one-cycles}
removes it in every dimension.  It still does not treat
all middle-codimension cycles in dimension at least four.

\begin{theorem}[Dominant images of projective-bundle towers]
\label{r16G:towers}
Assume \texttt{r16pb:dominant}.  Let $X/K$ be smooth
projective geometrically integral.  Suppose that after a
finite extension $F/K$ there is a smooth projective
geometrically integral $F$-variety $V$, a projective
dominant morphism $f:V\to X_F$, and a supplied finite
projective-bundle tower
\[
 V=V_t\longrightarrow V_{t-1}\longrightarrow\cdots
             \longrightarrow V_0=S,
            \qquad V_j=\mathbf P_{V_{j-1}}(N_j),
\tag{GC16.7}\label{r16G:tower}
\]
with $\dim S\leq3$ and every $N_j$ of positive rank.
Then every geometrically homologically trivial rational
cycle on $X$, in every codimension, has zero obstruction
and satisfies strong G on its exact support complement
against all original actual tests.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Regard the whole tower as smooth integral projective
$K$-varieties.  Frozen Theorem \texttt{r15tower:full-G},
in \path{round15/exceptional_classes/all_degree_projective_bundle_cycles.tex},
proves $\mathcal V_{*,K}(V)$ in this exact native-field
scope.  For a geometric homologically trivial class $z$
on $X$, pullback along the dominant $K$-morphism
$V\to X_F\to X$ remains homologically trivial on every
geometric component.  Its upstairs obstruction is zero
in every degree.  Apply
Proposition~\ref{r16G:dominant-witness} degree by degree.
The finite maps used inside the dominant theorem retain
their original native degrees.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Morphism versus rational-map scope.}
Theorem~\ref{r16G:towers} assumes an everywhere defined
dominant morphism.  Replacing it by an arbitrary rational
map would require control of new homologically trivial
exceptional classes on a resolution of the source.
The frozen Round 15 codimension-two rational-dominance
argument treats those exceptional classes by divisor
algebraic triviality; that argument does not automatically
extend to every codimension.  No all-degree stable
birational invariance is asserted here.

\begin{corollary}[Full strong G on geometrically uniruled fourfolds]
\label{r16G:uniruled-fourfolds}
Assume the independently accepted
\texttt{r16pb:dominant}.  Let $X/K$ be smooth projective
geometrically integral of dimension four and suppose
$X_{\bar K}$ is uniruled.  Then
\[
                         \mathcal V_{*,K}(X)
\tag{GC16.8}\label{r16G:uniruled-all}
\]
holds: every specified rational cycle with zero geometric
homology class, in every codimension, satisfies strong G
on its exact prescribed complement and against all
original actual tests.

The same full-kernel assertion holds on every supplied
projective-bundle tower over such a fourfold and on every
smooth projective dominant morphism image of such a tower.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Frozen Corollary \texttt{r15pbG:uniruled-fourfolds}, in
\path{round15/exceptional_classes/projective_bundle_dominated_cycles.tex},
already gives zero obstruction on the complete
codimension-two geometric homological kernel.  Its
codimension-two rational-dominance proof is used in its
original verified scope.  The new one-cycle theorem
gives degree three.  The frozen endpoint results give
degrees one and four.  These exhaust the positive
codimensions on a fourfold.

Frozen \texttt{r15tower:propagation} transfers the
all-degree vanishing up the projective-bundle tower.
Proposition~\ref{r16G:dominant-witness} transfers it
through an everywhere defined dominant morphism.  This
does not resolve a new rational map in higher degree
or assert a proper Gysin law for the obstruction.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Fourfolds with the new symmetry criterion]
\label{r16G:symmetric-fourfolds}
Assume \texttt{r16pb:dominant} and the independently
reviewed symmetry criterion
Corollary~\ref{r16sym:fourfold} of this round.
Let $X/K$ be a smooth projective geometrically integral
fourfold satisfying that criterion, or dominated after
a finite extension by a fourfold with such a supplied
model.  Then $\mathcal V_{*,K}(X)$ holds.

Every supplied projective-bundle tower over such an $X$
also has zero obstruction on its complete geometric
homological kernel in every degree.  The same is true
of every smooth projective dominant image of such a
tower, with finite constant extensions interpreted in
the original-field model-witness scope.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For $X$ of dimension four, degree two is the symmetry
criterion and its frozen degree-two dominant propagation.
Degree three is Theorem~\ref{r16G:one-cycles}.  Degrees
one and four are the frozen endpoint theorems for
geometrically homologically trivial divisors and
geometric degree-zero zero-cycles.  These are all positive
codimensions on a fourfold.

The all-degree projective-bundle propagation proposition
\texttt{r15tower:propagation} uses the Chow and geometric
cohomology projective-bundle decompositions and the
frozen graded obstruction ring.  Apply it along the
tower.  The last assertion follows from
Proposition~\ref{r16G:dominant-witness}.  Over a finite
constant extension, first use the model numerical
witness for the codimension-two assertion in the
original-field theory, the explicitly stated native
integral scope of the one-cycle theorem, and the
native endpoint statements already used in Round 15.
No current identity is relabeled as an identity on
another test category.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A concrete five-dimensional illustration]
\label{r16G:cubic-fivefold}
Let $X/K$ be a smooth cubic hypersurface of dimension five.
Then every specified geometrically homologically trivial
rational cycle on $X$, in every positive codimension, has
zero actual obstruction and satisfies strong G on its exact
support complement against all original actual tests.

If $X/F$ is a smooth cubic fivefold over a number field, the
separately reviewed height theorem of this round therefore
gives a canonical real bilinear pairing on the full group
\[
 \operatorname{CH}^3(X)_{\mathbf Q,\mathrm{geom\,hom}}
 \times
 \operatorname{CH}^3(X)_{\mathbf Q,\mathrm{geom\,hom}}.
\]
This is a concrete instance of the present local activation
and height bridge; no novelty of the corresponding classical
height, positivity, or nondegeneracy is asserted.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Choose a closed point of $X$ and pass to its finite residue
extension $L/K$.  Koll\'ar's primary theorem says that a
smooth cubic hypersurface of dimension at least two with
an $L$-point is $L$-unirational.  His Lemma 11 identifies
unirationality with a dominant rational map from affine
space of the same dimension; projective compactification
gives $\mathbf P^5_L\dashrightarrow X_L$.
Apply Corollary~\ref{r16cov:unirational}, whose proof uses
the independently established smooth-center obstruction
kernel theorem to handle the exceptional classes of the
resolution.  That corollary retains the original $K$-theory
and its native finite degrees, so no $L$-rational point
over $K$ itself is required.

At every completion of a number field the same argument
gives vanishing in all degrees, in particular degree three.
Theorem~\ref{r16h:global-height} now applies to the full
complementary groups, since $3+3=5+1$.
\end{proof}

The primary geometric input in the last proof is
J.~Koll\'ar, \emph{Unirationality of cubic hypersurfaces},
Theorem 1 and Lemma 11, \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0005146}.
The primary manuscript was checked directly; the geometric
unirationality theorem is not a new result of this report.

\paragraph{Remaining general gap.}
The independently accepted new analytic injectivity transports
every supplied global curvature primitive through a
dominant map.  It does not manufacture the upstairs
primitive for a general middle-dimensional cycle.
The numerical residual in codimension two on arbitrary
fourfold components, and the corresponding higher
codimension model-lift problems, remain genuine
separate existence questions.  The symmetry criterion
supplies one additional vanishing mechanism, not a
universal one.  The general unconditional BB-height
construction requires its separately audited global
interfaces and is not concluded from these local
statements alone.
