\subsection{Fixed native choices and an ordinary-open stability lemma}

All analytic spaces and functions below are over the original $K$
and absolute value.  Fix the original metric on the common line
bundle, write $R=c_1(\overline L)$, and use the literal native
SHT pair for each elementary shear and diagonal unit edge.
In every nested call to a capped witness fix the same real
constant $\varepsilon>0$ once and for all.  The formulas may
contain additional specified absolute-value sums of unit weights;
these are not replaced by bounds or by new primitives.

For a vector over a completed residue field use the sup norm;
for a matrix use the maximum of the absolute values of its entries.
Its operator norm is bounded by that maximum without a rank factor.
Let $S$ be any subset of an ordinary open $O$, and suppose analytic
matrix functions $F,\widetilde F\in\mathrm{GL}_r(\mathcal O(O))$
have the same value in $\mathscr H(x)$ for every $x\in S$.
Other matrices denoted $H,\widetilde H$ below are allowed to be
rectangular.  Their entries also have the same values on $S$.

\begin{lemma}[Finite whole-norm and positive-floor stability]
Fix $x\in S$ and a finite list of such matrices.  Suppose also
that a finite list of analytic units $u,\widetilde u$ have equal
values at $x$.  There is an ordinary neighborhood $O_x$ of $x$
on which all of the following hold simultaneously.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Every unit pair has exactly the same absolute value,
      hence the same valuation scalar and all actual derivatives.
\item For every section vector $f$ of the common line bundle,
\[
                         \|Ff\|=\|\widetilde Ff\|.
 \tag{GIC1}
\]
\item Suppose a specified capped norm in the list has the form
\[
 M_H(f)=\max\{\|Hf\|,\lambda\|Ff\|\},\qquad
 \widetilde M_H(f)=
       \max\{\|\widetilde Hf\|,\lambda\|\widetilde Ff\|\},
 \tag{GIC2}
\]
where $\lambda=e^{-\kappa-\varepsilon}>0$ and $\kappa$ is a
fixed continuous finite expression in the equal unit valuations.
Then $M_H(f)=\widetilde M_H(f)$ on $O_x$, including where
the passive subtuple itself is zero.  The same conclusion holds
for finitely many such capped norms at once.
\end{enumerate}
The neighborhoods may depend on $x$ and the finite presentation;
no global uniform lower bound or growth hypothesis is asserted.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a unit pair, shrink to
$|\widetilde u-u|<|u|$.  The ultrametric inequality gives
$|\widetilde u|=|u|$ everywhere on this ordinary open.
For each whole matrix shrink to
\[
           \|\widetilde F F^{-1}-I_r\|<1.
 \tag{GIC3}
\]
The multiplicative comparison $\widetilde F F^{-1}$ and its
inverse are sup-norm isometries pointwise; equivalently the perturbation of any vector
is strictly smaller than that vector.  This proves (GIC1).
The conditions hold near $x$ because their differences vanish
at $x$ and their right sides are strictly positive and continuous.

For (GIC2), impose in addition
\[
                  \|(\widetilde H-H)F^{-1}\|<\lambda.
 \tag{GIC4}
\]
The value $\lambda(x)$ is positive and finite, so (GIC4) holds
on an ordinary neighborhood.  At every point of it,
\[
       \|(\widetilde H-H)f\|<\lambda\|Ff\|
                    \quad\hbox{when }f\ne0.
 \tag{GIC5}
\]
If $\|Hf\|$ exceeds the common floor $\lambda\|Ff\|$,
the ultrametric inequality says the two passive norms agree.
If it does not exceed that floor, neither perturbed passive norm
exceeds the floor, so both maxima equal it.  This includes
the equality case at the floor.  At $f=0$ both sides are zero.
A finite intersection gives all required inequalities together.
The common metric contributes the same scalar factor to both
norms, so the statement is independent of its local frame.
\end{proof}

For the capped potentials in the exact witnesses, (GIC2) is
precisely the exponentiated form of
\[
        \Psi=\min\{\psi_{Hf},\Phi_{Ff}+\kappa+\varepsilon\}.
 \tag{GIC6}
\]
The equality is on a whole ordinary neighborhood, not merely
pointwise on $S$ or on an angular skeleton.  The same actual
scalar, all of its first jets, every corner form and every
mixed metric derivative are therefore equal there.  Equality
of the whole norms alone would not prove this passive-cap claim;
(GIC4) is the additional argument.

\subsection{A canonical ordinary partition for each two-row block}

Fix a smooth real function $\chi:\mathbb R\to[0,1]$ with
\[
          \chi(t)=0\ (t\le-\varepsilon),\qquad
          \chi(t)=1\ (t\ge\varepsilon).
 \tag{GIC7}
\]
For an analytic invertible block
$A=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\delta\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ put
$\Delta=\alpha\delta-\beta\gamma$.  Use the following specific
partition in GB22:
\[
 \vartheta_\alpha=\chi\bigl(v(\gamma)-v(\alpha)\bigr)
       \quad\hbox{on }\{\alpha\gamma\ne0\},\qquad
 \vartheta_\gamma=1-\vartheta_\alpha.
 \tag{GIC8}
\]
Extend $\vartheta_\alpha$ by zero near $\alpha=0$, and by
one near $\gamma=0$.  This is well defined: the determinant
unit excludes simultaneous pivot zeros; where one pivot vanishes,
the ratio of its norm to the other tends to zero on an ordinary
neighborhood, so (GIC7) already makes the function constant.
It is an ordinary smooth actual partition.  Its supports are
closed in the block domain and contained in the respective
pivot opens, and
\[
       \operatorname{supp}d'\vartheta_\alpha,
       \operatorname{supp}d''\vartheta_\alpha
                              \subset\{\alpha\gamma\ne0\}.
 \tag{GIC9}
\]
Here these supports are also closed in the full block domain.
Thus every singular-looking overlap expression is multiplied by
a coefficient which is identically zero on an ordinary neighborhood
of either pivot-zero locus, not just zero at its points.

Denote by $p_A(f)$ and $E_A$ the actual pair and residue in
GB22, with exactly (GIC8), its explicit GB16 overlap triple,
and all of the fixed native choices above.  They satisfy
\[
     G_{(A\oplus I)f}-G_f=\partial_1p_A(f)+E_A,
                         \qquad \partial_1(p',p'')=d'p'+d''p''.
 \tag{GIC10}
\]
The pair is defined off the common zero set; the residue is
defined on the entire block domain.  The residue depends on
the unit valuations, the chosen partition and $R$, not on a
choice of coordinates on the zero scheme.

\subsection{Literal conormal-germ invariance of the complete GB formula}

We compare two blocks $A,\widetilde A$, whose entries have the
same values at a point $x$, and two input tuples $Ff,\widetilde Ff$
with $F(x)=\widetilde F(x)$ invertible.  All block determinants
are units near $x$.  No assumption is made that the entries of
$f$ are units, nonzero at $x$, or independent tropical coordinates.

\begin{theorem}[Complete block-pair germ invariance]
On an ordinary neighborhood $O_x$ of $x$,
\[
 \begin{split}
 p_A(Ff)&=p_{\widetilde A}(\widetilde Ff)
                    &&\hbox{off the common zero set of }f,\\
 E_A&=E_{\widetilde A}
                    &&\hbox{on all of }O_x.
 \end{split}
 \tag{GIC11}
\]
These are equalities of the literal full actual formulas,
not merely equal boundaries or equal quotient classes.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
There are three exhaustive cases at $x$.

If $\alpha(x)=0$, then $\gamma(x)\ne0$ and the same holds
for the corresponding values of the other block.  Shrink so
both $\vartheta_\alpha$ vanish identically.  Formula GB22
then reduces to its second Gaussian word and residue
$v(\Delta)R^{r-1}$, with every differentiated-partition and
overlap-triple term identically absent.  The units $\gamma$
and $\Delta/\gamma$ have exactly equal norms for the two
blocks by the stability lemma.  In the second Gaussian word
the shear coefficient $-\alpha/\gamma$ vanishes at $x$;
both native pairs are identically zero near $x$ by SHT11.
The other shear coefficient $\delta/\gamma$ either vanishes
at $x$, when SHT11 again applies, or is a unit near $x$.
In the latter case its native middle normalizer and all the
other intermediate matrices are analytic and invertible near
$x$, with identical values for the two data sets.  Their
whole norms and all unit weights agree by (GIC1).
The entire SHT pair formulas are consequently identical.
The residue is also identical by the determinant valuation.
The case $\gamma(x)=0$ is the same argument with
$\vartheta_\alpha=1$ and the first Gaussian word; its possible
zero coefficients are $\gamma/\alpha$ and $\beta/\alpha$.

It remains to consider $\alpha(x)\gamma(x)\ne0$.
On a neighborhood put, exactly as in GB13,
\[
 t=\gamma/\alpha,\quad h=\Delta/\alpha,\quad
 u=\beta/\alpha,\quad z=\Delta/(\alpha\gamma),\quad
                         u+z=\delta/\gamma.
 \tag{GIC12}
\]
The six functions $\alpha,\gamma,\Delta,t,h,z$
are units.  Their versions for the other block have the same
values at $x$, and hence exactly equal valuations on an
ordinary neighborhood.  In particular the two partitions
(GIC8) and all their derivatives agree there.

We check every call in the fixed GB16 overlap expression.
The pivot subword GB10 and the remaining terms use the following
parameters, with the input tuples specified literally in GB10
and GB15:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 \text{call}&\text{unit or shear parameters}&
             \text{possible zero at }x\\\hline
 Q^{\rm W}_{21;t}&t&\text{none}\\
 Q^{\rm src}_{21;-t,t}&-t,t&\text{none}\\
 Q^{\rm tar}_{21;-1,t^{-1}}&-1,t^{-1}&\text{none}\\
 Q^{\rm tar}_{12;(\alpha t)^{-1},\alpha}
       &(\alpha t)^{-1},\alpha&\text{none}\\
 Q^{\rm src}_{12;z,h}&z,h&\text{none}\\
 Q^{\rm sl}_{1;\alpha,t}&\alpha,t&\text{none}\\
 Q^{\rm cs}_{1,\gamma;2,t^{-1}}&\gamma,t^{-1}&\text{none}\\
 Q^{\rm sl}_{2;h,t^{-1}}&h,t^{-1}&\text{none}\\
 Q^{\rm add}_{12;u,z}&u,z,u+z&u\text{ or }u+z.
\end{array}
 \tag{GIC13}
\]
The table includes the three nested pivot calls separately.
Every full tuple vertex in a nonzero-parameter call is
$Hf$ for an analytic invertible matrix $H$ obtained by a
finite rational expression in these parameters, the block
entries and the initial matrix $F$.  The corresponding
matrix for the other data set has exactly the same value
at $x$.  The same assertion holds for the matrix before
every passive-row projection used in a cap.

The only zero-parameter exception inside the overlap triple
is the last line.  If $u(x)=0$ or $(u+z)(x)=0$, the whole
SAR triple, not just its boundary, is identically zero near
$x$ for each data set: use respectively the proved ordinary
zero-gluing sets $|u|<1$ and $|u+z|<1$ in SAR7--SAR8.
In this case it is never necessary to form a singular
normalizer for that term.  Otherwise $u,z,u+z$ are all
units near $x$, and this last call is another finite
nonzero-parameter call.  The native first-level shear pairs
with coefficients $u$ or $u+z$ obey the same dichotomy,
using SHT11 when their coefficient vanishes at $x$.

It remains to check that the finite input data of all those
calls really agree, including their passive terms.  The
exact CS, SL, NSC, WNR and SAR cap definitions all have
the form (GIC6): their references are whole invertible
intermediate tuples; their passive terms are fixed row
projections; and their $\kappa$ are fixed finite sums,
absolute values or maxima of the unit valuations in the
nonzero-parameter call.  MX is a difference of two such
SL calls.  MT uses only the same complete vertices,
unit weights, reference and cap, including their full
derivatives.  Thus all unit weights agree by the first
part of the stability lemma, all whole norms agree by
(GIC1), and all caps agree by the positive-floor statement
(GIC2)--(GIC6).  This applies also at passive-subtuple
zeros.  There are only finitely many matrices and cap
floors, so one ordinary neighborhood suffices for all of
them.  The same $\varepsilon$ was fixed for corresponding
calls before making this comparison.

Each exact witness is a finite expression in these actual
scalars, their derivatives and $R$.  Therefore the whole
GB16 triple is identical there, with every metric term,
absolute-value wall and mixed coefficient retained.  Both
native Gaussian pairs are identical by the same full-norm
comparison.  Formula GB22, with the identical partitions,
proves the first equality in (GIC11).
Its extending error is exactly
$e_{v(\gamma),-v(t)}R^{r-2}$, so its determinant and
partition formula also gives the second equality on the
whole neighborhood.  No argument from a dense punctured
subset is being used for that residue.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[One supplied finite word]
Fix a finite ordered word of analytic two-row invertible blocks,
elementary shears, and diagonal units, with the same row labels
and factor types for two data sets.  Suppose corresponding
coefficient functions have equal values at $x$, and corresponding
initial invertible input matrices have equal values at $x$.
Use the canonical GB pair above for each block and the prescribed
SHT pair for each other factor.  The chronological sums of their
actual pairs are literally equal as punctured germs at $x$, and
the sums of their extending residues are equal as unpunctured
actual germs.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
All intermediate word matrices are analytic invertible and have
the same values at $x$.  For each block apply (GIC11) at that
intermediate tuple.  For a diagonal unit, use exact unit-weight
and whole-norm equality in SHT4.  For a shear, if its coefficient
has nonzero value use its analytic inverse normalizer and (GIC1);
if it has zero value, both entire pairs are zero on $|b|<1$
near $x$ by SHT11.  Sum the finitely many literal equalities.
This compares lifts of one fixed word, not arbitrary different
word decompositions of the same matrix.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Actual gluing from equal punctured germs}

Let $N$ be an ordinary open, $C\subset N$ relatively closed,
and $W=N\setminus C$.  Let $\omega$ be an actual diagonal
form on $W$.  Supply an ordinary cover $N=\bigcup_iN_i$ and
actual forms $p_i=(p_i',p_i'')$ on $N_i\cap W$ and $E_i$
on $N_i$ satisfying
\[
                          \omega=\partial_1p_i+E_i.
 \tag{GIC14}
\]
Assume that for every $x\in C\cap N_i\cap N_j$ there is
an ordinary neighborhood of $x$ on whose punctured part
$p_i=p_j$ literally.  This is a stronger input than pairwise
equality modulo a relative boundary.  It has just been proved
for congruent lifts of the prescribed finite native words.

Choose an ordinary smooth locally finite partition
$\sum_i\rho_i=1$ with supports closed in $N$ and contained
in $N_i$, using the frozen ordinary partition interface.
On $W$ define
\[
 p=\sum_i\rho_i p_i,\qquad
 H=\sum_i\bigl(d'\rho_i\wedge p_i'
                         +d''\rho_i\wedge p_i''\bigr).
 \tag{GIC15}
\]
Closed-support zero extension gives actual forms; locally these
are finite sums.  The following lemma specifies the extension
of $H$ rather than assuming an abstract sheaf descent.

\begin{lemma}[Literal-germ gluing]
The form $H$ is identically zero on a punctured ordinary
neighborhood of each point of $C$.  Its zero extension
$\widetilde H$ across $C$ is actual on $N$, and
\[
        E=\sum_i\rho_iE_i-\widetilde H\in B(N),\qquad
                        \omega=\partial_1p+E|_W.
 \tag{GIC16}
\]
No triple-overlap witness or chosen quotient inverse is needed.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $x\in C$.  Local finiteness first restricts to a neighborhood
meeting only finitely many partition supports.  Those finitely
many supports not containing $x$ can then be excluded by
shrinking.  Every remaining index contains $x$ in $N_i$.
Intersect the finitely many neighborhoods on which the pairs
are literally equal; write their common pair as $p_0$ there.
On its punctured part, the two sums of differentiated partition
coefficients vanish, so
\[
               H=d'\Bigl(\sum_i\rho_i\Bigr)\wedge p_0'
                   +d''\Bigl(\sum_i\rho_i\Bigr)\wedge p_0''=0.
 \tag{GIC17}
\]
Thus the actual form $H$ on $W$ glues with the actual zero
form on the union of these neighborhoods of $C$.  This
constructs $\widetilde H$ on an ordinary open cover of $N$.
Taking the full differentials of the finite local sum in
(GIC15), then using (GIC14), gives (GIC16) on all of $W$.
This proof does not infer equality of two extensions from
their agreement on a dense complement.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The shared-tail theorem without a single affine neighborhood}

Retain $X,L,s_0,s_1,Z,U,W_0,C_U$ of AST1--AST2, in every
$n\ge r\ge2$, but replace AST3 by the weaker hypothesis:
there is a Zariski open $V_{\rm alg}\subset X$, not assumed
affine, with
\[
 C_U\subset V_{\rm alg}^{\rm an},\qquad
 \mathcal I_{Y_0}|_{V_{\rm alg}}
        =\mathcal I_{Y_1}|_{V_{\rm alg}}=:\mathcal I.
 \tag{GIC18}
\]
The last $r-2$ global sections are still literally shared.
The common zero scheme on $V_{\rm alg}$ may be nonreduced.

Choose a finite affine open cover $V_{\rm alg}=\bigcup_iV_i$.
This is possible because $V_{\rm alg}$ is an open in a
noetherian algebraic variety.  The exact affine module argument
of AST6 gives on each $V_i$ a matrix
\[
    s_1=T_i s_0,\qquad
    T_i=\begin{pmatrix}A_i&B_i\\0&I_{r-2}\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{GIC19}
\]
The frozen ICC regular-sequence relation calculation, applied
locally in a common frame of $L$, proves two precise facts:
\[
 \begin{split}
 &\det A_i\text{ is a unit modulo }\mathcal I,\\
 &(T_i-T_j)_{ab}\in\mathcal I
                          \quad\hbox{on }V_i\cap V_j.
 \end{split}
 \tag{GIC20}
\]
For the second fact, every row of $(T_i-T_j)s_0=0$ is a
relation among a regular sequence, whose coefficients are in
the generated ideal.  This holds before taking a reduction.
In particular corresponding entries have equal values at every
analytic point of the common zero scheme on an overlap.

Set
\[
 N_i=\bigl(V_i\setminus V(\det A_i)\bigr)^{\rm an}\cap U,
                            \qquad N=\bigcup_iN_i.
 \tag{GIC21}
\]
These are ordinary opens, $N\subset U$, and $N$ contains
all of $C_U$.  On each $N_i$ execute the same finite word
\[
 T_i=\left(\prod_{a=1}^2\prod_{b=3}^r
                   E_{ab}((B_i)_{a,b-2})\right)(A_i\oplus I),
 \tag{GIC22}
\]
with the fixed lexicographic order.  Direct multiplication
proves (GIC22); no general factorization theorem is invoked.
Use the canonical GB block pair and the native tail-shear
pairs to construct $p_i,E_i$ as in (GIC14), for
$\omega=G_1-G_0$ and $W=N\cap W_0$.

The matrix congruence (GIC20) and the fixed-word corollary of
(GIC11) give literal equality of the punctured $p_i$ germs at
every point of $C_U\cap N_i\cap N_j$.  Apply (GIC15)--(GIC16).
This constructs one actual pair $p$ on $N\cap W_0$ and one
actual residue $E$ on $N$, with the complete identity
\[
                         G_1-G_0=\partial_1p+E.
 \tag{GIC23}
\]
The finite algebraic cover is used only to produce local
coefficient matrices; no global lift of their coefficients
on $V_{\rm alg}$ was required.

\begin{theorem}[Nonseparated common-ideal cancellation with a shared tail]
For the original regular same-line-bundle, same-metric tuples
with a literally shared tail and (GIC18), there is an actual
\[
                  g_Z\in B^{r-1,r-1}
                          (X^{\rm an}\setminus|Z|)
 \tag{GIC24}
\]
over the original $K$ and absolute value such that
\[
       \int_Ug_Z\wedge d'd''\eta=-\int_Z\eta
           \qquad\text{for every }
                    \eta\in B_c^{n-r,n-r}(X^{\rm an}).
 \tag{GIC25}
\]
Neither a single affine cancellation neighborhood nor closedness
of $C_U$ in $X^{\rm an}$ is assumed.  In rank $r=2$ the
shared-tail condition is empty: any such regular CI pair whose
ideals agree on a Zariski neighborhood of its canceled part has
this original-support construction, in every ambient dimension.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply the frozen RSP construction to (GIC23).  More explicitly,
choose its ordinary smooth actual cutoff $\zeta$ on $U$, with
support closed in $U$ and contained in $N$, equal to one near
$C_U$.  On $W_0$ put
\[
      g_Z=G_1-G_0-d'(\zeta p')-d''(\zeta p'').
 \tag{GIC26}
\]
The standard closed-support extensions make this actual.
Near $C_U$ it is the actual form $E$ by (GIC23), so it
glues to (GIC24) on the original exact $U$.
For every actual compact test in (GIC25), the frozen support
lemma makes $\operatorname{supp}D\eta$ compactly disjoint
from $|Y_0|\cup|Y_1|$.  Both correction products are compact
on $W_0$, and actual Stokes annihilates them.  The two individual
CI identities have the same curvature term, which cancels.
Their difference is exactly (GIC25), with the original cycles
and multiplicities.  No cutoff across $|Z|$ on $X$ was used.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Actual relations, topology and remaining boundaries.}
The stability comparisons are literal scalar equalities on whole
ordinary neighborhoods.  Thus they retain nonproduct units such
as $(T_1,T_2,T_1+T_2)$, all affine intercept walls, arbitrary
passive directions and mixed derivatives.  No property of
separated test products is used.  The estimates are over every
completed residue field, including non-rational analytic points;
they make no field extension or degree normalization.  The moving
curve calibration AST13--AST24 continues to apply and is not
recounted as a new example theorem.

All local expressions and all sums on a compact test support have
finite actual presentations.  The frozen support lemma gives the
usual full coefficient-final continuity interface for $I(g_Z)$.
There is no new auxiliary topology, completed representative,
or universal continuous linear primitive selection.

For $r\ge3$ the shared-tail hypothesis remains substantive.
The finite-word germ theorem compares lifts of one supplied word;
it does not factor an arbitrary conormal matrix into such a word.
Different ideals at the cancellation locus are not covered here.
General homologically trivial cycles, arbitrary high-order
original-field descent, the whole lower-middle kernel topology,
and the complete Beilinson--Bloch height axioms remain \textbf{OPEN}.
