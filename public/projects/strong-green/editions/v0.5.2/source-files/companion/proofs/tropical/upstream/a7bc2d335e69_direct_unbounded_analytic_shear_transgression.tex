\section{An actual transgression for an arbitrary analytic elementary shear}
\label{sec:r9-unbounded-analytic-shear-transgression}

An elementary shear with an unbounded analytic coefficient need
not have the global bounded-factorization certificate RAT14.
Nevertheless the relative rank-one primitive can be made exactly
antisymmetric.  Two such primitives cancel on an open neighborhood
of the coefficient's zero locus.  This gives actual sheaf gluing
across that locus without an extension across a valuation wall,
a step function, or a discarded mixed jet.

\subsection{A universal potential-pair primitive}

Let $r\ge2$, $s=r-1$, and let $R$ be the actual curvature
of the fixed metric on a line bundle.  Suppose two section
tuples differ by multiplying one entry by an analytic unit
$\lambda$.  Write their actual CI scalar potentials as
$\Phi_0,\Phi_1$ and put
\[
 a=v(\lambda),\quad C_i=R-D\Phi_i,\quad v=\Phi_1-\Phi_0,
 \quad B_j=-v\sum_{\ell=0}^{j-1}C_1^\ell C_0^{j-1-\ell},
 \quad P_i=\Phi_i\sum_{\ell=0}^{s-1}C_i^\ell R^{s-1-\ell}.
 \tag{SHT1}
\]
The literal RAT11 primitive, using $B_s=Q_s$, depends
only on $(\Phi_0,\Phi_1,a,R)$:
\[
 \begin{split}
 A(\Phi_0,\Phi_1,a)&=
    \sum_{j=1}^s\Phi_0\,d''B_j R^{s-j}
                   +a_+d''B_s-a\,d''P_0,\\
 B(\Phi_0,\Phi_1,a)&=
    \sum_{j=1}^sd'\Phi_0\wedge B_j R^{s-j}
                   +d'a_+\wedge B_s-d'a\wedge P_0.
 \end{split}
 \tag{SHT2}
\]
Here $a_+=\max(a,0)$.  The notation $B(\cdot)$ for
the second primitive is distinct from the indexed
even-degree forms $B_j$ in (SHT1).  No choice of
angular coordinates or compact primitive remains
in these formulas.  Every mixed metric and base
derivative appearing in RAT is displayed.

The rank-one identity RAT12 states
\[
 G_1-G_0=d'A(\Phi_0,\Phi_1,a)
              +d''B(\Phi_0,\Phi_1,a)+aR^s.
 \tag{SHT3}
\]
The inverse entry multiplication is another permitted
rank-one change, so it has the same formula with
$(\Phi_1,\Phi_0,-a)$.  Define the antisymmetric pair
\[
 \begin{split}
 A^-_{01,a}&=\tfrac12\bigl(A(\Phi_0,\Phi_1,a)
                                  -A(\Phi_1,\Phi_0,-a)\bigr),\\
 B^-_{01,a}&=\tfrac12\bigl(B(\Phi_0,\Phi_1,a)
                                  -B(\Phi_1,\Phi_0,-a)\bigr).
 \end{split}
 \tag{SHT4}
\]
Subtraction of the two exact identities gives
\[
 G_1-G_0=d'A^-_{01,a}+d''B^-_{01,a}+aR^s,
       \qquad (A^-_{10,-a},B^-_{10,-a})
                                  =-(A^-_{01,a},B^-_{01,a}).
 \tag{SHT5}
\]
The division by two is in the real coefficient field
of actual forms; no extraction of a square root in
$K$ or residue-characteristic restriction is involved.

There is also an expanded finite formula verifying
the inverse identity directly.  Write
$\overline\Phi=(\Phi_0+\Phi_1)/2$ and
$\overline P=(P_0+P_1)/2$.  Since the reverse change
replaces every $B_j$ by $-B_j$, and
$a_+-(-a)_+=a$, whereas $a_++(-a)_+=|a|$, direct
substitution in (SHT4) gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 A^-_{01,a}&=\sum_{j=1}^s\overline\Phi\,d''B_jR^{s-j}
                         +\tfrac12|a|\,d''B_s-a\,d''\overline P,\\
 B^-_{01,a}&=\sum_{j=1}^sd'\overline\Phi\wedge B_jR^{s-j}
                         +\tfrac12d'|a|\wedge B_s-d'a\wedge\overline P.
 \end{split}
 \tag{SHT6}
\]
Under $(\Phi_0,\Phi_1,a)\mapsto(\Phi_1,\Phi_0,-a)$,
the barred forms and $|a|$ are unchanged, the $B_j$
change sign, and $a,d'a$ change sign.  Every term in
(SHT6) therefore changes sign, including all wall and
mixed coefficients.  Equality of two input potentials
means literal equality of these entire actual formulas.

\subsection{The unbounded coefficient and its zero locus}

Let $N$ be an analytic open, let $f$ be an $r$-tuple
of analytic sections of the same metrized line bundle,
and let $b\in\mathcal O(N)$ be arbitrary.  It may
be unbounded, have zeros, or vanish identically on a
component.  For $i\ne j$ write
$E_{ij}(b)=I+b e_{ij}$, an analytic invertible matrix.
On the open $N_b=\{b\ne0\}$, set $a=v(b)$ and let
$D_i(b)$ multiply the $i$th entry by $b$.  The exact
matrix identity is
\[
                E_{ij}(b)=D_i(b)E_{ij}(1)D_i(b)^{-1}.
 \tag{SHT7}
\]
The middle matrix and its inverse have integral
constant entries, hence are maximum-norm isometries
over every completed residue field.

On the complement of the common zero locus, denote
the scalar potentials of the following tuples by
the symbols displayed:
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c}
 f&\Phi_0\\
 D_i(b)^{-1}f&\Phi_1\\
 E_{ij}(1)D_i(b)^{-1}f&\Phi_1\\
 E_{ij}(b)f&\Phi_2.
 \end{array}
 \tag{SHT8}
\]
The repeated potential is literal norm equality,
not an omission of the middle tuple.  Apply (SHT4)
to the first entry change with weight $-a$ and
to the last entry change with weight $a$, and put
\[
 \mathcal A_b=A^-_{01,-a}+A^-_{12,a},
                \qquad
 \mathcal B_b=B^-_{01,-a}+B^-_{12,a}.
 \tag{SHT9}
\]
The two residues in (SHT5) cancel.  Thus on $N_b$
off the common zero locus,
\[
                 G_{E_{ij}(b)f}-G_f
                                =d'\mathcal A_b+d''\mathcal B_b.
 \tag{SHT10}
\]

On the open set $N_{<1}=\{|b|<1\}$, the matrix
$E_{ij}(b)$ itself and its inverse are maximum-norm
isometries.  Therefore $\Phi_2=\Phi_0$ literally
on $N_b\cap N_{<1}$.  In (SHT9), the data
$(\Phi_1,\Phi_2,a)$ are then exactly the reverse
of $(\Phi_0,\Phi_1,-a)$.  Equation (SHT5), or the
termwise calculation (SHT6), gives
\[
                  \mathcal A_b=\mathcal B_b=0
                                      \quad\text{on }N_b\cap N_{<1}
 \tag{SHT11}
\]
as actual forms, including every derivative of $a$.

The two analytic opens $N_b$ and $N_{<1}$ cover $N$.
Glue (SHT9) on the first with zero on the second.
Equation (SHT11) is the required equality on their
open overlap, so this constructs actual forms on
the full complement of the tuple's zero locus in
$N$.  They continue to satisfy (SHT10): on the
second open both the CI difference and the right
side are identically zero.

Notice where no gluing is being done.  The locus
$|b|=1$ lies entirely in $N_b$, and is handled
there by the complete actual formulas RAT and
SHT1--SHT9.  We never extend an expression from
$|b|>1$ by a characteristic function or identify
zero boundary values with agreement of all jets.
The only gluing crosses $b=0$, across which the
forms are already zero on an open neighborhood.

\begin{theorem}[Actual elementary-shear transgression]
\label{thm:r9-actual-elementary-shear-transgression}
For every analytic coefficient $b$ and every tuple
$f$ as above, (SHT9)--(SHT11) construct actual
$\mathcal A_b\in B^{r-2,r-1}$ and
$\mathcal B_b\in B^{r-1,r-2}$ on the complement
of its zero locus in $N$, with the exact identity
(SHT10).  There is no boundedness or unit hypothesis
on $b$.  The construction is over the original
$K$ and retains the entire common metric.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Finite elementary factorizations and exact-support gluing}

Suppose a matrix comparison $A$ on $N$ is supplied
as a finite product of analytic elementary shears,
analytic-unit diagonal matrices, and pointwise
maximum-norm isometric matrices.  Apply SHT10 to
each elementary factor, RAT13 to each diagonal
factor, and literal norm equality to the isometric
factors.  Telescope along the actual intermediate
tuples.  This gives displayed actual primitives
\[
 G_{Af}-G_f=d'\alpha_A+d''\beta_A
                                  +v(\det A)R^{r-1}.
 \tag{SHT12}
\]
All factors, intermediate tuples and formulas
are finite.  This upgrades the input class in
RSP9--RSP10 without changing its proof: if such
a comparison holds on one analytic neighborhood
$N\subset U$ of the cancelled part $C_U$, RSP5
constructs the actual strong Green form on the
original exact complement, with all actual tests.

For example, ordinary Gaussian elimination supplies
such a finite factorization whenever the required
successive pivot minors are analytic units on the
whole stated $N$, after specified fixed row and
column permutations.  Its elementary coefficients
need not be bounded.  The pivot-unit conditions
are independently checkable analytic conditions.
An arbitrary invertible analytic matrix is not
declared to admit one such global factorization;
a cover by pivot domains is not by itself a
compatible gluing of their Aeppli primitives.

Every construction uses finite local PWS scalar
presentations, actual derivatives and finite sums.
The identities survive all analytic refinements.
On a fixed compact presentation, only finitely
many pieces of the two-open gluing are needed,
and all mixed seminorms of the finite formulas
are retained.  No completion, arbitrary current
representative, continuous global splitting, or
full analytic OPEN-KERNEL theorem is invoked.
