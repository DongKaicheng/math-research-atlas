\subsection{The actual geometry of coordinate deletion}

The full product staircase has rays indexed by component tuples
and cones indexed by chains in the coordinatewise order. On a
maximal chain, the first ray and successive coordinate differences
are an integral basis of the lattice with equal heights. Thus the
claimed reduced strictly semistable local model is correct.
The inductive projective binary construction gives this same fan:
lexicographic order agrees with the path order on every old chain,
and interleaving that chain with the next coordinate gives exactly
the full product chains. This establishes the particular global
projective model rather than presuming projectivity of an arbitrary
triangulation.

The dimension-free component-star induction of PX20 gives the
actual boundary-stratum blowup tower at each binary stage.
Pulling an earlier tower back by the next smooth component is
again a boundary-stratum tower. The quotient rays of two-cones
through the selected component ray are exactly its final boundary
divisors, and higher cones give their actual intersections. Thus
the whole boundary, including every exceptional divisor and higher
stratum, is retained in the induction. This is necessary both for
the later generator decomposition and for its restriction to a
chosen factor open set.

Coordinate deletion maps each chain to a chain after removing
repeated projected vertices. On a component quotient lattice,
the simultaneous maps to the fixed-factor boundary coordinates
and to the remaining-component lattice form an integral
isomorphism of the underlying lattice. For one deleted factor,
one checks this by choosing the representative of its omitted
coordinate to make its height equal to the remaining height;
the ambiguity is precisely the full component ray. The source
fan refines the target product fan. The resulting toroidal map
is therefore the asserted proper birational morphism
\[
 D_{\mathbf i}\longrightarrow
       X_{j,i_j}\times W_{-j,\mathbf i_{-j}},
\]
and the analogous maps for larger fixed sets commute. They are
over the original component product. Over $U_j$ the fixed factor
has one local branch, so the modification is the literal product
with the \emph{actual} remaining staircase model. The target is
not silently replaced by the unmodified remaining product.

For a same-label double, the projected two-ray cone remains a
two-ray cone. A coordinate of another branch of a fixed factor
has valuation zero on both rays, so no additional double component
can be supported over that fixed-factor boundary. Over the smooth
coefficient open the map is the unchanged product, proving the
degree-one birational assertion. Changing-label doubles do map
into the indicated factor boundary. These statements preserve
all connected components of each intersection.

\subsection{Projector commutation includes exceptional cohomology}

For a surface component, the rational self-dual KMP projector
$p_j$ has only the transcendental $H^2$ realization. It annihilates
the image of cohomology supported on its SNC curve boundary and
restricts to zero on that boundary. These facts were checked
against the primary construction in the independent PX20 review.
By proper pushforward with supports and Kunneth with supports,
$P_j$ consequently kills every class on $D$ supported over
$\partial X_j$, including exceptional classes there.

In (DP20.7), the operator $1-a^*a_*$ has image $\ker a_*$,
since $a_*a^*=1$. Because $a$ is an isomorphism off the
specified $k$-factor boundary, localization puts this image over
that boundary. The independent $j$-factor projector preserves
this support, and pullback to $D$ retains it. Thus $P_k$ kills
the entire difference in that formula. On the pullback from the
common deletion base, $P_k$ acts as the independent factor
operator $p_k$, since the intervening map modifies other factors
and commutes with it. This proves $P_kP_j=P_{\{j,k\}}$ on
all cohomology. Self-adjointness supplies the reverse order.
The identical argument with a fixed set $I$ proves the induction.

This proof is not an appeal to agreement on a dense open.
It explicitly controls the supported difference between the
two projector constructions. In particular it applies to the
exceptional copies highlighted by HE20.

The same-label divisor transport lemma of PX20 applies on the
actual deletion base and includes all exceptional inputs. At
an off-diagonal edge its push-pull is $f'_*f'^*=1$, with full
generic degree one. The diagonal term is the negative sum of
the boundary restriction--Gysin terms because the reduced total
fiber is principal. Changing-label terms vanish after $p_j$.
These computations prove (DP20.8), with no cancellation of an
ambient Gysin map and no loss of higher intersections. Factoring
through the common deletion base also identifies the remaining
projectors on $\operatorname{im}P_I$. Hence the orthogonal
$R_I$ sectors and formula (DP20.10) are valid.

\subsection{The crucial relative-Chow generator gate}

The all-stratum blowup formulas (DP20.11) are actual scheme
formulas, including the cases of Cartier restriction centers and
empty intersections. Applying the smooth blowup and projective
bundle formulas after every test product gives the finite
decomposition (DP20.12) by original closed product strata with
the actual Lefschetz exponents. Its injection maps are built
from structural pullbacks, stratum Gysin maps, and boundary or
tautological Chern classes. All structural maps are over the
original component product.

Fix a generator whose original stratum has full $j$th surface
factor, $S_\alpha=X_j\times M_\alpha$. It is essential that
the source now specifies a relative Chow representative, not
merely an ordinary correspondence supported over the base.
There is a concrete relative space here:
\[
 (X_j\times M_\alpha)\times_{X_j}D
                  \simeq M_\alpha\times D.
\]
The structural graph classes, the operational Chern operations,
and the refined intersections defining the generator retain
their support in this fiber product. Refined Gysin operations
with prescribed support and proper pushforward give its
relative class; restriction to an open base commutes with all
these constructions. Thus the source has supplied more than an
equality of absolute cohomological actions on an open set.

The whole tower restricted to $U_j$ is a literal product tower.
At the first stage every remaining boundary divisor is
$U_j$ times a divisor of the remaining product; a boundary of
the $j$th factor is empty there. At each subsequent stage the
chosen center is accordingly empty or $U_j$ times a closed
boundary stratum. Blowup commutes with this product. The
transformed boundary divisors, their normal line bundles, the
exceptional projective bundles, and their tautological line
bundles are all the corresponding product objects. In
particular no unverified line-bundle twist from $U_j$ is inserted:
the boundary divisor line bundles and the blowup ideal pull
back from the remaining pair. Consequently the standard
decomposition injection restricts term by term to
$1_{U_j}\boxtimes u_{\alpha,R_j}$ in the relative Chow group,
with its original Lefschetz exponent. A disappearing generator
has zero restricted injection.

The identification of $R_j$ with the exact $W_{-j}$ also passes.
The two product descriptions give an isomorphism over
$U_j\times B_{-j}^{\rm orig}$. Evaluating at any closed point
of $U_j$ gives an isomorphism after a finite residue extension.
Over their common original base this isomorphism is unique:
both varieties are integral proper birational modifications
and every such isomorphism is the identity on their common
dense birational open. Separatedness then gives uniqueness
everywhere. Its Galois conjugates agree, so it descends.
The original isomorphism over $U_j$ equals the product of
that descended map, again by uniqueness. Thus the restricted
generator takes values in the exact deletion component,
including all of its exceptional copies.

Now compare the relative class of $u_\alpha$ with the relative
class of $f_j^*(1_{X_j}\boxtimes u_{\alpha,-j})$. The literal
product construction just proved makes them equal on the
relative open over $U_j$. Chow localization in the displayed
fiber product supports their difference over $\partial X_j$.
For each integral cycle in a representative of this difference,
take a smooth projective generically finite alteration of full
degree $d$. The corresponding action is $d^{-1}b_*a^*$.
The $X_j$ coordinate of $a$ factors through a boundary curve
or a point. Its pullback of $p_jH^*(X_j)$ is zero; Kunneth
therefore kills every input after $p_j\boxtimes1$.
This proves (DP20.13).

This alteration is used only to justify an identity of
cohomological correspondences supported on a cycle. It is not
an analytic actual-form pushforward, not a replacement of the
semistable witness, and not asserted finite off any cycle support.
The coefficient is the complete generic degree, including any
inseparable part. The crucial gate is thus discharged by a
relative Chow equality before localization, rather than by the
invalid inference that two arbitrary correspondences agreeing
on an open set have the needed global projector relation.

For HE20's concrete example $D=V\times\operatorname{Bl}_Z B$,
the deletion target for $V$ is the actual blowup. The exceptional
generator becomes $1_V$ tensored with the exceptional injection
of that blowup. Formula (DP20.13) therefore captures precisely
the $H^2_{\rm tr}(V)$ exceptional copy which the unmodified-base
projector misses. The current proof survives that explicit
failed-induction test.

\subsection{The abelian core uses only homological idempotents}

Each original product-stratum motive splits as the tensor of
refined surface pieces and curve or point pieces. Separate the
summands containing at least one full-surface $t_2$ coefficient
from the finite abelian-type complement. For a summand of the
first kind, choose one such index $j$. The generator lemma puts
its image in $P_j$, so $Q$ kills it. Equation (DP20.14) follows
from the actual decomposition identity $uv=1$ and the
cohomological idempotence of $Q$.

Thus $u_Qv_Q=Q$ on all cohomology with actual rational
correspondences $u_Q,v_Q$. No Chow-level commutation of the
$P_j$ and no new Chow-idempotent lifting is required. The
remaining Artin actions are split by the finite constant
extensions allowed in the setup; the other pieces are actual
Tate and Picard/Albanese summands of abelian motives. The
argument applies to each deletion model because its full
tower and boundary were proved by the same induction.

Choose one abelian carrier for the finitely many such finite
sources, over all component labels and deletion subsets.
Deligne--Clozel's primary Theorem~1 and Remark~(b), independently
checked in the PX20 review, give one prime for all its powers.
Native numerical zero passes to geometric numerical zero by
finite-constant norm, full degree and projection formula.
Consequently every required numerical error on these carriers
has zero geometric class at that prime.

The corner argument is correct even though $e=v_Qu_Q$ need
not be a Chow idempotent. It is an actual correspondence,
idempotent in cohomology and therefore in numerical motives.
Jannsen splits the numerical map
$a=v_QAu_Q$ between the opposite Tate corners and supplies
a numerical generalized inverse. An actual Chow representative
has numerical-zero error on the already controlled abelian
carrier, so the chosen-prime comparison proves $aga=a$ in
cohomology. From $u_Qv_Q=Q$ and $AQ=QA$, one obtains
\[
 A u_Q=u_Qa,\qquad v_QA|_Q=a v_Q,
\]
and hence $AG_QA=A$ on the core for $G_Q=u_Qgv_Q$.
This never applies numerical semisimplicity directly to an
unidentified category of homological motives.
