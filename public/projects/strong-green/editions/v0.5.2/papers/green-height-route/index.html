Zero-Curvature Green Forms and Beilinson–Bloch Height Pairings

Local Green forms, principal divisors and descent to Chow groups

23 November 2026

Abstract

We describe a direct route from actual non-Archimedean Green forms to a Beilinson–Bloch height pairing on the full complementary homologically trivial Chow groups. Disjoint-polar Stokes gives reciprocity and independence of Green choices. A scalar principal Green function on a normal carrier, together with supported point residues, extension of actual germs and projective-bundle integration, gives the exact principal-divisor norm law. We prove the global descent by the product formula and record the finite-extension normalization. The local existence conjecture and the additional analytic constructions are stated separately. The resulting programme identifies both the obstruction to constructing actual Green forms and the compatibility needed to turn them into global heights.

These hypotheses are the analytic tasks of the route, rather than consequences inferred from the existence assertion alone.

6. A programme for completing the direct route

6.1. Constructing actual Green forms

Problem 6.1 (Existence on the exact support complement). Prove Conjecture 1.1 for all smooth projective X/KX/KX/K and all codimensions. The construction must yield a section on UZU_ZUZ​ and (1.1) for every compact actual test, over the original valued field.

One sufficient construction begins with a curved pair D[GZ]+δZ=[ΩZ],D[G_Z]+\delta_Z=[\Omega_Z],D[GZ​]+δZ​=[ΩZ​], where ΩZ\Omega_ZΩZ​ is global actual, and produces a global actual primitive DΨZ=ΩZD\Psi_Z=\Omega_ZDΨZ​=ΩZ​. Then GZ−ΨZ∣UZG_Z-\Psi_Z|_{U_Z}GZ​−ΨZ​∣UZ​​ is the required form by Stokes. This is the obstruction route developed separately in [5]. A direct construction may instead produce γZ\gamma_ZγZ​ without identifying this selected curvature class.

A tropical approach seeks compatible primitives on charts and refinements. Its required output is a global actual form, not just a compatible family of current classes. If local primitives Ψi\Psi_iΨi​ satisfy DΨi=ΩD\Psi_i=\OmegaDΨi​=Ω on a cover, their differences are DDD-closed on overlaps. The gluing problem is to modify them by DDD-closed actual forms so that they agree. A satisfactory comparison must carry these overlap relations, both differentials, and the original integration pairing through refinements. A topological inverse-limit description by itself supplies none of those differential conclusions.

Problem 6.2 (Descent and gluing of primitives). Construct a comparison from a specified tropical or Aeppli complex to the ordinary actual form complex which sends the proposed curvature class to ΩZ\Omega_ZΩZ​, sends the constructed primitive to a section of Br−1,r−1(Xan)\mathcal B^{r-1,r-1}(X^{\mathrm{an}})Br−1,r−1(Xan), and commutes with d′,d′′d',d''d′,d′′ and integration on all original tests. Prove compatibility with refinements and with finite-extension descent in Hypothesis 5.1.

For this purpose the comparison has to recover actual representatives and their relations. Exactness after enlarging the test space dual, or after replacing a form by an arbitrary current, does not establish the required map into Br−1,r−1\mathcal B^{r-1,r-1}Br−1,r−1.

6.2. The analytic operations needed for the principal law

Problem 6.3 (Supported residues and principal push). Establish Hypothesis 2.1 and Hypothesis 3.2 and Hypothesis 5.1 for the stated actual categories. In particular, prove the supported point-residue formula on normal carriers, presentation-independent finite trace with all-test adjunction, extension of the actual germs used in Lemma 3.3, and the projective-bundle restriction identity (3.3).

The point-residue calculation must retain the refined intersection coefficients. A normal carrier need not be Cohen–Macaulay, so lengths of an underived intersection cannot replace refined graph intersection without a separate justification. The omitted fibre is treated by the actual vanishing of −log⁡∣u∣-\log|u|−log∣u∣ near u=−1u=-1u=−1; no intersection formula on that larger fibre is used. These are the two geometric features responsible for the exact norm in Proposition 3.4.

The hypotheses have different roles. Actual Stokes supplies independence of Green choices and reciprocity. Supported residues and projective integration supply the scalar norm of a principal cycle. Good reduction makes the global sum finite. The weighted product formula then gives rational-equivalence descent. Thus solving the existence problem must be accompanied by these compatibilities, as stated in Theorem 4.2.

6.3. Identification with the classical height

Problem 6.4 (Adjunction and classical comparison). For the resulting heights, establish correspondence adjunction hX(Γ∗z,w)=hY(z,tΓ∗w)h_X(\Gamma_*z,w)=h_Y(z,{}^t\Gamma_*w)hX​(Γ∗​z,w)=hY​(z,tΓ∗​w) on the complementary domains on which the operations are defined. Identify the pairing with the classical Beilinson–Bloch constructions on their common domains and with the same absolute normalization. For a pointed curve, match the scalar convention with the Poincaré convention in which −hC-h_C−hC​ is the positive Néron–Tate form.

The companion comparison proofs [4, Sections 8–9] use proper-intersection residues and, for arithmetic-model comparison, supplied numerically admissible lifts on regular models. Existence of those lifts is additional to the comparison theorem. Establishing the pairing, identifying it with the classical constructions, and proving positivity of its primitive part are separate mathematical assertions. This route is complete for the existence problem once the hypotheses of Theorem 4.2 are established; the adjunction and normalization problem identifies the resulting object as the expected canonical height system.
