Plan for the full theory

The bounded potential calculus supports three parallel directions: intrinsic curvature operations, relative capacity and measure equations. Their results feed into geometric applications.

1. Intrinsic curvature operations

The main target is an action independent of analytic presentations, over the original field and on one ordinary neighborhood fixed before the background and its tests. The quadratic problem is the first case in which a relation can couple distinct curvature factors. Descent is established for finite presentations of joint residue rank at most two. In the supplied normal-three models, the remaining action is concentrated at the centre and has an explicit spherical pairing.

The immediate task is to prove cancellation of this jointly realized pairing. A geometric wall-flux identity must exploit the joint realization of the relation and the background. An alternative is a full-exact construction on one ordinary representative, chosen before the background and its tests. Cellwise primitives exist, but their overlap discrepancies can contribute at the critical scale. Following this route beyond the supplied models requires a return to general ordinary germs and a treatment of higher coupled relations.

2. Relative capacity

The relative theory uses the full family of normalized classical plurisubharmonic testers. Its next regularity question is whether the outer capacity is inner regular on ordinary Borel sets. A positive argument should control the Borel remainder uniformly over this tester family and extend the estimate through exhaustion.

A companion problem asks which geometric hypotheses recover the intended compact masses and detect canonical representatives. These questions build on the CLN estimates, quasicontinuity and qualified comparison results. The existing counterexamples to universal raw-to-outer equality and strong subadditivity guide the choice of additional hypotheses.

3. Measures and source equations

The strong first-energy completion and its energy-difference norm provide the setting for measure equations. The continuity question concerns a fixed background: for a positive Radon measure satisfying the null-set, integrability and boundedness hypotheses of Question 6.1, does strong convergence of potentials imply convergence in L1(μ)L^1(\mu)L1(μ)? Fixed-background uniform integrability, or the stated ordered-rooftop and finite-minorant estimates, would supply a route.

Nonlinear solvability also requires membership in the source image. The variational projection and normal-probe criterion identify the remaining compatibility condition. The next task is to recognize substantial compatible classes directly from the datum and known reference geometry: analyze the linearization at a known source, prove inverse estimates and control the nonlinear boundary and moment constraints. Such classes can be studied before the general continuity question is settled.

  • Problem 6.3 · Checkable compatible source classes

4. Domains and geometric applications

The broader theory requires domain and boundary conditions that preserve Green formulas, mass and the specified energy completion under exhaustion. It also requires representative theorems connecting completed potentials to classical plurisubharmonic functions, and invariance results for relative singularity types.

  • Problem 7.1 · Domain and boundary extension
  • Problem 7.2 · Classical representatives of completed classes
  • Problem 7.3 · Relative singularity invariants

The applications seek finite-energy Green products and local intersection pairings, with approximation independence and comparison to the smooth, formal and delta-form theories. Adelic applications add placewise summability and explicit boundary conditions. Concrete next steps are a controlled class of Green expressions, a local metric-change formula and a global domination estimate compatible with the product formula.
