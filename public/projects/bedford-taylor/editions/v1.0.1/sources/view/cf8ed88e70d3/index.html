A differential module with actual piecewise smooth coefficients

All currents and tests in this section are original CLD objects over the fixed K/QpK/\mathbf Q_pK/Qp​ with its original absolute value. Piecewise smooth (PS) means the actual codimension-zero sheaf, with tangential matching and the full actual moment equivalence, as in the frozen R6 PS multiplication theorem. It does not mean an arbitrary Borel coefficient array. Backgrounds remain arbitrary original symmetric middle-positive separately closed currents.

The inputs used below are precisely: the frozen R4 uniform normal-wall variation estimate for positive separately closed currents; the frozen weighted joint bounded BT theorem; the R6 canonical PS multiplication and tangency theorem; and the codimension-zero case of the R6 anchored Courant presentation. No analytic positive-codimension relation quotient is assumed.

The exact frozen pins are thm:r6-delta-module-generation in delta_module_generation.tex, lem:r6-level-tangency and thm:r6-pws-multiplication in pws_tangent_extension.tex, lem:r4-wall-normal-factor and thm:r4-piecewise-test-continuity in potential_quotient_next.tex, and thm:relative-monotone in the frozen foundational relative_monotone.tex. Their full archived paths, SHA256 values, and exact uses are recorded in PWS_DEPENDENCY_CHECK.json.

The exact limits of the extension.

The argument proves a differential module and corrected insertion, not OPEN-GQ. A finite PS/PL response belongs to this module once its pure-cycle slots have their prescribed bounded presentations. It does not follow that a zero actual positive-codimension delta-form has zero response. In particular no equality on an auxiliary-field cap background is inferred from an unproved analytic quotient. The smooth freezing argument alone does not transfer ordinary order on an arbitrary nonclosed PS response: piecewise constant coefficient backgrounds are generally not separately closed. Theorem thm:r10-ps-module-positivity resolves this finite actual PS positivity question by a genuinely closed conormal penalty, followed by exact tangency on each carrier. No claim of signed actual-relation descent follows from that order theorem.

The scalar class cannot be enlarged silently to arbitrary Borel coefficient functions. On an original annulus let ξ\xiξ be its unit Gauss point, S=[U]S=[U]S=[U], and u=(log⁡∣T∣)+u=(\log|T|)_+u=(log∣T∣)+​. Original smooth top-form integration has no atom at ξ\xiξ, so 1{ξ}S=0\mathbf1_{\{\xi\}}S=01{ξ}​S=0, whereas 1{ξ}D(uS)=δξ\mathbf1_{\{\xi\}}D(uS)=\delta_\xi1{ξ}​D(uS)=δξ​. A coefficient-linear extension over all Borel witnesses would therefore send the same zero current to both zero and a nonzero measure. This is a concrete FALSE extension, not a GQ counterexample. In (P5), the Borel point values of bounded classical weights are admitted only through the frozen weighted BT theorem.

\subsection{A simultaneous smoothing estimate with moving closed tails}

On a compact stage in one original moment presentation, the frozen

\[

\]
where the $\lambda_i$ are continuous real PL functions in those
moments and $F$ is smooth on a neighborhood of the relevant finite
coordinate image.  Finite sums and original smooth coefficient forms
are allowed.  The latter can also be included by adding the original
affine moment coordinates to the list in (P1).

codimension-zero case, every lifted constant full-dimensional
balanced cycle on a connected real neighborhood is a constant
multiple of the ambient integration current.  Its printed anchored
coefficient is $g(\lambda_A)d'_P\lambda_I\wedge d''_P\lambda_J$,
which is (P1).  Its Courant functions are continuous PL but need not
be convex.  Refining by their finitely many walls and adding a
sufficiently large positive sum of absolute affine wall functions
writes each one locally as
\[
                         \lambda_i=\lambda_i^+-\lambda_i^-,
 \tag{P2}
\]
with both $\lambda_i^\pm$ convex PL.  Indeed their gradient jumps
are bounded normal multiples along finitely many walls; a large
sum of absolute wall functions makes every jump nonnegative.
Convexity on all segments then proves (P2).  This is the PL case
of the frozen R6 continuous-PS difference lemma.

Use one nonnegative compact smooth convolution kernel of total mass
one and zero first moment, with radius $\varepsilon$, for all real representatives on a
slightly larger stage.  Write
$\lambda_{i,\varepsilon}^\pm=\lambda_i^\pm*\rho_\varepsilon$ and
$\lambda_{i,\varepsilon}=\lambda_{i,\varepsilon}^+
                       -\lambda_{i,\varepsilon}^-$.
Each positive row is smooth convex and decreases as
$\varepsilon\downarrow0$, by the convexity/Jensen argument already
printed for PL slots.  The signed row is the convolution of
$\lambda_i$ itself.  It converges locally uniformly, and all its
first coefficient derivatives have one compact-stage bound.
Let $\alpha_\varepsilon$ be the smooth form obtained from (P1)
by these replacements.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform normal-factor error for the differentiated rows]
\label{lem:r10-pws-word-uniform-error}
Fix a compact original test stage, a PS form $\alpha$ as above,
and an original compact smooth complementary form $\eta$.
There are finitely many fixed original positive compact smooth
controls $\Theta_l$ and a constant $C$ such that, for every
allowed positive separately closed diagonal current $T$ of the
degree needed to pair $\alpha\wedge T$ with $\eta$,
\[
 \left|\mu_{(\alpha_\varepsilon-\alpha)\wedge T,\eta}\right|
        (\operatorname{supp}\eta)
 \le C\varepsilon^{1/2}\sum_l\langle T,\Theta_l\rangle.
 \tag{P3}
\]
The same estimate holds after multiplication by a Borel scalar
of absolute value at most one.  It applies to each word arising
by differentiating the coefficient part of (P1) once or twice.
All actual refinements are allowed by making a common original
moment presentation with the chosen test.
\end{lemma}

\noindent\textbf{Status: \texttt{DIRECT\_COROLLARY}} of the
frozen R4 normal-factor variation estimate, with the required
special smoothing rows verified here.

\begin{proof}
Refine the finitely many PL subdivisions by one finite arrangement
of affine hyperplanes $\ell_H=0$.  Choose on each face an ambient
gradient from any incident chamber.  These are legitimate PS
representatives by the frozen tangency theorem.

Across a facet, continuity of $\lambda_i$ makes the difference
of its two constant gradients a scalar multiple of $d\ell_H$.
Compare two points at distance at most $\varepsilon$ by the
segment between them.  The successive gradient changes are
bounded normal multiples at the finitely many crossed walls.
Every crossed wall satisfies $|\ell_H(x)|\le C_H\varepsilon$
at the initial point $x$.  At a point on several walls the
same statement holds after choosing an incident chamber; its
ambiguity is a sum of the same conormals.  Averaging the
gradient differences against the kernel therefore gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 d'\lambda_{i,\varepsilon}-d'_P\lambda_i
       &=\sum_H b'_{i,H,\varepsilon}\,d'\ell_H,\\
 d''\lambda_{i,\varepsilon}-d''_P\lambda_i
       &=\sum_H b''_{i,H,\varepsilon}\,d''\ell_H,
 \end{split}
 \tag{P4}
\]
where the Borel coefficients have a common bound and are supported
in the indicated shrinking wall tubes.  All identities are
understood modulo the face conormal ambiguity, which every
allowed $T$ annihilates before Borel restriction.

The scalar functions $\lambda_{i,\varepsilon}-\lambda_i$ are
$O(\varepsilon)$ uniformly, so smoothness of $F$ on a compact
coordinate neighborhood gives the same bound for their scalar
coefficient error.  Expand the difference of the finite wedge
products in (P1) by replacing one factor at a time.  It is a sum
of an $O(\varepsilon)$ bounded coefficient form and bounded
forms with one factor $d'\ell_H$ or $d''\ell_H$, supported in
the corresponding wall tube.  All other first-derivative
coefficients are uniformly bounded.  Multiplication by the fixed
smooth $\eta$ preserves this description.

Original coefficient domination bounds the first part by
$C\varepsilon\sum_l\langle T,\Theta_l\rangle$.  The R4
normal-factor variation estimate bounds every remaining part by
$C\varepsilon^{1/2}\sum_l\langle T,\Theta_l\rangle$.
That frozen estimate is a total-variation estimate and already
permits arbitrary bounded Borel coefficients in its other
factors.  It therefore proves (P3) and its scalar-weight version.
If the complementary degree of $T$ is zero, only scalar words
can occur, and uniform scalar convergence proves the estimate
without a wall argument.

Replacing $F$ by one of its finitely many derivatives and
deleting or adding finitely many first-derivative letters gives
exactly the same coefficient description.  This proves the
assertion needed for once- and twice-differentiated words.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Weighted BT convergence against these moving PS rows]
\label{lem:r10-weighted-pws-moving-tails}
Let $T_m$ be any positive mixed bounded BT tail over a fixed
allowed background, whose finitely many classical slots decrease
to locally bounded limits, and let $T$ be the limiting tail.
Let $u_m\downarrow u$ be bounded classical weights with locally
bounded limit; a fixed bounded classical $u_m=u$ is allowed.
Let $\alpha_{\varepsilon_m}$ be one of the rows just constructed,
where $\varepsilon_m\downarrow0$.  Then
\[
             u_m\alpha_{\varepsilon_m}\wedge T_m
                          \longrightarrow u\alpha\wedge T
 \tag{P5}
\]
on every original compact smooth complementary test.
The assertion also holds with the fixed PS coefficient $\alpha$
in place of its moving row.
\end{lemma}

\noindent\textbf{Status: \texttt{NEW\_PROVED}} as the
weighted PS convergence used in the intrinsic extension.

\begin{proof}
Unweighted frozen BT convergence bounds the finitely many
positive controls of $T_m$ in (P3), uniformly in $m$.
The weights have a common finite compact-stage bound.
Thus (P3) shows that replacing
$\alpha_{\varepsilon_m}$ by $\alpha$ in (P5) has error tending
to zero, even after multiplication by $u_m$.

To handle the fixed $\alpha$, choose another fixed smoothing
parameter $\delta>0$.  The test
$\alpha_\delta\wedge\eta$ is original smooth, so the frozen
weighted joint theorem gives
\[
 \langle u_m\alpha_\delta\wedge T_m,\eta\rangle
                  \longrightarrow
 \langle u\alpha_\delta\wedge T,\eta\rangle.
\]
The errors in replacing $\alpha_\delta$ by $\alpha$ on either
side are uniformly $O(\delta^{1/2})$, by (P3) and the common
weight and control bounds.  First let $m\to\infty$ and then
$\delta\downarrow0$.  This proves both assertions.  In
particular the passage with a possibly discontinuous classical
weight uses the frozen weighted BT theorem; it is not inferred
from weak convergence of Radon measures against Borel tests.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Intrinsic derivatives and the corrected operator}

\begin{definition}[The full PS coefficient module over allowed backgrounds]
\label{def:r10-full-pws-background-module}
For $0\le a,b\le n$, let $\mathscr N_{\rm PS}^{a,b}$ consist
of original currents locally admitting a finite expression
\[
                         R=\sum_{\nu=1}^N\alpha_\nu\wedge S_\nu,
 \tag{P6}
\]
where each $S_\nu$ is an allowed arbitrary original positive
separately closed current and $\alpha_\nu$ is an actual PS form
of the needed bidegree.  Products in (P6) are the frozen
canonical PS products.  Equality means equality of original
currents on all original tests, not equality of witnesses.
Neither symmetry nor separate closedness of $R$ is assumed.
\end{definition}

Every such $R$ belongs to the frozen tangential Radon module,
even when it is not closed.  Indeed, write a PS multiplier by
its finite smooth-face/Borel-stratum formula.  For every original
smooth level $h=c$, both conormals annihilate the corresponding
restriction of each closed $S_\nu$ before any face restriction.
Bounded coefficient multiplication and further Borel restriction
preserve these zero measures, so the same conormal identities
hold for $R$.  The R6 PS multiplication theorem consequently
applies to $R$ itself.  Its finite face formula is intrinsic:
a zero coefficient-Radon current has zero coefficient measures,
which remain zero after each bounded coefficient multiplication.
Thus further PS multiplication and associativity do not depend
on a witness (P6), and do not require $R$ to be separately closed.

\begin{theorem}[Derivative-Radon closure with every interface retained]
\label{thm:r10-pws-derivative-radon-closure}
The module $\mathscr N_{\rm PS}$ is stable under actual PS
exterior coefficients, $d'$, and $d''$.  In particular
\[
                        R,\quad d'R,\quad d''R,\quad DR
                   \quad\hbox{are coefficient-Radon currents}.
 \tag{P7}
\]
Their derivatives are their intrinsic original distribution
derivatives.  They can be computed in any local anchored
presentation by the usual graded product rule, using the
following replacements for every continuous PL generator
$\lambda=\lambda^+-\lambda^-$ over a closed tail $T$:
\[
 \begin{array}{c|cc}
 &d'&d''\\ \hline
 \lambda&d'_P\lambda&d''_P\lambda\\
 d'_P\lambda&0&-D(\lambda T)\\
 d''_P\lambda&D(\lambda T)&0 .
 \end{array}
 \tag{P8}
\]
In the bottom two rows the entry means insertion into that
word and absorption of the curvature into the closed tail;
$D(\lambda T)=D(\lambda^+T)-D(\lambda^-T)$.
Several curvature factors mean the frozen mixed BT products,
expanded linearly in their positive and negative slots.
All identities hold on every original test and every actual
moment refinement.  Degrees outside $0,\ldots,n$ are zero.
\end{theorem}

\noindent\textbf{Status: \texttt{NEW\_PROVED}} for the
intrinsic differential module and its interface formulas.
The existence of the undifferentiated module is
\texttt{INTERNAL\_INPUT} from R6.

\begin{proof}
PS multiplication and its associativity are frozen inputs.
Consider one word $\alpha\wedge S$ in (P6), using (P1)--(P2),
and its smooth row $R_m=\alpha_{\varepsilon_m}\wedge S$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:r10-pws-word-uniform-error} gives
$R_m\to R$ in local coefficient variation on every fixed test.

Differentiate the smooth word once or twice.  A coefficient
derivative of $F$ creates another first-derivative letter.
Differentiating $d'\lambda_m$ by $d''$ gives
$-D\lambda_m$, and differentiating $d''\lambda_m$ by $d'$
gives $D\lambda_m$.  The other same-prime derivatives and
all derivatives of curvature factors are zero.  After
expanding (P2), every term in $d'R_m$, $d''R_m$, or $DR_m$
is another row of the type in Lemma
~\ref{lem:r10-pws-word-uniform-error} multiplied by a positive
closed BT tail over $S$, with at most two inserted convex
PL smoothing slots.  The signs are exactly the usual graded
signs; no derivative of a Borel face indicator is discarded.

Lemma~\ref{lem:r10-weighted-pws-moving-tails}, with weight
one, gives a coefficient-Radon limit for each finite term.
It is its stated PS coefficient times the bounded BT tail
of the limiting PL potentials.  Distributional differentiation
is continuous on the original test space, so these limits are
precisely $d'R$, $d''R$, and $DR$.  Their finite expressions
belong to $\mathscr N_{\rm PS}$, because every positive
mixed tail is again an allowed closed background.  This also
proves (P8).

The intrinsic distribution derivative is independent of every
choice of representative, subdivision, DC splitting, and
smoothing row.  The finite calculation therefore proves
the asserted independence rather than assuming it.
The frozen anchored presentation and PS multiplication apply
after every common actual refinement.  Localizing on compact
supports and gluing gives the full original test statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Intrinsic corrected bounded insertion on the PS module]
\label{thm:r10-pws-corrected-bounded-insertion}
For $R\in\mathscr N_{\rm PS}^{a,b}$ and an original locally
bounded classical psh function $u$, with
$\max(a,b)+1\le n$, the intrinsic distribution
\[
 \mathfrak B_uR
    =D(uR)-d'(u\,d''R)+d''(u\,d'R)+uDR
 \tag{P9}
\]
is a coefficient-Radon current belonging to
$\mathscr N_{\rm PS}^{a+1,b+1}$.  For every witness (P6),
\[
                   \mathfrak B_uR
                         =\sum_\nu\alpha_\nu\wedge D(uS_\nu).
 \tag{P10}
\]
In particular (P10) is independent of that witness.
It is linear over actual PS exterior coefficients, local in
the ordinary topology, and commutes with $d'$ and $d''$ in
every permitted bidegree.  Iteration gives
\[
 \mathfrak B_{u_k}\cdots\mathfrak B_{u_1}R
        =\sum_\nu\alpha_\nu\wedge
                          T_{S_\nu,k}(u_1,\ldots,u_k),
       \qquad \max(a,b)+k\le n.
 \tag{P11}
\]
These products are mixed symmetric and have joint decreasing
continuity on every original compact smooth test.
No middle-order preservation for an arbitrary positive
nonclosed member of $\mathscr N_{\rm PS}$ is asserted.
\end{theorem}

\noindent\textbf{Status: \texttt{NEW\_PROVED}} for the
PS coefficient extension, including bounded classical weights
which are not assumed continuous.

\begin{proof}
All four scalar multiplications in (P9) are intrinsic
coefficient-Radon multiplications by (P7).  Thus its left
side is an intrinsic distribution before proving the right
side.  For one witness term, use the smooth rows $R_m$
of the previous proof.  The already proved smooth corrected
Leibniz identity gives
\[
                       \mathfrak B_uR_m
                         =\alpha_{\varepsilon_m}\wedge D(uS).
 \tag{P12}
\]
On the right, the fixed positive closed current $D(uS)$
and (P3) give convergence in local coefficient variation to
$\alpha\wedge D(uS)$.

On the left, each of $R_m,d'R_m,d''R_m,DR_m$ is the finite
sum described in the derivative proof.  Lemma
~\ref{lem:r10-weighted-pws-moving-tails}, now with the
fixed classical weight $u$, proves convergence after
multiplying each of these currents by $u$.  Passing their
printed distribution derivatives to the limit proves
(P10).  This passage is the reason the normal-wall estimate
and weighted theorem were established before defining the
extension.  It does not differentiate a PS coefficient
through $S$ without accounting for its corners.

Every right side in (P10) belongs to the module.  Since the
left side depends only on $R$, representation independence
follows.  Canonical PS associativity proves PS coefficient
linearity.  Ordinary locality and compatibility with all
refinements follow from the intrinsic formula and the
already original PS products.

For completeness the differential commutation is an
intrinsic operator calculation, not an unproved PS
Leibniz rule.  The anticommutation of the two differentials
gives, with all scalar factors defined by (P7),
\[
 \begin{split}
 d'\mathfrak B_uR
       &=D(u\,d'R)+d'(uDR)=\mathfrak B_u(d'R),\\
 d''\mathfrak B_uR
       &=D(u\,d''R)+d''(uDR)=\mathfrak B_u(d''R).
 \end{split}
 \tag{P13}
\]
Module closure permits both sides.  Formula (P11) follows
by induction from (P10); every tail is positive and closed.
Frozen mixed symmetry proves its symmetry.  For a decreasing
tuple the frozen products converge with uniform compact
positive controls.  The fixed-PS version of (P5), with
weight one, gives the stated continuity.  Finite localization
handles all original test supports.
\end{proof}

\subsection{An explicit nonclosed first-derivative example}

\begin{corollary}[The full nonsymmetric PL gradient product]
\label{cor:r10-nonsymmetric-PL-gradient-product}
Let $S$ be any allowed $(p,p)$-background, $p+2\le n$,
and let $\phi,\psi$ be locally bounded convex PL functions
of original moments.  Define by canonical PS multiplication
\[
                         R=d'_P\phi\wedge d''_P\psi\wedge S.
 \tag{P14}
\]
Then all the following are intrinsic coefficient-Radon
identities on the complete original test sheaf:
\[
 \begin{split}
 d'R&=-d'_P\phi\wedge D(\psi S),\\
 d''R&=-d''_P\psi\wedge D(\phi S),\\
 DR&=-T_{S,2}(\phi,\psi),\\
 \mathfrak B_uR&=
          d'_P\phi\wedge d''_P\psi\wedge D(uS)
 \end{split}
 \tag{P15}
\]
for every locally bounded classical $u$.  No symmetry of
$R$ is required.  For $\phi=\psi$, $R$ is the positive
gradient-energy current and
\[
 R=\tfrac12D(\phi^2S)-\phi D(\phi S),\qquad
                         DR=-T_{S,2}(\phi,\phi).
 \tag{P16}
\]
The first equality in (P16) is the frozen R6 scalar PS
product rule; the new assertion is stability under the
intrinsic operator with every printed derivative retained.
\end{corollary}

\noindent\textbf{Status: \texttt{DIRECT\_COROLLARY}} of
the two new module theorems and the indicated frozen energy
identity.

\begin{proof}
Apply (P8) to the two first-derivative letters, remembering
their total degree one.  This gives the first three lines
of (P15), including the negative sign in $DR$.
Formula (P10) gives the last line.  The frozen scalar
PS Leibniz formula for $\phi^2$ gives (P16).
All background products in the formulas are the existing
bounded BT products over the same arbitrary $S$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The exact limits of the extension.}
The argument proves a differential module and corrected
insertion, not OPEN-GQ.  A finite PS/PL response belongs to
this module once its pure-cycle slots have their prescribed
bounded presentations.  It does not follow that a zero
actual positive-codimension delta-form has zero response.
In particular no equality on an auxiliary-field cap
background is inferred from an unproved analytic quotient.
The smooth freezing argument alone does not transfer ordinary
order on an arbitrary nonclosed PS response: piecewise constant
coefficient backgrounds are generally not separately closed.
Theorem~\ref{thm:r10-ps-module-positivity} resolves this finite
actual PS positivity question by a genuinely closed conormal
penalty, followed by exact tangency on each carrier.  No claim
of signed actual-relation descent follows from that order theorem.

The scalar class cannot be enlarged silently to arbitrary
Borel coefficient functions.  On an original annulus let
$\xi$ be its unit Gauss point, $S=[U]$, and
$u=(\log|T|)_+$.  Original smooth top-form integration has
no atom at $\xi$, so $\mathbf1_{\{\xi\}}S=0$, whereas
$\mathbf1_{\{\xi\}}D(uS)=\delta_\xi$.  A coefficient-linear
extension over all Borel witnesses would therefore send
the same zero current to both zero and a nonzero measure.
This is a concrete \texttt{FALSE} extension, not a GQ
counterexample.  In (P5), the Borel point values of bounded
classical weights are admitted only through the frozen
weighted BT theorem.
