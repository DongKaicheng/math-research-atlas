Complex geometry ↔ Non-Archimedean geometry

The complex comparison

What carries over, what has been constructed, and where the analogy needs new hypotheses.

In place: the bounded calculus and a relative finite-energy framework.

Still to bridge: full analytic-relation descent, general relative capacity regularity and data-side source compatibility.

Scope of the comparison

The complex column distinguishes local domains from compact Kähler manifolds. The non-Archimedean bounded theory starts with classical psh potentials over the original finite extension K/QpK/\mathbf Q_pK/Qp​ and a fixed symmetric, middle-positive CLD background SSS, separately closed under d′d'd′ and d′′d''d′′. The bounded complex theory also allows a fixed positive closed background; its ambient capacity theorems do not automatically transfer to arbitrary relative backgrounds.

Energy and source results use the specified Laurent domains, boundary assumptions and relative completions. “Partial correspondence” includes proved components together with the indicated gap; it does not retract those components. Status refers to this comparison, with the original source-qualified claims and proofs linked below.

Local regularization

Complex theory

On relatively compact coordinate subdomains, psh functions admit decreasing smooth psh regularizations.

DEM · §1, proof of Proposition 1.2
This projectEstablished in scope

Locally bounded classical psh potentials admit decreasing semipositive rational PL approximation over the original field, on one fixed ordinary neighbourhood.

Regularization theorem
Gap or boundary

The decreasing approximants are rational PL; continuous stages also admit smooth positive approximation. This does not classify all current-positive functions as classical psh.

Scope & source records

The fixed-neighbourhood construction retains the original finite extension K/QpK/\mathbf Q_pK/Qp​ and its norm. The separate continuous current-positive regularization input has its own algebraic analytification hypotheses; it is not silently identified with this result.

Mixed products and continuity

Complex theory

Locally bounded psh potentials have positive, symmetric mixed products against a fixed positive closed current, with decreasing continuity.

BT82 · Theorems 2.1, 2.4; Corollary 2.2DEM · Theorem 1.7; Corollary 1.10
This projectEstablished in scope

The fixed-background calculus is positive, separately closed, symmetric and local, with CLN estimates and weighted joint decreasing continuity.

Bounded-products theorem
Gap or boundary

The weight is also a decreasing classical psh potential. Discontinuous potentials with an arbitrary moving background are not covered.

Scope & source records

The background is fixed, symmetric, middle-positive and separately d′- and d″-closed. The original compact smooth CLD tests are retained. The theorem includes the undifferentiated psh weight; it is not continuity for arbitrary moving Borel weights.

Capacity and quasicontinuity

Complex theory

A psh function is continuous outside open sets of arbitrarily small ambient Bedford–Taylor capacity.

BT82 · Theorem 3.5
This projectEstablished in scope

Relative quasicontinuity and bounded decreasing convergence in capacity are established for the specified potential class and background.

Relative quasicontinuity
Gap or boundary

Relative capacity depends on the background and tester family. It is not automatically the ambient capacity of the complex benchmark.

Scope & source records

The relative theory uses the full family of normalized classical psh testers, not a selected finite test family. The ambient S=[1] theory and arbitrary-background relative theory are kept distinct.

Borel regularity and extremals

Complex theory

On bounded strongly pseudoconvex domains, ambient outer capacity is Choquet; compact capacity equals the extremal Monge–Ampère mass.

BT82 · Theorem 8.2; Propositions 5.3, 6.4
This projectPartial correspondence

The qualified ambient Choquet theory is available. Relative inner regularity is proved for specified finite-Hausdorff saturated sets and controlled backgrounds.

Ambient Choquet resultRelative positive cases
Gap or boundary

Borel inner regularity of relative outer capacity remains open. Universal raw-to-outer equality and extremal total-mass formulas are false.

CAP-BOREL · exact questionCapacity counterexamples
Scope & source records

The relative positive case has remaining degree q=1 on a torus box, with S₀=βⁿ⁻¹[X] and fully saturated inverse images of Borel moment sets of finite (n−1)-dimensional Hausdorff measure, plus the stated one-sided background comparison. It is not a theorem for arbitrary Borel sets. Classical Borel capacitability is compact inner regularity of outer capacity; that is the open target, not raw=outer on every set.

Polar sets and comparison

Complex theory

On bounded strongly pseudoconvex domains, ambient outer-null sets are pluripolar. Bounded psh functions satisfy comparison and domination with boundary hypotheses.

BT82 · Theorems 6.9, 4.1; Corollary 4.5
This projectPartial correspondence

For S=[1], local polar, outer-null and negligible sets coincide. Compact-boundary-gap comparison and the specified relative domination principles are proved.

Ambient polar equivalenceRelative comparison
Gap or boundary

An arbitrary background may charge ambient polar sets. Pointwise detection from a relative capacity class needs additional geometry.

The S=[1] equivalence is local; the Choquet and extremal package retains its exhaustion-domain assumptions. The relative domination proposition initially gives a raw-capacity conclusion: it must not be relabelled as outer-null without an additional argument.

C02C03

Unbounded, non-polar products

Complex theory

On compact Kähler manifolds, non-pluripolar products are well-defined and closed. Monotone Monge–Ampère continuity is available in the full-mass class.

BEGZ10 · Proposition 1.6; Theorems 1.8, 2.17
This projectLocal construction established

Common truncations yield closed non-polar products when coefficient masses are locally finite. In the stated boundary class, no loss is characterized by a capacity tail.

Relative products and energy
Gap or boundary

The local relative construction does not automatically supply the global compact-Kähler finiteness package. Finiteness and no-loss assumptions matter on both sides.

Scope & source records

Within the stated zero-boundary class, no loss is equivalent to tqCS({u<−t};V)→0t^q C_S(\{u<-t\};V)\to0tqCS​({u<−t};V)→0. The negative sublevels must have ordinary closures compact in V. Closedness belongs to the locally coefficient-finite limit, not necessarily to each restricted truncation current. BEGZ Example 1.3 also excludes unrestricted local unbounded products in complex geometry.

Finite energy and completion

Complex theory

On a compact connected Kähler manifold, (E1,d1)(\mathcal E^1,d_1)(E1,d1​) is the metric completion of smooth Kähler potentials.

DAR15 · Theorem 4.17
This projectRelative completion established

Power-energy classes and mixed Green–Hölder estimates are constructed. The relative strong first-energy completion has an intrinsic Borel realization and is complete.

Strong-energy theoremEnergy estimates
Gap or boundary

Canonical ambient classical psh representatives and extension beyond the specified domains remain separate problems; the completion alone does not supply them.

Representative problemDomain problem
Scope & source records

The domain is a strict Laurent box with closure compact inside an affinoid interior and a background extending to an ordinary neighbourhood of that closure. Strong convergence is capacity convergence plus energy convergence. All positive power classes and the stated weighted-current degree budgets are retained. Order completion of currents, strong completion of potentials and capacity-Borel realization are not identified.

The energy-difference space is Banach. Strong literal integral continuity is proved under fixed-source domination or the stated capacity-control hypotheses.

Fixed-source continuity
Gap or boundary

For fixed S, does global literal energy-dual boundedness imply strong L1(μ)L^1(\mu)L1(μ)-continuity? The source-class benchmark does not answer this larger question.

The question assumes a positive Radon measure annihilating the relative null ideal, literal integrability of every completed Borel difference, and a bounded integral functional on the entire energy-difference Banach space. Total mass need not be finite. Nonliteral functionals, bounds on only one witness sequence, and changing-background examples do not refute this fixed-background question.

Prescribing a source measure

Complex theory

In a fixed compact Kähler class, a normalized finite-energy measure is the Monge–Ampère measure of a unique finite-energy current.

BBGZ13 · Theorem A; Theorem 4.7
This projectPartial correspondence

The source image is norm closed. Strongly continuous data have a unique variational projection. For such literal positive data, the equation holds exactly when Ξ=0\Xi=0Ξ=0.

Source theoremScalar defect criterion
Gap or boundary

Data-side conditions forcing Ξ=0\Xi=0Ξ=0 remain to be found beyond compatible classes. Smooth positive perturbations need not remain in the source image.

SOURCE-COMPAT · compatibility problemPerturbation counterexample
Scope & source records

Here the positive Radon datum defines a literal, norm-bounded integral functional on all completed differences, with a strongly continuous restriction to the cone. The defect is evaluated using the variational projection; its vanishing is an exact criterion, not an independent test already computable from arbitrary data. A variational maximizer need not satisfy the equation in the prescribed potential cone. Source compatibility is separate from strong integral continuity.

Boundary-value problems

Complex theory

On bounded strictly pseudoconvex domains, continuous boundary data and a nonnegative continuous density admit a unique continuous psh Monge–Ampère solution.

BT76 · Theorem D
This projectLinear case only

For remaining degree q=1, finite signed energy-diffuse data have a unique renormalized zero-boundary solution in the faithful part-energy space.

Linear Dirichlet theorem
Gap or boundary

Not the same equation: this is a signed linear problem, not a general nonlinear Monge–Ampère boundary-value theorem.

Scope & source records

Here q=n−p=1 is the remaining degree, not a restriction to ambient dimension one. The faithful, regular, strongly local, coercive part-form hypotheses and energy-capacity diffuseness are retained. The solution space is not identified with the classical psh cone or the nonlinear strong first-energy cone; clipping and total-variation stability hold in the stated linear space.

Q05Q06

Limits of the analogy

What does not hold in general

Four precise strengthenings with internal counterexamples. They are not four failures of the entire theory, nor four claims that are all classical theorems.

01

Unconditional relative capacity identities

False in general

Raw capacity need not equal its outer regularization, even on compact sets. A universal extremal total-mass formula also fails.

Raw versus outer · N01Extremal mass · N03
What the example does—and does not—show

This contrasts with the stated ambient complex theorem, not with every complex relative capacity. The ratio in the internal mass example varies with scale.

02

Strong subadditivity of relative outer capacity

False in general

An original torus example with q=1 and n≥2 violates strong subadditivity. Ordinary subadditivity survives.

Counterexample · N02The Borel question
What the example does—and does not—show

This does not settle Borel inner regularity. No general classical strong-subadditivity theorem is being asserted here.

03

A universal coefficient-one rooftop bound

False in general

For the project’s rooftop energy inequality in remaining degree one, constant 1 is false; constant 2 is sharp across allowed backgrounds.

Sharp obstruction · N04Full example

False in general

A fixed strict source admits smooth positive density perturbations converging uniformly to 1 that lie outside the strong-completion source image.
