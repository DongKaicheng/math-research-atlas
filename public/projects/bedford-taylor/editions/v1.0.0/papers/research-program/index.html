Abstract

The local Bedford–Taylor construction with a positive closed background provides a common setting for curvature products, relative capacity, finite energy and measure equations on Berkovich spaces. We formulate a program for extending this construction to an intrinsic calculus and for determining the correct range of its potential theory. Its principal conjecture asserts that every actual analytic relation between admissible finite PL or piecewise-smooth curvature products acts trivially on every allowed background. The quadratic problem has a degree-one transgression; it is settled for finite presentations of residue rank at most two. On supplied smooth normal-three model germs, the remaining action is a central coefficient expressed by a paired spherical tensor and a realized positive closed tangent current. We use these results to identify the next geometric cancellation problem. Two further questions concern Borel regularity of relative outer capacity and strong continuity of literal energy-dual measures. The nonlinear equation is organized around its closed source image and the exact normal-probe obstruction. Explicit counterexamples determine which capacity, rooftop and solvability principles can belong to the theory. We conclude with precise extension problems for domains, singularities, Green currents and arithmetic intersection theory.

1 Introduction

The Bedford–Taylor operation begins with a simple recursion. If a potential can be multiplied by a closed current, its curvature can be inserted by applying a second derivative. The force of the construction comes from proving that the resulting product depends on the curvature, is symmetric in its differentiated slots, and survives monotone limits. In the non-Archimedean setting these requirements interact with an additional geometric issue: a differential expression is represented in local moment coordinates, and a relation can become zero only after it is pulled back to the analytic space. Such a relation must remain zero when an arbitrary positive closed background is inserted.

The differential forms and currents of Chambert-Loir–Ducros provide the analytic language for this question [1, 2]. Delta-forms retain polyhedral boundary terms and connect local calculations with tropical and arithmetic intersection theory [3, 4]. The global theory of semipositive metrics and finite-energy potentials provides a second source of constructions and questions [5, 6, 7]. The program developed here starts from the established local calculus with a fixed positive closed background and asks how far its intrinsic differential, capacitary and variational structures extend.

Recent tropical work makes the equation side of this distinction particularly concrete. Amini–Piquerez–Wu develop regularization, Bedford–Taylor measures and energy on compact prekähler tropical varieties, and prove failure of the tropical Calabi–Yau theorem when a codimension-two star satisfies their genericity condition [11, Theorems 5.13 and 11.4]. Their comparison with non-Archimedean solutions uses a strongly faithful tropicalization and specified support hypotheses [11, Theorem 12.11]. These results make source compatibility and the analytic realization of tropical data central questions in the present program.

The choice of background is essential. A current of positive degree can detect a small analytic set and can impose additional restrictions on the sources of a Monge–Ampère operator. Consequently, the relative theory contains more than a change of notation in the ambient theory. Its basic objects are a potential class, an allowed background, a specified capacity, and, for a global equation, a specified boundary completion. We keep these objects explicit throughout.

1.2 Three interacting directions

The intrinsic relation problem is one branch of the program. A second branch asks which regularity and comparison principles belong to the relative capacity defined by the full class of normalized classical testers. A third studies literal measures on the strong energy completion and the image of its source map. These branches share the bounded calculus, but their remaining arguments have different inputs. The capacity and source questions can be studied with the existing potential calculus while relation descent remains open.

This distinction determines the form of the statements below. Conjecture 1.3 is a positive structural conjecture. Borel inner regularity of relative outer capacity and literal energy-dual continuity are posed as questions: their full original formulations remain undecided. Nonlinear source compatibility is a classification problem, because positive smooth density perturbations can already leave the source image. The examples in Section 5 and Section 6 are therefore part of the mathematical specification of the program.

2.1 Bounded products and the curvature quotient

The scalar input identifies locally bounded classical psh functions with limits of decreasing semipositive rational PL sequences on a single fixed ordinary neighborhood [14]. In the finite continuous setting, current positivity is also related to locally uniform smooth positive approximation in the geometric scope of [13]. These regularizations serve different purposes: decreasing approximation constructs bounded products, whereas uniform approximation is suited to continuous coefficients and moving continuous data.

For comparison, Wu constructs top-degree local mixed Monge–Ampère measures for his locally PL-regularizable bounded psh class, with weighted convergence for decreasing PL approximating rows [10, Theorem 4.2, pp. 25–26]. The dependence of a regularizing neighborhood on the member of a general decreasing psh sequence is isolated in his Remark 4.6. His Appendix A compares the one-step smooth- and PL-approximable classes; comparison with the iterated CLD closure uses an envelope property [10, Remark A.2 and Theorem A.3, pp. 52–53]. 8 A proof architecture for the program

The program has three immediate mathematical targets. The relation branch seeks cancellation of the critical pairing (4.11), followed by a return from supplied normal-three models to general ordinary germs and by treatment of higher coupled relations. The capacity branch seeks the uniform tester remainder estimates needed for (5.3). The equation branch seeks fixed-background control sufficient for (6.4) and independent conditions describing the closed source image.

These targets suggest distinct forms of progress. For the first, one needs an actual geometric wall-flux identity, an actual full-exact construction, or a counterexample retaining the complete original analytic relation. For the second, a positive result should exhibit a remainder estimate uniform in the original normalized testers; a negative result should use those same testers and ordinary Borel sets. For the third, an existence theorem should derive compatibility from data and known reference geometry, while an obstruction should identify the exact constraint that the datum violates.

The foundations support these directions without a circular dependence. The bounded recursion is available before the analytic relation conjecture. The capacities and strong energy spaces are built from that recursion. Literal continuity would enlarge the data to which the variational projection applies; it would not remove its normal residual. Source compatibility is a further condition. Conversely, explicit source classes can be constructed and analyzed without first settling literal continuity for every energy-dual datum.

A complete theory in this sense would give a coherent account of objects, operations, limits and compatible equations. It would include the nonexistence and strict-inequality phenomena already described here, together with the hypotheses that replace the false universal assertions. The present conjectures and problems specify the mathematical steps from the established potential calculus to that theory.
