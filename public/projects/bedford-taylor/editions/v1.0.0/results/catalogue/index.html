Regularization and analytic foundations

11 records
R01

Fixed-neighborhood regularization of continuous current-positive functions

For a finite continuous function, positivity as an original CLD current is equivalent to local uniform approximation by smooth functions with positive curvature on one fixed ordinary neighborhood. After one further shrinking, the approximation can be chosen decreasing and uniform.

R02

Contact rigidity and curve detection

On a smooth projective Y with an ample line bundle, a uniformly Fubini–Study lower metric and a finite continuous curve-positive upper metric coincide if the upper metric dominates the lower one and their contact integral against the lower Monge–Ampère measure vanishes.

A single normalized complete-intersection curve realizes the specified approximation of Green pairings and measures while preserving the original valuation and normalization.

R04

Envelopes on regular models and the curve criterion

Under the regular-model hypotheses, the envelope is continuous and orthogonal, one-sided Fubini–Study obstacles have the stated O(1/m) approximation, and projective semipositivity is detected on curves.

R05

Continuous potentials with moving closed positive backgrounds

Recursive mixed products of continuous current-positive potentials and symmetric closed positive backgrounds are positive, closed and symmetric in the differentiated slots. Locally uniform convergence of the potentials and weak convergence of the backgrounds give the stated joint continuity for nets and independence of curvature representatives.

N04

Constant one fails for the rooftop energy inequality. Across the specified backgrounds, constant two is sharp, already in an actual two-dimensional q=1 model with a smooth strongly positive background.

N09

N10

Limits of finite unit lifting and Hensel boundary extension

Finite unit lifts do not give a residue-field section preserving every algebraic relation. Norm recognition on a chosen Hensel root domain does not give continuous extension over the whole central fiber; the V²−V−A example separates these claims.

N11

Positivity, closedness, bounded piecewise-smooth data and point support do not suffice to eliminate a critical central mass. Gauss atoms also obstruct the proposed lower-rank mass-null argument.
